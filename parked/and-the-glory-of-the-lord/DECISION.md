# Decisions row (text for docs/decisions.md, append only)

- 2026-11-18, the arranger (Hillel lifted the pause on new songs; the 2026-11-18 batch by search demand): **And the Glory of the Lord is built, reviewed and ready to open, still hidden.** Handel's first chorus of Messiah (No. 4) in his own four voice parts, from Ebenezer Prout's full score (The Messiah, "edited, and the additional accompaniments largely re-written, by Ebenezer Prout", London: Novello, "Copyright, 1902"; University of Toronto copy, archive.org/details/messiahsacredora00hand_0, pp. 19 to 35, leaves n44 to n60; title page n6), the edition the For Unto Us arranger also read. Makers: Handel (music, died 1759, Grove), Charles Jennens (words, died 1773, DNB vol. 29), Prout (edition, died 1909, Grove vol. III); firstPublished 1902, GB. The words are Isaiah 40:5 in the King James Version, so the UK Crown letters patent question already with the lawyer (The Lord Bless You) applies. Decisions:
  - **The whole chorus, not a section.** 138 bars; with the orchestra's bars out it is 375 beats at 100, 3 minutes 45, under the app's 600-second take and the format's no limit (the longest open song, Were You There, runs 3 minutes 32). Nothing else of Handel's is cut.
  - **The orchestra left out, as in the Ave verum:** the opening ritornello (bars 1 to 10; the alto enters alone, as Handel has it) and the two interludes (bars 39 to 42 and 74 to 75) each become one bar's rest.
  - **In F, a major third under Handel's A.** No key fits all four parts to beginner voices (the soprano runs E4 to A5, the bass G sharp 2 to E4, 17 and 20 semitones). F leaves 6 notes outside the beginner ranges, A 73, A-flat 33, G 21, F sharp 13; F also takes the soprano's four bars on top F sharp (83 to 86) down to D. The app's Higher button reaches Handel's A in four taps. Advanced: Handel's melismas, entries alone, 3 minutes 45.
  - **Tempo 100**, Prout's own mark (Allegro, crotchet = 100). **The Adagio** (bars 136 to 138) is written half as slow again (1.5 times its printed values), the last chord held 4 beats, since a song has one tempo; "hath spoken it" then fits one breath (8.4 s).
  - **Words as Prout prints them:** "glo-ry of" with "ry of" on one note (Handel's elision, which Prout restored, preface p. vi); "re-veal'd," in the soprano's bar 33; the second "and" left out at bar 107, as Prout and every edition do (p. viii).
  - **Breaths:** Handel's own rests are the breaths wherever they come in time; 28 more are written in where a line ran past 10 seconds, each at a comma or before "shall be revealed" or "hath spoken it", the note before giving up a beat (0.6 s), or half a beat with the next pickup half a beat later (rule 1). None inside a melisma.
  - **Chords** from the four voices on each beat, with Handel's bass line (the orchestra's Bassi, read from the same pages) where the bass voice rests, and set by hand in the soprano's unaccompanied bars and the soprano and alto's duet (bars 84 to 88), from Handel's harmony for the same notes elsewhere.
  - **For 4 singers only**, like the Ave verum and Deep River: the parts take turns with the themes, so 2 or 3 singers would leave whole bars silent.
  Review fixes (a reviewer who didn't build it compared every voice of bars 11 to 138 with the scan, at Handel's pitch; both applied through trans.txt and rebuilt, no new parallels):
  - bass, bar 77, beat 1 ("glo-"): MIDI 52 to 54 (E3 to F sharp 3; in F, 48 to 50), the dotted crotchet on the second line, so the line falls by step as in every other "glory, the";
  - soprano, bars 100 to 101: the E5 on bar 100's third beat is tied to bar 101's first quaver (printed tie): one note of a beat and a half, not two.
  - breaths: the tenor's in bar 94 removed (not needed: 8.4 s), so its "the glory" starts on the beat; the soprano's "the mouth | of the Lord" (bar 84) moved to "together, | the mouth" (bar 83); its two in bars 122 and 125 replaced by one, "together, | For the mouth of the Lord" (bar 124), which keeps "the mouth of the Lord" whole at the cost of 5.1 s on D5 (rule 4, accepted).
  The reviewer confirmed the theme entries, the dotted "hath spo-ken" cadence of bars 127 to 128, the Adagio's proportions (2x would break rule 2 at 10.2 s), the words, and that every parallel and crossing is Handel's.
