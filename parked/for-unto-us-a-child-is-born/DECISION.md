- 2026-11-18, the arranger (Hillel lifted the pause on new songs and approved this batch by search demand): **For Unto Us a Child Is Born is built, reviewed and ready to open, still hidden.** Source: Handel, The Messiah, "edited, and the additional accompaniments largely re-written, by Ebenezer Prout", full score (London: Novello and Company, Limited, and Novello, Ewer and Co., New York; "Copyright, 1902"; plate 11570), No. 12, pp. 93 to 113, University of Toronto copy, archive.org/details/messiahsacredora00hand_0 (title page n6, the chorus n118 to n138); bars 7 to 32 also read on Prout's vocal score (messiah0000gfha_z0r4, an undated H. Makers: Handel (music and his own four parts, died 1759), Charles Jennens (words, Isaiah 9:6 in the King James Version, died 1773), Ebenezer Prout (edition, died 1909); firstPublished 1902, GB. The KJV's UK Crown note is the one already with the lawyer.
  - **The whole chorus, not a section.** The voices sing bars 7 to 91 of 105; only the orchestra's six-bar introduction and its closing bars are left out, and the rests between entries are kept as printed (no bar where all four rest). 340 beats, 4 minutes 15 at 80, under the app's 10-minute take and longer than any open song (Were You There, 3 minutes 32): a choir singer looking for "For unto us alto part" wants the whole part, and the four "Wonderful, Counsellor" calls only make sense together.
  - **4 singers only**, like Ave verum, Deep River and The Lord Bless You: a fugal chorus whose parts enter one by one; the soprano sings 11 bars alone before the tenor comes in, so 2 or 3 singers would leave whole entries and every "Wonderful" chord hollow.
  - **E-flat, a major third under Handel's G.** In G the soprano climbs to A5, the tenor to G4 and the bass to E4: 153 notes outside the beginner ranges, and the tenor's and bass's sung middles out of their comfortable halves. E-flat has the fewest (8, every one listed below); E has 27, F 40, G 153. The app's Higher moves it back up to G for a choir that sings it there.
  - **Tempo 80, against Prout's crotchet = 76.** A twentieth faster, so the longest unbroken lines stay near 8 seconds (at 76 the soprano's "and the government" line after bar 82 is 8.7 s); the semiquavers are 0.19 s.
  - **Intermediate, and the runs are hard.** The four parts' semiquaver runs on "born" (soprano bars 14 to 18 and 74 to 78, tenor 39 to 41, bass 20 to 24, alto 57 to 61 and 74 to 78) are Handel's and cannot be made easy; the description says so.
  - **Breaths (ours).** Each run is cut once, never more: the last three semiquavers of the beat before the new line become the breath and the next note re-sings the vowel, "(o)" (soprano before bar 16, bass before 22, tenor before 40, alto before 59, soprano and alto together before 76, all at the sequence's joint, the bar line); held notes give up a beat before "der" of "shoulder" (soprano bars 30 and 81, tenor 28); the alto's six-beat D on "shoul-" (bars 45 and 46) breaks after three beats and re-sings "(ou)"; and a last note is shortened before a new line (tenor bar 63, "Fa-ther," in the alto bar 36 and tenor 52, so their "Peace." keeps its full beat, "Peace." in the bass bar 72, "born" in the bass bar 24 and in soprano and alto bar 78, "Peace," in every part bar 89). Every line is now 8.2 s or less except the tenor's and bass's "and the government, the government shall be upon His shoulder; and His Name shall be called" (bars 81 to 84, 9.0 s), where Handel's line has no rest at all: kept, under singable's 10 s.
