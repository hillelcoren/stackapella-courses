- 2026-11-18, the arranger (Hillel lifted the pause on new songs; the new-song brief of 2026-11-18): **Hallelujah Chorus is built, reviewed and ready to open, still hidden** (`"reviewed": false`, `"hidden": true`). Source: Handel's Messiah, "edited, and the additional accompaniments largely re-written, by Ebenezer Prout" (London: Novello and Company; New York: Novello, Ewer and Co.; "Copyright, 1902"; plate 11570), full score, No. 44, pp. 260 to 278, Allegro, crotchet = 72, in D, read from the University of Toronto, Faculty of Music copy, archive.org/details/messiahsacredora00hand_0 (n285 to n303), every bar of the four voices compared with Prout's vocal score of the same edition (a later, undated printing with the H. W. Gray Co. as New York agents; archive.org/details/bwb_KT-315-920, pp. 149 to 157). Prout's preface (p. xv) says he restored Handel's own text from the autograph where all editions followed Walsh: "jah, Hal-" on one note (alto 24, soprano 30, bass 31 and 32) and the tenor's "and He shall reign, and He shall reign" (bars 70 and 71); his rewriting is in the orchestra (Mozart's additional accompaniments), which is not sung here; in the voices he reproduces Handel's notes without reharmonising them ("in no single instance have I arbitrarily added anything of my own", preface), and his only addition there is a small optional lower D for the alto at bars 46 and 79 (we sing the large note). Makers: Handel (music and his own voice parts, died 1759), Charles Jennens (words, died 1773, DNB), Ebenezer Prout (edition, died 1909, DNB 1912 supplement); firstPublished 1902, GB. Words: the King James Version of Revelation as Jennens set them; the KJV's UK Crown letters patent is the same question with our lawyer as for The Lord Bless You.
  - **Whole chorus, voices only.** The format sets no length and a take may run 600 s; the whole chorus, bars 4 to 94 (the three bars of orchestral introduction left out), is 364 beats, about 6 minutes at 60: the longest song in the library (the longest open song was Were You There, 212 s). The choir is never silent for more than three beats, so nothing is bridged; the three beats' rest before "The kingdom of this world" and the two before the last "Hallelujah" are kept. For 4 singers only, like Ave verum corpus: the fugal entries and the long-note tune pass between the parts.
  - **Key B-flat (printed D), tempo 60 (Prout: crotchet = 72).** Down a major third is the transposition with the fewest seconds out of the beginner ranges (18.5 s; 97.5 s in D, where the bass is out for 60 s and the soprano climbs to A5). Tempo 60 is the fastest at which Handel's quaver rests count as a breath (half a beat, half a second) and his semiquaver "Hal-le" lasts a quarter second (singable rules 1 to 3); at 72 every Hallelujah line runs on without a breath. Remaining excursions, all Handel's: soprano B-flat 3 and C4 ("Om-" bar 13; "this world is become" bars 34 to 37; "for ever" bars 72 to 74) and its top F once ("He", bar 49, printed A5); alto F3 (bars 14, 25); tenor B-flat 2 (bars 13, 19, 26, 34); bass E-flat 2 (bar 18). Intermediate.

The tenor's "for" at bars 25 and 29 starts half a beat late, after a breath (Handel's "Hallelujah!" runs into it on a quaver). The alto's octave drop on "Om-" (bars 18 and 30) stays on the upper E-flat: the lower one is a semitone under the app's hard alto range in this key. Chords are read from the four voices, half a beat at a time; a unison takes the chord around it.
