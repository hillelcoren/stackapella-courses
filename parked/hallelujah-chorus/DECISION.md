- 2026-11-18, the arranger (Hillel lifted the pause on new songs; the new-song brief of 2026-11-18): **Hallelujah Chorus is built, reviewed and ready to open, still hidden, in Handel's key, D, as choirs sing it (Hillel)** (`"reviewed": false`, `"hidden": true`). Source: Handel's Messiah, "edited, and the additional accompaniments largely re-written, by Ebenezer Prout" (London: Novello and Company; New York: Novello, Ewer and Co.; "Copyright, 1902"; plate 11570), full score, No. 44, pp. 260 to 278, Allegro, crotchet = 72, in D, read from the University of Toronto, Faculty of Music copy, archive.org/details/messiahsacredora00hand_0 (n285 to n303), every bar of the four voices compared with Prout's vocal score of the same edition (a later, undated printing with the H. W. Gray Co. as New York agents; archive.org/details/bwb_KT-315-920, pp. 149 to 157). Prout's preface (p. xv) says he restored Handel's own text from the autograph where all editions followed Walsh: "jah, Hal-" on one note (alto 24, soprano 30, bass 31 and 32) and the tenor's "and He shall reign, and He shall reign" (bars 70 and 71); his rewriting is in the orchestra (Mozart's additional accompaniments), which is not sung here; in the voices he reproduces Handel's notes without reharmonising them ("in no single instance have I arbitrarily added anything of my own", preface), and his only addition there is a small optional lower D for the alto at bars 46 and 79 (we sing the large note). Makers: Handel (music and his own voice parts, died 1759), Charles Jennens (words, died 1773, DNB), Ebenezer Prout (edition, died 1909, DNB 1912 supplement); firstPublished 1902, GB. Words: the King James Version of Revelation as Jennens set them; the KJV's UK Crown letters patent is the same question with our lawyer as for The Lord Bless You.
  - **Whole chorus, voices only.** The format sets no length and a take may run 600 s; the whole chorus, bars 4 to 94 (the three bars of orchestral introduction left out), is 364 beats, about 6 minutes at 60: the longest song in the library (the longest open song was Were You There, 212 s). The choir is never silent for more than three beats, so nothing is bridged; the three beats' rest before "The kingdom of this world" and the two before the last "Hallelujah" are kept. For 4 singers only, like Ave verum corpus: the fugal entries and the long-note tune pass between the parts.
  - **In Handel's key, D, as choirs sing it (Hillel, 2026-11-18: choir singers practise for their own choir's performance in D, and a part page a third lower would teach them the wrong pitches); tempo 60 (Prout: crotchet = 72); advanced.** Tempo 60 is the fastest at which Handel's quaver rests count as a breath (half a beat, half a second) and his semiquaver "Hal-le" lasts a quarter second (singable rules 1 to 3); at 72 every Hallelujah line runs on without a breath. Every range excursion is Handel's own writing, listed in licence.arrangement: soprano F-sharp 5 (bars 8 to 11, 15, 16, 22, 23, 29, 37, 49, 63, 64, 66, 68, 75 to 79, 86, 87), G5 (23, 66 to 68, 75 to 78, 87) and A5 ("He", bar 49), with its held F-sharp and G on the climbing "King of Kings" (63 to 68); alto D5 ("He", 46 and 79; Prout's small lower D is an option we leave); tenor F-sharp 4 and G4 in most Hallelujahs, the long-note tune and "King of Kings", and A4 (24, 44); bass B3, C-sharp 4 and D4 in most Hallelujahs, the long-note tune, "King of Kings" and "and He shall reign" (8 to 86); so the soprano's, tenor's and bass's middles sit above the middle of their beginner ranges. The alto's octave drop on "Om-" (bars 18 and 30) is sung as Handel wrote it: G3 is inside the app's hard alto range in D. (An earlier build of the same day was a major third lower, in B-flat; Hillel's call replaced it.)
  - **Changes from the print, all for beginners' breath, each named in licence.arrangement and listed in parked/hallelujah-chorus/build/build.py:** a line's last note gives up half a beat (or a beat after a long line) before "reigneth" in each "for the Lord God Omnipotent reigneth", after "become" and "Lord" and the first "Christ," of "is become the Kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ", before "for" in each "and He shall reign for ever and ever", before each "King of Kings" that follows "ever", and at "Lords, and" (bass, bar 69) and "ever, for" (bass, bar 88); the held "Kings" and "Lords" give up one to four beats so no line is over 9 s (soprano 52, 55, 58, 61, 64, 67; alto 52, 55; tenor 75), and "world" (bar 35) a beat in every part. The tenor's "for" at bars 25 and 29 starts half a beat late, after a breath (Handel's "Hallelujah!" runs into it on a quaver). Chords are read from the four voices, half a beat at a time; a unison takes the chord around it.
