## Headline

* **The tune sung today is NOT Sulzer's printed Shema.** It is the "so-called Sulzer Sh'ma", which
  current scholarship (Geoffrey Goldberg, 2019) attributes to Cantor **Jacob Beimel** (1875 or
  1880 to 1944). It shares its closing phrase and general shape with Sulzer's Torah-service Shema
  (Schir Zion I, No. 104), but most of the notes differ.
* **Earliest printing found (and the only pre-1931 printing found): Israel and Samuel E. Goldfarb,
  _Synagogue Melodies for the High Holy Days_, Brooklyn, 1926, No. 18 "Sh'ma Yisroel" with No. 19
  "Boruch Shem" (Yom Kippur), page 17, printed as "Traditional".** Goldberg calls it "the first
  printed occurrence of the so-called 'Sulzer' Sh'ma".
* **RULE CONFLICT:** the 1926 compilers died after 1955 (Israel Goldfarb 1879 to 1967; Samuel E.
  Goldfarb 1891 to 1978). The page is a bare unison melody marked "Traditional" with no harmony,
  so there is no arrangement of theirs on it, but under the "no editor/arranger who died after
  1955" rule it fails. I found no other pre-1931 printing (see section 4). The caller must decide.
* The genuine Sulzer prints (Schir Zion, J. Kauffmann, Frankfurt, 3rd ed. of Joseph Sulzer's
  revision) are fully transcribable and pass the maker rule, but they are a different melody from
  what shuls sing.

## 1. Source A (the familiar tune): Goldfarb 1926

* **Book:** _Synagogue Melodies for the High Holy Days_ / Hebrew title "Shirim l'Yamim Nora'im",
  "Arranged for Congregational Singing by Israel Goldfarb, Rabbi, Congregation Beth Israel Anshei
  Emes, Brooklyn, Instructor in Hazanuth, Jewish Theological Seminary of America, and Samuel E.
  Goldfarb, Head, Music Department, Bureau of Jewish Education, New York". Brooklyn, N.Y.,
  "5686 - 1926" (title page). No publisher named on the title page.
* **Number and page:** "18. SH'MA YISROEL" with the heading "On Yom Kippur add 19. BORUCH SHEM",
  printed page 17.
* **Scan:** shulmusic.org (Shelf to Shul project), zip
  `https://www.shulmusic.org/sheetmusic/Goldfarb%20-%20Yamim%20Noraim/Goldfarb%20-%20Synagogue%20Melodies%20for%20the%20High%20Holydays%20%28pdf%20+%20midi%29%20%20-%201926.zip`
  (listed on `https://www.shulmusic.org/sheetmusic/index.html`), PDF page 9 of 32 (a two-page
  spread, printed pages 16 and 17; the Shema is on the right-hand page 17). Local copy:
  `scans/goldfarb1926/g1926.pdf`; crops `crops/g26_shma1.png`, `g26_shma3.png`, `g26_b12.png`.
* **Key:** G major (one sharp). **Time:** 3/4. **Tempo mark:** none printed. **Clef:** treble.
  **Dynamics:** none printed.
* **Voices:** one line, marked "Cong." (congregation, unison). No cantor part, no harmony.
* **Credit on the page:** "Traditional" (top right). (Goldberg's re-engraving labels it
  "Maestoso, S. Sulzer"; the 1926 page itself says neither.)
* **Words as printed (transliteration, two verses):**
  * Verse 1: "Sh'ma yis - ro - el a-do-[shem] e-lo-[kei]-nu a-do-[shem] e - chod."
  * Verse 2 (marked with an asterisk, footnote "* For Yom Kippur"):
    "Bo - ruch shem k' - vod mal' - chu - so - - l' - o - lom vo - ed."
  * Below the music, pointed Hebrew: the Shema verse (with the four-letter name in full, twice) and
    "Baruch shem k'vod malchuto l'olam va'ed".
* **Makers:** melody printed as "Traditional"; attributed by tradition to Salomon Sulzer
  (1804 to 1890) and by Goldberg to Jacob Beimel (born 1875 per most sources, 1880 per SNAC and
  others; died 17 November 1944, New York; Goldberg prints 1880 to 1952, which disagrees with
  SNAC's 1880 to 1944). Compilers/arrangers: Israel Goldfarb (1879 to 1967), Samuel Eliezer
  Goldfarb (1891 to 1978).

## 2. Source B (Sulzer's own Shemas): Schir Zion, 3rd ed. of Joseph Sulzer's revision

* **Book:** "Schir Zion. Gesänge für den israelitischen Gottesdienst von Salomon Sulzer.
  Revidiert und neu herausgegeben von Joseph Sulzer. Dritte Auflage." J. Kauffmann Verlag,
  Frankfurt am Main. Undated on the title page; shulmusic.org labels it "1922 edition" (the
  revision itself is 1905, J. Kauffmann; see section 5). Pages carry the plate mark "S. 1."
* **Scan:** shulmusic.org page images, one page per URL:
  `https://www.shulmusic.org/sheetmusic/sulzer/sulz_97.htm` (image `sulz_97.gif`, 2288 x 3132),
  likewise `sulz_44`, `sulz_81`, `sulz_98`, index `sulz_539`, `sulz_540`, title `sulz_1`.
  The book's page number equals the URL number. Local copies `scans/shul/s97.png` etc.
* **Makers:** Salomon Sulzer (1804 to 1890), composer; Joseph Sulzer (1850 to 1926), reviser
  and editor of this edition.
* The index (p. 539 to 540) lists three Shema settings, all marked "Sch. Z. I." (= first volume of
  the original Schir Zion) and all the same melody in different keys and scorings:
  * **No. 28, p. 44** (Friday evening, after Bor'chu): B-flat major, 3/4, "Andante maestoso",
    cantor line in thirds for "Tenor I. II." over "Bass I. II." (male choir), plus an
    "Ossia" cantor line, then a short choir answer "a-do-[shem] e-chod" (Soli).
  * **No. 70, p. 81** (Sabbath morning, Shacharit, after Bor'chu): G major, 3/4, "Andante
    maestoso", four-part chorus (Sopran, Alt, Tenor, Bass) alone, f; harmonised chant: the
    soprano sits mostly on G4 ("sch'ma jis-ro-el" G4 half, G4, G4 | G4.. G4 G4 ...).
  * **No. 104, p. 97** (Torah service, after No. 102 "Wajhi binsoa" and No. 103 "Boruch
    schennosan toroh"): A major, 3/4, "Andante maestoso", Cantor, then Soli (S A T B) on
    "a-do-[shem] e-chod", then "Chor." (S A T B) sings the whole verse. Followed by **No. 105
    "Echod [Elokeinu]", p. 98** (E major, 3/4, "Andante maestoso", cantor then chorus, words
    "e-chod e-lo-[kei]-nu go-dôl [adoneinu] ko-dôsch sch'-mô") and No. 106 "Gadlu". Footnote on
    p. 98: "*) gilt, wenn שמע, אחד und לך י״ in B dur vorgetragen wird" (the ossia applies
    when Shema, Echad and L'cha are sung in B-flat major).
* Transliteration printed in No. 104 (cantor): "sch'- ma jis - ro - el a-do-[shem] e-lo-[kei]-nu
  a-do-[shem] e - chod" (the a and e of "[the Name]"/"[Elokeinu]" carry small superscript/subscript
  vowel marks in the print).

## 3. Tune check

**Today's familiar Shema** (Reform and Conservative, sung after Bar'chu and in the Torah
service): in G, 3/4: B G A | B G G | C G A | B A(fermata) G A | B A | G (fermata), i.e.
mi do re | mi do do | fa do re | mi re do re | mi re | do. Evidence:

* Geoffrey Goldberg, "The Development of Congregational Song in the American Conservative
  Synagogue 1900-1955", _Journal of Synagogue Music_ 44/1 (September 2019), 35 to 88
  (`https://www.cantors.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/919-Compressed.pdf`), p. 44:
  "Of particular interest is Beimel's notation of the Sh'ma. A setting of the Sh'ma with the same
  opening motif has been located in a manuscript of Cantor Alois Kaiser (1840-1908), but the rest
  of Kaiser's melody is not identical. We conclude, therefore, that this so-called "Sulzer Sh'ma"
  should be attributed to Beimel. Beimel's manuscript reveals the evolution of the melody, from an
  earlier version to the one sung ubiquitously today (Example 12)." Example 12 (p. 45) gives
  Beimel's "Later version" exactly as above; his "Earlier version" differs only in bar 3
  (C B A instead of C G A). (Footnote 56 cites Ethan Goldberg, "In the Shadow of Sulzer: The Mixed
  Legacy of Cantor Alois Kaiser", Brandeis senior honors thesis, 2012, 35 to 37.)
* Same article, p. 52: "1. Sh'ma, the first printed occurrence of the so-called "Sulzer" Sh'ma
  (no. 19; ZL II, p. 83; ZL/NG I, p. 68)", in the list for Goldfarb's _Synagogue Melodies for the
  High Holy Days_ (1926); Example 26 reproduces it, identical to the 1926 page.
* Matthew Austerklein, "Where The Tunes Come From", _Masorti Journal_ 70/1
  (`https://masortijournal.org/where-the-tunes-come-from/`), note 63: "On the likely attribution
  of the "Sulzer Shema" to Cantor Jacob Beimel, see Goldberg, 44-45"; the text speaks of "the
  ubiquity of the traditional "Sulzer Shema."".
* Cantor Ellen Sussman, "Musical Settings: Sh'ma", ReformJudaism.org
  (`https://reformjudaism.org/beliefs-practices/spirituality-prayers-blessings/musical-settings-shma`):
  the familiar unison setting "has been attributed to Sulzer" but sounds quite different from
  Sulzer's own; "Musicologists believe this setting is based on the one we heard in the first
  track" (Sulzer's), changed "in a way familiar to us from the children's game of 'telephone.'"
  (Paraphrased in part; the page is audio-led.)
* My own memory of the tune agrees with the contour above (mi do re mi, do do fa, ...).

**Verdict against the Sulzer print (No. 104, transposed to G for comparison):**
Sulzer: (B) | B B G | D, D G-A | B B A-B | D B G G-A | B A | G
Familiar:   | B G A | B G G   | C G A   | B A G A    | B A | G
Matches **in part only**: same key feel, meter (3/4) and final cadence; "a-do-[shem] e-chad" at the
end is the same scale steps (do-re | mi re | do) in both; the opening "mi" on "Sh'ma" is shared.
The middle (yisrael, Adoshem [Elokeinu]) differs: Sulzer drops to low sol and uses dotted rhythms,
the familiar tune stays in the do-mi-fa range in plain half + two eighths. Not note for note.

Also checked and NOT the familiar tune:
* Union Hymnal, CCAR, Cincinnati, 1897, Appendix "Hebrew Hymns and Responses", No. 2 "Sh'ma",
  p. 128, credited "Alois Kaiser" (index p. 217; archive.org `unionhymnal00rabbgoog`, leaf n132;
  also `unionhymnal0000unse_l7i5`). F major, 3/4, "f Maestoso", SATB on two staves. Soprano
  opens B-flat4 half, then low eighths (about D4), then G4 half: a different tune. Also printed
  in the 1914 Union Hymnal (No. 228, still "Alois Kaiser"). The 1897 book has No. 3 "Boruch Shem
  K'vod" (Kaiser) set to the same music.
* Goldfarb, _Friday Evening Melodies_, Brooklyn, August 1918, p. 41 "Sh'ma Yisroel"
  (archive.org `fridayeveningme00goldgoog`, leaf n44): B-flat major, 3/4, "Moderato maestoso",
  uncredited; a different tune (opens F4 pickup, D5 half...).
* "Shir Zion", A Friday Evening Service arranged for use in American Synagogues by the Society of
  American Cantors from the Schir Zion of Salomon Sulzer, Bloch, New York, 1904, No. 4 "Sh'ma and
  Boruch Shem", pp. 12 to 13 (archive.org `shirzionafriday00sulzgoog`, leaves n18 to n19): A
  major, 3/4, Andante, cantor + chorus with piano. The Google scan is damaged (most of p. 12 is
  blank). Cantor begins "Sh'ma Yis-ro-" C#5 half, C#5, C#5: Sulzer's tune, not the familiar one.

## 4. Second printing / cross-check

* Familiar tune: the only other pre-1931 printing I could locate is again by the Goldfarbs:
  _The Jewish Songster (ha-Menagen)_, Parts 1 and 2, Brooklyn, 1928 (archive.org `nybc202420`),
  which has "SH'MA YIS'RO-EL 1. Sh'ma ... 2. E-chod e-lo-[kei]-nu go-dol A-do-ne-nu ko-dosh sh'mo"
  (p. 95) and a responses page; I did not confirm whether it carries music for this tune.
  Same rule problem (Goldfarbs).
* Not found online / not checked: A. Z. Idelsohn, _The Jewish Song Book for Synagogue, School and
  Home_, Cincinnati, 1928 (Idelsohn 1882 to 1938, passes the rule). Designed for Reform and
  Conservative use, unison congregational singing. If it prints this Shema it is the best
  candidate. HathiTrust and the Frankfurt library blocked automated access (bot checks); IMSLP's
  download needs its disclaimer step, which I did not bypass.
* Beimel's own manuscripts (JTS Library, ARC.MUS.5) are unpublished.
* Sulzer cross-check: No. 28 (p. 44, B-flat) and No. 70 (p. 81, G) are the same Sulzer melody as
  No. 104 in other keys and scorings; the original Schir Zion I (Vienna, Artaria, [1839], scanned
  by UB Frankfurt, IMSLP #343657, `https://imslp.org/wiki/Schir_Zion_(Sulzer,_Salomon)`) was not
  downloaded.

## 5. Continuation

* Torah service (Conservative today): cantor sings Shema, congregation repeats, then "Echad
  Elokeinu gadol [adoneinu] kadosh shemo" (and Gadlu). Sulzer prints this as No. 104 then No. 105
  "Echod [Elokeinu]" (p. 98, E major, 3/4, Andante maestoso, cantor then chorus; a different tune,
  not transcribed), then No. 106 "Gadlu".
* The 1926 source continues with **"Boruch shem k'vod malchuso l'olom vo-ed" sung to the same
  six bars** as a second verse, "On Yom Kippur add" / "* For Yom Kippur" (the one day it is said
  aloud). That is transcribed above. Reform usage after Bar'chu (Union Hymnal 1897) also pairs
  "Sh'ma" with "Boruch Shem K'vod" (Kaiser's Nos. 2 and 3), and the 1904 Society of American
  Cantors book titles its number "Sh'ma and Boruch Shem".

## 6. Research notes for the history page

* Salomon Sulzer: 1804 to 1890; chief cantor in Vienna (Seitenstettengasse); _Schir Zion_ vol. 1
  c. 1838 to 1840, vol. 2 1865/66 (IMSLP quotes Grove: "two separate volumes (1838-40 and
  1865-6)"). IMSLP's Teil I scan is "Wien: Artaria & Co., n.d. [1839]", reprint "Wien: H. Engel &
  Sohn", from UB Frankfurt, noted "Unvollständiger Druck, Inhaltsverzeichnis fehlt".
* Joseph Sulzer's revision: Leipzig/Frankfurt, J. Kauffmann, 1905 (Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek
  record of the Frankfurt copy); the shulmusic.org scan is its "Dritte Auflage". Each number
  carries "Sch. Z. I." or "Sch. Z. II." to show which original volume it came from; all three
  Shemas are "Sch. Z. I.". The Torah-service page (97) holds Nos. 102 (Wajhi binsoa, Sch. Z. II),
  103 (Boruch schennosan toroh) and 104 (Schma jisroel); No. 104 opens "Andante maestoso", ff,
  cantor first, the choir answering with the whole verse.
* Teruah Jewish Music blog (2007), citing Marsha Bryan Edelman, _Discovering Jewish Music_:
  "The result was Shir Zion, a Torah service for festivals." "It included a melody for Shema
  Yisrael and Ki mi-Tziyon." (`http://teruah-jewishmusic.blogspot.com/2007/03/shema-was-composed-really.html`)
  (Popular but loose: Schir Zion is a whole-year collection.)
* Union Hymnal 1897 credits its Sh'ma to Alois Kaiser (1840 to 1908); the 1914 edition still
  does; the caller reports the 1932 third edition credits Sulzer (Beimel contributed fifteen
  hymns to the 1932 Union Hymnal per biographies).
* Jacob Beimel: born Parichi (Minsk province), cantor in Berlin from 1903 and Copenhagen from
  1911, to the US 1914/15, cantor of Mordecai Kaplan's Jewish Center (86th Street, New York)
  1918 to c. 1920, then Adath Jeshurun, Philadelphia 1920 to 1929; founded the journal _Jewish
  Music_ (1934 to 1935); died New York 17 November 1944 (SNAC `https://snaccooperative.org/view/21166981`:
  "Beimel, Jacob, 1880-1944").
* Goldberg (p. 52): Goldfarb's 1926 book was made at the request of the Young People's League of
  the United Synagogue and Young Israel; "Owing to the impact of his publications, Goldfarb has
  been called "the father of congregational singing"".
