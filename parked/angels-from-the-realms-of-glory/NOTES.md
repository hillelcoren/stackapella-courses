## Printings seen (page images read myself)

1. **Hutchins, The Church Hymnal (Boston, 1894)**, the set source. Title page: "Edited by the Rev. Charles L.
   Hutchins", Boston, The Parish Choir, 1894. Copyright lines 1879, 1889 (James Pott and Co.), 1893, 1894. Front
   matter (read in the scan's OCR text): an attestation of the 1892 General Convention vote signed "Chas. L.
   Hutchins, Secretary"; a CERTIFICATE that the edition's text was compared with the Standard Book, signed "On
   behalf of the Commission empowered to superintend the publication of the Hymnal. William Croswell Doane,
   Chairman. Henry W. Nelson, Jr., Secretary."; the preface is the editor's own (dated Concord, Massachusetts,
   Conversion of S. Paul, 1894; a note to the organ edition dated Advent 1894), thanking "for advice and critical
   assistance" Horatio W. Parker, Warren A. Locke and Arthur Whiting (not named as editors).
   No. 60, p. 82 (leaf n133), heading "CHRISTMAS", "8. 7. 8. 7. 4. 7.", "Regent Square / H. Smart", crotchet =
   100, mf. Four parts on two staves, C major, 4/4 (C), no pickup, three systems of four bars, then "A-men".
   Verse 1 under the music: "An-gels from the realms of glo-ry, Wing your flight o'er all the earth; Ye, who sang
   cre-a-tion's sto-ry, Now pro-claim Mes-si-ah's birth: Come and wor-ship, Come and wor-ship, Wor-ship Christ the
   new-born King." Verses 2 to 4 below, each with "Come and worship, / Worship Christ, the new-born King." (cr/f
   marks): 2 "Shepherds in the field abiding, / Watching o'er your flocks by night; / God with man is now residing,
   / Yonder shines the infant-light:"; 3 "Sages, leave your contemplations; / Brighter visions beam afar: / Seek the
   great Desire of nations, / Ye have seen His natal star:"; 4 "Saints before the altar bending, / Watching long in
   hope and fear, / Suddenly the Lord, descending, / In His temple shall appear:". Index of first lines (OCR):
   "Angels from the realms of glory 60 James Montgomery, 1819 Regent Square." Alphabetical index of tunes, p. xxix
   (leaf n32, read on the image): "Regent Square ... 60, 250, 386, 399, 483 ... 8.7.8.7.4.7 ... Henry Smart,
   1867." Other Smart entries in that index read "Henry Smart, d. 1879" (OCR).
   My reading of the four parts is in build.py (SMART), checked note by note with a notehead finder and by eye.
   One reading worth a second look: the tenor on "wor-" (bar 9) sits on the C4 ledger line's top edge; I read D4
   (the 1866 and 1871 pages agree: D over G).
2. **Psalms and Hymns for Divine Worship (London: James Nisbet and Co., 1866)**, title page dated 1866 (leaf n4);
   catalogued as the Presbyterian Church of England's book. P. 58, "Regent Square", "8,7,4.", in C, 4/2 with a
   half-bar pickup, set to Nos. 126 "Bright with all his crowns of glory" (King of Kings) and 127 "Glory be to God
   the Father" (Praise). Its tune index (OCR) lists "Regent Square ... Original Melody, by Henry Smart ... 58".
   archive.org/details/fordi00pres/page/n273. I compared its last line (the music of "Come and worship ... new-born
   King") chord by chord with Hutchins: the same four parts, including the tenor's B-flat on the second "and", the
   quaver pairs under "Christ" and "the", and the alto's dotted note into "born". I did not compare the first
   four lines note by note. This is the earliest printing of the tune I saw, with other words.
3. **The Baptist Praise Book: for Congregational Singing (New York: A. S. Barnes and Co., 1871)**, "prepared by
   Richard Fuller, E. M. Levy, S. D. Phelps, H. C. Fish, Thomas Armitage, E. T. Winkler, W. W. Everts, Geo. C.
   Lorimer, and Basil Manly, Jr.; J. P. Holbrook, Special Musical Editor" (title page, OCR). P. 108 (leaf n117 of
   archive.org/details/baptistpraiseboo71full; the coordinator's lead gave another scan of the same book,
   baptistprai00full), "REGENT SQUARE. 8s & 7s.", "Sir H. Smart.", in C, 4/2 with a pickup, six lines: hymn 270
   ("Shepherds! hail the wondrous stranger") under the music, its last two lines repeating "Lo! in yonder humble
   manger"; hymn 271, "Angels, from the realms of glory", five stanzas signed "Montgomery", printed below on the
   same page, each with "Come and worship, / Worship Christ the new-born King." Its last line's four parts match
   Hutchins chord by chord. The earliest printing of these words with this tune that I saw: the template's
   firstPublished (1871, US). Its editors' death years were not checked (it is not the printing we sing).
4. **James Montgomery, The Christian Psalmist (Glasgow, 1825)**, No. 487, "Good tidings of great joy to all
   people", pp. 389 and 390, archive.org/details/christianpsalmis00montiala/page/n406: five stanzas (Angels,
   Shepherds, Sages, Saints, Sinners), each with "Come and worship, / Worship Christ the new-born King." Hutchins's
   verses 1 to 3 have the same words; the punctuation differs a little (Hutchins: "Ye, who sang", "infant-light:",
   "His natal star"; Montgomery 1825: "Ye who sang", "infant-light;", "his natal star;").

## People

- **James Montgomery** (1771 to 1854), words. archive.org catalogue record for The Christian Psalmist:
  "Montgomery, James, 1771-1854". Hutchins dates the hymn 1819 in his index.
- **Henry Smart** (Henry Thomas Smart, 1813 to 1879), tune and harmony. Hutchins, Carols Old and Carols New
  (Boston, 1916), index: "Smart, Henry Thomas (1813-1879)" (OCR of archive.org/details/carolsoldcarolsn00huth);
  Hutchins 1894's tune index: "Henry Smart, d. 1879" (OCR). The 1871 page's "Sir H. Smart" is the book's own
  styling; a lead, not checked: Smart was not knighted, and the title may be a confusion with Sir George Smart.
- **Charles L. Hutchins** (Charles Lewis Hutchins, 1838 to 1920), editor of the 1894 book: archive.org catalogue
  records "Hutchins, Charles L. (Charles Lewis), 1838-1920" (one record for an 1889 printing says 1838-1921;
  either way before 1925).
- Named in the 1894 front matter but not as editors (death years NOT checked; leads only): William Croswell
  Doane and Henry W. Nelson, Jr. (the Hymnal Commission's certificate); Horatio W. Parker, Warren A. Locke and
  Arthur Whiting (thanked in the preface for advice). See the rights note in the report.

## The tune and the words, as the sources state them

- Hutchins: words "J. Montgomery" (index: 1819); tune "Regent Square", "H. Smart" (index: 1867).
- The 1866 London book prints Regent Square on p. 58 to Kelly's and Bonar's words, not Montgomery's.
- Leads, not seen in a scan (hymnary.org via a web search summary): the words first appeared in the Sheffield
  Iris, 24 December 1816, as "Nativity"; the tune is named for Regent Square Presbyterian Church, London, whose
  minister James Hamilton edited Psalms and Hymns for Divine Worship; Smart wrote it for that book, first to
  Bonar's doxology; the pairing with Montgomery spread in North America from the 1871 Baptist Praise Book.
- The form we sing ("Come and worship" twice, then "Worship Christ, the new-born King") fits Montgomery's
  8.7.8.7.4.7 stanza to Smart's six-line 8.7.8.7.8.7 tune; Hutchins prints it so under verse 1. Some books set the
  words to other tunes (for example Iris, "Gloria"); not used here.

## Verses chosen, and why

Verses 1, 2 and 3 as Hutchins prints them (angels, shepherds, sages): the Christmas scene, about 91 seconds at
his crotchet = 100. Verse 4 ("Saints before the altar bending") is left out for length. Verses 2 and 3 take
"Come and worship" twice, as verse 1 is printed under the notes.

## Uncertain or worth checking

- The 1866 and 1871 harmonies were compared closely only in the last line.
- Henry W. Nelson, Jr.'s death year, and the advisers' (Locke and Whiting probably after 1925: a lead), if the
  rights reading counts a certificate or a preface's thanks.
- Montgomery's 1816 first printing and its first refrain are leads only.
