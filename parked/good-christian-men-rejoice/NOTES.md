## The people

  (archive.org authority heading "Neale, J. M. (John Mason), 1818-1866").
- **Thomas Helmore** (1811 to 1890), who set the tune to Neale's words ("set to Ancient Melodies by the
  Rev. T. Helmore"). Death year from archive.org's authority heading "Helmore, Thomas, 1811-1890" (as in
  the Good King Wenceslas notes). Listed in `makers` as edition.
- **John Stainer** (1840 to 1901) and **Henry Ramsden Bramley** (1833 to 1917; archive.org says 1916, the
  library keeps the later year), editors of Christmas Carols New and Old, listed as edition because its
  chords were the check (as in Good King Wenceslas).
- Everyone in `makers` died in 1925 or earlier, so the 1925 bar is met.

## Origins (as the sources state them)

- Words: Neale's, "principally in imitation of the original" (1854 title page), "free imitations, for the
  most part, of these and other ancient Christmas Carols" (the Notice). The Notice dates the folio
  edition to before 28 October 1853 (SS. Simon and Jude), so `firstPublished` is 1853, GB.
- Tune: the 1854 book says only "Ancient Melodies", and its Notice says the music ranges "from the end of
  the twelfth to the beginning of the fifteenth century" and is given "without alteration". Bramley and
  Stainer's index: "Air: Old German". The name "In dulci jubilo" (in the credits and description) is a
  LEAD from the brief: no scan read here prints that name. Also LEADS, not checked on a scan: that
  Helmore took the tune from Piae Cantiones (1582), and that the repeated notes of "News! News!" come from
  his reading of its notation.

## What was made, and why

- All three verses: the carol has only three, each with its own centre ("News!", "Joy!", "Peace!"), and
  three verses at 60 come to 1 minute 42.
- Tune and words from the 1854 print, verse by verse: verses 1 and 2 sing the first exclamation as a
  crotchet and a quaver rest, verse 3 as a dotted crotchet. "before him bow" and "Peace Peace!" as printed.
  Syllables split for the notes: "Je-sus", "to-day", "be-fore", "man-ger", "end-less", "heav'n-ly",
  "bless-ed", "ev-er-more", "e-ver-last-ing" (Helmore's own hyphens for "e-ver"), "re-joice", "Christ-ian".
- Key F as printed (tune D4 to D5, inside the soprano's beginner range). 6/8, a dotted crotchet = 1 beat,
  pickup 0.333333; each verse 34 beats (pickup, 16 bars, and the last bar's dotted crotchet and crotchet
  rest), sections at 0, 34, 68, ending at 102.
- Tempo 60 (dotted crotchet). "Animato" would allow more, but at 60 a half-beat breath is half a second
  (rule 1) and quavers are a third of a second (rule 3).
- Breaths: a `br` at each verse's start, "Give"/"Now", "News!"/"Joy!"/"Peace", "Ox"/"He"/"Calls" and the
  first "Christ"; the note before each gives up a semiquaver (verse 3's dotted "grave:" a dotted quaver),
  so every part rests half a beat. No line is over 7.9 seconds. The last "save!" holds through the
  printed crotchet rest so the song ends a pickup short of the bar line with the parts' last notes.
- Harmony: our own alto, tenor and bass on Bramley and Stainer's chords (unsigned, so only chords used).
  Where ours differ, and why, is in `licence.arrangement`; in short, the duet keeps a third under the
  tune's C and D (A minor and G minor 7 where they have F/A and Bb; C under the held "joice" and "voice"
  where their alto doubles the tune an octave down) and the parts avoid parallels. One note a syllable in
  every lower part, held under the tune's three slurs.
