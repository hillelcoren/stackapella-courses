Eberle & Co.; plate T.R. 41; undated, catalogued as 1886, the first edition; no editor named), the Austrian National Library's copy (stamped M.S. 5216) in the IIIF scans Bruckner Online publishes (iiif.acdh.oeaw.ac.at/iiif/images/bruckner/drucke/WAB023/WAB023-01.jp2 to -04.jp2; IMSLP's #437277 is the same print but its download sits behind a bot check). Makers: Anton Bruckner (music and his own four parts, died 11 October 1896, AT); the words are the anonymous Latin gradual for the dedication of a church; the publisher Theodor Rättig is not an editor and nobody else is named. firstPublished 1886, AT (the earliest known printing; the print itself is undated). What we sing: all 48 bars, all four of Bruckner's parts, every pitch, rhythm and rest as printed, in C as printed (C has the fewest notes outside the beginner ranges of any key, 18), the tenor (printed in the treble clef) at sounding pitch, words as printed under each part ("æ", capitals and commas included). For 4 singers only, like Ave verum, The Lord Bless You and Deep River: the tenor sings bar 21 alone, the basses rest for nine bars, and the chords need all four voices. Intermediate (soprano B3 to G5, bass F2 to C4, a chromatic middle). Tempo 60 to a crotchet: the print says only "Allegro moderato"; at 60 a quick breath is half a beat (singable rule 1), so the breaths can come out of crotchets without moving Bruckner's on-beat entries, and the whole motet runs 3 minutes 11 seconds, about what choirs take (an estimate, not a printed mark).
  Breaths written in (each half a beat from the note before a new line; no line over 8 seconds): S, A, T before "sa-" in bars 15 and 19; S, A before the mf "pre-" of bar 27 (without it the soprano would sing 11.5 s) and S, A, T before "est" in bar 29; the bass, which has no printed rest in bars 1 to 4 and 5 to 20, before "a" (bars 2, 6, 31, 35), at the printed comma "est, a" (8, 37), before the second "De-o" (10, 39), before "fac-tus" (11), before "in-" (12, and 16 at the printed comma "tum,"), and before "sa-" and "men-" of each "sa-cra-men-tum" (14, 15, 18, 19); everyone at bar 41, inside the twelve-beat melisma on "De-", singing the vowel again as "(e)-" (as Ave verum's "(o)-"), and before "fac-" (bar 46) and "est." (bar 48). The bass's breath between "cra-" and "men-" splits the word at a syllable on a repeated note; dropping it in bar 15 (the reviewer's first thought) makes "sa-cra-men-tum," 8.5 s, which then needs a full beat's breath before "in-" that the crotchet "tum," can't give, and in bar 19 it would leave 10 s at the top of the range; so both stay. Bar 41's group breath falls in Bruckner's cresc. to f: the teaching note is to crescendo through it.
