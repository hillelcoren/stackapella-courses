# Decisions row (text for docs/decisions.md, append only)

- 2026-11-18, the arranger (Hillel's new-song batch of 2026-11-18): **Cantique de Jean Racine (Fauré) is built, reviewed and ready to open, still hidden.** Source: Écho des Maîtrises, first issue, No. 10 (Paris: F. Schoen, undated, plate F. 309 S.; IMSLP dates a Schoen printing "[1876?]"), "A Monsieur César Franck", Fauré "Organiste de la Maîtrise de St Sulpice", pp. 2 to 9, read from the Gallica scan of the Bibliothèque nationale de France copy, Vmg. 17107 (I,10). Fauré's own four voice parts (no editor's or arranger's notes), D-flat as printed, common time, tempo 60 (Andante, no metronome mark). Makers: Fauré (music and arrangement, died 4 November 1924, Library of Congress authority citing New Grove), Racine (words, died 1699), Romain Bussine (director of the series, died 21 October 1899); firstPublished 1876, FR, the earliest known printing. Every maker died by 1925.
  - **The whole choral piece, 4 singers only (like Ave verum).** Bars 13 to 89, 272 beats, 4 minutes 32 at 60: the library's longest song (Were You There is 3:32), well inside the app's 10-minute take. Fauré's parts enter one by one (bass, tenor, alto, soprano) and cross, so no 2- or 3-singer voicing would keep them.
  - **The organ left out, as the Ave verum model does:** its 12-bar introduction (the song starts on the bass's "Ver-be", pickupBeats 1); its bars 32 to 38, where the choir rests (the rest of seven and a half bars becomes five beats); the last chord held three beats in place of two tied bars under a fermata (singable rule 6, and the song ends a pickup short of a bar line).
  - **Key kept (D-flat).** The bass sits exactly in F2 to B-flat3 and the alto in C4 to C-flat5; any transposition would push the bass out, and choirs sing it in D-flat. The soprano's excursions are Fauré's: G-flat5 three times ("ta voix, Dis-", bar 47), F5 ten times, D-flat4 on the last two "comblé!"; the tenor's G-flat4 twice ("Dis-", bar 48; "bli", bar 54). Difficulty "advanced".
  - **Breaths:** written in at Racine's commas and where a choir breathes, never inside a word; every line 10 seconds or less. Where a line's last note could not give the time, the next line's first syllable starts half a beat later in every part that sings it ("Di-vin", bar 27; "que tout", bar 44; "Dis-si-pe", bar 47), and the bass's "nous" (bar 21) starts with the other parts on beat 4.
  Choral review (a strict reviewer, then rechecked; version stays 1, never synced):
  - bass, bar 61, "ble à ce" (one F3 on "ble à", one on "ce"): sung as "ble", a half beat's breath, then "à ce" in two eighths (F3, F3), the tenor's rhythm, in place of a breath between "ce" and "peuple".
  - bass, bar 70, "gloi-re im-" (D-flat3 held two beats): "re" a half beat, a breath, "im-" on beat 2 (D-flat3), where the tenor breathes; the bass's "qu'il offre à ta gloire immortelle" was 12.5 seconds on one breath, now 6.5 and 5.5. The half-beat delays on "et de tes dons" (bar 72) this made unnecessary are gone.
  - soprano and alto, bars 49 and 50, "d'une â-me": underlaid "d'u-" / "ne â-" (the elision), the words as printed.
  - one misreading found by the harmony scan and fixed before the review: the bass in bar 41 is C3 on all five notes ("nous le feu de ta"), a C major chord.
  - alto, bar 76, "tes" (the reviewer asked whether D4 should be E-flat4): rechecked on the scan at 8x and in character pictures: the head sits in the space under the staff, D (the D natural of the beat before, a natural restated), so D-natural4 stays: a passing D-natural under E-flat minor, then F-flat over G-natural (a diminished seventh) into A-flat over C.
  Exceptions, kept with reasons (all Fauré's notes), as licence.arrangement lists them: the soprano's and tenor's ranges above; 4 to 6 seconds near a range's top or bottom; the slurred notes in the alto, tenor and bass; the bass's octaves (bars 23, 78) and tritone (bar 77), the tenor's sixths (bars 41, 42), the alto's tritones (bars 28, 48, 72); the bass's sixteenth "be é-" (a dotted pickup); doubled C in bars 22, 23, 41, 50, 70 (the dominant of F minor, not a leading note); tenor above alto in bars 48 and 50; soprano and bass octaves D-flat to C on "gloi-re" (bar 70); the tenor's D-flat appoggiatura over C in bar 50.
