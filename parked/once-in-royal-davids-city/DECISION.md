The later printing it was checked against is the Revised and Enlarged Edition (title page: London, Clowes, Limited, undated; catalogued as New York, Pott, Young and Co., 1874), No. 329, not a New York printing as the build said: licence.source corrected. Makers, on the safe side: Cecil Frances Alexander (words, died 1895), Henry John Gauntlett (music and arrangement, died 1876), William Henry Monk (musical editor, died 1889), Henry Williams Baker (chairman of the compilers, not named in the book, died 1877); firstPublished 1868, GB. The three open points:
  - **Tempo: 60, kept.** Neither the 1868 book nor the revised edition prints a tempo or metronome mark. Lines 1 and 3 end on a crotchet straight into the next line's pickup on the beat, so singable rule 1 (half a second's breath, the last note kept to half a beat) holds only up to 60; at 72 the line ends would shrink to a quarter beat, or Gauntlett's on-beat pickups would move off the beat. Choirs take it slowly too: King's College has opened its Christmas Eve service with it since 1919, and its listed recordings run 4:21 to 5:16, about 55 to 66 a crotchet if all six verses are sung (an estimate from track lengths, not a printed mark). The tune moves in quavers in every bar, so 60 does not drag. **No solo first verse:** the treble solo is A. H. Mann's King's College practice, not in the 1868 print, which sets every verse in four parts; our hymn carols (Hark, O Come, All Ye Faithful, O Little Town, The First Noel, Away in a Manger) sing every verse in parts, solos only where the source or the form has them (shanty calls, rounds, Riu Riu's print); and a solo verse would leave three singers silent for its first 48 seconds. The King's practice is a fact for the story.
  - **The duet without the chord's third 18.8% of the time: accepted.** None of it falls on a downbeat. A verse's 9 of 48 beats: under "laid her" (2) the duet sings G over E, a third, heard as A minor seven going to D7, and the tenor's C sharp makes it A7 at four (Gauntlett's alto C sharp would make the duet a tritone); the ends of lines 1 and 3 (2), G over D on the tonic, beat 2 of the bar (Gauntlett's alto sings the tune's G in unison, emptier); the cadential six-fours on "tle", "His", "tle" (1.5), whose third comes half a beat later; the quaver pickups "al", "ther", "that" (1.5), where the bass has the B at three; the tune's 4-3 appoggiatura on "Da-" (0.5); passing chords on "vid's" (0.5) and "sus" (1, beat 4). The app's check lists them as advice (27 places for two singers, 24 for three).
  - **The underlay, ours: kept.** The book prints the words apart from the music (n552). One syllable a note, each beamed quaver pair one syllable, as the beams show; verse 2's eight-syllable fifth and sixth lines sing their last minim as two crotchets ("low-ly", "Ho-ly"), the usual way. Every syllable of verses 1, 2 and 6 was checked against n552.
