Locus iste, "this place", is a short Latin motet for four voices without accompaniment by the Austrian composer Anton Bruckner, who finished it on 11 August 1869 for the dedication of the first finished part of the new cathedral in Linz [^howie][^imslp][^dioezese2017]. Its words are the gradual, the chant sung between the readings, of the Mass for the dedication of a church: "Locus iste a Deo factus est", this place was made by God [^graduale1908][^dioezese2017]. It is 48 bars long, about three minutes. We sing Bruckner's own four parts, in Latin, from the earliest known printing (1886), made in Vienna.

## Where it comes from

**A new cathedral for Linz.** In 1855 Franz Joseph Rudigier, bishop of Linz, resolved to build a new cathedral in honour of the Immaculate Conception of Mary, and its foundation stone was laid on 1 May 1862 [^dioezese-rudigier][^ndb-rudigier]. The first part to be finished was the votive chapel. Bishop Rudigier consecrated it on 29 September 1869, with a procession through the town; the consecration began at seven in the morning and was finished about eleven, and the High Mass followed [^lv1869][^dioezese2019][^ndb-rudigier].

**Bruckner and the chapel.** Anton Bruckner, born at Ansfelden near Linz in 1824, had been the cathedral organist in Linz since the 1850s, and in the autumn of 1868 he moved to Vienna as professor at the Conservatory and court organist in waiting [^ndb-bruckner][^oeml-bruckner][^howie]. At the High Mass on 29 September Bruckner conducted his Mass in E minor, a work Bishop Rudigier had commissioned, for choir with only wind instruments, and the Linz Catholic paper called it "eigens für die Feier componirt", written specially for the celebration, though Howie says Bruckner had finished it in November 1866 [^lv1869][^dioezese2017][^howie]. Bruckner also wrote a new gradual for the occasion, Locus iste, finished on 11 August 1869 [^howie][^imslp][^dioezese2017].

**When was it first sung?** The catalogues give 29 October 1869, a month after the consecration, in the votive chapel; the Diocese of Linz says so too, while one record note puts it in the same service as the Mass [^imslp][^dioezese2017][^brchor][^hyperion]. None of these cites a document from 1869. The Linzer Volksblatt reported the consecration and praised the Mass, and three days later the bishop's office thanked everyone who took part in "the newly composed Mass", but neither names a gradual [^lv1869][^lv1869b]. So the record shows Locus iste was written for the chapel's dedication; when it was first sung is not proven, and 29 October 1869 is the catalogues' date.

**A gift to a young priest.** Years later Bruckner dedicated a copy to his friend and pupil Oddo Loidol, a monk of Kremsmünster abbey [^howie][^poetsch2025]. Loidol was born in 1858 and said his first Mass on 2 August 1885, with Bruckner at the organ, and Bruckner dedicated Locus iste to him as a new priest, as he had dedicated Christus factus est to him. That copy is now missing [^poetsch2025].

**Into print.** The earliest known printing is Theodor Rättig's in Vienna, catalogued as 1886: "Vier Graduale für Sopran, Alt, Tenor u. Bass", four graduals in two books, with "Christus factus est" and "Locus iste" in the first [^raettig1886][^imslp][^howie]. The book itself is undated. A score of each book cost 60 kreuzer, the four voice parts 75, and a single part 20, and the plate number on every page is T.R. 41 [^raettig1886]. Locus iste is headed "2. Graduale", marked "Allegro moderato", with no dedication printed [^raettig1886].

## What the words mean

The gradual says three things about a church: God made this place; it is a holy thing beyond price; it is without fault [^graduale1908]. "Locus iste" is "this place", "a Deo factus est" is "was made by God". "Inaestimabile" means beyond any price, not to be valued, and "sacramentum" in church Latin is a sacred thing or a mystery [^ls]. "Irreprehensibilis" means beyond blame, with nothing to find fault in [^ls].

In the Roman Gradual the chant has a verse, "Deus, cui adstat angelorum chorus, exaudi preces servorum tuorum", O God, before whom the choir of angels stands, hear the prayers of your servants. Bruckner set only the first part, without the verse [^graduale1908][^raettig1886]. The same Mass begins with the introit "Terribilis est locus iste: hic domus Dei est, et porta coeli", awesome is this place, it is the house of God and the gate of heaven, the words Jacob spoke in Genesis on waking from his dream at Bethel [^graduale1908][^novavulgata].

### The full words

These are the words as Bruckner set them and as we sing them, from the 1886 printing, which spells "inaestimabile" with "æ"; he sings the first line twice at the start and twice when it comes back, and repeats "a Deo". The text is ancient liturgy and Bruckner died in 1896, so all of it is in the public domain. The English is our own.

> Locus iste a Deo factus est,
This place was made by God,
> inaestimabile sacramentum,
a holy mystery beyond price;
> irreprehensibilis est.
it is without fault.

## How it is sung

A gradual is sung at Mass between the readings. This one belongs to the Mass for the dedication of a church and for the yearly anniversary of that day, the church's own feast [^graduale1908][^dioezese2017]. In the Roman Gradual it is a plainchant; Bruckner wrote it for four-part choir alone, soprano, alto, tenor and bass, in C major [^graduale1908][^raettig1886][^howie].

The piece is in three parts, with the first coming back at the end [^raettig1886]. All four voices begin together, softly, "Locus iste", and grow loud on the second "a Deo". In the middle the basses fall silent for nine bars, the tenors begin "irreprehensibilis est" alone, very softly, and the upper voices answer them over chromatic chords. Then "Locus iste" returns, with one long run of notes on "Deo", a whole bar of silence for every voice, and a quiet close on "a Deo factus est" [^raettig1886].

Our arrangement is read from the 1886 printing: all 48 bars, Bruckner's own four parts, every pitch and rhythm as printed, in C, in Latin, for four singers only, because the parts take turns and the chords need all four voices [^raettig1886]. The tenor, printed in the treble clef an octave higher, is written at the pitch it sounds. The print gives no metronome mark; we sing it at 60 beats a minute, about three minutes, with breaths written in, one of them in the middle of the long "Deo", where everyone sings the vowel again.

## Did you know?

- The 1869 report of the consecration says the houses along the procession's way were decked with flags, carpets, pictures and wreaths of flowers [^lv1869].
- Bruckner's biographer Crawford Howie hears Mozart-like phrases at its start, and in "inaestimabile sacramentum" a falling turn like ones in Bruckner's own E minor Mass [^howie].

## Singing it

- [Soprano](/songs/locus-iste/soprano): you carry Bruckner's tune, mostly on E and D, then F. Your top G comes only on the "o" of the "a Deo" after the second "locus iste", at the start and again when it comes back, and your low B only on the "est" of the first section's last "factus est", so let the line fall gently.
- [Alto](/songs/locus-iste/alto): you sing the Latin under the tune, mostly on C, E and G. Your top B comes once, on "men-" of the second "sacramentum", and your low G on "tus" and "est" of the first section's last "factus est".
- [Tenor](/songs/locus-iste/tenor): you sing the middle of the chords, mostly on G and D, and you start the quiet middle section alone, on "irreprehensibilis est". Each time you sing it, it falls a semitone, C to B, B-flat to A, A-flat to G, so tune each step. Your top E comes in the second "inaestimabile sacramentum".
- [Bass](/songs/locus-iste/bass): you sing Bruckner's bass line, mostly on C, G and D, with hardly a printed rest in the first twenty bars, so take the written breaths. Then you rest through the nine bars of the middle. Your top C comes on "sacramen-" in the second "sacramentum", and your low F once, on "De-" of the last "Deo" before "factus est".

## Questions

### What does Locus iste mean?

"Locus iste" means "this place". The whole text, "Locus iste a Deo factus est, inaestimabile sacramentum, irreprehensibilis est", says: this place was made by God, a holy mystery beyond price; it is without fault [^graduale1908][^ls]. It is the gradual, the chant between the readings, of the Mass for the dedication of a church and its yearly anniversary, so "this place" is the church itself [^graduale1908][^dioezese2017].

### When did Bruckner write Locus iste?

He finished it on 11 August 1869, for the dedication of the votive chapel, the first finished part of the new cathedral in Linz, which Bishop Rudigier consecrated on 29 September 1869 [^howie][^imslp][^lv1869][^dioezese2019]. The catalogues date its first performance to 29 October 1869, in that chapel, though no report from the time confirms it [^imslp][^dioezese2017]. It was printed in Vienna in 1886, as No. 2 of his Vier Graduale [^raettig1886][^howie].

### Why is Locus iste sung at church dedications?

Because its words are the gradual of the Mass for the dedication of a church, sung again each year on the anniversary of that day [^graduale1908][^dioezese2017]. They praise the church as a place made by God, and the same Mass opens with Jacob's words from Genesis on waking from his dream, that this is the house of God and the gate of heaven [^graduale1908][^novavulgata]. Bruckner wrote his setting for the dedication of a chapel in Linz in 1869 [^howie].

### Which part sings the tune?

The soprano sings the tune, mostly on E and D, rising to a top G on "a Deo" [^raettig1886]. In the quiet middle the tenor leads alone on "irreprehensibilis est", and the basses rest for nine bars, so all four parts are needed for Bruckner's chords. Our arrangement is for four singers.
