## 1.jpg: Nevsky Prospekt and the Admiralty, photochrom, about 1890 to 1900

- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, Photochrom Print Collection. LOT 13419, no. 100;
  LC-DIG-ppmsc-03885; LCCN 2001697493.
- Item page: https://www.loc.gov/item/2001697493/ (loc.gov refuses plain requests; read through the Wayback Machine,
  http://web.archive.org/web/20260112202408/https://www.loc.gov/item/2001697493/).
- Rights statement, exact words: "Rights Advisory No known restrictions on reproduction."
- Record also gives: "Created / Published [between ca. 1890 and ca. 1900]"; "Title from the Detroit Publishing Co.,
  catalogue J foreign section, Detroit, Mich. : Detroit Publishing Company, 1905."; "Print no. \"8822\""; "Medium 1
  photomechanical print : photochrom, color."; "Source Collection Views of architecture and other sites primarily in
  Poland, Russia, and the Ukraine".
- File used: the Library's own scan as mirrored on Wikimedia Commons
  (File:The Newsky, (i.e., Nevskii), prospekt and the Admiralty, St. Petersburg, Russia-LCCN2001697493.jpg; Commons
  marks it Public domain, credit "Library of Congress", catalogue link lccn.loc.gov/2001697493). Black mount border
  trimmed; the printed label kept.
- PD by age:
  - US: published (sold as a catalogued print) by 1905 at the latest, before 1931. Public domain.
  - EU: an anonymous photographic print of the 1890s; the term for an anonymous work is 70 years from publication,
    long expired.
  - Israel: an anonymous work is protected 70 years from publication, and a pre-2007 photograph 50 years from the
    negative. Both expired.
- Everything printed on it was read: the printed label "8822 S.-Peterburg. Nevskii prospekt i Admiralteistvo", a faint
  French label at lower left, and shop signs ("Photographie Martini & Cie", "F. Meltzer & Co", "Magazin", "Mekhovoi
  magazin" (fur shop), "Flora", "Muzykalnaya shkola" (music school), and part of a sign ending "-erny..."). Nothing
  demeaning; no trademark front and centre. People in the street are photographed, not drawn; none is identifiable.

## 2.jpg: Brass Hanukkah lamp, Russia, 1890

- Holder of the object: National Museum of American Jewish History, Philadelphia (as named on the file page and its
  category "National Museum of American Jewish History").
- Photograph: Wikimedia Commons,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hanukkah_menorah,_Russia,_1890,_brass,_National_Museum_of_American_Jewish_History.JPG
- File page wikitext, exact: Description "Hanukkah menorah, Russia, 1890, brass, National Museum of American Jewish
  History"; Source "{{own}}"; Author "[[User:Wmpearl|Wmpearl]]"; Date "{{taken in|2011}}"; licence "{{self|Cc-zero}}".
  The API's extmetadata gives LicenseShortName "CC0", Credit "Own work", Artist "Wmpearl".
- A photograph of a 3D object, so the photographer's own licence is what counts: the photographer released it under
  CC0 1.0 (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).
- The object: a cast brass lamp dated 1890, maker unknown. Any copyright in its design is long expired in the US
  (pre-1931), the EU and Israel (anonymous work, 70 years).
- Looked at in full: lions, a crown, a small branched candlestick in an oval, a Star of David, nine candle cups. No
  human figure, no text, no logo or watermark.
- Caption claims: "labelled Russia, 1890", "photographed in 2011 at the National Museum of American Jewish History"
  are from the file page; "Philadelphia" is the museum's city (Commons category). The line the caption points to,
  "geshvinder tsindt, kinder, di dininke likhtelekh on", is in the story's verse 1 gloss ([^k4], p. 32).

### dropped/songbook-1923-p32.jpg: Lider-zamelbukh, No. 24, p. 32 (the printing the story cites)

- Scan: Internet Archive nybc202667, leaf n142, https://archive.org/details/nybc202667/page/n142/mode/1up. Contributor
  and sponsor: National Yiddish Book Center. Publisher "Berlin : Yuval", date "[192-?]" in the IA record; Penn
  Libraries dates this 4th edition [1923].
- **Why held back:** this brief allows a Yiddish songbook page "only when the item page itself states public domain or
  a free licence". The IA item page and metadata state no rights at all (no licenseurl, no rights field), and the Yiddish
  Book Center's own item page returned 403. Wikimedia Commons has a scan of the same page from the 1912 edition
  (File:Hanukkah song "Khaneke, oy Khaneke" first publication with sheet music (Lider-zamlbukh, 1912).jpg, and its
  parent File:Oy Chanuka 01.jpg), tagged {{PD-old}}, but its Source line is only the book's citation: no holder or
  scanning library is named, so it fails the Commons rule too.
- PD by age, if the lead accepts the reasoning used for Silent Night (Hollister) and Shchedryk (Polishchuk):
  - Printed Berlin [1923]; first edition St Petersburg 1911 or 1912.
  - Creators: Mordkhe Rivesman, words, died 1924 (story sources [^leksr], [^posenr]); Pesach Lvov, arranger, died 1913
    ([^k4] memorial page, [^penn]); Susman Kisselgof, compiler, died 1939 (Commons author line; to confirm); A.
    Zhitomirsky, co-arranger, died 1937 (from memory; to confirm). Latest death 1939, so life plus 70 ended by 2010 in
    the EU and Israel. US: published 1923, before 1931.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright.
- Read in full: title and credits in Latin and Hebrew letters ("Oj chanuke, oj chanuke. A chanuke lid. (Chsidisch.)
  Werter fun M. Riwesman. Arrangirt fun P. Lwow."), tempo marks, the sung Yiddish underlay of verse 1 (including
  "lejbt Gott far di nissim", printed as it stands), and the footer "Aufführungsrecht vorbehalten" (performance right
  reserved, a 1923 notice long lapsed). No figure, nothing demeaning.

## Rejected

- Wikimedia Commons "Hanukkah Lamp - Lemberg F 5119.jpg" (Jewish Museum New York): CC BY-SA 3.0.
- "WLA jewishmuseum Polish Hanukkah Lamp.jpg": CC BY-SA 2.5.
- "Hanukkah lamp from Lodz, Poland, prior to 1881" (CC0, Wmpearl): reflections of wall lettering across the photo; the
  Russian lamp is the clearer picture.
- Dreidel photos on Commons: CC BY 2.0, CC BY-SA 4.0, or undated modern objects.
- Commons portrait of Rivesman (183 x 260 px, "Unknown author", "before 1925", credited to the Jewish Music Research
  Centre) and the 1912 expedition photo of Kisselgof (credited "YIVO institute collection"): publication history and
  US status unclear, so dropped.
- The Šiauliai Aušros Museum lamps (Public Domain Mark in Europeana): undated, and the holder page could not be read.
- Met Museum lamps (CC0 on the holder's page): Italian, Dutch and German; no tie to this song's Russian and Lithuanian
  setting.
- Vilna ghetto Hanukkah posters of 1942 and 1943 (Lithuanian Central State Archives, PDM): not this song's story, and
  not something to use as decoration.
