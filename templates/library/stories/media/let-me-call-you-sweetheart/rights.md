# Let Me Call You Sweetheart: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. Makers in the song file (templates/library/let-me-call-you-sweetheart.json): Beth Slater
Whitson, words, died 1930; Leo Friedman, music, died 1927. First published 1910, US. Life + 70: Friedman's term
ended 31 December 1997, Whitson's 31 December 2000. Under life + 100 (Mexico) Whitson's term runs to 31 December 2030,
so the song page itself is withheld there to the end of 2030. Rule applied: any non-maker whose text or music is on a
published image must be free under life + 100 by then, or be cropped out.

  Compositions, 1910 (January to June), p. 808, entry 14039 (the story's [^cce1910]).

- Crop: entry 14039 only (1160 x 400 px, not upscaled). Words in the crop, all read: "Let me call you sweetheart;
  words by Beth Slater Whitson, music by L. Friedman. [14039 © Apr. 8, 1910; 2 c. Apr. 11, 1910; E 230427; Leo
  Friedman, Chicago, Ill." Neighbouring entries left out.
- Rights: a work of the US government, published 1910: public domain in the US (17 U.S.C. 105) and, published before
  1931, by age too. EU and Israel: an unsigned official catalogue by a public body, 70 years from publication, ended
  1980. Mexico: no non-maker text (catalogue entry by a federal office, 1910). Faithful scan of a flat page.

## 2.jpg: 1911 Rossiter advertisement (detail)

- Shelton Brooks, There'll Come a Time (Chicago: Harold Rossiter Music Co., 1911), back cover (the story's
  [^jhu1911]). Holder: Johns Hopkins University, Sheridan Libraries, Sam Carner sheet music collection, MS-MUS-0060,
  box 5, folder 237. Scan: Internet Archive item carner_5_237, image https://archive.org/download/carner_5_237/page/n4.jpg
  (3227 x 4307), saved as raw/pages/carner_n4.jpg; metadata raw/meta-carner_5_237.json (no rights field; a
  collection-wide content warning about offensive songs in the collection, not about this page).
- Crop: the right-hand column only (1540 x 3150 px, final 782 x 1600): the boxed advertisement text and the chorus with
  its two copyright notices. Left out: the miniature of the cover painting (a woman: a painted human figure), the
  "Best Sellers" list of other songs and writers, the order line and the Rossiter logo (it holds a portrait).
- Words in the crop, all read: "Let Me Call You Sweetheart / By Leo Friedman and Beth Slater Whitson / This is the new
  song sensation by the writers of "Meet Me To-Night in Dreamland" and from present indications will be the biggest
  song hit this Country has ever seen. In addition to having the prettiest melody you have ever heard, it has the most
  beautiful title-page ever printed on sheet music. The cover is in colors, reproduced from original painting by one of
  the well known artists of the day, and the miniature shown here can give you only a faint idea of the real beauty of
  the title page itself. Be sure you get a copy of this song at once." Then the chorus (words and music) and "Copyright
  MCMX by Leo Friedman, Chicago,- British Copyright Secured. Copyright Assigned Dec. 13, 1910 to Harold Rossiter
  Music Co., Chicago." Nothing demeaning.
- Public domain by age: published 1911 in the US (IA date 1911; the advert calls the song "new"), so public domain
  there. Authors in the crop: Friedman (died 1927) and Whitson (died 1930), life + 70 ended 2000; the advertising copy
  is unsigned publisher's text (anonymous: 70 years from publication in the EU and Israel, ended 1981; Mexico: 100
  years from publication, ended 2011). EU and Israel: public domain. Mexico: the makers' own term (to 2030) is the
  only one still running, and the page is withheld for it. Shelton Brooks (died 1975) wrote the host song: none of his
  words or music is in the crop. Faithful scan of a flat page.

## 3.jpg: 1925 Rossiter advertisement, opening paragraph (detail)

- Let Me Call You Sweetheart (Chicago: Harold Rossiter Music Co., printing of 1925 or later), inside front cover:
  advertisement for "Since You Called Me Sweetheart", copyright MCMXXV by Harold Rossiter Music Co. (the story's
  [^rossiter1925]). From the collection of Martin R. Lucas and Corinne Becknell Lucas; scanned by Public Resource.
  Internet Archive item letmecallyouswee00whit, image https://archive.org/download/letmecallyouswee00whit/page/n1.jpg
  (3171 x 4391), saved as raw/pages/whit25_n1.jpg; metadata raw/meta-letmecallyouswee00whit.json: licenseurl
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ , date 1925, contributor Public Resource.
- Crop: the heading "Read This---It's Interesting" and the first paragraph only (1865 x 588 px, final 1600 x 504).
  Left out: the miniature of the answer song's cover (a drawn woman), the headline, the rest of the copy and the
  answer song's printed chorus (its writers are unnamed, so their death years are unknown: excluded).
- Words in the crop, all read: "Read This---It's Interesting / THERE seems to be some sort of magic charm about the
  song "LET ME CALL YOU SWEETHEART," for it has actually established two conditions hitherto unknown in the music
  world. In the first place "Let Me Call You Sweetheart" is the first and only song that has ever "come back" into a
  second wave of popularity. It was one of the big song hits of 1911 and is now once again the biggest selling
  popular song on the market." Nothing demeaning.
- Public domain by age: published 1925 in the US, public domain there since 1 January 2021 (95 years). The text is
  unsigned publisher's copy (anonymous: 70 years from publication in the EU and Israel, ended 1995; Mexico: 100 years
  from publication, ended 2025, and the page is withheld there to 2030 anyway). Faithful scan of a flat page.

## 4.jpg: page 2 of a later Rossiter printing

  "Copyright MCMX, by Harold Rossiter Music Co.", so printed after the assignment of 13 December 1910; the exact year
  is not printed. From the collection of Martin R. Lucas and Corinne Becknell Lucas; scanned by Public Resource.
  Internet Archive item letmecallyouswee00whit_0, image https://archive.org/download/letmecallyouswee00whit_0/page/n1.jpg
  (3538 x 4755, final 1190 x 1600, whole page), saved as raw/pages/whit0_n1.jpg; metadata
  raw/meta-letmecallyouswee00whit_0.json: licenseurl https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ , contributor
  Public Resource. Its cover (Mary F. Kidder, a harlequin and a woman) is not used.
- Words on the page, all read: title and subtitle, "Music by Leo Friedman and Words by Beth Slater Whitson", "Ask Your
  Dealer for a Player-Roll or Phonograph Record of This Piece", "Writers of "Meet Me To-Night in Dreamland"",
  "Moderato", "rall.", the verse lines "I am dream-ing Dear of you Day by day Dream-ing when the" and "Long-ing for you
  all the while More and more Long-ing for the", "Copyright MCMX, by Harold Rossiter Music Co. Chicago, Ill.
  International Copyright Secured", "NOTE: This song is also published for Male and Mixed Quartette, and also for Band
  and Orchestra." No figures, nothing demeaning.
- Public domain by age: copyright 1910, US, published before 1931: public domain there. The page names only Friedman
  (died 1927) and Whitson (died 1930); no arranger is credited. Life + 70 ended 2000: EU and Israel public domain.
  Mexico: makers only, and the page is withheld for them. Faithful scan of a flat page.
