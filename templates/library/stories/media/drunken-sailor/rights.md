# Drunken Sailor: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. All four are public domain by age, all faithful scans of flat printed pages from the Internet
Archive. Records saved beside this file: pages/olmsted_meta.json, pages/whall_meta.json, pages/terry_meta.json (IA
metadata), pages/olmsted_pn.json and pages/whall_pn.json (page maps), pages/olmsted_djvu.txt and pages/whall_djvu.txt
(OCR). Every image was looked at in full and every printed word read.

## 1.jpg: plate "Barque NORTH AMERICA - New-London", Olmsted, Incidents of a Whaling Voyage (1841)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/incidentsofwhali00olmsrich (scan image n46, an unnumbered plate between printed
  pages 42, running head "SHIP AND SHIPMATES.", and 43, running head "SHIP NORTH AMERICA.").
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/incidentsofwhali00olmsrich/page/n46.jpg (2008 x 3490), rotated 90 degrees
  to landscape and trimmed to the plate's printed area (caption lines kept).
- IA record (exact): title "Incidents of a whaling voyage : to which are added observations on the scenery, manners
  and customs, and missionary stations of the Sandwich and Society Islands"; creator "Olmsted, Francis Allyn,
  1819-1844"; publisher "New York : D. Appleton and Co."; date "1841"; contributor "University of California
  Libraries"; call_number "ucb_banc:GLAD-33321"; possible-copyright-status "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published New York 1841. Drawn by Francis Allyn Olmsted, died 1844; lithographed by the Endicott firm of New
    York ("Lith of Endicott N.Y.").
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel (life + 70): Olmsted's term ended 1914; anyone at the lithographer's who worked on an 1841 plate
    died long before 1956.
  - A faithful scan of a flat print adds no new copyright.
- Words on the plate, all read: "Drawn by F. A. Olmsted", "Lith of Endicott. N.Y.", "Barque NORTH AMERICA -
  New-London." No human figure: the deck was checked at full size (work/deck.jpg); it shows only the hull, three
  whaleboats on davits, rigging and a flag.

## 2.jpg: Olmsted, Incidents of a Whaling Voyage (1841), p. 116, detail

- Item: same as above, scan image n127 (printed page 116, running head "116 CAPE HORN."). The text that introduces
  the song (11 February 1840, "Mr. Freeman usually officiates as chorister ...") is on p. 115, scan image n126.
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/incidentsofwhali00olmsrich/page/n127.jpg (2008 x 3490); cropped to the
  running head and the first two staves.
- Licence: PD-old. Reasoning as for 1.jpg: published 1841; author died 1844; the tune is traditional; IA
  "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"; faithful scan of a flat page.
- Words in the crop, all read: "116", "CAPE HORN.", "Ho! Ho! and up she ris-es. Ho! Ho! and up she ris-es.", "Ho!
  Ho! and up she ris-es, Ear-ly in the morn-ing." Faint show-through of the reverse page only.
- Left out by the crop: the rest of p. 116, "And another song, accompanied with the chorus, which vies with the song

  Hurrah! Hurrah! for Nancy Fannana", and a closing paragraph on sailors' songs. Nothing on the page demeans anyone;
  it is cropped only to keep the picture on our song.

## 3.jpg: Whall, Ships, Sea Songs and Shanties, 3rd edition (1913), p. 107

- Item: https://archive.org/details/shipsseasongssha00whal (scan image n126, printed page 107).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/shipsseasongssha00whal/page/n126.jpg (2439 x 3416); whole page.
- IA record (exact): title "Ships, sea songs and shanties"; creator "Whall, W. B" and "Whall, R. H"; publisher
  "Glasgow, J. Brown & son"; date "1913"; contributor "Smithsonian Libraries"; call_number "39088006794259";
  external-identifier "urn:oclc:record:1157212870"; no rights or licence field.
- Title page (OCR, pages/whall_djvu.txt): "Collected by W. B. WHALL, Master Mariner / The Songs harmonised by R. H.
  WHALL, Mus.Bac., F.R.C.O., Etc. / Illustrations by VERONICA WHALL / THIRD EDITION. ENLARGED / [ALL RIGHTS STRICTLY
  RESERVED] / GLASGOW: JAMES BROWN & SON ... 1913".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published Glasgow 1913. The page's note and transcription are W. B. Whall's (William Boultbee Whall, master
    mariner, died at Barry, Glamorgan, 31 January 1917: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._B._Whall). The tune and words
    are traditional.
  - The page carries single-line melodies only, with no piano or vocal harmony, so nothing on it is R. H. Whall's
    harmonisation (his death year was not found), and it has no drawing by Veronica Whall.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain. "All rights strictly reserved" on the title page does not change
    that.
  - EU and Israel (life + 70): W. B. Whall's term ended 31 December 1987. The UK publisher's typographical
    arrangement right (25 years) ended in 1938.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page.
- Words on the page, all read: "Early in the Morning."; "IT is generally supposed that shantying was never allowed in
  the Royal Service; this is not quite correct, as this song and "Cheer'ly Man" were sometimes allowed, particularly
  in revenue cutters and similar craft, and sotto voce in larger vessels. Both songs were used in the old Indiamen of
  "John Company.""; "Lively. All Chorus."; "Hoo-rah! and up she ri-ses; Hoo-rah! and up she ri-ses; Hoo-rah! and up
  she ri-ses Ear-ly in the morn-ing."; "What shall we do with a drunken sail-or? What shall we do with a drunken
  sailor? What shall we do with a drunken sail-or Ear-ly in the morn-ing?"; "Put him in the long-boat and make him
  bale her, Put him in the long-boat and make him bale her, Put him in the long boat and make him bale her, Ear-ly in
  the morn-ing."; "What shall we do with a drunken soldier? Put him in the guardroom till he gets sober."; "These were
  the only two verses. Each verse began with- Hoo-rah and up she rises."; "The whole was sung in chorus, in quick
  march time, with no pause between the verses. It was the only song used for a "stamp and go," and when crews were
  reduced and it was no longer possible to "walk away" with anything, the song at once dropped out of use."; "107".
  Nothing demeans anyone. ("John Company" is the old nickname of the East India Company.)

## Rejected

- "Parma - crew at the capstan, weighing anchor" (Wikimedia Commons, from the National Maritime Museum's Flickr
  Commons stream, 1932-33): "No known copyright restrictions" is not one of our licences, the photographer is not
  named, and a 1932-33 photograph's US status is not clear.
- Loch Etive (ship, 1877), "Another view of the foc'sle head, showing capstan, anchors, bell" (State Library of
  Victoria H99.220/2308, Commons): tagged PD-Australia only; unknown photographer, dated only "between 1885 and 1911",
  so an unpublished anonymous photograph could still be in US copyright (120 years from creation). Not clearly OK.
- New Bedford Free Public Library whaling photographs on Digital Commonwealth ("Crew at Work on Deck of the Charles W.
  Morgan", 1886; "Deck of Bark Kathleen", 1889, and others): licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, "Rights status not
  evaluated". Not allowed.
- Swedish National Maritime Museum (Sjohistoriska) photographs on the four-masted barque Archibald Russell, 1929
  ("Ankarlattning Gangspel", Fo58901A; "Ra hissas med hjalp av gangspel pa stordack", Fo58902A), Public Domain Mark:
  unknown photographer and 1929, so the US status is not clear. Not used.
- Library of Congress photographs: loc.gov answers 403 to plain requests from here, so none could be checked.
- Rijksmuseum capstan models (NG-MC-194, about 1842, and others): available as CC0, but a model is weaker than the
  real ship in 1.jpg; not used.
