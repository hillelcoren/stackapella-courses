## 1.jpg: the Fisk University Jubilee Singers, carte de visite, 1870s

- Item: "Jubilee Singers, Fisk University, Nashville, Tenn." 1 photographic print on carte de visite mount: albumen;
  6 x 10 cm. [Nashville, Tenn.]: American Missionary Association, publisher, [between 1870 and 1880]. Photographer:
  "Black, James Wallace, 1825-1896" (record note: "Photographer attribution from similar photograph: LOT 14105, no. 8").
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division. Call number LOT 14022, no. 75; digital ID
  LC-DIG-ppmsca-11008 (front); LCCN 2010647805. Purchased from William A. Gladstone, 1995 (Gladstone Collection of
  African American Photographs).
- Rights statement, exact words, catalogue record field 540: "No known restrictions on publication."
  Source: https://lccn.loc.gov/2010647805/marcxml (saved as raw/records/lccn2010647805.marcxml). Item page:
  https://www.loc.gov/item/2010647805/ (old URL https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2010647805/). Note: www.loc.gov
  answers 403 to plain curl and to WebFetch from here, so the item page itself was not loaded; the catalogue record is
  the Library's own record for the same item.
- Record's description (field 520), used for the names in the alt text: "Photo shows a studio group portrait of the
  Fisk University Jubilee Singers, with (from l. to r.) Minnie Tate, Greene Evans, Isaac Dickerson, Jennie Jackson,
  Maggie Porter, Ella Sheppard, Thomas Rutling, Benjamin Holmes, and Eliza Walker."
- File: the LoC's own JPEG (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/ppmsca/11000/11008v.jpg) is only
  1024 x 608, saved as raw/loc-ppmsca11008v.jpg for comparison. The final is made from the full-size scan of the same
  LoC file on Wikimedia Commons, used only as a host:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jubilee_Singers,_Fisk_University,_Nashville,_Tenn._LCCN2010647805.jpg
  (5126 x 3044, saved as raw/commons-LCCN2010647805.jpg). The Commons page names the source as the Library of Congress
  (catalog http://lccn.loc.gov/2010647805, digital ID ppmsca.11008), licence "Public domain", tags PD-old-100-expired
  and CC-PD-Mark. Same image and same aspect ratio as the LoC JPEG (compared by eye).
- Public domain by age as well:
  - US: a photograph issued in the 1870s, before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel (life plus 70): the attributed photographer died in 1896, so the term ended by 1966; if instead
    anonymous, 70 years from publication in the 1870s ended long ago.
- People shown: the record names them; all were students in 1871 and died long ago (none could be living). A
  dignified studio portrait, allowed by IMAGES-SPIRITUALS-NOTE.md. The card's front carries no printed words.
- Caption facts:
  - "students who left Nashville on a concert tour in 1871": The Story of the Jubilee Singers (1892 edition), p. 16,
    chapter III: "The company as it left Nashville, October 6, 1871, ... numbered thirteen persons. These were Mr.
    White, ... Miss Wells, ... and eleven students: Ella Sheppard, Maggie L. Porter, Jennie Jackson, Minnie Tate, Eliza
    Walker, Phoebe J. Anderson, Thomas Rutling, Benjamin M. Holmes, Greene Evans, Isaac P. Dickerson, and George
    Wells." (raw/text/stesi00mars_djvu.txt, line 803 onward; the page head before it reads "THE FORLORN HOPE
    STARTS. 15", so the passage is on p. 16.) All nine in the photo are in that list.
  - "the 1892 edition of their story ... holds this song's earliest known printing": story, [^loudin1892],
    [^hymnarchive]. The 1892 book's own note says Fisk "disbanded the company on its return from Europe in 1878"; the
    caption does not claim the 1892 company was these singers.
  - The date "1870s" is the LoC's "[between 1870 and 1880]"; the caption claims no exact year for the photo.

## 2.jpg: The Story of the Jubilee Singers (1892), No. 131, "The Crucifixion", p. 302

- Item: J. B. T. Marsh, Story of the Jubilee Singers [with their songs], new edition with supplement by F. J. Loudin
  (Cleveland: Cleveland Printing & Publishing Co., 1892). Copy: Princeton Theological Seminary Library (IA
  "contributor" and "sponsor"), scanned 2012. https://archive.org/details/stesi00mars ; page
  https://archive.org/details/stesi00mars/page/n319 ; file https://archive.org/download/stesi00mars/page/n319.jpg
  (2171 x 3451), saved as raw/ia-loudin1892-n319.jpg. OCLC 1085310993. Whole page, resized.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence; PD by age:
  - Published in Cleveland in 1892 (IA "date": "1892"; the book's own note is signed "J. B. T. M. ... 1892").
  - J. B. T. Marsh: 1840 to 1887 per the Smithsonian Libraries record of the book
    (https://www.si.edu/object/story-jubilee-singers-their-songs-jbt-marsh:siris_sil_44947, as shown in search
    results). F. J. Loudin died 3 November 1904 in Ravenna, Ohio (BlackPast, "Loudin, Frederick J. (1840-1904)",
    https://blackpast.org/aah/loudin-frederick-j-1840-1904, as shown in search results; others give birth about 1836).
    The song is anonymous traditional; the harmony is unsigned.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD. EU and Israel: the named authors died in 1904 or before, and an anonymous
    1892 work's 70 years from publication ended in 1962; PD.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019
    DSM Directive).
- Words on the page, all read: "No. 131. The Crucifixion."; verse 1 under the notes; "2 Were you there when they
  crowned him with the thorns? etc. / 3 ... pierced him in the side? etc. / 4 ... laid him in the tomb? etc."; "302".
  Nothing else.
- Caption facts: story [^loudin1892] (p. 302, No. 131, four verses with "crowned him with the thorns").

- Rights: the IA record states no licence; PD by age:

  - US: published before 1931, so PD. EU and Israel: life plus 70 from 1942 ended at the close of 2012; PD.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page, as above.

- Caption facts: story [^work1907] (p. 46, four parts in G, five verses, echoes, "the shape sung today"),
  [^cdh-fjwork] (Frederick J. Work, Fisk). "Edited at Fisk University": the story says "At Fisk University in
  Nashville, Frederick J. Work edited ...".

  [^dett1927]; IA "contributor"). https://archive.org/details/religiousfolkson00dett ; page
  https://archive.org/details/religiousfolkson00dett/page/n277 ; file
  https://archive.org/download/religiousfolkson00dett/page/n277.jpg (2850 x 4553), saved as
  raw/ia-dett1927-n277.jpg. Whole page, resized. This is the story's "original" link.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence; PD:
  - Title verso (OCR, raw/text/dett1927_djvu.txt line 240): "Copyright 1927 by The Hampton Normal and Agricultural
    Institute, Hampton, Virginia".
  - US: works published in 1927 entered the public domain on 1 January 2023 (95-year term).
  - EU and Israel (life plus 70): R. Nathaniel Dett died 1943 (IA creator "Dett, R. Nathaniel, 1882-1943"; story
    [^lva-dett]), so his term ended at the close of 2013. This page reprints the 1909 Hampton setting, which is the
    1907 Fisk setting (story [^hampton1909], [^work1907]); its makers, Frederick J. Work (died 1942) and Hampton's
    musical directors of 1909, are all long out of term, and the 1909 book itself was published in the US before 1931.
