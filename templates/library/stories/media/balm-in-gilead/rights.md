- Every word on the crop: "Balm in Gilead." / "There is a Balm in Gil-e-ad, To make the wounded whole, There is a

- Rights: no licence stated by IA; PD by age:
  - Published Nashville 1915. Author John Wesley Work died 1925 (LoC name authority n92053236, "Work, John Wesley,
    1873-1925", in the HathiTrust record, pages/hathi-record-100967825.json). The four-part setting first appeared in
    Frederick J. Work's book of 1907; Frederick Jerome Work died 24 January 1942 (Canterbury Dictionary of Hymnology,
    https://hymnology.hymnsam.co.uk/f/frederick-jerome-work , saved as pages/cdh-fjwork.html: "b. Nashville,
    Tennessee, 11 August 1877; d. Bordentown, New Jersey, 24 January 1942"). (One IA record, for Number Two, gives
    him as 1871-1925; either way the conclusion is the same.) Words and tune traditional.
  - US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 runs out by 1 January 2013 at the latest.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.
- Why not the 1907 first printing itself: Number One is full view only on HathiTrust (University of Michigan
  mdp.39015074207047 and Emory emu.010001355235, both rights code "pd", "US bib date1 < 1931"); HathiTrust answered
  403 to plain requests from here, so the page could not be seen and no image is made from it. The Hymnology Archive
  PDF is not the holding library's copy, so it was not used. The 1915 page reprints the 1907 setting (story,
  [^work1915]: "prints the 1907 setting again"), and the caption says 1915.

## Rejected

- Matson LC-DIG-matpc-15830 ("Air views of Palestine ... The land of Gilead", 1931): the aeroplane's wheel and wing
  fill the frame, and the record's place field reads "West Bank", which does not fit Gilead east of the Jordan.
- Matson LC-DIG-matpc-15831 (Gilead's paths leading to Jordan, 1931): a large blank white label patch across the
  picture.
- Matson "Villages in the Hauran (Land of Gilead)" series: the Hauran lies north of biblical Gilead; the caption
  would have needed a claim we have not sourced.
- The full frame of matpc-02462: people in the middle ground (see above), so only the cropped detail is used.
- The Victor catalogue of 1912 (Fisk quartet listing): a photograph of people would make a second photograph, and
  the rule is one, with no people.
- Work 1907 Number One p. 31 and the Hymnology Archive PDF: see 2.jpg.
