## 1.jpg and 2.jpg: The Scots Musical Museum, vol. 5, pp. 430 and 431, Nos. 417 and 418 (1796 engraving, 1839 printing)

- Item: "The Scotish musical museum : consisting of upwards of six hundred songs, with proper basses for the
  pianoforte", vol. 5 (Edinburgh: William Blackwood and Sons; London: Thomas Cadell, 1839), originally published by
  James Johnson. Contributor University of Western Ontario, call number MZ2083; sponsor University of Western Ontario -
  University of Toronto Libraries. Item https://archive.org/details/scotishmusicalmu05john . This is the same copy
  the Auld Lang Syne set uses for p. 426 (No. 413); these are different pages.
- Files: https://archive.org/download/scotishmusicalmu05john/page/n28.jpg (2479 x 4219, p. 430, raw/uwo1839-n28.jpg;
  the final is the whole page) and .../page/n29.jpg (2571 x 4222, p. 431, raw/uwo1839-n29.jpg; the final keeps the
  top 3360 rows, ending under the last verse of No. 418 and above the ornament row and No. 419, "The Duke of Gordon
  has three daughters"). Page numbers confirmed from the item's page_numbers.json (leaf 30 = p. 430, leaf 31 = p. 431).
- Rights: the IA metadata has no rights, licenseurl or possible-copyright-status field (checked via
  https://archive.org/metadata/scotishmusicalmu05john/metadata), so this rests on PD by age:
  - Engraved pages first issued in vol. 5 of Johnson's Museum, dated 1796 by the National Library of Scotland (the
    story's [^smm]); this printing 1839.
  - Creators per the IA record: James Johnson (about 1750 to 1811), Robert Burns (1759 to 1796), Stephen Clarke (died
    1797), William Stenhouse (1773 to 1827), David Laing (1793 to 1878), Charles Kirkpatrick Sharpe (1781 to 1851).
  - US: published before 1931, PD. UK, EU and Israel: life plus 70, every author dead by 1878, PD.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM
    Directive; UK: the IPO's 2015 guidance).
- Compared by eye with the story's own scan of the 1796 printing (NLS Ing.43, archive.org/details/scotsmusicalmuv300john,
  images n26 and n27; CC BY-NC-SA, viewed for comparison only, deleted, not kept): same numbers, titles, tempo,
  layout, words and verse text. Differences seen: the 1839 p. 430 adds the line "Written for this Work by Robert
  Burns." under the title, and the engraved "B" at the foot of the 1796 p. 430 is not on the 1839 page; a few bass notes
  differ on p. 431. The captions therefore say "here in the 1839 reissue", claim nothing about the plates, and do not
  mention the B. Caption 1 quotes the 1839 heading as what the reissue shows.
- Words on the pages, all read: p. 430 title, "Written for this Work by Robert Burns.", "Very Slow", "The last part
  repeated in Chorus", the sung text ("Comin thro' the rye, poor body ... She draigl't a' her petticoatie ... Oh Jenny's
  a' weet poor body, Jenny's seldom dry"), and the two verses with "Cho. Oh Jenny's a' weet &c." p. 431 (as cropped):
  title, "Very Slow", the sung verse, and the "well" and "town" verses ("Ilka Jenny has her Jockey": Scots names for a
  lass and a lad). Nothing mocking or demeaning. No figures, vignettes, stamps or watermarks.
- Caption facts: No. 417, 1st Sett, Burns, Jenny's soaked petticoat (weet, draigl't): story "The two sets of 1796" and
  "The bawdy versions" ([^smm], [^dick], [^dick-gloss]); "How it is sung"/"The full words" call it "Burns's own first
  set, the Jenny song". No. 418 unsigned and the popular version: story opening paragraph and "Who wrote the second
  set?" ([^graham], [^nq1876], [^rpo]); Graham calls it "the most popular" (seen on p. 11 of 4.jpg's book). 1796:
  front matter and [^smm].

## 3.jpg: The Miller's Wedding, Strathspey, A Collection of Scots Reels or Country Dances, p. 41 (about 1790)

- Item: "A collection of Scots reels or country dances : with a bass for the violincello or harpsichord" (London:
  Printed and sold by Preston and Sons, no. 97 Strand), IA date 1790, "Date from British Library catalogue online".
  Contributor and sponsor Boston Public Library, call number M1746.C6 1780 (Allen A. Brown Collection, bound with
  Campbell's dance books). The record's tune list includes "The Millers wedding : strathspey". Item
  https://archive.org/details/collectionofscot00bost ; page https://archive.org/details/collectionofscot00bost/page/n48/mode/1up ;
  file https://archive.org/download/collectionofscot00bost/page/n48.jpg (2898 x 2076), raw/bremner-n48.jpg. The final
  is the whole leaf, resized. IMSLP describes this Preston issue as a "Re-issue of Bremner's plates"
  (https://imslp.org/wiki/A_Collection_of_Scots_Reels_or_Country_Dances_(Bremner,_Robert)); the caption does not rely
  on that, saying only "the same collection".
- Rights: the IA record has no rights, licenseurl or copyright field (https://archive.org/metadata/collectionofscot00bost).
  PD by age: engraved music published about 1790; Robert Bremner, compiler and first publisher, died 1789; the
  publishers John Preston and his firm were active in the 1770s to 1830s. US: published before 1931, PD. UK, EU and
  Israel: any author or engraver of a work published about 1790 died far more than 70 years ago; anonymous works run
  70 years from publication. PD. Faithful scan of a flat page, as above.
- Words on the page, all read: "The Millers Wedding", "Strathspey", "41", a faint pencilled "47" in the margin, a
  tuning note at the left edge, and "Number 6" at the foot. No figures, ornaments or stamps. The title is slightly
  clipped at the top edge of the scan but readable.
- Caption facts: story "The tune": "Both Dick and the collector John Glen trace it to a tune printed as The Miller's
  Wedding in Robert Bremner's collection of reels of 1759" ([^dick], [^glen]). Glen, note to No. 413, pp. 191 to 192 (raw/glen1900-djvu.txt,
  from https://archive.org/details/cu31924022326353): "'The Miller's Daughter,' which tune, in its turn, is taken from
  'The Miller's Wedding,' Bremner's Reels, 1759." Strathspey: the page heading.

- Words on the page, all read: "10", "Scottish Songs", "Comin' thro' the rye.", "Arranged by T. M. Mudie", "= 72",

## Rejected or not found

- Watlen's printing of the second set (Edinburgh, August 1794 by Glen, the eighth number of his Old Scots Songs):
  no scan found. The Internet Archive's Watlen collection (Shand10, PD Mark) has only Numbers 1 to 6, and its text
  has no "rye" or "Gin a body" (raw/shand10-djvu.txt). A National Trust for Scotland listing of a 1795 Watlen sheet "If
  a Body Meet a Body" was seen in search results only (the page refused the fetch); not used.
- The 1796 NLS scan (the story's own): CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 on the Internet Archive, so linked as "original", not hosted.
- No photograph: no free photo of Rye Water or of a Scottish rye field with a clear licence and no people was found
  (Rijksmuseum rye photos are 1970s Dutch farm scenes with people; LoC refuses automated fetches). Burns's Alloway
  cottage is already on the Auld Lang Syne page.
