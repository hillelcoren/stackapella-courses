## 1.jpg and 2.jpg: Valerius, Neder-landtsche Gedenck-clanck (Haarlem, 1626)

- Item: Ghent University Library copy (barcode 800000196610, as the story's [^valerius1626] gives it), digitized by
  Google (Google Books wxxKAAAAcAAJ), uploaded to the Internet Archive as bub_gb_wxxKAAAAcAAJ (2016).
  Item: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_wxxKAAAAcAAJ
  Metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/bub_gb_wxxKAAAAcAAJ/metadata): title "Nederlandtsche gedenckclanck. Kortelick
  openbarende de voornaemste geschiedenissen ...", creator "Valerius", date "1626", publisher "gedruckt voor
  d'erfgenamen vanden autheur", contributor "Ghent University", scanner "google", collection "europeanlibraries".
- Rights statement, exact field on the IA record: "licenseurl": "http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/"
  (Public Domain Mark).
- 1.jpg: title page, image n6, https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_wxxKAAAAcAAJ/page/n6.jpg (2500 x 2022), saved as
  raw/val-n6.jpg. The final is cropped at the foot, just under "Met previlegie voor ses Iaren", to leave out the Ghent
  library ownership stamp (a round stamp with a heraldic lion) and the small "Digitized by Google" mark.
- 2.jpg: p. 170, image n183, https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_wxxKAAAAcAAJ/page/n183.jpg (2500 x 1944), saved as
  raw/val-n183.jpg. The final is cropped at the foot, under the catchword "De Ro-", to leave out the "Digitized by
  Google" mark.
- Public domain by age:
  - Printed at Haarlem in 1626; Adriaen Valerius died at Veere on 27 January 1625 (Meertens 1942, quoting the
    grave text: "Den 27 Januari 1625 overleet den eersamen Adriaen Valerius").
  - US: published 1626, PD. EU and Israel (life plus 70): PD since 1696 at the latest; no copyright existed then.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v. Corel, and in the EU
    under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive.
- What they show:
  - 1.jpg: type only, plus one line of music (the "Canon met 4." on "Neemt my inder hand, Hoort i'nt kort
    verklaren, Wat ons hier in't Land Al is weder-varen": "Take me in hand, hear told in short what has befallen us
    here in the land"). No figures, no ornaments with people, no logos once cropped.
  - 2.jpg: type, music and lute tablature. The one ornament is the decorated initial W. I looked at it at full scan
    resolution (crop 3x): foliage and scrolls only, no face, mask or figure visible. Checker: please look again.
  - Every word read: the Latin motto "Timenti dominum omnia cooperantur in bonum" (all things work together for good
    to him who fears the Lord), two lines from Heinsius on the Redeemer, Psalm 33:12 in French, Valerius's four
    verses (God strikes down "our enemy"; the devil as a roaring lion; watch and pray), and the catchword. Nothing
    names or demeans a people or faith.
  - The title page says the book is "Verciert met verscheydene aerdige figuerlicke platen" (adorned with figured
    plates). Those engravings are on other pages; neither final contains one.
- Caption facts: title page itself (Haerlem, 1626, "Gedruct voor d'Erfgenamen vanden Autheur, woonende ter Veer in
  Zeeland"); p. 170 itself ("Stem: Hey wilder dan wild", no title, tablature). Both are in the story under
  [^valerius1626].

## 3.jpg: the Grote or Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk, Veere (1850 to 1880)

- Item: "Gezicht op de Grote of Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk in Veere", albumen print, anonymous photographer ("anoniem",
  place Veere), 1850 - 1880; photo 208 x 232 mm on a mount 378 x 475 mm; inscription "title: 'Vere'"; "Gift of
  Jonkheer B.W.F. van Riemsdijk", 1924. Object number RP-F-1924-7.
- Holder: Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Item page:
  https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/RP-F-1924-7--8dc40a24d705c381a27fa100be18f08b ; persistent id
  https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200142154 ; EDM record
  https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/200142154?_profile=edm&_mediatype=application/rdf%2Bxml
- Rights statement: item page "Public domain", linking https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/deed.en ;
  EDM record: `<edm:rights rdf:resource="http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/"/>`,
  `<edm:isShownBy rdf:resource="https://iiif.micr.io/gOnTg/full/max/0/default.jpg"/>`.
- Downloaded: https://iiif.micr.io/gOnTg/full/max/0/default.jpg (5748 x 4506), saved as raw/rijks-RP-F-1924-7.jpg.
  The final crops to the photograph with its rounded corners, leaving out the blue-grey mount and its empty printed
  label frame.
- Public domain by age:
  - A photograph made between 1850 and 1880 by an anonymous photographer.
  - Netherlands and the EU: an anonymous work is protected 70 years from lawful publication (or creation, if never
    published); ended by 1950 at the latest.
  - US: if published, it was published before 1931; if unpublished, the anonymous term is 120 years from creation,
    which ended by the end of 2000.
  - Israel: anonymous works 70 years, and a pre-2007 photograph 50 years from the making of the negative; both
    expired.
  - The holder's own Public Domain Mark agrees. This is the photograph itself (a PD work), not a modern photo of a
    building, so no photographer's licence is needed.
- What it shows: the church from above, with houses, a walled churchyard, young trees and fields. No statues or
  carvings of people can be made out on the church. On the path by the young trees, at the lower left, there may
  be one tiny blurred shape that could be a passer-by in the long exposure (a few pixels at 1600 wide; I cannot
  tell). It is a photograph, not a drawn figure, and no one alive. No logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: P. J. Meertens, "Adriaen Valerius' leven en werken" (the story's [^meertens1942]),
  https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/vale001nede01_01/vale001nede01_01_0002.php :
  - "Hij werd in de Groote Kerk in hetzelfde graf begraven, waar reeds zijn vrouw, zijn jonggestorven kinderen en
    zijn schoonouders rustten" (he was buried in the Groote Kerk in the same grave where his wife, his children who
    died young and his parents-in-law already lay).
  - "In 1616 was hij voor de eerste maal tot schepen gekozen, het jaar daarop tot raad van de stad" (not used in the
    caption; available if the lead wants it).
  - The story already says he lived at Veere and died there in January 1625 ([^meertens1942]). The burial is a new
    fact on the page; it comes from that same cited source.
  - Rijksmuseum names the church "de Grote of Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk in Veere"; Veere has one great church, so
    Meertens's "Groote Kerk" is this building.

## 4.jpg: The Beacon Hymnal (Boston, 1924), No. 17

- Item: The Beacon hymnal, for church schools, young people's services, day schools, and the home (Boston, Mass.:
  Beacon Press, 1924), Florence Buck, editor. No. 17, "We gather together", "Kremser. 12, 11, 12, 11", "Old
  Netherlands Melody, 1625", "Anon.", "Sing in unison".
- Holder of the copy: Princeton Theological Seminary Library (IA "contributor" and "sponsor"), call number
  "BV520 .B422 1924"; scanned by the Internet Archive in 2022. Item: https://archive.org/details/beaconhymnalforc00unse
  ; page https://archive.org/details/beaconhymnalforc00unse/page/n147/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/beaconhymnalforc00unse/page/n147.jpg (2859 x 4014), saved as raw/beacon-n147.jpg. The
  final is the whole page, resized.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Copyright page (image n5, saved as raw/beacon-n5.jpg): "Copyright, 1924, by THE BEACON PRESS, Inc. All rights
    reserved. Printed in U.S.A." US works published in 1924 entered the public domain on 1 January 2020.
  - No separate copyright notice is printed at the foot of No. 17 (the preface says such notices appear at the foot
    of the page where they apply).
  - People involved: Florence Buck, editor, 1860 to 1925 (Dictionary of Unitarian and Universalist Biography,
    https://www.uudb.org/?p=2708 , and Wikipedia, as given in search results: died 12 October 1925); Eduard Kremser,
    arranger, died 1914 (Oesterreichisches Musiklexikon, the story's [^oeml]); Theodore Baker, English words, died
    1934 (the story's [^bakerdict]); Valerius died 1625.
  - EU and Israel, life plus 70: every author died in 1934 or before, so PD since 2005 at the latest.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (Bridgeman v. Corel; DSM Directive Art. 14).
- What it shows: printed music and words only. No figures, ornaments, logos or watermarks. Words read: Baker's three
  verses; "The wicked oppressing" names no people.
- Caption facts: the page itself, and the story: "Our arrangement sings verses 1 and 2 of Baker's English ... on the
  four parts The Beacon Hymnal prints" ([^beacon1924]).
