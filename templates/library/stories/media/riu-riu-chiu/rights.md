# Riu, Riu, Chiu: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata).

The holder's own item page (Alvin, https://www.alvin-portal.org/alvin/view.jsf?pid=alvin-record:55063) could not be
read from here: it answers scripts with an Anubis "Access Denied" page, so its rights field was not seen. Images 1 and
2 are therefore taken from Wikimedia Commons as host, where each file page names the holder's Alvin viewer as source
and carries a PD tag. Their status rests on age, with the reasoning below. A checker with a normal browser should look
at the Alvin record's licence field and note it here.

## 1.jpg: Riu riu chiu, fols. 42v to 43r of the 1556 print

- Item: *Villancicos de diversos autores, a dos, y a tres, y a quatro, y a cinco bozes* (Venice: Girolamo Scotto,
  1556), the opening fols. 42v and 43r (recto headed XLIII), "Riu riu chiu", no. 46 in Mitjana's numbering.
- Holder: Uppsala University Library, shelfmark Utl. vok. mus. tr. 611; Alvin record alvin-record:55063.
- Host file: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cancionero_de_Upsala_(scan_52).tif (5138 x 3534 TIFF).
  - Source field, exact: "https://www.alvin-portal.org/alvin/imageViewer.jsf?dsId=ATTACHMENT-0009&pid=alvin-record%3A55063&dswid=-3025"
  - Licence section, exact wikitext: "{{PD-Art|1=PD-old-100|country=Spain}}"; Commons extmetadata LicenseShortName
    "Public domain", UsageTerms "Public domain", DateTimeOriginal "1556".
  - Author field: "Attributed to Mateo Flecha el Viejo (1481 - 1553)" (the story explains why we do not follow that).
- Downloaded: Commons 3840 px rendering
  https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Cancionero_de_Upsala_%28scan_52%29.tif/lossy-page1-3840px-Cancionero_de_Upsala_%28scan_52%29.tif.jpg
  saved as raw/scan52.jpg (3840 x 2641). The final is cropped to the two pages (the 1913 binding's gilt turn-ins at the
  edges are cut away) and resized to 1600 px.
- Public domain by age:
  - Printed in Venice in 1556. No poet or composer is named in the print. The printer Girolamo Scotto died on
    3 September 1572 (Ricercar Data Lab, cited in the story). Every possible author, Flecha (died about 1553) or
    Càrceres (16th century), died more than four centuries ago.
  - US: published long before 1931, so PD.
  - EU (Italy, Spain, Sweden) and Israel: life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for anonymous works; both ended
    centuries ago.
  - A faithful photograph of a flat printed page adds no new copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v. Corel, and in the
    EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive (Sweden has implemented it) and for lack of originality.
- What it shows: printed music and words only. Decorated initials are plain letters R, with no figures. No logos or
  watermarks.
- Words on the page: the song's refrain and seven verses (Christmas and the Immaculate Conception; nothing that
  demeans anyone). The show-through is the Catalan carol "E la don don" and "Señores, el qu'es nascido", both
  Christmas songs.
- Caption facts: story, "The page" paragraph and [^uppsala-riu]: "fols. 42v to 43r", "tiple ... and alto on the
  left page, tenor and bass on the right", "six more verses stand in italic blocks between the staves"; intro: "The
  only known copy of that book is in Uppsala University Library". Checked against the image: the tenor staff is top
  right, the bass staff with "SOLO LAS COBLAS" bottom right.

## 2.jpg: the Uppsala copy's front flyleaf (detail)

- Item: the same copy, front flyleaf, with the binder's stamp "Gust. Hedberg, K. Hofbokbindare, Stockholm" and the
  pencil note "Bunden på bekostnad av Ambassadrådet R. Mitjana, St. Petersburg. 1913".
- Holder: Uppsala University Library, Utl. vok. mus. tr. 611; alvin-record:55063.
- Host file: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cancionero_de_Upsala_(scan_6).tif (5112 x 3572 TIFF).
  - Source field, exact: the same Alvin imageViewer URL as above.
  - Licence section, exact wikitext: "{{PD-Art|1=PD-old-100|country=Spain}}".
- Downloaded: Commons 3840 px rendering
  https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Cancionero_de_Upsala_%28scan_6%29.tif/lossy-page1-3840px-Cancionero_de_Upsala_%28scan_6%29.tif.jpg
  saved as raw/scan6.jpg (3840 x 2683). The final is a crop of the plain paper only (sips -c 480 1520 --cropOffset 140 162 on
  the 3840 px image), at native size, leaving out the binding's gilt border.
- Why it is free to use:
  - The Commons PD-Art/PD-old-100 tag is written for the 1556 book; this page is a 1913 addition, so it is reasoned
    separately here.
  - The stamp is a trade name and address ("GUST. HEDBERG / K. HOFBOKBINDARE / STOCKHOLM") and the note one factual
    line about who paid for the binding. Neither has the originality copyright needs, in the US (Feist), the EU or
    Israel.
  - Even if the note were a work, it was written in 1913 by an unnamed hand (presumably library staff). The EU term for
    an anonymous work, 70 years from creation if not made public, ran out by 1984. In Israel the term is 70 years and
    has also run out. In the US, an unpublished anonymous work lasts 120 years from creation (to 2033), which is why
    this rests on the lack of originality, not on age.
  - The gilt-tooled border (a 1913 binding, a three-dimensional object) is cropped out, so only the flat paper is
    shown, as photographed by the library.
- What it shows: a stamp and handwriting. No figures, no logos beyond the binder's name, no watermark.
- Caption facts: story, "Did you know?" first item and [^uppsala-flyleaf] (the stamp and note, transcribed and
  translated). "Four years after his book named it": story timeline, "1909: Rafael Mitjana prints the book's words at
  Uppsala and names it the Cancionero de Uppsala". Read against the image: "Bunden på bekostnad av / Ambassadrådet R.
  Mitjana, St. Petersburg. 1913".

## 3.jpg: Mitjana, Cincuenta y cuatro canciones españolas del siglo XVI (1909), p. 33

- Item: Rafael Mitjana, *Cincuenta y cuatro canciones españolas del siglo XVI: Cancionero de Uppsala* (Uppsala:
  Akademiska Bokförlaget, 1909), p. 33: the end of no. XLV ("E la don don") and the start of no. XLVI, "Riu, riu,
  chiu, / La guarda ribera, / Dios guarde el lobo / De nuestra cordera."
- Copy: University of Toronto, Robarts Library (IA "contributor: Robarts - University of Toronto"; call number
  AEU-5145). Item: https://archive.org/details/cincuentaycuatro00mitjuoft ; page (printed p. 33 is leaf n36 per the
  item's page_numbers.json): https://archive.org/details/cincuentaycuatro00mitjuoft/page/n36/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/cincuentaycuatro00mitjuoft/page/n36.jpg (2132 x 3328), saved as raw/mit_n36.jpg. The
  final is the whole page, resized to 1600 px high.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence ("possible-copyright-status" and "rights" empty), so this rests on PD by
  age:
  - Published in Uppsala in 1909 (IA metadata: date 1909, publisher "Uppsala, Akad. Bokförlaget"; dedication dated 30
    January 1909 per the story).
  - Rafael Mitjana, 1869 to 1921 (IA creator field "Mitjana, Rafael, 1869-1921"; Baker's, cited in the story). The
    texts he printed are anonymous 16th-century songs.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70; Mitjana died in 1921, so the term ended at the close of 1991.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (as above).
- What it shows: printed text in a ruled frame. No figures or ornaments with people, no logos or watermarks.
- Words on the page: the Catalan Christmas carol "E la don don" (shepherds, the Virgin, Bethlehem) and the Riu riu
  chiu refrain. Nothing that demeans anyone.
- Caption facts: story, "Mitjana's text": "every refrain in his book reads 'Dios guarde el lobo' ... The print ... has
  'guardo'"; "The finder": "His book of 1909 printed the words alone, Riu, riu, chiu as no. 46". Read against the
  image: heading "XLVI.", line 3 "Dios guarde el lobo".

## Rejected

- The 1556 title page (Commons File:Cancionero_de_Upsala_(scan_8).tif, cited in the story as [^uppsala-title]): PD,
  but Scotto's printer's device is a woodcut of a winged figure blowing a trumpet (Commons category "Tuba angels").
  A drawn human figure, so not used. The story's "See the original" link covers the book.
- Carolina Rediviva, Uppsala University Library's main building, photographed before 1915, attributed to Henri Osti
  (1826 to 1914) (Commons File:Carolina_Redivera_pre_1915.jpg, tagged {{PD-old}}, source Upplandsmuseet's Flickr
  https://www.flickr.com/photos/uppmus/5464656123/ ): a fine place image, but the holder's page now returns 404 and
  has no Wayback copy, so the Osti attribution and the museum's own statement could not be seen. Its sibling OC01
  (File:OC01_by_Upplandsmuseet.jpg, 1860s) shows the museum published these on Flickr as "CC BY 2.0", a licence the
  brief no longer accepts, and the US status of an Osti print turns on an unknown publication history. Not clearly
  OK, so dropped.
- Modern Carolina Rediviva photos on Commons: the free ones are "PD-self" releases (File:Carolina_Redeviva1.jpg,
  File:Carolina_Rediviva.jpg, both 2007), which is not one of the five allowed licence values; the others are CC BY-SA
  (File:Carolina_Rediviva_1.jpg, File:Uppsala_Carolina_Revidiva.jpg) or CC BY 2.5 (KMB 16000300028698). Dropped.
- The KMB photos by Fredrik Bruno (1943, 1947) are PD in Sweden as plain photographs, but not clearly PD in the US.
  Dropped.
- A photograph of Mitjana (died 1921): not searched for; any such photo would need its photographer's death year.
