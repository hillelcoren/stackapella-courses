- Item: Internet Archive SongStoriesForTheKindergarten, image ss001.gif (1823 x 2654),
  https://archive.org/download/SongStoriesForTheKindergarten/ss001.gif ; metadata saved as raw/ss_meta.json.
  Community upload of 2007, date "1896", creator "Mildred J. Hill", collection opensource_image, licenseurl
  "http://creativecommons.org/licenses/publicdomain/". No holding library is named. This is the story's [^hill1896]
  scan and its "original" link.
- Words on the page: "Song Stories", "New Edition, Revised, Illustrated and Enlarged", "For the Kindergarten.",
  "Music Composed and Arranged by Mildred J. Hill.", "Words Written and Adapted by Patty S. Hill.", "Illustrations by
  Margaret Byers", "With an Introduction by Anna E. Bryan.", "Boards, $1.00. Cloth, 1.50.", "Chicago Clayton F. Summy
  Co., 64 E. Van Buren St.", "Copyright, 1896, by Clayton F. Summy." Nothing demeaning.
- What it shows: lettering, rules and one small star or sunburst printer's ornament. No figures, no drawn people (the
  Byers illustrations are elsewhere in the book, not on this page), no logo or watermark.
- Public domain by age:
  - Published Chicago, copyright 1896. US: published before 1931, public domain.
  - EU and Israel, life plus 70: Mildred J. Hill died 1916, Patty S. Hill died 1946 (term ended 31 December 2016),
    Anna E. Bryan died 1901 (Filson item record: "Anna Bryan (1858-1901)"). Clayton F. Summy's typography of 1896 is
    long out of any term. Margaret Byers is named only in type; no art of hers is on the page. In any case a US work of
    1896 is also covered by the rule of the shorter term (see 3.jpg).
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright: US (Bridgeman v. Corel), EU (Art. 14 DSM Directive 2019).
- Caption facts: the title page itself and the story's paragraph "The book" ([^hill1896]).

## 3.jpg: The Elementary Worker and His Work (1911), p. 63

- Item: Internet Archive elementaryworker00jaco, leaf 69 (printed page 63 per the IA page-number file),
  https://archive.org/download/elementaryworker00jaco/page/n68.jpg (2524 x 4211), saved as raw/ew1911_n68.jpg;
  metadata raw/ew_meta.json; page numbers raw/ew_pages.json. Contributor and sponsor "The Library of Congress",
  scanned 2011 at the LC (scanning centre capitolhill), LCCN 12001134. The scan's first leaves carry the LC stamp
  "Copyright deposit" and the registration "©Cl.A303752". This is the story's [^ew1911] scan.
- Rights statement, IA record field possible-copyright-status (exact words): "The Library of Congress is unaware of
  any copyright restrictions for this item." (https://archive.org/details/elementaryworker00jaco). This is not the LoC
  "No known restrictions on publication" statement, so the licence rests on age.
- Title page (from the IA text): "By Alice Jacobs and Ermina C. Lincoln", "Authorized and issued by The Board of
  Sunday Schools of the Methodist Episcopal Church, Fourteen West Washington Street, Chicago", "Printed for the Board
  by Jennings and Graham", "Copyright, 1911, by The Board of Sunday Schools of The Methodist Episcopal Church".
- US: published 1911 with notice; public domain (published before 1931).
- EU and Israel. **Flag for the checker:** neither author's death year was found (LC catalogue: "Lincoln, Ermina
  Chester, 1881-"; Alice Jacobs, no dates; web, LC name authority and Wikidata searches found nothing). Life plus 70
  cannot be shown directly, because Lincoln, born 1881, could have died after 1955. The page rests instead on the rule
  of the shorter term:
  - EU, Term Directive 2006/116/EC, Art. 7(1): where the country of origin is a third country and the author is not a
    Community national, the term "shall expire on the date of expiry of the protection granted in the country of
    origin of the work". Country of origin: the US (first and only publication, Chicago). The authors were Methodist
    Sunday school workers publishing through the Chicago board; their nationality is not stated anywhere seen, but
    nothing suggests either was a European national.
  - Israel, Copyright Act 2007, s. 44(a) (English translation, saved as research/hb-law/il2007.pdf, p. 13): "The period
    of copyright in a work listed below shall not be longer than the period of copyright prescribed for such work in
    the law of its country of origin: ... (2) A work in which copyright subsists pursuant to an Order in accordance
    with section 9"; s. 44(b)(1): the country of origin of "a work first published in a single Member State" is that
    State. A US work is protected in Israel through the s. 9 Order, so its Israeli term ends with its US term.
  - If the checker wants life plus 70 shown, drop this image (the page then has two).
- Words on the page, all read: the welcome verse ("A welcome to you ... We're glad to see you.") twice, "(Sung to same
  tune as the "Good Morning.")", the Nellie and John passages, the birthday verse, "(Sung to the same tune as the
  "Good Morning.")", the wish "Many happy returns of the day of thy birth, May sunshine and gladness be given; And may
  the dear Father prepare thee on earth For a beautiful birthday in heaven.", and the cradle-roll note. Gentle
  Sunday-school text, nothing demeaning. Page furniture: running head, page number, signature mark "7".
- What it shows: type only. No figures, no ornaments, no stamps on this leaf.
- Caption facts: story paragraph "The birthday words" ([^ew1911], [^order2015]): earliest known printing of the full
  words, Methodist handbook copyright 1911, names no author; the page's own words "Sung to the same tune as the
  "Good Morning."".

- **3.jpg DROPPED** (now dropped/3-elementary-worker-1911-p63.jpg; old media.json, placement.md and links.md beside it).
  - IA record re-read (https://archive.org/metadata/elementaryworker00jaco): creators "Jacobs, Alice" and "Lincoln,
    Ermina Chester, 1881- [from old catalog] joint author"; possible-copyright-status "The Library of Congress is
    unaware of any copyright restrictions for this item." No death year for either author; the checker's own web
    searches found none either.
  - US: public domain (published 1911). Israel: s. 44 of the 2007 Act limits a US work to its US term, so public
    domain.
  - EU: the rule of the shorter term (Term Directive 2006/116/EC, Art. 7(1)) does not hold in every member state.
    Art. 7(3) lets a state keep a longer term it owed under an older treaty, and Germany still applies its 1892
    copyright agreement with the United States (RGBl. 1892 S. 473) to US works. German courts and commentators hold
    that this agreement still binds (the Steamboat Willie discussion, NJW-Forum 2024, beck-aktuell; BGH I ZR 49/13,
    "Tarzan", 2014, on the comparison of terms for US works). Under it a US work can be protected in Germany for the
    author's life plus 70 years. Lincoln was born in 1881 and may have died after 1955, so the page may still be
    protected there.
  - The song page itself is shown in Germany: its makers (Mildred J. Hill, died 1916; Patty Smith Hill and Robert H.
    Coleman, both died 1946) are out of life plus 70. So the image cannot ride with a withheld page.
  - The lead's rule is "unsure = drop". The page stays as the "original" link (a link to the library's scan, not a
    copy), and the story keeps citing it.
  - Minor, for the record: the IA record's publisher line is "[Cincinnati] Printed by Jennings and Graham"; the dropped
    caption said "(Chicago, 1911)", after the issuing board's Chicago address.
- 1.jpg and 2.jpg: IA record re-read (https://archive.org/metadata/SongStoriesForTheKindergarten): date 1896, creator
  "Mildred J. Hill", licenseurl "http://creativecommons.org/licenses/publicdomain/", community upload (2007) with no
  holding library. The case rests on age, as above. Makers check: the song file lists Patty Smith Hill (died 1946) as
  a maker, so wherever a longer term (life plus 100) still runs for her, the song page is withheld and these pages go
  with it. Kept.
