# O Come, All Ye Faithful: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-12. raw/ was empty at the start; everything here was found and downloaded in this run. Finals made
with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata).

## 1.jpg: An Essay on the Church Plain Chant, part second (London, 1782), p. xiii

- Item: "An essay on the church plain chant part second, containing several anthems, litanies, ... as they are sung
  in the public chapels at London. With approbation. 1782". IA identifier
  bim_eighteenth-century_an-essay-on-the-church-p_essay-on-the-church-plai_1782_0, "Digitized from IA40313605-02"
  (microfilm), collection-catalog-number t065389 (ESTC T065389), notes "Horizontal chain lines". The copy filmed is
  not named. Parts first and third are the sister items ..._1782 and ..._1782_1.
  Page: https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_an-essay-on-the-church-p_essay-on-the-church-plai_1782_0/page/n14/mode/1up
  File .../page/n14.jpg (5488 x 8241), saved as raw/essay1782_part2_n14.jpg; metadata raw/essay1782_part2_meta.json.
  Found by its OCR ("Another Prose on the Nativity of our Lord. Sixth Tone. G. major." ... "A-deste fi-deles laeti
  triumphantes ve-nite ve-nite in Beth-le-hem") and the hOCR page index (leaf 14), then looked at. The final crops
  away the black microfilm border and a strip of the facing page.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so PD by age:
  - Printed London 1782. The Essay is anonymous; some name Samuel Webbe (died 1816) as its editor. The tune and words
    are in Wade's copies (Wade died 1786).
  - US: published before 1931, PD. EU and Israel: an anonymous work, 70 years from publication, ended in 1852; on
    Webbe's life, plus 70 ended in 1886.
  - A microfilm and its scan are faithful copies of a flat page and add no new copyright (US Bridgeman v. Corel; EU
    Art. 14 DSM Directive and no originality).
- What it shows: square-note plainchant and Latin text. No figures, ornaments with people, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: story paragraph "The tune": "The even, four-beat tune sung today is first found in print in 1782, in
  An Essay on the Church Plain Chant, published in London by J. P. Coghlan" ([^zon][^phh][^hymnarchive]). The page
  itself shows the barred, even setting. The caption says "earliest known printing", per the story guide.

## 2.jpg: Hymns of Worship and Service (New York, 1909), No. 89, p. 63

- Item: Hymns of worship and service (New York: Century Co., 1909). IA identifier hymnsofworship00newy, contributor
  Princeton Theological Seminary Library, call_number 6157, scanned 2009, "possible-copyright-status":
  "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". This is the story's cited copy, [^hws1909] ("20th ed. ... No. 89, 'Adeste Fideles', p. 63,
  Princeton Theological Seminary Library copy").
  Page: https://archive.org/details/hymnsofworship00newy/page/n102/mode/1up (page_numbers.json: leaf 102 is printed
  page 63; checked by eye). File .../page/n102.jpg (2132 x 3195), saved as raw/hws1909_n102_p63.jpg; metadata
  raw/hws1909_meta.json. The final is the whole page, resized.
- Rights: PD by age, with IA's own "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT":
  - Published New York 1909. US: before 1931, PD.
  - EU and Israel: the page credits only "Anon. 1751 (?)" for the tune and "Anon. (Latin, 17th Cent.) Tr. F.
    Oakeley, 1841" for the words. Oakeley died 30 January 1880 (Hymnary.org, Julian), so life plus 70 ended in 1950.
    The four-part harmony is uncredited, so it is anonymous: 70 years from publication ended in 1979.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page (as above).
- What it shows: printed music and words, a blackletter heading "Nativity". No figures, no logos, no watermarks.
- Caption facts: story, "The full words": "as Hymns of Worship and Service (New York, 1909) prints them at No. 89, the
  page our arrangement is read from; it spells 'Oh' where we sing 'O', and sings the refrain after each verse". The
  page prints "After each verse" over the refrain. The caption does not repeat the page's own attributions ("Anon.
  1751 (?)", "Latin, 17th Cent."), which the story does not support.

## Rejected

- Commons "File:Adeste, fideles (earliest version).jpg" (Wade manuscript, about 1745): marked {{PD-old}}, but its
  source is a private website (hymnsandcarolsofchristmas.com) and no holder is named. If it is the Jacobite MS, whose
  location Hymnology Archive gives as unknown, it is probably taken from Stephan's 1947 reproduction. Source unclear:
  dropped.
- Wade's surviving manuscripts (University of Glasgow MS Euing, 1750; Durham University Vesperale Novum, 1750;
  Stonyhurst College Cantus Diversi, 1751; St Edmund's College, Ware): no online scan with a free licence was found;
  Hymnology Archive's figures are reproductions from Stephan (1947) or the holders, with no licence.
- Commons "File:Frederick Oakeley.jpg": source is discogs.com, author unknown, not clearly a photograph. Dropped.
  Balliol College's painted portrait of Oakeley (via Art UK) is a painted figure: not allowed.
- All Saints, Margaret Street (built on the site of Oakeley's Margaret Chapel): the only non-SA Commons files are a
  CC BY 2.0 photo (no longer accepted) and an 1873 engraving from Old and New London (engraved, may hold figures, and
  shows the later church). Not used.
- The Evening Office of the Church (1760), the words' earliest printing: no scan of the 1760 edition on IA (only 1719,
  1737, 1738 and 1748 editions). Murray's Hymnal (1852): not found on IA in this run; not looked for elsewhere.
