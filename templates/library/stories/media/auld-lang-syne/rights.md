## 1.jpg: Burns's cottage at Alloway, George Washington Wilson, 1870 to 1890

- Item: "Geboortehuis van dichter Robert Burns" (Birthplace of the poet Robert Burns), Rijksmuseum RP-F-AA3402-FU.
  Albumen print ("albuminedruk") from an album ("Deze foto maakt deel uit van een album"), 116 x 191 mm. Printed
  inscription on the recto, as the museum records it: "Burns' Cottage. Alloway. Ayr. 90. G.W.W." Handwritten album
  caption: "Burns' Cottage at Alloway". Photographer: "George Washington Wilson (mentioned on object)". Date "1870 -
  1890". Place: Alloway. Subject: Robert Burns.
- Holder: Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam. Item page https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/RP-F-AA3402-FU (redirects to the
  object page); persistent id https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200777166 ; Dublin Core record
  https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/200777166?_profile=dc
- Rights statement: the record's "rights" field is https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ (Public Domain
  Mark), in both the Dublin Core and the Linked Art records.
- Downloaded file: https://iiif.micr.io/GsMjd/full/max/0/default.jpg (the record's image relation), 2844 x 1922,
  saved as raw/rijks-RP-F-AA3402-FU.jpg. The final crops away the album mount and its handwritten caption, keeping the
  whole photograph.
- Public domain by age:
  - George Washington Wilson, Aberdeen photographer, 1823 to 1893 (died 9 April 1893). Made 1870 to 1890.
  - UK (country of origin) and EU: life plus 70, so it ended at the end of 1963.
  - US: published (as a sold view, numbered and signed G.W.W.) before 1931, so PD; even if unpublished, life plus 70
    ended 1963.
  - Israel: life plus 70 (and photographs under the old law, 50 years from the negative): long expired.
- What it shows: the thatched cottage, an empty road and a wheelbarrow. No people, no drawn figures, no watermark.
  Two small signboards by the door: the left reads "BURNS COTTAGE" with lines too small to read; the right reads
  "A. HUNTER(?) / WINES / SPIRITS & ALES", a publican's board. Nothing mocking or demeaning; no trademark front and
  centre (the board is small and unreadable at page size).
- Caption facts:
  - Birthplace at Alloway: the Rijksmuseum's own title ("Geboortehuis van dichter Robert Burns") and the print's
    inscription ("Burns' Cottage. Alloway. Ayr."); Charles S. Dougall, The Burns Country (London: A. and C. Black,
    1904), pp. 45 to 46, chapter "Alloway and Mount Oliphant": in the cottage's kitchen "stands the bed in which the
    poet was born" (https://archive.org/details/burnscountrybych00dougrich , text saved as raw/dougall1904-djvu.txt).
  - Born 1759: the story's front matter and [^rpo] ("Birth Date January 25, 1759").
  - Photographer and date range: the Rijksmuseum record.
  - Background, not in the caption: Dougall p. 45 says the cottage was a public-house for nearly a century after 1781
    until the Burns Monument trustees bought it in 1881, which fits the "Wines, Spirits & Ales" board.

## 2.jpg: The Scots Musical Museum, vol. 5, p. 426, No. 413 (1796 engraving, 1839 printing)

- Item: "The Scotish musical museum : consisting of upwards of six hundred songs, with proper basses for the
  pianoforte", vol. 5 (Edinburgh: William Blackwood and Sons; London: Thomas Cadell, M.DCCC.XXXIX), "originally
  published by James Johnson", with Stenhouse's notes. Contributor University of Western Ontario, call number MZ2083;
  sponsor University of Western Ontario - University of Toronto Libraries. Item:
  https://archive.org/details/scotishmusicalmu05john ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/scotishmusicalmu05john/page/n24/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/scotishmusicalmu05john/page/n24.jpg (2571 x 4222), saved as raw/uwo1839-n24.jpg. The
  final is the whole page, resized.
- Rights: the IA record has no rights, licenseurl or copyright field, so this rests on PD by age:
  - The engraved page was first issued in vol. 5 of Johnson's Museum, dated 1796 by the National Library of Scotland
    (the story's [^smm5]); this printing is 1839.
  - Creators per the IA records: James Johnson (about 1750 to 1811), Stephen Clarke (died 1797), Robert Burns (1759 to
    1796). The 1839 edition's added notes are by William Stenhouse (died 1827) and David Laing (died 1878), with
    additions by Charles Kirkpatrick Sharpe (died 1851); none of their notes is on this page.
  - US: published before 1931, PD. EU, UK and Israel: life plus 70, every author dead by 1878, PD.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM
    Directive; UK: the IPO's 2015 guidance and THJ v Sheridan on originality).
- Same engraving as 1796: I compared it by eye with the NLS 1796 copy (scotsmusicalmuv300john, image n22, the story's
  own scan; viewed for comparison only, not kept): same number 413, same layout of the four music systems and two
  verse columns, same asterisk footnote and Z. The 1839 volume also reprints the engraved vol. 5 preface in the old
  long-s type, mourning "the late celebrated Scottish Bard". The only difference seen is a page number "426" at the
  top right, which the NLS scan does not show. The caption says only "here in the volume's 1839 Edinburgh reissue"
  and makes no claim about the plates.
- Words on the page, all read: the title, the music text of verse 1 and chorus, the four further verses, "For auld,
  &c.", the footnote "* Some Sing, Kiss, in place of Cup." and "Z". Nothing mocking. No figures, ornaments, stamps or
  watermarks.
- Caption facts (story sources): No. 413, p. 426, signed Z, "to an older tune", earliest known printing 1796:
  [^smm5], [^stenhouse], [^dick] as cited in the story's "The Museum, 1796" paragraph and its front matter. Slower:
  story FAQ "an older, slower tune" [^smm5][^mccue].

## 3.jpg: Thomson, Select Collection of Original Scottish Airs, vol. 2, No. 68 (1799 volume, 1801 issue)

- Item: "A select collection of original Scottish airs for the voice : with introductory and concluding symphonies &
  accompaniments for the piano forte, violin & violoncello ..." (Edinburgh: G. Thomson), IA date 1801. Creators per
  IA: Pleyel, Ignaz, 1757-1831; Burns, Robert, 1759-1796; Kozeluch, Leopold, 1747-1818. Contributor University of
  Western Ontario, call number MZ1703. Item: https://archive.org/details/selectcollection00pley ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/selectcollection00pley/page/n176/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/selectcollection00pley/page/n176.jpg (3068 x 4532), saved as
  raw/thomson-1801-n176.jpg. The final is the whole page, resized. This is the copy the story cites ([^thomson],
  images n175 and n176).
- Rights: the IA record has no rights, licenseurl or copyright field, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Vol. 2 published Edinburgh 1799; the page's own note "The Sym. & Accompt. simplified as above by Mr. K. 1801"
    dates this issue to 1801.
  - George Thomson (1757 to 1851), Leopold Kozeluch (died 1818), Ignaz Pleyel (died 1831), Burns (died 1796).
  - US: published before 1931, PD. EU, UK and Israel: life plus 70, every author dead by 1851, PD.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: as for 2.jpg.
- Words on the page, all read: "Should auld acquaintance be forgot." with "68", "Allegretto", "Chorus", the words of
  verse 1 and the chorus (twice in the chorus, for the two voices), and the footnote above. Nothing mocking. No
  figures, ornaments, stamps or watermarks. (The facing text page, n175, has one word struck out in ink by a former
  owner; that page is not used.)
- Caption facts (story sources): No. 68 in vol. 2, 1799, the tune sung today, "lively", Allegretto, this copy issued
  1801: [^thomson], [^dick], [^mccue], the story's "The tune we sing" paragraph.

## Rejected

- National Library of Scotland scans on the Internet Archive (the story's own [^smm5] copy scotsmusicalmuv300john,
  and its vols. 1 to 4 and the 1839 set): each record says "National Library of Scotland holds full rights in this
  digital resource and agrees to license the resource under the Creative Commons License: Attribution-Noncommercial-
  Share Alike 2.5 UK: Scotland" (licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/2.5/scotland/). NC and SA:
  not usable, as for God Rest Ye's Chappell. Nothing from them is in the set.
- Ellisland Farm, where Burns wrote the 1788 letter:
  - Commons File:Ellisland_Farm,_Dumfries,_c_1900.jpg: a user scan from Dougall's The Burns Country (1904), whose
    photographs are "from photographs by Thomas Ferguson" of Kilmarnock. I could not find Ferguson's death year, so
    life plus 70 cannot be shown for the EU, UK or Israel. Dropped.
  - Commons File:Ellisland_Farm,_Dumfries,_Scotland_in_1800.jpg: an engraving from a user's scan, tagged with a
    "might not be in the public domain outside of the United States" warning, holder not named. Dropped.
  - Every modern Ellisland photo on Commons (geograph.org.uk and others) is CC BY-SA. Dropped.
  - Rijksmuseum has no Ellisland item (searched titles "Ellisland", "Nith", "Dumfries").
- Library of Congress (loc.gov, including the Photochrom views and the Manuscript Division's photocopy of an Auld Lang
  Syne manuscript the story's [^loc] blog mentions): loc.gov returns a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to plain
  requests here, so nothing there could be checked.
- Burns's manuscripts: the Morgan Library (MA 47.27, the 1793 letter to Thomson) and the National Trust for Scotland
  (archive-110004712, archive-110005411) turned up in a search with no free licence shown in the results; I did not
  open their pages, so nothing there is claimed either way. Not used.
- Burns portraits (all paintings or engravings) and the Rijksmuseum's Burns statue and monument photographs: drawn
  or sculpted figures, and off the story. Not considered.
