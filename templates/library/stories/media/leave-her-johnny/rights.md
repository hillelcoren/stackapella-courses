- Rights statement on the image in the record: "_label": "Public Domain", content
  "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Photographer anonymous; made 1860 to 1890; the printed number and title show a commercial stereo series, so it was
    published then.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain. (Even if treated as unpublished, an anonymous work's term is 120
    years from creation, ended by 2010.)
  - EU: an anonymous work's term is 70 years from being made available (ended by 1960).
  - Israel: the same (anonymous works, 70 years from publication).
  - The holder itself marks it Public Domain Mark 1.0.
- Crop: the left half of the stereo pair only, without the slide's mount, the handwritten "7", the paper label or the
  printed title strip.
- Looked at closely: the signs read "GEORGE'S DOCK." and "TO THE GEORGES LANDING STAGE"; a small bill on the shed and

  street are small and not identifiable. No drawn figures, nothing demeaning, no trademark.

## 2.jpg: Whall, Ships, Sea Songs and Shanties (1913), p. 68, "Across the Western Ocean" (detail)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/shipsseasongssha00whal (scan image n87, printed page 68).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/shipsseasongssha00whal/page/n87.jpg (2444 x 3404).
- IA record: title "Ships, sea songs and shanties"; creator "Whall, W. B" and "Whall, R. H"; publisher "Glasgow, J.
  Brown & son"; date 1913; contributor and sponsor "Smithsonian Libraries"; call_number "39088006794259"; no rights
  or licence field.
- Title page (OCR, pages/whall_djvu.txt): "The Songs harmonised by R. H. WHALL, Mus.Bac., F.R.C.O."; "THIRD EDITION.
  ENLARGED"; "1913"; preface dated "November, 1910".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published Glasgow 1913. Author William Boultbee Whall, born December 1846, died 1917 (Wikipedia, "W. B. Whall").
  - The crop holds only W. B. Whall's prose: no music, so nothing of R. H. Whall's harmonising (and the shanty tunes
    in this book are printed as bare melodies anyway).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 (1917 + 70) ended 31 December 1987. The UK typographical-arrangement right (25 years)
    ended in 1938.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Crop and words read: the heading and Whall's first two paragraphs, ending "But first the original words may be
  given." Every word was read; nothing in it demeans anyone (it names "thousands of Irish emigrants" as passengers,
  plainly).
- Left out on purpose: the tune and verses below, which print the dialect "whar'" ("Amelia, whar' you bound to") and
  "Liverpool Pat"; and p. 69 (scan n88), whose top verse repeats "whar'" and which adds that Amelia "was probably
  O'Melia originally, a typical Irish name". p. 69 also has an engraved ship ("The 'Virginian,' 20 guns, a privateer
  of 1778"), not used.
- Page numbers: the scan shows "Across the Western Ocean" beginning on printed p. 68 and "Leave her, bullies, leave
  her" on p. 69. The story's source line cites "pp. 67 and 68"; the lead may want to check that citation.

  Orpheus Music Pub. Co."; date 1914; contributor "University of Toronto, Faculty of Music Library"; call number

- Library MARC record (pages/bullen_marc.xml): 245 "Songs of sea labour, chanties, by Frank T. Bullen and W.F.
  Arnold, with an appreciation by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle."; 260 "London, Orpheus Music Pub. Co., c1914."
- W. F. Arnold: IMSLP, Category:Wilcock, Roland (https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Wilcock,_Roland), gives "(1888 -
  9 November 1916)", lists "W.F. Arnold" as an alternative name, "Son of Harry Wilcock of the Orpheus Music Publishing
  Company" (the book's publisher), "died from wounds received in combat in France", and lists Songs of Sea Labour among
  his collaborations; its source is "Musical Opinion and Music Trade Review, Dec., 1916". I did not read the 1916
  article itself. Checker: if this identification is not accepted, drop 3.jpg (the set stands without it).
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London 1914. Frank T. Bullen died 1915; W. F. Arnold (Roland Wilcock), the co-author, died 1916.
    Tune and words traditional.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 ended 31 December 1985 (Bullen) and 1986 (Arnold).
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
  - Conan Doyle's appreciation (he died 1930) is not on this page.
- Crop and words read: No. 9 only. Every word: "9. Leave Her Johnny.", "Slow, and with expression.", "Chantyman.",
  "Chorus.", "Chanty-man.", "Leave her Johnny and we'll work no-o more Leave her Johnny, leave her! Of pump or drown
  we've had full store; Its time for us to leave her." Nothing demeaning; no figures or decoration.
- Left out: No. 8, "Storm-Along", above it on the same page (words "Stormy he was a good old man ... Mister Storm
  along", nothing objectionable), and the page number "8".

## Considered and not used

- Rijksmuseum RP-F-F01212-20-2, "Waterloo graanpakhuizen in Liverpool, op de voorgrond schepen" (1878 to 1890), Public
  Domain Mark, raw file in dropped/: the record says "anonymous", but the print itself carries "FRITH'S SERIES", and the
  picture is mostly a warehouse with small craft; George's Dock shows the sailing ships far better.
- Wikimedia Commons photographs of Watchet harbour: all modern Geograph uploads (CC BY-SA), not allowed; one older
  Watchet item is a painting.
- Whall p. 69 (scan n88): the "Leave her, bullies" verses are clean, but the top of the page has dialect and an
  engraved ship; the p. 68 prose tells the story's point better.
