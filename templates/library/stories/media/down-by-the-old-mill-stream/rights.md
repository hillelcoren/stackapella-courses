# Down by the Old Mill Stream: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. Makers in the song file (templates/library/down-by-the-old-mill-stream.json): Tell Taylor, words
and music, died 1937; Earl K. Smith, words and music (claimed co-author; the 1945 court found the claim unproven),
died 1954 (song file; secondary sources, unconfirmed). Life + 70: Taylor's term ended 31 December 2007, Smith's
31 December 2024. Every printed page below is public domain even counting Smith.

## 1.jpg: 1910 copyright catalogue entry

- Library of Congress, Copyright Office, Catalogue of Copyright Entries, Part 3, Musical Compositions, new series,
  vol. 5, nos. 35 to 39 (September 1910), p. 1133, entry 19709 (the story's [^cce1910]).
- Scan: Internet Archive item catalogofcopyrig523libr, image n240,
  https://archive.org/download/catalogofcopyrig523libr/page/n240.jpg (2516 x 4288), saved as raw/cce1910-n240.jpg.
- Crop: entry 19709 only, from the right column (1150 x 380 px, not upscaled). Words in the crop, all read: "Down by
  the old mill stream; words and music by Tell Taylor. Chicago, Star music pub. [19709 © Aug. 12, 1910; 2 c. Aug. 15,
  1910; E 238432; Tell Taylor, Chicago, Ill." Neighbouring entries (one of them with a dialect title) are left out.
- Rights: a work of the US government (Copyright Office, Library of Congress), published 1910: public domain in the US
  (17 U.S.C. 105) and, published before 1931, by age too. EU and Israel: an unsigned official catalogue by a public
  body, 70 years from publication, ended 1980. A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright. The IA item's
  page carries no rights claim.

## 2.jpg and 4.jpg: pages 2 and 4 of the copy we read

- Tell Taylor, Down by the Old Mill Stream (Chicago: Tell Taylor, Music Publisher, Grand Opera House; "Copyright,
  1910, by Tell Taylor"; printed 1912 or later, per the story's dating from the back-page advert, [^cce1912]).
  Printed by H. S. Talbot & Co., Chicago (p. 5 footer).
- Holder: Connecticut College, Greer Music Library, Historic Sheet Music Collection 1357 (MacIntosh Collection),
  https://digitalcommons.conncoll.edu/sheetmusic/1357 (saved as raw/conncoll-1357.html). The record gives no rights
  statement; its note reads "Some of these resources may contain offensive language or negative stereotypes."
  (a collection-wide note).
- Scans: the Internet Archive mirror of the college's scan, item sm_downbytheoldmillstream (metadata "Source:
  digitalcommons.conncoll.edu/sheetmusic/1357", contributor "Connecticut College"; saved as raw/ia-meta.json, no
  licence field), images https://archive.org/download/sm_downbytheoldmillstream/page/n2.jpg and .../page/n4.jpg
  (3092 x 4018 each). Same handling as danny-boy (Conn College record, IA mirror).
- Public domain by age: published 1910 in the US, so public domain there. Pages 2 and 4 name only Tell Taylor (died
  1937); even counting Earl K. Smith (died 1954) life + 70 ended 31 December 2024, so public domain in the EU and
  Israel since 1 January 2025. The printer and engraver add no authorship. Faithful scans of flat pages.
- Words checked: p. 2 (title, "Andante espressivo.", "Tell Taylor.", "Slow.", both verses' first halves, "British
  Copyright Secured.", "Copyright, 1910, by Tell Taylor."); p. 4 (end of the chorus, "very slow", "With the voice",
  "rit.", "D.C.", footer "Down by the old mill stream 4"). No figures, no ornament, nothing demeaning.
- Not used from this copy:
  - The cover (image n1): a drawing of a couple by the mill, and a photograph of three performers ("Fox, Peck &
    Franks") in which the middle figure appears to be in blackface (the story says so). Never used, not even cropped.
  - Page 5, the male quartet chorus (image n5): it is "Arr. by Chas. Miller", whose death year is unknown (IMSLP:
    "Charles Miller (fl. 1893-1919)"), so its EU and Israel term cannot be shown; dropped. Page 4 (Taylor's own
    chorus) is used instead.
  - The back page (image n6): Taylor's advert carries a drawn woman and a small photograph of a singer, and lists
    "Way Down South" and rag titles; not used.
