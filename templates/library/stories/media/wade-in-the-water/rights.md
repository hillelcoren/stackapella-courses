# Wade in the Water: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13.

## The 1904 book (1.jpg, 2.jpg, 4.jpg)

All three are crops from one scan: Frederick J. Work, collector and harmonizer, *New Jubilee Songs as Sung by the
Fisk Jubilee Singers of Fisk University*, second edition (Nashville: Fisk University, "Copyrighted, June, 1902, and
August, 1904"), the copy in the Adam Knight Spence and John Wesley Work collection, Box 1 Folder 1, Archives Division,
Auburn Avenue Research Library on African-American Culture and History, Atlanta-Fulton Public Library System;
digitized by the Digital Library of Georgia (NHPRC Digitizing Historical Records grant).

- Item page: https://dlg.usg.edu/record/dlg_aaed_aarl90-001-001-001 (record id dlg_aaed_aarl90-001-001-001).
- Downloaded: the item's own PDF, https://dlg.galileo.usg.edu/data/dlg/aaed/pdfs/dlg_aaed_aarl90.001-001-001.pdf
  (reached from the record's "View PDF" link, https://dlg.galileo.usg.edu/aaed/do:aarl90.001-001-001). The PDF holds
  24 two-page spreads. Pages were rendered from it with macOS PDFKit at the scan's own text-layer resolution (about
  4,880 px per spread), then cropped:
  - 1.jpg: PDF image 1, right half: the title page.
  - 2.jpg: PDF image 5, left half, lower part: page 8 from the heading "WADE IN THE WATER" to the page number.
  - 4.jpg: PDF image 5, right half, upper part: page 9, "WADE IN THE WATER Concluded", down to the last verse line
    (the next song, "When I'm Dead", is cropped off).
- The record's Rights field: the rightsstatements.org icon "Copyright Not Evaluated"
  (http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/CNE/1.0/). That statement only says the library has not evaluated copyright; it
  imposes no restriction. Our licence is therefore public domain by age:
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published Nashville, Tennessee, 1904 (first edition registered 19 June 1902; title page "Copyrighted, June, 1902,
    and August, 1904"; verso "Copyright, 1902, by The Fisk University").
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - Creators: the song is traditional, no maker named. The book's only named person is Frederick J. Work
    (1879 to 1942), "Collected and Harmonized by". The DLG record lists John Wesley Work II (1873 to 1925) as
    creator. The preface is signed by a Committee on Publication (Roscoe A. Grant, Jennie A. Robinson,
    E. C. Stickel; read from the preface page, PDF image 2), all writing in 1902.
  - EU and Israel (life + 70): Frederick J. Work died 1942, so his rights ended at the end of 2012; John W. Work II
    died 1925 (ended 1995). The committee members, adults in 1902, are presumed dead before 1956. Public domain.
  - The scan is a faithful photograph of flat printed pages and adds no new copyright.
- Words checked on every crop (read in full against the minstrel-stage rule and the spirituals note):
  - 1.jpg: "NEW JUBILEE SONGS / AS SUNG BY THE / FISK JUBILEE SINGERS / OF / Fisk University / Collected and
    Harmonized by / FREDERICK J. WORK / Second Edition / FISK UNIVERSITY / Nashville, Tennessee / Copyrighted, June,
    1902, and August, 1904". No figure, no ornament with people, no dialect.
  - 2.jpg: "WADE IN THE WATER", "(A favorite melody among Southern Baptists while a baptism is in progress.)", the
    chorus words, "8". No dialect spelling.
  - 4.jpg: "WADE IN THE WATER Concluded" and the four verses as the story prints them. No dialect spelling.
  - Left out by the crops: page 8's upper half, "Somebody's Buried in the Graveyard", with a footnote by the editors
    on "the mind and songs of the slave" (not mocking, but it uses "the slave", which our prose avoids), and page 9's
    lower half, "When I'm Dead". Neither is needed.
- Credit: Auburn Avenue Research Library on African-American Culture and History, Atlanta-Fulton Public Library
  System, via the Digital Library of Georgia (the record's "Cite as" line names the Adam Knight Spence and John Wesley
  Work collection, Archives Division).

## 3.jpg: "Jerusalem (El-Kouds). Pool of Bethesda", American Colony, Jerusalem, between 1898 and 1914

- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, G. Eric and Edith Matson Photograph Collection.
  Reproduction number LC-DIG-matpc-06591 (also LC-M361-353; call number LC-M36- 353); LCCN 2019698297; old record id
  mpc2004006959/PP. Handle: https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/matpc.06591
- The record was read from https://lccn.loc.gov/2019698297/marcxml (www.loc.gov answers 403 to plain requests from
  here; LoC's SRU server lx2.loc.gov:210/lcdb found it on a title search for "pool of bethesda"). Saved as
  pages/lccn-2019698297.xml. Fields, exact words:
  - 110: "American Colony (Jerusalem). Photo Department, photographer." (a firm; no individual is named, so the
    lead's Matson test passes)
  - 245: "Jerusalem (El-Kouds). Pool of Bethesda [graphic] / American Colony, Jerusalem."
  - 260: "[between 1898 and 1914]"
  - 300: "1 negative : glass, dry plate ; 10 x 12 in."
  - 500: "Title from: Catalogue of photographs made by the American Colony ... 1914." and "Caption on negative: Pool
    of Bethesda."
  - 540: "No known restrictions on publication. For information see: \"G. Eric and Edith Matson Photograph
    Collection,\" https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/res.258.mats"
  - 541: "Gift; Episcopal Home; 1978."
- Collection rights page, as LoC's rights guide data gives it (https://guides.loc.gov/ld.php?content_id=76336558,
  row "258_mats.html", Publication field): "No known restrictions. The Kensington Episcopal Home conveyed the
  collection to the Library in 1978. In 2003, the Home dedicated the intellectual property and related rights to the
  collection to the American public, subject to \"whatever rights, if any, that may have previously been conveyed to
  others.\"" The one exception it names is lantern slides used in book form; this item is a glass negative, not a
  lantern slide.
- Downloaded: the LoC master, https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/matpc/06500/06591u.tif
  (5748 x 7282, a positive scan of the plate). Final made with sips, BMP round trip, 1600 px tall.
- Licence: LoC-no-known-restrictions. Also public domain by age: made by 1914 (it is in the 1914 catalogue) by a
  firm's photo department with no named photographer, so as an anonymous work its EU and Israeli term (70 years from
  being made public) ran out by 1984 at the latest; in the US it was made public before 1931. Caveat for the checker,
  as with the Lecha Dodi Matson photo: some American Colony photographers are known by name; LoC names none here.
- What is on it: no people. Words on the plate: "353 Pool of Bethesda. Bethesda Teich. Piscine de Béthesda." along
  the bottom, and the studio mark "American Colony, Jerusalem" at the lower left. Nothing else; nothing demeaning.
- Caption care: the caption says the photographers "labelled" it the Pool of Bethesda, because which vault or basin
  at the St Anne's site is the pool of John 5 is a matter of identification we have not sourced.
- Credit: "Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, LC-DIG-matpc-06591" (the collection's credit line
  form).

## Looked at and not used

- Fisk Jubilee Singers carte de visite, LoC Gladstone Collection, LC-DIG-ppmsca-11008 (LCCN 2010647805; 540 "No
  known restrictions on publication."; photographer attributed: James Wallace Black, 1825-1896). Rights are fine and
  the portrait is dignified, but it already appears on Swing Low, Steal Away and Were You There, and the singers it
  shows (1870s) are not connected with this song, which is not in their books. Dropped to keep this page its own.
- Matson negatives matpc-04958 and 04867 (Pool of Bethesda): previews only; 06591 is the clearer view of the water.
- Matson slide matpc-23124 ("John 5:2-9 ... troubling of the water"): a painting by William Hole with drawn human
  figures. Never used.
- 1907 reprint (Folk Songs of the American Negro, Number Two, pp. 8 and 9, IA folksongsofameri00work): the same
  pages line for line, and its pages are already used on Were You There. The 1904 printing is preferred.
- Page 8's upper half (the footnote under "Somebody's Buried in the Graveyard") and page 9's lower half ("When I'm
  Dead"): cropped off, not needed.
