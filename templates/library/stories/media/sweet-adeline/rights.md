# Sweet Adeline: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. Makers in the song file (templates/library/sweet-adeline.json): Richard H. Gerard, words, died
1948; Harry Armstrong, music, died 1951. First published 1903, US. Life + 70: Gerard's term ended 31 December 2018,
Armstrong's 31 December 2021. Under life + 100 (Mexico) Armstrong's term runs to 31 December 2051, so the song page
itself is withheld there; these are the makers' own pages and ride with it. Rule applied: no published image carries
text or music by anyone else still protected under life + 100.

## The source item (all three images)

- Richard H. Gerard and Harry Armstrong, You're the Flower of My Heart, Sweet Adeline, ballad (New York: M. Witmark &
  Sons, Black and White Series, a later printing), the story's [^byu]. Holder: Harold B. Lee Library, Brigham Young
  University (IA metadata: contributor "Harold B. Lee Library", call_number a4100274, OCLC 373820308; cover stamped
  "Humanities Ref, Early Sheet Music Collection", pencilled "ESM 12-19").
- Scan: Internet Archive item youreflowerofmyh00arms, https://archive.org/details/youreflowerofmyh00arms ; metadata
  saved as raw/meta-youreflowerofmyh00arms.json (no rights or licence field; IA's "date: 1903" is the copyright year,
  not the printing). Pages saved as raw/pages/00arms_n0.jpg to n7.jpg (n0 cover 3014 x 4150; n1 p. 2; n3 p. 4).
- Date of this printing: 1937 or later. Page 2 is marked "Copyright Renewed" (the renewal was signed 20 November 1930,
  story [^witmark1939]); the inside back cover (n4, not used) lists "Bei Mir Bist Du Schoen" (1937) and "Lullaby of
  Broadway from 'Gold Diggers of 1935'", distributed by Music Publishers Holding Corporation, RCA Building, Rockefeller
  Center.
- US status: copyright 1903, renewed 1930; the renewal term ran 28 years and ended 31 December 1959, before the 1962
  extensions, so the words and music are public domain in the US. The later printing adds no new words or music on the
  pages used (same plate, M.W.& Sons 6003-4; the piano part is credited only to Armstrong).
- EU and Israel (life + 70): only Gerard's and Armstrong's work, ended 2018 and 2021. Public domain.
- Mexico (life + 100): no non-maker text or music on any page used; the makers' own term is the only one running.
- Faithful scans of flat printed pages; no new copyright in the photograph.
- Not used from this item: n4 and n5 (inside and outside back cover, "Hits of Bygone Days" lists). They carry many other
  writers' titles, including coon-song titles ("Coon! Coon! Coon!", "Bake Dat Chicken Pie", "Plant a Watermelon on My
  Grave"): dropped under the minstrel rule. n6 and n7 are scanner colour cards.

## Rejected

- The Lilly Library copy (the story's [^lilly1903], our melody source): its cover's top line reads "Sung with Great
  Success by William R. Moore with Haverly's Mastodon Minstrels" (minstrel material: drop), and its last page of music
  advertises You Planted a Rose in the Garden of Love (1914; J. Will Callahan died 1956, protected in Mexico to 2056:
  drop). IN Harmony reproductions carry the Lilly "Conditions of Use and Copyright" terms; we link only (links.md).
- The second BYU scan, youreflowerofmyh01arms (a printing of 1943 or later, "New York 20, N. Y."): its music pages add
  guitar diagrams and ukulele and banjo symbols by an unnamed arranger, and its inside back cover is headed "Amateur
  Minstrel Shows" with "Dixie, Southern Dialect and Negro Songs". Dropped.
- The Edison Phonograph Monthly (May 1904; IA edisonphonograph02moor), which lists and reviews Edison record 8677 by
  Campbell and Harrison: the same list and review columns carry "Yaller Gal, an old time coon song" and other coon and
  minstrel records. Dropped. (Its review also misnames the lyricist "Albert H. Gerard".)
- The 1903 copyright catalogue entry: not found in the OCR of any 1903 quarter of the Catalog of Title Entries; not
  used rather than guessed.
- The plate facing p. 353 of Witmark and Goldberg, From Ragtime to Swingtime (1939): a photograph of people (the two
  writers at the renewal signing), and Goldberg (died 1938) and Witmark (died 1941) are protected in Mexico. Dropped.
- Hammerstein's Victoria Theatre, 1908 (Wikimedia Commons, "Hammerstein's Victoria.jpg"): Commons source "Internet",
  a street photograph with people. Dropped.
- Record labels on the National Jukebox items: brand trademarks front and centre. Not used.
