# Away in a Manger: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-12. Originals in raw/; finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata).

About raw/: the four page images there came from an earlier run whose notes were lost. Before using any of them I
found each item page myself, downloaded the same page again with plain curl from
`https://archive.org/download/<id>/page/<n>.jpg`, and compared MD5 checksums: all four are byte-identical to the
Internet Archive's current files (cynosure 9be871f4..., jp1908 aee83d0e..., lcb1885 9610a953..., bonner1905
6c82a9ba...). The *_meta.json files beside them are leftovers of that run and were not used; all metadata below was
fetched fresh from `https://archive.org/metadata/<id>`.

## 1.jpg: The Christian Cynosure, 2 March 1882, p. 11, "Children's Corner: Luther's Cradle Song"

- Item: Christian Cynosure (Chicago, IL: Ezra A. Cook), volume for 1881-82, bound weekly issues. IA metadata:
  creator "Blanchard, Jonathan, 1811-1892."; publisher "Chicago, IL: Ezra A. Cook"; contributor "Buswell Memorial
  Library, Wheaton College (IL)"; sponsor CARLI. Item https://archive.org/details/christiancynosurv14blan ; page
  https://archive.org/details/christiancynosurv14blan/page/n374/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/christiancynosurv14blan/page/n374.jpg (3448 x 5057), raw/cynosure1882_n374.jpg.
  The page header reads "March 2, 1882 THE CHRISTIAN CYNOSURE. 11", matching the story's [^cynosure1882].
- The final is cropped (sips) to the top of the first column: the date, "Childrens' Corner", "Luther's Cradle Song",
  the bracketed note and both verses.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Published in Chicago on 2 March 1882. The poem and its note are unsigned: an anonymous work.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel: an anonymous work is protected 70 years from publication, which ended in 1952. The paper's
    founder and named creator, Jonathan Blanchard, died in 1892 (IA's Library of Congress heading), so even a
    life-plus-70 reading ended in 1962.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (Bridgeman v. Corel in the US; Art. 14 of the 2019
    DSM Directive and lack of originality in the EU).
- What it shows: type only. No figures, no ornaments with people, no logos or watermarks. Neighbouring columns
  (Freemasonry, an article on glass) are cropped away.
- Caption facts: the page itself (heading, date, the note "composed by Martin Luther for his children, is still sung
  by many of the German mothers"); story lines on [^cynosure1882] and "the earliest known Luther claim is in
  American papers of 1882" ([^hymnarchive][^hill]). The caption says "one of the earliest known printings", not
  "the first", because the story reports The Congregationalist of 4 January 1882 as earlier.

  the words outside the United States. In 1905 the Sunday School Union in London printed it" ([^hill][^bonner1905]).

## Rejected

- Little Children's Book for Schools and Families (Philadelphia, 1885), No. 113, p. 140 (Princeton copy, IA
  littlechildb00unit n145; the file in raw/lcb1885_n145.jpg is verified identical to IA's). It is the earliest
  known printing with music and would be a fine image, but its tune ST. KILDA is by "J. E. Clark", and no source I
  could reach gives his dates (Hymnary.org has no biographical information for him). Without a death year I cannot
  show the music is out of copyright in the EU and Israel (life plus 70; a composer active in 1885 who lived past
  1955 is unlikely but possible). Not clearly OK, so dropped. If the lead finds Clark's death year (before 1956),
  it can go back in as the second image (story paragraph "The earliest known printing with music of its own").
- Murray 1887, Gabriel 1892 and Kirkpatrick 1895: the only scans are the facsimiles reproduced on Hymnology
  Archive, a secondary site with no holder or licence statement. Not used.
- Places and objects: nothing real and on point (the carol has no place of origin; a crib or manger picture would
  be generic). A Kirkpatrick photograph: no source with a clear free licence found. Nativity scenes contain figures.
