## 1.jpg: Briz, Cansons de la terra IV (1874), music plate of "Lo cant dels aucells"

- Item: Cansons de la terra. Cants populars catalans, vol. IV, published by Francesch Pelay Briz (Barcelona: D.
  Alvar Verdaguer; Paris: Maisonneuve et Cie, 1874). Image n14 is the half-title "LO CANT DELS AUCELLS. (CANSÒ DE
  NADAL)."; image n16 is the music plate (6/8, sharps, the first verse under the notes); the text and "Notas"
  follow (pp. 7 to 10), as the story's [^briz1874] cites.
- Holder of the copy: University of California (its perforated stamp "UNIV. OF CALIFORNIA" is on the plate; the
  final crops it off). Digitized by Google; uploaded to the Internet Archive by ARLIMA.
  Item: https://archive.org/details/CansonsDeLaTerra4 ; page: https://archive.org/details/CansonsDeLaTerra4/page/n16
  File: https://archive.org/download/CansonsDeLaTerra4/page/n16.jpg (2669 x 4227), saved as raw/briz-n16.jpg.
- Rights statement, IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/CansonsDeLaTerra4/metadata):
  "licenseurl": "http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/"; "creator": "Pelay Briz, Francesc, 1839-1889";
  "date": "1874"; "description": "Book digitized by Google and uploaded by ARLIMA (http://www.arlima.net)".
- Public domain by age:
  - Published in Barcelona in 1874. The editor Francesc Pelay Briz died in 1889. Words and tune are traditional,
    with no author named.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU (Spain) and Israel: life plus 70; Briz died 1889, so PD since 1960; anonymous traditional material has no
    living term either.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive; Bridgeman v.
    Corel in the US).
- Watermark: every page carries a small "Digitized by Google" mark at the foot; the final crop leaves it out, along
  with the library stamp. What remains is printed music and words only: no figures, no logos.
- Caption facts: story, "The words in print" ([^briz1874]): Briz opened vol. IV with "Lo cant dels aucells", a
  "cansò de Nadal"; its verses match Millet's 1917 "with small differences of wording"; "Briz's tune, though, is in
  6/8 and is not the one we sing".

## 2.jpg: Capmany, Cançoner popular (1903), sheet I, the tune

- Item: Cançoner popular, first series, ed. Aureli Capmany (Barcelona: Cançoner Popular, 1903; IA "publisher":
  "Barcelona, Cançoner Popular", "date": "1903"). Image n12 is Cançó I, "Lo cant dels aucells": a drawing at the top,
  then the hand-lettered tune in 2/4 with five flats (B-flat minor).
- Holder: University of Toronto, Robarts Library; IA "call_number": "AEM-0849"; scanned by the Internet Archive
  (Toronto) in 2009. Item: https://archive.org/details/canonerpopular00barcuoft ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/canonerpopular00barcuoft/page/n12 . File:
  https://archive.org/download/canonerpopular00barcuoft/page/n12.jpg (2378 x 3510), saved as raw/capmany-n12.jpg.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Published in Barcelona in 1903 (foreword "Quatre mots de lletra" signed March 1903, per the story).
  - Editor Aureli Capmany i Farrés: born Barcelona 1868, died Barcelona 1954, per the Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana,
    https://www.enciclopedia.cat/gran-enciclopedia-catalana/aureli-capmany-i-farres
  - The tune is traditional ("Canço popular catalana" on the facing sheet).
  - The drawing is by Joseph M. Comas: the foreword thanks the artists by sheet, "En Joseph M.ª Comas, que ilustra la
    fulla I" (IA OCR text, https://archive.org/download/canonerpopular00barcuoft/canonerpopular00barcuoft_djvu.txt).
    I could not find Comas's death year, and the drawing shows the Christ child (a drawn human figure), so the final
    crops away the whole drawing, its signature "J. M. Comas" and the decorative title lettering. Only the staves
    and the words under them remain.
  - The hand lettering of the music is credited to no one, so it is an anonymous contribution to a 1903
    publication (or Capmany's own, d. 1954).
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU: anonymous works 70 years from publication (expired 1973); Capmany died 1954, so life plus 70 ended at the
    close of 2024. PD from 1 January 2025.
  - Flag for the checker, Spain only: authors who died before 7 December 1987 keep the old law's life plus 80 in
    Spain, so if Capmany were counted as the author of this transcription, Spain would protect it to the end of
    2034. I do not think he is: the lettering is uncredited and the content is a traditional tune, and a plain
    transcription of a folk melody is not his original work. Under the brief's test (US, EU life plus 70, Israel)
    it is clear. If the checker wants Spain's 80 years honoured for editors too, drop 2.jpg; the set stands at three.
  - Israel: life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for an anonymous work; both expired.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.
- What it shows: five lines of hand-lettered music and words; faint show-through of the next page's verse text.
  No figures, no logos.
- Caption facts: story, "The tune we sing" ([^capmany1903]): "Sheet I, "Lo cant dels aucells", has a drawing
  signed J. M. Comas of birds around the stable, then the tune we sing, hand-lettered in 2/4 in B-flat minor, its
  last three lines repeated"; "gathered as a first series in 1903".

## 3.jpg: Millet, Pel nostre ideal (1917), Exemple XIV

  "EXEMPLE XIV / EL CANT DELS AUCELLS / Harm. de'n Lluís Millet", Moderat, Solo, Tenors I, Tenors II, Baritons,

- Holder: Arxiu Municipal del Masnou, which uploaded its own copy to the Internet Archive.
  Item: https://archive.org/details/pel-nostre-ideal-lluis-millet ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/pel-nostre-ideal-lluis-millet/page/n237 . File:
  https://archive.org/download/pel-nostre-ideal-lluis-millet/page/n237.jpg (3851 x 4933), saved as
  raw/millet-n237.jpg.
- Rights statement, IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/pel-nostre-ideal-lluis-millet/metadata):
  "contributor": "Arxiu Municipal del Masnou"; "uploader": "arxiu@elmasnou.cat";
  "licenseurl": "http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/"; "date": "1917".
- Public domain by age:
  - Printed in Barcelona in 1917 (colophon: finished 26 May 1917, per the story).
  - Lluís Millet 1867 to 1941 (LoC Name Authority n83212921, "Millet, Lluís, 1867-1941"). The preface's author
    Joan Llongueres (d. 1953) is not on this page.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU (Spain) and Israel: life plus 70; Millet died 1941, so PD since 2012. (Spain's transitional 80-year term

  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.
- What it shows: printed score and headings only. No figures, no logos.
- Caption facts: story, "The page" ([^millet1917]): "Exemple XIV, "El cant dels aucells", "Harm. de'n Lluís
  Millet", is marked Moderat. The four men's staves ... carry pp and "(Boca tancada)", closed mouth: the men hum,
  three bars alone before the solo's first "Al"."
