## 1.jpg and 2.jpg: Prague Haggadah, 1590

- Item: "Prague Haggadah (1590)", Internet Archive identifier PragueHaggadah1590, https://archive.org/details/PragueHaggadah1590
  - Metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/PragueHaggadah1590): title "Prague Haggadah (1590)"; creator "Gershom Katz";
    date "1590"; description: "The third printed edition of Gershom Katz's Prague Haggadah (the other two being from
    1526 and 1556). This is a copy of a digital edition prepared by the National Library of Israel." The link in that
    description is an NLI Aleph media link, doc_library NNL01, doc_number 001229423.
  - Licence shown on the item page: "Public Domain Mark 1.0" (licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/).
    The mark was set by the uploader (the Open Siddur Project's account), not by NLI. NLI's own record could not be
    read from here: www.nli.org.il answers plain requests with 403. The PD case rests on age, below.
  - The volume itself: image n1 is a flyleaf pencilled "R 2° 93 A 695 / 296.313"; the title page (n2) carries an oval
    stamp of the Jewish National and University Library, Jerusalem (NLI's former name), which agrees with the
    description.
  - Date check on the page itself: the title page reads "Seder Haggadot shel Pesach" and "nidpas po k"k Prag
    ha-birah" (printed here in the holy community of Prague, the capital), and its year line sets "shemi" in large
    type in the verse "... et shemi al bnei Yisrael va'ani avarachem" (Numbers 6:27); shemi = 300 + 40 + 10 = 350,
    that is [5]350 = 1589/90. This agrees with IA's date of 1590.
- Files: 1.jpg from https://archive.org/download/PragueHaggadah1590/page/n2.jpg (2245 x 3292, raw/ia-prague1590-title.jpg);
  2.jpg from https://archive.org/download/PragueHaggadah1590/page/n75.jpg (2245 x 3292, raw/ia-prague1590-n75.jpg).
  Both whole pages, uncropped.
- Public domain by age:
  - Printed in Prague in 1590. Its printers, the Katz (Kohen) family of Prague, and the anonymous Yiddish translator
    all died centuries ago; no term anywhere (life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for anonymous works) can
    still run. PD in the US (published before 1931), the EU and Israel.
  - The scan is a faithful photograph of a flat page and adds no copyright: US (Bridgeman v. Corel), EU (DSM Directive
    2019/790 art. 14: reproductions of PD visual art are not protected unless themselves original), Israel (a
    faithful copy has no originality).
- What they show (looked at whole and in zoomed strips):
  - 1.jpg: Hebrew and Yiddish type, a border of abstract interlace, fleurons, the library stamp. No figures.
    Words read: the title, the contents (Havdalah, Kiddush, the order, grace after meals and a commentary, with
    pictures of "the miracles and wonders"), a Yiddish note to readers who don't understand the holy tongue that the
    whole order has been put into Yiddish (as far as the worn type reads), the place line and the printers' line, and the year verse. Nothing
    demeaning.
  - 2.jpg: verses 8 to 13 of Echad Mi Yodea, each in large Hebrew with two small lines of Yiddish translation ("dos
    ist aber mer und dos selbig weis ich: ... sein der ..."), a leafy band down the right, a small ornament at the
    foot. The band was checked in three zoomed sections: abstract leaf and scroll work, no faces or masks. Every
    line is the song's Hebrew or its Yiddish counting; nothing demeaning.
  - **Show-through, for the checker:** in the blank lower right corner of the original scan, beside verse 13, there
    is a very faint mirror-image ghost (a pale circle and a vertical line) of the woodcut on the other side of the
    leaf (the Chad Gadya page, image n76, which has two woodcut figures). In the raw scan it is not readable as a
    figure; in the 1600 px final it cannot be seen. I kept the page; drop it if the rule covers show-through.
- Caption facts: story [^jmrc] and [^jrb]: "The earliest known printing is the Prague Haggadah of 1590, which set a
  Yiddish translation beside it." The page itself shows the Yiddish under each verse and ends on thirteen; the next
  page (n76) is Chad Gadya.

## 3.jpg: Union Haggadah, revised edition, 1923, p. 93

- Item: "The Union Haggadah :home service for the Passover /", Internet Archive unionhaggadahhom00unse,
  https://archive.org/details/unionhaggadahhom00unse ; page 93 is leaf 113 (from its _page_numbers.json).
  - Item page and metadata: date "c1923"; publisher "[s.l.] : The Conference"; contributor and sponsor "University of
    Florida, George A. Smathers Libraries"; "Possible copyright status: Copyright status reviewed by UF staff - Out of
    copyright".
  - The book: "EDITED AND PUBLISHED BY THE CENTRAL CONFERENCE OF AMERICAN RABBIS / COPYRIGHT 1923 / Printed in the
    United States of America".
- File: https://archive.org/download/unionhaggadahhom00unse/page/n112.jpg (2361 x 3683, raw/ia-union1923-p93.jpg),
  whole page, uncropped.
- Public domain:
  - US: published in the US in 1923 with notice; every US copyright in works published in 1923 ended on
    31 December 2018.
  - Who made the music: page 93 names nobody. The book's history (pp. 157 to 158) says the 1908 edition "contained
    the familiar Passover music, as edited by the Society of American Cantors"; the 1923 additions it lists (by Lucas,
    Brandt, Singer, Davis, Cohen, Halevy, Naumbourg, Kaiser) do not include this song. So the setting is a 1908
    collective edit with no named person, and the tune is traditional (story: "no known composer").
  - EU and Israel: an anonymous or collective work runs 70 years from publication: from 1908 that ended in 1978, and
    even from 1923 it ended in 1993. The Hebrew words are traditional; the English verses are on other pages.
  - Residual risk, for the checker: if a named cantor were ever shown to have made this harmonisation, life plus 70
    would apply to him. None is named in the book or the story's sources.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright (as above).
- What it shows: title "Ehod Mi Yodea", melody with verses 1 and 2 transliterated ("Ehod mi yodea ... ehod elohenu
  shebashshomayim uvo-oretz"; "Sh'nayim mi yodea ... sh'ne luhos hab'ris"), "(Omit ....)" and "(Omit for first
  stanza)", piano part, page number 93. No figures, no ornament, nothing demeaning. (The printed divine name is as
  the book prints it; our captions say Hashem and do not quote it.)
- Caption facts: story, "How it is sung": "Our arrangement is read from page 93 of that book. There it is 'Ehod Mi
  Yodea', in D minor and 4/4." and "The tune we sing has no known composer."

  Rejected for drawn figures.
- Prague 1590, image n74 (verses 2 to 7): a woodcut of a seated man in the lower left panel of its border. Rejected
  for a drawn figure. Not cropped: the rule is to reject the page.
- Prague 1590, image n76 (Chad Gadya): two woodcut figures. Not wanted anyway.
- National Library of Israel, the handwritten Hebrew and Yiddish copy added to a 1526/27 Prague Haggadah (record
  NNL_ALEPH990044053030205171, linked from the JMRC article): nli.org.il refused plain requests (403), so its rights
  statement could not be read. The HUC Jewish Language Project shows a page of it "Courtesy of the National Library
  of Israel", which is not a licence. Not used.
- No place photograph: nothing found for Prague's Jewish Town with a clear free licence on the holder's own page.
