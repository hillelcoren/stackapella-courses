# London's Burning: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; Internet Archive item metadata in notes/. Finals made with sips only (BMP round
trip strips metadata), scaled to 1600 px on the long side. A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new
copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v. Corel, in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive, and in Israel for
lack of originality. Every image looked at in full; none shows a drawn, painted or engraved human figure, a photograph,
a logo or a watermark. No photographs in this set, so none can repeat another song page's photo.

Mexico (life plus 100) check of every maker on each final image: 1.jpg, 2.jpg and 4.jpg carry only text and music by
people who died before 1926. 3.jpg carries only "London's Burning" as edited by John Goss (died 1953), who is listed in
the song file's makers (edition, words), so he is a maker, not a non-maker; the page's other two rounds (one credited
to Matthew White) are cropped out, and the round has no piano part, so nothing by the book's pianoforte arrangers
(Gerrard Williams and others) is on the crop.

## 1.jpg: Supplement to Johnson and Steevens's Shakespeare (London, 1780), vol. 1, p. 133

- Item: "Supplement to the edition of Shakspeare's plays published in 1778 by Samuel Johnson and George Steevens. In
  two volumes ... 1780: Vol 1", date 1780; Eighteenth Century microfilm (source IA40313301-34). No rights or licence
  field. notes/bim_eighteenth-century_supplement-to-the-editio_1780_1-meta.json.
- Record https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_supplement-to-the-editio_1780_1 ; leaf n164 = p. 133.
  File .../page/n164.jpg -> raw/bim_eighteenth-century_supplement-to-the-editio_1780_1-n164.jpg. The story cites
  "p. 133, note to vol. III, p. 476 ... image n164" [^supp1780].
- PD by age: printed London 1780. Edmond Malone (editor, signs most notes here) died 1812; George Steevens died 1800;
  the note's writer signed "E." in 1780 and so died long before 1926. US: published before 1931. EU, UK and Israel:
  life plus 70 ended in the 19th century. Mexico: life plus 100 ended long ago.
- Crop: the text block only (the facing page's edge and the black microfilm border removed); nothing on the page left
  out but blank space below the last note.
- Every word read: notes on Taming of the Shrew lines 436 to 511 ("rope-ripe chiding" with a Chapman quotation, the
  Biondello speech, "from a wild Kate to a Kate", "the old Pantaloon" and "grey-beard Gremio", note 476 with the catch,
  "too phlegmatick a meat", "every thing I look on seemeth green"). Nothing demeans a people or faith.
- Caption facts: the story: "In 1780 a note in a supplement to Johnson and Steevens's edition explained the line by
  quoting 'an old popular catch of three parts': 'Scotland burneth ...' That note is signed only 'E.'" Seen on the page.
