# Ye Banks and Braes o' Bonnie Doon: rights evidence for each image

Checked 2026-11-13. Every image was opened and looked at, edges and feet zoomed: no drawn, painted or engraved human
figures, no vignettes, no logos. One photograph in the set (1.jpg), with no people in it (bridge, path, banks and
water zoomed). Every printed word on all four crops was read (listed below). Nothing mocks or
demeans any people or faith.

Reuse check (every stories/media/*/media.json): none of these four items is on another song page. The 1839 Museum
reissue is used elsewhere only in vol. 5 (Auld Lang Syne, Comin' Thro' the Rye, A Red, Red Rose); this is vol. 4, a
separate volume and scan (scotishmusicalmu04john). Gow's collections (collectionofstra00gown) appear on no song page
(A Red, Red Rose only kept an unused alternate from its first collection, p. 7). Annie Laurie's Graham and Cunningham
and A Red, Red Rose's Urbani, Museum vols. 1 and 5 and Smith vol. 3 are not used. Burns's birthplace (Auld Lang
Syne's photograph, Rijksmuseum album RP-F-AA3402) is a different subject and album; Rijksmuseum photographs of the
Brig o' Doon in that same album (RP-F-AA3402-FS, -FX) were passed over for this one from a different album.

Not hosted, on purpose: the story's primary scan of the 1792 Museum, vol. 4 (National Library of Scotland 4453751,
scotsmusicalmuse04rugg). Its item record carries licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/2.5/scotland/
(NC and SA are not allowed), so the 1839 reissue of the same page from another holder is used, and the NLS item is
only linked as "original".

Makers and life plus 100 (Mexico): every maker of text or music on every page died by 1878 (Burns 1796, Clarke 1797,
Johnson 1811, Gow 1807; the 1839 editors Stenhouse 1827, Sharpe 1851, Laing 1878). James Miller, the Edinburgh writer
who made the air, is a listed maker of the song; his death year was not found, but he composed it in the 1780s. The
Gow crop shows only the Caledonian Hunt's Delight; the page's other two tunes ("Sandy o'er the Lee, or Mrs. Baird's
favorite Reel" and "Garthland's Strathspey", makers unnamed) are cropped out. No page carries anything by anyone
still protected anywhere.

## 3.jpg: Gow, A Second Collection of Strathspey Reels, title page (London: W. Boag issue)

  https://archive.org/details/collectionofstra00gown/page/n38/mode/1up
- Downloaded from https://archive.org/download/collectionofstra00gown/page/n38.jpg (3077 x 4250); the full page is
  used. Saved: raw/gow-n38.jpg, record raw/meta-collectionofstra00gown.json.
- Item record: creator "Gow, Niel, 1727-1807"; date "1795"; publisher "London : W. Boag", "London : W. Boag",
  "Edinburgh : N. & M. Stewart"; description "Originally published 1784, 1788 and 1792, respectively", "A second
  collection of Strathspey reels &c. with bass for the violoncello or harpsichord". No rights or licence field.
- This is not the Edinburgh first issue the story dates 1788 (Dick, Glen) or about 1789 (Newhailes, Corri &
  Sutherland): it is a London issue by W. Boag that offers "the First & Third Setts", so from 1792 or later. The
  caption calls it "a later London issue sold by W. Boag". The dedication wording matches the story's quotation
  from IMSLP and Newhailes ("the Noblemen & Gentlemen of the Caledonian Hunt").
- Public domain by age: engraved in the 1790s. US: before 1931. EU and Israel: Gow died 1807; the engraver is
  unnamed (anonymous, long ended); Boag was a 1790s London seller.
- Words on the page: "A Second Collection of Strathspey Reels &c. With a Bass for the Violoncello or Harpsichord.
  Dedicated (By Permission) to the Noblemen & Gentlemen of the Caledonian Hunt, By Price 6s Neil Gow, at Dunkeld.
  London Printed & Sold by W. Boag at his Music Shop No. 11 Great Turnstile, Lincolns Inn Fields. Where may be had the
  First & Third Setts Price 6s. Each". No figures; flourishes only.
