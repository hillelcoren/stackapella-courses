# Greensleeves: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata), 1.jpg, 3.jpg and
4.jpg resized to 1600 px on the long side, 2.jpg kept at its original 777 x 1169 (no upscaling). Nothing cropped.
Saved pages used as evidence are in pages/.

1.jpg, 3.jpg and 4.jpg are Internet Archive scans of flat printed pages with no licence field in the record, so each
rests on public domain by age, with the reasoning below. A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright: in the
US under Bridgeman v. Corel, and in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive and for lack of originality; Israel
likewise requires originality.

## 1.jpg: A Handful of Pleasant Delights (1584), ed. Edward Arber (London, 1878), p. 17

- Item: "A handful of pleasant delights, containing sundry new sonnets and delectable histories in divers kinds of
  metre &c. 1584. [By] Clement Robinson and divers others. Edited by Edward Arber", London, 1878. Creators "Robinson,
  Clement, fl. 1566-1584" and "Arber, Edward, 1836-1912". Contributor Robarts - University of Toronto; call number
  ABF-5636. IA field "possible-copyright-status": NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/handfulofpleasan00robiuoft (metadata
  https://archive.org/metadata/handfulofpleasan00robiuoft). Page 17 is leaf 39 in page_numbers.json (index n38):
  https://archive.org/details/handfulofpleasan00robiuoft/page/n38/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/handfulofpleasan00robiuoft/page/n38.jpg (2690 x 3866), saved as
  raw/handful-arber-p17-n38.jpg.
- PD by age: text 1584, anonymous ("Finis", unsigned); Arber's reprint 1878, Arber died 1912. US: before 1931. EU and
  Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1982.
- What it shows: running head "Sonets and Histories, to sundrie new Tunes.", margin note "J. P. 1584.", page 17;
  the last stanza of the preceding poem signed "I. P." (its words: "Thy self hath sought the meane and way ... your
  cowards heart we see."; nothing that demeans anyone), then "A new Courtly Sonet, of the Lady Green sleeues. To the
  new tune of Greensleeues.", the refrain, stanza 1 under a decorated initial A (floral ornament, no figure), stanza
  2. Footer "ENG. SCH. LIB. No. 3." No figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the page itself (heading, refrain above the first stanza); the story: earliest known printing of the
  words, 1584, heading quoted, "each followed by the refrain, which is also printed above the first stanza"
  [^handful][^chappell][^pittaway2][^balladindex]. This is the copy the story cites.
- Note: this is Arber's 1878 type reprint, not a photograph of the 1584 book. The only 1584 copy (British Library)
  has no free image I could find; the caption says "as Edward Arber reprinted it in 1878".

## 2.jpg: Trinity College Dublin MS 408 (William Ballet's lute-book), p. 83

- Image: https://www.tcd.ie/library/exhibitions/in-tune/images/MS408_0085.jpg (777 x 1169), the lightbox image on the
  Library's exhibition page "In Tune", "Early Music Treasures",
  https://www.tcd.ie/library/exhibitions/in-tune/early_music_treasures.php (saved as pages/tcd-early-music.html),
  titled there "William Ballet's lute-book", "TCD MS 408". Saved as raw/tcd-MS408_0085.jpg.
- Rights statement, The Library of Trinity College Dublin, "Rights Information",
  https://www.tcd.ie/library/dris/copyright.php (saved as pages/tcd-dris-copyright.html), exact words: "The Board of
  Trinity College Dublin has approved a Policy on the Open Licensing of the Library's Digitised Content. This means
  that where the original material is out of copyright, and not subject to other licence agreements, that the digital
  images can be used under the terms of a CC-BY 4.0 licence, which allows it to be reused or shared as long as credit
  is given to The Board of Trinity College." And: "There are some exceptions to this policy, including The Book of
  Kells (IE TCD MS 58) so permission for use should still be requested."
- PD by age as a second basis: the exhibition page says MS 408 is "a composite volume comprising two unrelated
  manuscripts dating from the late 16th or early 17th centuries"; anonymous scribes. Out of copyright everywhere
  (US, EU, Israel: no author alive in the last 300 years). The image is a straight photograph of a flat page, so under
  Art. 14 of the DSM Directive (and Bridgeman in the US) it carries no new copyright either.
- For the checker: the policy names its exceptions only as "including" the Book of Kells; MS 408 is not named, and the
  exhibition gives no other licence. The item-level record on digitalcollections.tcd.ie could not be read (it is behind
  a CAPTCHA, which I did not try to pass). Licence set to CC-BY-4.0 with the credit, since that is the holder's stated
  term and costs nothing; the PD-old basis stands on its own if the checker prefers that.
- What it shows: page "83" (pencilled top right), eight systems of French lute tablature with rhythm flags, a short
  note in the right margin (I read it as "y^e ground to passing measure pavin", not certain, not used in the caption).
  No figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: this is NOT the Greensleeves page (IMSLP's index places "Green Sleeves" at p. 104), so the caption
  says only that the volume holds the copy usually called the oldest. Sources: the story ("The copy usually called the
  oldest is in Trinity College Dublin MS 408, known as William Ballet's lute book" [^hymnarchive][^tcd]) and the TCD
  exhibition page ("Amongst these the best-known is Greensleeves"). I do not know which of the two bound manuscripts
  p. 83 belongs to, so the date is the library's "late 16th or early 17th centuries".
- Low resolution (777 px wide); fine for the page, but the lead may prefer to drop it if a larger image is wanted.

- Commons: no usable Greensleeves source scans (only modern engravings, audio and a Rossetti painting, which is a
  painted figure: never).
