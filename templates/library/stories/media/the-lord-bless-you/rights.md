  blesse them." A paragraph mark before verse 22. No figures, ornaments, logos or watermarks. Nothing demeaning.

- Item: "The Lord Bless You and Keep You", creator "Peter C. Lutkin", Internet Archive TheLordBlessYouAndKeepYou_700,
  added 2012-05-27, collection folkscanomy_music; the story's own cited scan ([^summy], images n0 to n3; also IMSLP

  evidence, and the reasoning below rests on age alone.
- Page: https://archive.org/download/TheLordBlessYouAndKeepYou_700/page/n3.jpg (2148 x 3102), saved as
  raw/summy_n3.jpg. Crop: the whole page less a dark scanner band at the right edge (sips crop 3060 x 2015 at
  offset y 20, x 0).
- Public domain: page 2 of the same copy reads "Copyright 1900 by Clayton F. Summy Co. / Copyright renewed 1928 by
  Peter C. Lutkin / Copyright renewal assigned to Clayton F. Summy Co.", plate C. F. S. Co. 524-3; page 4 carries
  plate "C. F. S. Co. 524". US: published 1900, so public domain (every work published before 1931 is; the 1928
  renewal term ran out long ago). EU and Israel: Lutkin died 1931; life plus 70 ended at the end of 2001. Mexico

  printing is later (1928 or later, from its renewal notice; the cover's design looks mid-century), but it is

- Caption facts: the story: "The earliest known printing is Clayton F. Summy's octavo, published in Chicago in 1900

## 3.jpg: The Methodist Hymnal (1905), No. 748, p. 532

- Item: "The Methodist hymnal: official hymnal of the Methodist Episcopal Church and the Methodist Episcopal Church,
  South" (New York: Eaton & Mains; Cincinnati: Jennings & Graham, 1905), Internet Archive methodi00meth; contributor
  and sponsor Princeton Theological Seminary Library, call_number SCC; the story's own cited scan ([^mh1905], image
  n543). The IA record states no licence; public domain by age.
- Page: https://archive.org/download/methodi00meth/page/n543.jpg (2768 x 4124), saved as raw/mh1905_n543.jpg. Whole
  page, resized.
- Public domain: published 1905; US public domain (before 1931). The page credits only "Peter C. Lutkin" (died 1931):
  EU and Israel life plus 70 ended at the end of 2001; Mexico: Lutkin is the song's own maker. The words are the KJV
  (1611) in Lutkin's wording. The two-bar "A-MEN." is the hymnal's house formula: the book closes its hymns with the
  same two-chord Amen (raw/mh1905_n40.jpg, p. 29, Nos. 39 and 40, checked), a stock cadence, not a work by any
  person. The hymnal's other music editor, Karl P. Harrington (died 1953), is not credited on this page. The line
  "Copyright, C. F. Summy Co. Used by permission" refers to the 1900 copyright, long expired.
- What it shows, every word read: "Chants and Occasional Pieces"; "748 THE LORD BLESS YOU AND KEEP YOU"; "PETER C.
  LUTKIN"; "Smoothly and deliberately"; the blessing's words as Lutkin set them, with the lower line "and give you
  peace ... and give you peace; ... the Lord"; "poco cres.", "dim. e rit.", "p", "pp"; "A - MEN."; "Copyright, C. F.
  Summy Co. Used by permission"; "532". No figures, ornaments, logos or watermarks; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the story: "Lutkin was one of the two music editors of the Methodist Hymnal of 1905 ... It prints
  his blessing as No. 748, on page 532 ... with the same four parts on two staves ... but a short two-bar Amen in
  place of the sevenfold one" [^price][^mh1905][^fenner].
