only: BMP round trip (strips metadata), crops with sips -c and --cropOffset on the BMP, then -Z 1600 to JPEG at
quality 82. Every original and every final was opened and looked at. Total 1.77 MB (1.jpg 388 KB, 2.jpg 539 KB,
3.jpg 452 KB, 4.jpg 396 KB). No photograph in this set (all four are scans of flat documents), so no photo is shared

## 1.jpg: Anna Spafford's cable, "Saved alone", received Chicago 2 December 1873

- Item: LoC Manuscript Division, American Colony in Jerusalem Collection, Part I, Box 1, Folder 6; digital id
  mamcol.011 (http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/mamcol.011); item page https://www.loc.gov/item/mamcol.011/ . Title
  "Telegram from Anna Spafford to Horatio Gates Spafford re being "Saved alone" among her traveling party in the
  shipwreck of the Ville du Havre"; created_published "December 2, 1873"; contributor_names include "Spafford, Anna
  T., 1842-1923 (Author)". Notes: "Handwritten cablegram, Atlantic Cable Message, The Western Union Telegraph
  Company. To [Horatio Gates] Spafford, 159 LaSalle St., Chicago ... Received at Chicago, Ill., Dec. 2d, 5:40 AM,
  1873." and "Document conserved by the Library of Congress Conservation Division."
- File: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/mss/mamcol/011/0001.tif (2422 x 1710), raw/loc-mamcol011-0001.tif
  (record lists it as http://cdn.loc.gov/master/mss/mamcol/011/0001.tif).
- Crop: master rows 458 to 1707, columns 1 to 2420 (the whole width). Cut away: the printed heading of the form,
  "ATLANTIC CABLE MESSAGE. THE WESTERN UNION TELEGRAPH COMPANY.", its conditions and officers' names, because
  Western Union is a living trademark and the brief says nothing with a trademark front and centre. What is left is
  the handwritten message with the form's ruled lines, the small printed words "Received at" and "To", and the
  fragment "(See other side)" at bottom left.
- Public domain:
  - The message's author is Anna T. Spafford, died 1923 (LoC name heading "Spafford, Anna T., 1842-1923"). The handwriting is the receiving clerk's copy, which adds no authorship. The printed form was
    published by Western Union in 1873.
  - US: if treated as unpublished, life plus 70 ended at the end of 1993; anything published from it before 1931 is
    PD. Point for the checker: a work unpublished before 1978 but first published 1978 to 2002 would be protected to
    2047 (17 U.S.C. 303(a)); I found no such first publication, the message is a short factual cable, and LoC says
    it knows of no U.S. or other restrictions.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 31 December 1993.
  - A faithful scan of a flat document adds no new copyright: US (Bridgeman v. Corel), EU (Art. 14 DSM Directive).
- Words read: every word of the crop (above). Names of others lost in the wreck (Mrs Goodwin, Willie Culver) and the
  minister Lorriaux; nothing demeaning. No figures, no logos left.
- Caption facts: "from Wales" (story, "The wreck": the survivors were landed at Cardiff, from there Anna cabled,
  [^loc-family][^hymnarchive]); "written out on arrival in Chicago on 2 December 1873" (LoC record: handwritten
  cablegram, "Received at Chicago, Ill., Dec. 2d, 5:40 AM, 1873", also visible on the form). Note: the LoC 2005
  exhibition dated it 1 December; the item record and the form say 2 December, as does Hymnology Archive for the
  sending; the story already lays out the disagreement.

## 2.jpg: Spafford's manuscript of the hymn, on Brevoort House notepaper, undated

- Item: LoC Manuscript Division, American Colony in Jerusalem Collection, Part I, Box 1, Folder 11; digital id
  mamcol.016 (http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.mss/mamcol.016); item page https://www.loc.gov/item/mamcol.016/ . Title "Draft
  manuscript copy of hymn "It is Well With My Soul" by Horatio Gates Spafford"; created_published "ca. 1873-1878";
  author "Spafford, Horatio Gates, 1828-1888". Notes: "Handwritten manuscript copy of verses of "It is Well With My
  Soul," written in ink on Brevoort House, Madison Street between Clark and La Salle Streets, Chicago, hotel
  letterhead. Pencil notes on verso."
- File: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/mss/mamcol/016/0001.tif (2432 x 3207), raw/loc-mamcol016-0001.tif.
  Page 2 (the verso with the pencil stanzas) not downloaded.
- Crop: master rows 745 to 3204, columns 1 to 2430 (the whole width). Cut away: the whole printed letterhead. Its
  engraving of the hotel shows small figures and carriages in the street (checked at full size), which the
  no-human-figures rule forbids; the letterhead lettering went with it, since a rectangle cannot keep one without
  the other.
- Public domain:
  - Author Horatio Gates Spafford, died 1888 (LoC heading). The hymn's text was published with his consent in 1876
    (Gospel Hymns No. 2, "P. P. Bliss, by per.") and by himself in 1878 (Waiting for the Morning).
  - US: published text before 1931, PD; the manuscript as an unpublished work, life plus 70, ended at the end of
    1958. Same 303(a) note as 1.jpg for the few words that differ from the printings (the last line); none found,
    and LoC states no known U.S. or other restrictions.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 31 December 1958.
  - Faithful scan of a flat document: no new copyright (as above).
- Words read: the four verses as Spafford wrote them, a pencilled "(My God)" in the left margin, and a faint pencil
  line under the last verse. Nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: Brevoort House notepaper (LoC note above; story, "The words", [^hymnarchive][^spaffordhymn-ms]);
  undated and "when he wrote it out is not known for certain" (story, "The words" and "Myth and record";
  Hymnology Archive: "An undated manuscript copy of the hymn is held by the Library of Congress"). The LoC exhibition
  dated it 1871; the item record says "ca. 1873-1878"; the date field follows the item record.

## Checker's changes and notes (IMAGE-CHECK-16)

- Read by me: LoC JSON for mamcol.011 and mamcol.016 (Wayback captures of 25 Oct 2015, loc.gov answers 403), both
  with the collection rights text quoted above; IA hymnsno02blis (no rights field), waitingformornin00auth ("The
  Library of Congress is unaware of any copyright restrictions for this item."), and the listen item
  loc-jukebox-422502 (PD statement, 1906, Bliss).
- 1.jpg, the lead's 303(a) and publication-right questions:
  - US: a work unpublished on 1 Jan 1978 is protected for life + 70 or to the end of 2002, and to 2047 only if first
    published, with the owner's authority, from 1978 to 2002. LoC first showed the cable in its 2005 exhibition, after
    that window. A family printing before 1978 (for example in Bertha Vester's Our Jerusalem, 1950) could not be
    checked: the IA copy is lending-only and its full-text search refused. Even then, what is on the form is a
    telegram of about 25 words, mostly names, an instruction and an address, round one short phrase ("Saved alone
    what shall I do"): short phrases and facts carry no US copyright (37 CFR 202.1(a)). The handwriting is the
    receiving clerk's; the printed form is Western Union's of 1873.
  - EU publication right (Directive 2006/116, art. 4): it attaches to a previously unpublished work first lawfully
    published after its term ends. LoC, a US body, published it online in the US; the member states that grant the
    right limit it to EEA publishers or first publication in the EEA (UK 1996 Regulations, reg. 16; Germany sections
    71 and 124 UrhG). So no publication right arises. Anna's own term ended 1993 (life + 70) and 2023 (life + 100).
  - Kept. If the lead wants zero residual doubt on the 1950 point, drop 1.jpg; the set stands at three.
- 2.jpg: the poem itself was published with Spafford's consent in 1876, so no publication right can attach to it. The
  only unpublished words are a variant last line, "A song in the night, oh my soul!" (a phrase Spafford printed as a
  poem title in 1878), and two faint pencil notes ("(My God)" and a line under verse 4): de minimis. Kept.
  No letterhead remains: the final starts at the first verse line.
- 1.jpg Spanish caption: the gloss now uses the story's own wording, "(salvada, sola; qué debo hacer)".
