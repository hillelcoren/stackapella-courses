## 1.jpg: Safed above its fields, 1898 to 1914

- Item: "Northern views. Safed "A city built on a hill" / American Colony, Jerusalem." 1 negative: glass, dry plate,
  10 x 12 in. Creator: "American Colony (Jerusalem). Photo Dept., photographer". Date: "[between 1898 and 1914]".
  Reproduction number LC-DIG-matpc-07029; call number LC-M36- 836 [P&P]. Notes: "Title from: Catalogue of photographs
  made by the American Colony ... 1914. Caption on negative: Safed "City built on a hill." Gift; Episcopal Home; 1978."
  Subject: Israel--Tsefat. Collection: G. Eric and Edith Matson Photograph Collection.
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division. Item page:
  https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/mpc2004007387/PP/ . loc.gov refuses plain requests from here (Cloudflare
  challenge), so the record was read from the Wayback Machine copy of 2017-06-08:
  http://web.archive.org/web/20170608112644/http://www.loc.gov:80/pictures/item/mpc2004007387/PP/
- Rights statement, exact words on the item page: "Rights Advisory: No known restrictions on publication."
- Files:
  - raw/loc-matpc-07029.jpg: LoC's own 1024 px derivative,
    https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/matpc/07000/07029v.jpg (to show the holder's file matches).
  - raw/commons-matpc-07029.jpg (7251 x 5875): the full-size copy on Wikimedia Commons,
    https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Northern_views._Safed_%27A_city_built_on_a_hill%27_LOC_matpc.07029.jpg ,
    whose page names the source as Library of Congress (catalog mpc2004007387, "Original url:
    https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/matpc.07029"), permission "No known restrictions on publication.", licence tags
    PD-Matson and PD-old-70-1923. Commons is only the host of LoC's master; the final was made from it.
  - The final crops away the plate's black edge, the caption strip on the negative ("836 Safed "City bulit on a Hill"
    Safed. Safed.") and the studio mark "American Colony, Jerusalem" at the lower right. So no words are left on the
    image.
- Public domain reasoning, beside the LoC statement:
  - US: made 1898 to 1914. LoC takes the title from the American Colony's printed "Catalogue of photographs" of
    1914, and the photo department sold its prints commercially, so it was published before 1931: PD. (If it were
    ever held unpublished, LoC's statement still stands, and it is LoC's accepted category in the brief.)
  - EU: no individual photographer is named (corporate credit to the Photo Dept.), so the anonymous-work term
    applies: 70 years from being made available, ended by 1985 at the latest.
  - Israel: photographs made under the old law (the Copyright Act 1911 as in force in Mandate Palestine) had 50 years
    from the making of the negative, so the term ended by 1964; works already free when the 2007 law came in stay
    free. As an anonymous work it would also be past 70 years from publication.
  - Caveat for the checker: some American Colony photographers are known by name (Eric Matson died 1977). If a named
    individual were ever proved the author, life plus 70 in the EU would run to 2047. LoC names none, and the brief
    lists LoC "No known restrictions on publication" as accepted, so it is kept.
- What it shows: a rocky hillside, olive trees and stone field walls, with Safed's houses along the ridge. One
  person, photographed (not drawn), sits on the rocks in the foreground, seen from behind; anyone in a photo of
  1898 to 1914 is long dead. No drawn figures, no logos left, no watermark.
- Caption facts: date and maker from the LoC record. "the mystics of this town walked out into the fields on Friday
  afternoons to meet the Sabbath" is the story's own sentence, sourced there to the Encyclopaedia Judaica [^ej]
  ("walked out into the fields on Friday afternoons, praying and singing, to meet the Sabbath as a queen"). The
  caption does not claim these are the fields they walked in.

## 2.jpg: Singer's Authorised Daily Prayer Book (1904), p. 112, Hebrew

- Item: The Authorised Daily Prayer Book of the United Hebrew Congregations of the British Empire, with a new
  translation by the Rev. S. Singer, published under the sanction of the late Chief Rabbi Dr. Nathan Marcus Adler.
  Seventh edition. London: Eyre and Spottiswoode, His Majesty's Printers, 5664 - 1904 (all from the title page's
  OCR). Page 112, Hebrew, headed "קבלת שבת" (Kabbalat Shabbat).
- Copy: University of Toronto, Robarts Library (IA "contributor": "Robarts - University of Toronto"). Item:
  https://archive.org/details/authoriseddailyp00sing ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/authoriseddailyp00sing/page/n233/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/authoriseddailyp00sing/page/n233.jpg (1507 x 2440), saved as
  raw/singer-1904-p112-hebrew.jpg. IA metadata creators: "Singer, Simeon, 1848-1906", "Adler, Nathan Marcus,
  1803-1890".
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Published in London in 1904. The Hebrew text is Alkabetz's 16th-century poem; the edition's editor and
    translator Simeon Singer died in 1906, Nathan Marcus Adler in 1890.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70; Singer's term ended at the close of 1976, and the printing house (Eyre and
    Spottiswoode) is not an author. PD.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM
    Directive and for lack of originality).
- Crop: the top of the page to the end of Lecha Dodi ("...בואי כלה : לכה"). The page goes on with Psalm 92, whose
  first line prints the four-letter divine name in full; that is cropped out. What is left prints the usual
  abbreviation יְיָ twice (verses 5 and 8), as every prayer book does.
- Every word read: the header "קבלת שבת", the page number 112, and Lecha Dodi from "מראש מקדם נסוכה" (second half of
  verse 2) to verse 9, each paragraph ending "לכה" (the refrain cue). Faint show-through of the English on the
  reverse (Singer's translation of Psalms) is not legible. Nothing demeaning. No figures or ornaments.
- Caption facts: the page itself. Israel Abrahams, "A Companion to the Authorised Daily Prayer Book" (1922), is the
  story's [^abrahams]; its title names the book it accompanies.

## 3.jpg: The Jewish Encyclopedia, vol. 7 (1904), p. 677

- Item: The Jewish Encyclopedia, vol. 7, "Italy - Leon" (New York and London: Funk and Wagnalls Company, MDCCCCIV;
  "Copyright, 1904, by Funk & Wagnalls Company", from the title page's OCR). Article "Lekah Dodi", music on p. 677
  headed "LEKAH DODI (Melody for 'Omer Weeks)"; the music section's bibliography is signed "F. L. C." (Francis L.
  Cohen), and the story credits the article to Cyrus Adler and Francis L. Cohen.
- Copy: University of California Libraries. Item: https://archive.org/details/jewishencyclopedia07sing ; IA field
  "possible-copyright-status": "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". Page:
  https://archive.org/details/jewishencyclopedia07sing/page/n712/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/jewishencyclopedia07sing/page/n712.jpg (1945 x 3016), saved as raw/je7-p677.jpg.
- Public domain by age:
  - Published in New York in 1904. Authors: Cyrus Adler, 1863 to 1940 (IA creator field); Francis L. Cohen, 1862 to
    1934 (the story, from the Encyclopaedia Judaica and Jewish Encyclopedia).
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70; the later author died in 1940, so PD since the end of 2010.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page, as above.
- Crop: only the Omer-weeks melody, from its heading to its final bar line. Left out: the end of the melody above it,
  the bibliography and the next articles (Lel Shimmurim, Lelio de la Torre, Lemans).
- Every word on the crop: "LEKAH DODI (Melody for 'Omer Weeks)", "p Andantino", "cres.", "mf", "Refrain", and the
  English words "How lovely is the Day of Rest, Rich source of blessing, welcome guest; 'Twas first in thought, if
  last express'd, Ordained ere aught was form'd beside. Then come, my friend, to meet the Bride, to greet the joyous
  Sabbath-tide." Nothing demeaning. I also read the rest of the page (Lemans: a Dutch educator; neutral). No figures.
- Caption facts: the story ("Francis L. Cohen printed the tune in that 1904 article, with English words"; "Our
  arrangement is read from Cohen's 1904 printing"), and the page itself.

## Rejected

- Other scans of JE vol. 7: Cornell cu31924091768246 is a BookLab photocopy; Wellcome b29000488_0007 carries the
  rightsstatements.org "In Copyright" statement on IA. The UC copy has neither problem.
- The earliest known printing (a Sephardi prayer book of 1583/4): no scan with a free statement found. A UPenn
  catalogue record for a 1584 Spanish-rite siddur is "restricted ... by site license"; auction-house photos are not
  free.
- Singer p. 111 (Hebrew), where the refrain and verse 1 begin: they sit at the foot of a page of Psalm 29 thick with
  the abbreviated divine name; p. 112 is cleaner and carries most of the poem.
- Singer p. 111 (English): Singer's translation and footnote on the acrostic. Good, but plain English prose; the Hebrew
  page was preferred.
- LoC matpc.01113 "Safed looking east" (about 1900, same rights): a stereo pair of houses on the slope; usable, left
  out as it adds less than the fields view. matpc.01114 likewise.

## Checker's changes (IMAGE-CHECK-3, 2026-11-13)

- **1.jpg kept.** The lead's test: keep only if authorship is anonymous or corporate on the record. The LoC item
  record (Wayback 2017-06-08) names only "American Colony (Jerusalem). Photo Dept., photographer"; no individual.
  The collection page (Wayback 2017-12-29) describes the negatives as "created by the American Colony Photo
  Department and its successor firm, the Matson Photo Service" (firms, not a named photographer). The collection's
  own rights page, http://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/258_mats.html (Wayback 2017): "Photographs in the G. Eric and
  Edith Matson Photograph Collection are in the public domain. ... In 2003, the Home dedicated the intellectual
  property and related rights to the collection to the American public". Caption reworded to quote the negative's
  own title ("a city built on a hill") instead of the unsourced "olive groves".
- **2.jpg re-cropped.** Verse 7 printed אֱלֹהָיִךְ (Elohim with a suffix, spelled in full; the story's own
  transliteration substitutes it as "Elokayich"). Under the lead's rule only the abbreviation יְיָ may show, so the
  page now ends after verse 6 (ffmpeg crop of raw/singer-1904-p112-hebrew.jpg to 1506 x 1378 from the top, then the
  sips BMP round trip, jpeg 82, not enlarged). What is left shows יְיָ once (verse 5). The old final is in
  dropped/2-uncropped-shows-elohayikh.jpg. Alt now "five short paragraphs"; caption now "to verse 6".
