# Shenandoah: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. All three are public domain by age. Records saved beside this file: pages/nara_516607.json
(NARA catalogue record), pages/riverside_djvu.txt (OCR of the 1868 issue). www.loc.gov and sciencebase.gov answered 403
(bot check) to plain requests from here, so no LoC or USGS item is used.

## 1.jpg: "Shannydore", The Riverside Magazine for Young People, April 1868, p. 185 (detail)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/sim_riverside-magazine-for-young-people_1868-04_2_16 (scan image n40, printed
  page 185; running head "1868.] Sailors' Songs. 185").
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/sim_riverside-magazine-for-young-people_1868-04_2_16/page/n40.jpg (2528 x 3616).
- IA record: title "The Riverside Magazine for Young People 1868-04: Vol 2 Iss 16", collections sim_microfilm,
  periodicals; no rights or licence field. (IA's "publisher: Open Court Publishing Co" is an error of the serials
  record and is not repeated anywhere.)
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published April 1868 in the US; the article "Sailors' Songs" is unsigned.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: an anonymous work's term is 70 years from lawful publication (1868 + 70 = 1938). Even if the writer were
    known, anyone writing in 1868 of a "recent voyage" died long before 1956.
  - Israel: the same (life + 70; anonymous works 70 years from publication).
  - The scan is a faithful photograph of a flat page and adds no new copyright.
- Crop and words read: the image is a detail of the left column only, from "Come boys, lend a hand here! Man the
  capstan-bars! Give us a song now! Old Dave is our "chanty-man." Tune up, David!" to the end of the first verse
  ("O Shannydore, I long to hear you! / Chorus. Away, you rollin' river! / O Shannydore, I long to hear you! / Full
  Chorus. Ah ha! I'm bound away / On the wild Atlantic!"). Every word in the crop was read; nothing in it demeans
  anyone. The crop leaves out the next verses, one of which names "Jim-along-Joe" (the name of a blackface minstrel song
  of about 1840) as skipper; nothing on the page is cruel in itself, but we keep that out of the picture.
  The footnotes ("Chanter (French), to sing ... taken from the lips of a veritable "old Dave" during the writer's
  recent voyage across the Atlantic." and "† Shenandoah.") fall below that verse and are left out too; the story
  quotes them.

## 2.jpg: Terry, The Shanty Book, Part I (1921), No. 9 "Shenandoah", p. 20

- Item: https://archive.org/details/shantybooksailor00terr (scan image n41, printed page 20).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/shantybooksailor00terr/page/n41.jpg (2950 x 4010).
- IA record: "The shanty book; sailor shanties. Collected and edited with piano acc"; creator "Terry, Richard
  Runciman, 1865-1938"; publisher "London, J. Curwen"; date 1921; contributor "University of Toronto, Faculty of
  Music Library"; call number "M1977 .S2 T4"; no rights or licence field.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London 1921. Editor and arranger (the piano accompaniment and violin part) Richard Runciman Terry, died
    1938. The tune and words are traditional (from "a Captain Robertson", per the story's source).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: life + 70 (1938 + 70) ended 31 December 2008. The UK publisher's typographical-arrangement right (25 years)
    ended in 1946.
  - Israel: life + 70, the same.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Words on the page, all read: "20", "9. Shenandoah.", "(Windlass and Capstan.)", "Slowly and with much expression.",
  "M. = 50.", "Violin. (ad lib.)", "Voice.", "Piano.", "Solo.", "Chorus.", "1. Oh Shenandoah, I long to hear you.
  A-way you rolling", and the footnote "The small notes in the piano part are to be played when there is no violin."
  Nothing that demeans anyone; no figures or decoration.

## 3.jpg: Missouri River near Omaha Indian Agency, 1869, photograph by William Henry Jackson

- Item: https://catalog.archives.gov/id/516607 (record JSON fetched from
  https://catalog.archives.gov/proxy/records/search?naId=516607, saved as pages/nara_516607.json).
- Record fields (exact): title "Missouri River near Omaha Indian Agency. Mrs. W.H. Jackson in foreground.";
  productionDates year 1869; contributor "Jackson, William Henry, 1843-1942", contributorType "Photographer";
  localIdentifier "57-HS-1"; NAIL control number "NWDNS-57-HS-1"; useRestriction status "Unrestricted";
  accessRestriction status "Unrestricted"; series 516606 "Hayden Survey, William H. Jackson, Photographs"; record
  group 386 "Records of the U.S. Geological Survey"; held at "National Archives at College Park - Still Pictures".
- Downloaded: https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/stillpix/057-hs/057-HS-0001_17-0015M.TIF (3000 x 2143), the file
  the record lists.
- Also mirrored on Wikimedia Commons (not used as the source):
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Missouri_River_near_Omaha_Indian_Agency._Mrs._W.H._Jackson_in_foreground._-_NARA_-_516607.tif
  (tagged PD-USGov and Public Domain Mark).
- Licence: PD-old. Not marked PD-US-gov: the record does not say the 1869 photograph was made for the Government
  (Jackson is generally said to have joined the Hayden Survey in 1870), so we do not rely on government authorship.
- Reasoning:
  - Photograph taken 1869; photographer William Henry Jackson died 1942.
  - US: if published, it was published before 1931; if never published, an unpublished work by an author who died
    before 1956 is public domain (life + 70 ended 2012). Either way public domain. NARA lists no use restriction
    ("Unrestricted").
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 (1942 + 70) ended 31 December 2012.
  - A photograph of a landscape taken in 1869; the one person in it (Mrs. W. H. Jackson, named by the record) is not
    a living person.
- Content check: no text or markings on the print; the caption keeps the record's place name.

## Rejected

- The New Dominion Monthly, April 1876, p. 262 ("Shanadore" with its tune), Canadiana oocihm.8_06581_104, image 24:
  (1) the printed verses include "Shanadore's a bright mulatto", a racial term; it could be cropped out, but
  (2) Canadiana's terms say the site is for "your personal, non-commercial use" and give no reuse statement for the
  scan, so it is not clearly OK. Dropped.
- "On the banks of the Missouri" (Edward S. Curtis, about 1908, LoC cph.3a47151): a posed photograph of an unnamed
  Mandan man used as scenery; against the brief (no named person, decoration). Not considered further.
- "Missouri River, Omaha, 1900" (H. H. West, UCLA, Calisphere ark:/21198/zz002hrfc4): rights "Please contact the
  contributing institution"; West died 1958, so not PD in the EU. Dropped.
- "Steamer Deer Lodge ... Missouri River" (Missouri Historical Society N13881, via DPLA/Commons): rights statement
  "No Copyright - United States" only. Dropped under the "PD in the US only" rule.
- No capstan photograph with a clear free licence was found (LoC unreachable; Commons hits were modern US Navy photos
  or paintings with figures).
