Gathered 2026-11-13. Makers: the song file lists none (makers []); the page's note names the spirit "Jack" and the
medium Abraham Perkins (1807 to 1900). No printed page below carries words or music by anyone still protected under
life plus 100 (Mexico).

## 1.jpg: Runnels, History of Sanbornton, vol. 2 (1881), p. 550, Perkins family Nos. 10 to 16

- Item: https://archive.org/details/historyofsanborn02runn (University of New Hampshire Library copy), leaf n593 =
  printed page 550 (seen). Downloaded https://archive.org/download/historyofsanborn02runn/page/n593.jpg (1996 x 3223).
- Title page (read in the scan's text): "HISTORY OF SANBORNTON, NEW HAMPSHIRE. In Two Volumes. VOL. II.-GENEALOGIES. BY
  Rev. M. T. RUNNELS. ... BOSTON, MASS.: ALFRED MUDGE & SON, PRINTERS, 34 School Street. 1881."
- Internet Archive record: creator "Runnels, M. T. (Moses Thurston), 1830-1902"; "possible-copyright-status:
  NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"; sponsor and contributor University of New Hampshire Library.
- PD reasoning: published 1881, so public domain in the US (before 1931). The author died 1902: life plus 70 ended
  1972 (EU, Israel), life plus 100 ended 2002 (Mexico). The printers are not authors. No other maker's text is on the
  crop. Licence: PD-old.
- Every printed word on the crop read: entry 10, Jonathan Perkins (Revolutionary service, West Point, marriage to Hannah
  Taylor, "a great Bible reader", died 25 July 1852; his wife died 1837 "of consumption"); 11 to 15 his children John
  Brewer, Chase, Matthew, Phebe, Sally M.; 16 "ABRAHAM, b. Oct. 13, 1807; joined the family of Shakers, at Enfield, in
  1827, and has risen to prominence and distinguished usefulness in that community, both at E. and Canterbury, as a
  preacher and temporal adviser." Nothing that demeans anyone. No figures (the page has none; a portrait plate sits on
  other leaves and was not used).
- Caption claims: born 13 October 1807 and joined at Enfield in 1827, "a preacher and temporal adviser" at Enfield and
  Canterbury: all on the crop itself, and the story's [^runnels] cites this page.

## 2.jpg: HABS NH,5-ENFI.V,1A-12, Shaker Church Family Dwelling House, Enfield, west and south elevations, June 1978

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/nh0019.photos.104904p/ (www.loc.gov answered 403, a bot check, from here).
  The LoC record as mirrored on Wikimedia Commons, field by field:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WEST_AND_SOUTH_ELEVATIONS_-_Shaker_Church_Family_Dwelling_House,_State_Route_4A,_Enfield,_Grafton_County,_NH_HABS_NH,5-ENFI.V,1A-12.tif
  (Commons licence "Public domain", source https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/nh0019.photos.104904p). The mirrored LoC
  record for 1A-12 carries the rights advisory: "No known restrictions on images made by the U.S. Government; images
  copied from other sources may be restricted.(http://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/114_habs.html)".
- Downloaded from the Library of Congress itself:
  https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nh/nh0000/nh0019/photos/104904pu.tif (5000 x 3615).
- Who made it: the HABS index to photographs, https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nh/nh0000/nh0019/data/nh0019cap.pdf
  (read): "Jack Boucher, Photographer June 1978 ... NH-75-12 WEST AND SOUTH ELEVATIONS" (the 12 written by hand over
  an earlier 3; the film edge reads "NH-75-3", cropped away). Jack E. Boucher was the National Park Service's HABS staff
  photographer (died 2012), so this is a work of the U.S. Government: PD-US-gov. A photograph of a building needs the
  photographer's own free licence; a U.S. Government work has no copyright in the US. Licence: PD-US-gov.
- Building facts, HABS data pages https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nh/nh0000/nh0019/data/nh0019data.pdf
  (read): "Construction began in the spring of 1837 and the dwelling was dedicated and occupied June 8, 1841";
  "The large dwelling house was built to provide living quarters for all the male and female members of the Enfield
  Church Family"; sold to the Missionaries of Our Lady of La Salette on 3 January 1928 (so the cross on the cupola and the
  "Shaker Hall 1837" sign by the door are of the later owners' time; the Shakers' own date stone is in the west gable).
  Its name: the Enfield Shaker Museum calls it the Great Stone Dwelling
  (https://shakermuseum.org/discover/village-walking-tour/great-stone-dwelling/); HABS: "Shaker Great Stone House".
- Perkins and the family: Enfield Shaker Museum, "Elder Abraham Perkins" (the story's [^enfield]): "in 1845 he was
  appointed an associate Elder of the Church family"; "Abraham Perkins, Shaker school teacher, caretaker, medium, and
  Elder".
- Words in the photograph: "Shaker Hall" and a date on a small sign by the door. No people (checked at full size).

## 3.jpg: A Collection of Millennial Hymns (Canterbury, 1847), p. 100, hymn 73 "Choice of Pleasures", verse 4

- Item: https://archive.org/details/mill00shak (Princeton Theological Seminary Library copy), leaf n105 = printed page
  100 (seen). Downloaded https://archive.org/download/mill00shak/page/n105.jpg (2330 x 3256).
- Title page (seen, leaf n6): "A COLLECTION OF MILLENNIAL HYMNS, ADAPTED TO THE PRESENT ORDER OF THE CHURCH. ...
  PRINTED IN THE UNITED SOCIETY, CANTERBURY, N. H. 1847." Internet Archive record: creator "Shakers (East Canterbury,
  N.H.)", date 1847, "Without music".
- PD reasoning: printed 1847, so public domain in the US. No author is named: hymn 73 is signed only "Watervliet, N. Y."
  (p. 101, seen). An anonymous work published 178 years ago is out of copyright in the EU and Israel (70 years from
  publication) and in Mexico (100 years from publication). Licence: PD-old.
- Every printed word on the crop read: "4 The pleasures of earth are but transient and vain, / They flourish, and soon
  pass away, / While heavenly treasures the faithful do gain, / That never'll grow old or decay. / The garments of
  righteousness, goodness and peace, / That God's faithful servants do wear, / With brightness will shine, and with
  endless increase / Their crowns are most beautifully fair." Nothing that demeans anyone. Faint show-through of the
  facing page is not legible. No figures.
- Caption claims: the hymn's title (p. 99, "73. Choice of Pleasures."), its place (signed "Watervliet, N. Y."), the book,
  printer, place and year (title page), the quoted line (on the crop). The song's year, 1847, is from the story.
