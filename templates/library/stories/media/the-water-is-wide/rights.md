## 2.jpg: The Tea-Table Miscellany, vol. 2 (Dublin, 1729), p. 176, "Waly, waly, gin Love be bony"

- Item: https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-tea-table-miscellany_ramsay-allan-the-elde_1729_2
  (metadata saved as raw/ramsay1729-meta.json): title "The tea-table miscellany: or, a complete collection of Scots
  songs. ... 1729: Vol 2"; creator "[Ramsay, Allan, the elder]."; date "1729"; collections bim_microfilm, microfilm;
  source "IA40310710-80", "microfilm"; contributor "Internet Archive". The copy filmed is not named in the record.
  This is the scan our story cites ([^ramsay1729], ".../page/n67").
- Page: https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-tea-table-miscellany_ramsay-allan-the-elde_1729_2/page/n67
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/bim_eighteenth-century_the-tea-table-miscellany_ramsay-allan-the-elde_1729_2/page/n67.jpg
  (4173 x 7160), raw/ramsay1729-n67.jpg. The final is cropped (sips) to the page, removing the black film border and the
  edge of the facing page.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so public domain by age:
  - Printed Dublin 1729 ("printed for E. Smith", per the story's source note). Compiler Allan Ramsay died 1758; the song
    itself is anonymous.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1828 (Ramsay); an anonymous work's 70 years from publication ended in 1799.
  - A microfilm and a scan of it are faithful copies of a flat page and add no new copyright (as above). The same
    microfilm series (bim_eighteenth-century) was accepted for Amazing Grace (Olney Hymns, 1779) and Hark the Herald.
- What it shows: page number "( 176 )", the heading "Waly, waly, gin Love be bony.", and the song's first lines through
  "But my Love's Heart grown cauld to me.", with the catchword "When". It includes the oak verse ("I lean'd my Back unto
  an Aik ... But first it bow'd and syne it brak"), "fades away like Morning Dew", and "Now Arthur-Seat shall be my
  Bed" and "Saint Anton's Well shall be my Drink". Type only, with show-through from the back of the leaf. No figures,
  logos or marks. The words were read in full: a lament, with "O gentle Death, when wilt thou come"; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the page itself; the story (the Dublin edition of 1729 on page 176; the oak that "bow'd and syne it
  brak"; "fades away like morning dew").

## 3.jpg: George Washington Wilson, St. Anthony's Chapel, Queen's Park, Edinburgh, about 1850 to 1880

- Holder: Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam, RP-F-F04843. Object page:
  https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/object/RP-F-F04843--f718004373f6e08037c11879a1ebf849 ; Linked Art record
  https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200327263 (saved as raw/rijks/200327263.json).
- Record: title "St. Anthony's Chapel, Queen's Park, Edinburgh" (Dutch: "Gezicht op de ruïne van de St. Anthony's
  Chapel te Edinburgh"); "photographer: George Washington Wilson (mentioned on object), Edinburgh"; date "c. 1850 -
  c. 1880"; albumen print ("albuminedruk"); 85 x 170 mm; verso printed "Registered. G. W. Wilson, Photographer,
  Aberdeen." and "No. 527.".
- Rights statement (in the record): the image (digital object https://iiif.micr.io/hwLjx/full/max/0/default.jpg) is
  "referred_to_by" a statement labelled "Public Domain" with content
  "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/". The record's own metadata is under
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/.
- Downloaded: https://iiif.micr.io/hwLjx/full/max/0/default.jpg (4654 x 2414), raw/rijks-gww-st-anthony.jpg. It is a
  stereo card; the final is the left half only, cropped (sips) inside the yellow mount.
- Public domain by age, as well as the museum's mark:
  - Photograph made about 1850 to 1880 and sold as a registered stereo view. Photographer George Washington Wilson died
    1893.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU (UK) and Israel: life plus 70 ended 31 December 1963.
  - This is a photograph of a building, so it needs a free licence for the photograph itself: the photograph is the
    1850 to 1880 original, public domain by age and marked so by its holder. The museum's scan of a flat print adds no
    new copyright.
- What it shows: the ruin of St Anthony's Chapel on its rock ridge, with paths up the slope, the crag behind and flat
  ground and water below. Two or three tiny figures (visitors, a few pixels high) are on the paths; they are 19th-century
  people in a photograph, not drawn figures, and none is living. No logos or marks on the front.
- Caption facts: Historic Environment Scotland, scheduled monument SM13032, "Holyrood Park"
  (https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/designation/SM13032): "St Anthony's Chapel, a picturesque ruin perched on
  the crag above St Margaret's Loch, is of late medieval date" and "St Anthony's Well, on the slope leading up to the
  chapel". The story: the Scottish song "names Edinburgh landmarks, Arthur's Seat and Saint Anton's Well, a spring on
  the hill's side" [^chambers1862][^hes]; Ramsay 1729, p. 176, prints both names.
