# Pastores a Belén: licence evidence

Sourced 2026-11-13. Every image opened and looked at in full. No drawn, painted or engraved human figure appears in any
of the four. Every printed word on the three book pages was read: devotional text, song titles and a book advert
("que tenemos de venta en nuestra Librería"); nothing demeans any people or faith.

- PD by age:
  - Published in the United States in 1916 ("COPYRIGHT 1916, BY REVISTA CATOLICA" on the title page; the index page
    carries the copyright office stamp "JAN 11 1916" and "©Cl.A418409"). US: every work published before 1 January
    1931 is in the public domain (the 1916 copyright, renewed or not, ran at most 95 years, to the end of 2011).
  - Who made it: the song is unsigned; the note is unsigned (editors of the Revista Católica press, a corporate work).
    The Internet Archive record names "Ralliere, P." as creator/editor: Father Juan Bautista (J. B.) Ralliere died at
    Tomé on 18 July 1915, aged 83 (Florence Hawley Ellis, "Tomé and Father J. B. R." (continued), New Mexico Historical
    Review 30, no. 3, July 1955, p. 217, quoting a townswoman's memorandum book: "El dia 18 de Julio murió el Padre Juan
    B. Ralliere ... el año 1915" (OCR "murié", "afio" read as "murió", "año"); https://archive.org/details/sim_new-mexico-historical-review_1955-07_30_3).
    Ralliere's term (life plus 70) ended at the close of 1985. The one tune the book signs on the note's facing page,
    Trisagio 2, is "Lambillotte" (Louis Lambillotte, died 1855), and it is cropped out anyway.
  - EU (life plus 70; anonymous or corporate works 70 years from publication): ended by 1986 at the latest.
  - Israel (Copyright Act 2007: life plus 70; anonymous works 70 years from publication): ended by 1986 at the latest.
  - A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Article 14 of
    Directive 2019/790), and the library itself marks the scan PDM.

## 4.jpg: the Catholic church of Tomé, New Mexico (NPS photograph, 2014)

- Holder page: https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/599f7ff9-ac88-4349-a97a-aa64d65a9bb5 (fetched 2026-11-13).
  Exact words: "Title: A sidewalk leads to Tome Catholic Church in Tome, NM"; "Publisher: U.S. National Park
  Service"; "Constraints Information: Public domain"; "Photographer: National Trails Office US National Park
  Service"; "Create Date: 20140606"; "Locations: Tome, Valencia County, New Mexico". (The page's general boilerplate,
  "Permission must be secured from the individual copyright owners to reproduce any copyrighted materials contained
  within this website", is the site-wide notice; this asset's own constraint is "Public domain".)
- File: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/599f7ff9-ac88-4349-a97a-aa64d65a9bb5/original.jpg (2592 x 1936).
  The same file is on Wikimedia Commons as "File:A sidewalk leads to Tome Catholic Church in Tome, NM
  (599f7ff9-ac88-4349-a97a-aa64d65a9bb5).JPG", licence "Public domain", author "National Trails Office (US National
  Park Service)", credit NPGallery.
- Why it is free: a photograph made by staff of a US federal agency as part of their duties, so no US copyright
  (17 U.S.C. 105). It is a photograph of a building, so the photographer's own licence is what counts, and here the
  photographer is the agency. The building itself: US law gives no copyright in pictures of a building seen from a
  public place (17 U.S.C. 120(a)); the church is in any case far older than 1990.
- Caveat for the checker: US government works are PD in the US by statute; the US does not generally assert
  copyright in them abroad, but no foreign law makes that explicit. This is the "PD-US-gov" class the brief allows.
- People: the original frame shows one small figure, a person walking at the far left by the parish building. It is
  cropped out (crop x 450 to 1840, y 40 to 1936 of the original); no person is visible in 4.jpg. The picture
  shows no trademark or sign.
- Metadata: the original carried iPad EXIF and GPS. The BMP round trip removed it; 4.jpg holds only the sips JFIF/sRGB
  header (checked with sips -g all and strings).
- Caption facts come from the story: Ralliere was parish priest of Tomé by 1859 ([^salpointe1898], [^ellis1955a]).
  Not claimed in the caption: that this is the building he knew. Kessell (The Missions of New Mexico since 1776,
  1980, NPS History copy) records that the nave and facade collapsed in a 1920 flood and were rebuilt, and that the
  church was towerless from 1975, so today's towers are later work.

## Rejected

- Bird's-eye view of Las Vegas, N.M., 1882 (J. J. Stoner; Beck & Pauli; LoC G&M 75694741, PD): it shows the Las Vegas
  of the Revista Católica well, but at full size it carries tiny drawn human figures in the streets and plaza.
  Dropped under the no-drawn-people rule.
- "Rays of sunshine illuminate the Tome Catholic Church" (NPGallery 55f0cb72-..., PD): strong lens glare, and the
  sidewalk view shows the same church better.
- Historic photographs of the Tomé church (Kessell 1980, fig. 131, "probably about 1900"): no credit or rights given
  in the copy seen; the 1980 book is "All rights reserved". Not used.
- The 1916 index page (image n1), "Para la Noche Buena": fine on rights, left out as a fourth page of the same book.
