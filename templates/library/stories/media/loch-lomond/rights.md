# Loch Lomond: rights evidence for each image

Checked 2026-11-13. Every image was opened and looked at, the photographs zoomed along their shores: no drawn, painted
or engraved human figures, no people at all, no living people, no logos. Every word on both printed pages was read:
nothing mocks or demeans any people or faith (the pages are Jacobite song verses and notes on authorship).

## 1.jpg: Brown's Book-Stall, No. 75, December 1911, p. 7 ("Bonnie Loch Loman")

- Item: https://archive.org/details/brownsbookstalln02aber (University of Guelph Library, call number
  "DA 890.A2 B7 v. 2", scanned by the Internet Archive, Toronto, 2013). Page 7 is image n666:
  https://archive.org/details/brownsbookstalln02aber/page/n666/mode/1up
- Downloaded from https://archive.org/download/brownsbookstalln02aber/page/n666.jpg (2917 x 3789); the full page is used.
- Item record (archive.org metadata): title "Brown's bookstall. no. 1-77"; publisher "Aberdeen, Scotland : A. Brown";
  date "1892-1913"; "possible-copyright-status": "In the Public Domain"; contributor "University of Guelph Library".
- Issue date: the issue's masthead in the item's OCR text reads "No. 75. DECEMBER. 1911." just before the article
  heading "ABOUT SOME \"AULD SANGS.\"" (images n660 to n666).
- Public domain by age: published December 1911.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - The article is unsigned (anonymous): 70 years from publication, ended 31 December 1981 in the EU and Israel. The
    collector it quotes, Gavin Greig, died 1914 (life plus 70 ended 1984). The reprinted sheet "Bonnie Loch Loman"
    dates from about 1840 and is anonymous ("written by a lady").
- A scan of a flat public-domain page: no new copyright in the photograph (and EU DSM Directive art. 14).
- What the page shows (matches the story): "As far as is known, this is the parent text of the song", the seven verses
  of "BONNIE LOCH LOMAN" with chorus, signed "Published by Paterson & Roy, 27 George Street, Edinburgh"; in the right
  column "Its latest form, that in use by singers now, consists of verses 1, 2, and 7", and the verses Lady Scott said
  "she and her husband picked up from the singing of a little boy in the streets of Edinburgh". The preceding page
  (p. 5, image n664) quotes Greig: "The first appearance of the song in print was about 1840, when it was published
  [by Messrs. Paterson & Roy, Edinburgh]".

- The page shows what the story quotes: "LOCH LOMOND.", "Old Scottish Song.", "Traditional Melody arranged by

## Not used

- The first printing itself, "Bonnie Loch Loman" (Paterson & Roy, [1840 or 1841]), is catalogued by the Morgan
  Library (https://themorgan.org/node/1257396, per a search snippet: first printing, "not in fact included in any of
  the four volumes of Vocal melodies of Scotland"). The page refused automated access (HTTP 403) and no scan or rights
  statement could be seen, so it is not used. No digitised copy of the sheet or of The Vocal Melodies of Scotland
  page was found (Internet Archive's "Vocal melodies of Scotland" items are Nathaniel Gow's different 1816 to 1820
  collection). Worth asking the Morgan for if the lead wants it.
- Songs of the North p. 12 (image n31), the words page: fine, but the music page carries more (title, "Old Scottish
  Song", Lawson, tempo); one page from the book is enough.
- Rijksmuseum stereographs of Loch Lomond (RP-F-F04875 banks with trees, RP-F-2016-122 islands): Public Domain Mark,
  but weaker pictures than the two album prints.
- Bonington's 1826 lithograph and Le Blond's Baxter print of Ben Lomond (Met; isPublicDomain false), and Frances
  Waring's 1803 print (Rijksmuseum): prints, not photographs, and some with figures.
- Library of Congress photochroms: not needed, since real photographs with no people were found.
