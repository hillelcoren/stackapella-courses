## 1.jpg: a great cherry tree in flower, hand-coloured albumen print, about 1870 to 1900

- Item: Rijksmuseum RP-F-F01177-J, "Grote kersenbloesemboom van Mukaijima in Kyoto", title on object "Mukaijima
  daishu no sakura". Object type: photo; "ingekleurde albuminedruk" (hand-coloured albumen print), height 210 mm x
  width 267 mm. Maker: "photographer: anonymous, Kyoto". Date: "ca. 1870 - ca. 1900". Part of "Leporelloalbum met 30
  foto's van bezienswaardigheden in Japan". Inscription: "negative number: '39'".
- Holder's record: http://hdl.handle.net/10934/RM0001.COLLECT.276388 (redirects to https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200345813;
  saved as raw/rijks-F01177-J.html, Linked Art JSON).
- Rights statement, exact from the record: the digital image's "subject_to" right is
  "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/" (CC0); the object is referred to as "_label": "Public Domain",
  "content": "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/". Wikimedia Commons' copy says the same ("CC0",
  "Creative Commons Zero, Public Domain Dedication", Credit http://hdl.handle.net/10934/RM0001.COLLECT.276388), but the
  final was made from the Rijksmuseum's own file: https://iiif.micr.io/zvodw/full/max/0/default.jpg (5146 x 4004),
  saved as raw/rijks-RP-F-F01177-J.jpg.
- Public domain reasoning, beside CC0: photograph made about 1870 to 1900 by an anonymous photographer. US: an album
  print sold to travellers, published before 1931: PD. EU: anonymous work, 70 years from being made available,
  long over. Israel: under the 1911 Act as in force, photographs had 50 years from the making of the negative, so
  free by 1950 at the latest; free works stay free. Mexico: no text or music on it; a photograph from before 1900.
- People: none. Looked at the whole print at full size in four quarters (work/rk-*.jpg): trunks, branches, blossom
  and sky only. No drawn figures, logos or watermarks.
- Words: the final is cropped to the photograph, so the mount's printed label is not shown. The label reads, right
  to left, 向島大樹ノ櫻: 向島 (a place name, Mukaijima or Mukōjima), 大樹 (great tree), ノ (of), 櫻 (cherry): "the
  great cherry tree of 向島". The only mark left on the image is the negative number scratched in the lower left,
  "No 39" (looks like "N.r 39"). The mount's pencil number "F01177-J" is cropped away.
- Caveat for the checker: the Rijksmuseum reads 向島 as Mukaijima in Kyoto (a district of Fushimi). The same
  characters are also Mukōjima, Tokyo's famous cherry embankment on the Sumida. The caption therefore names no city
  and quotes the holder's reading, "Mukaijima", as the label.
- Caption facts: the date, process and label are the holder's; "the blossom the song calls everyone out to see" is
  the story's own first paragraph ([^sokyokushu][^tsuge]).

  JAPAN"; item record "rights":{"code":"pdm"}, "permission":{"rule":"internet"}. NDL's reuse page
  (https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/use/reproduction/index.html): "Content for which the Use status is インターネット公開

- Public domain reasoning: published in Tokyo in 1888 by the Ministry of Education. US: published before 1931, PD.
  Japan: a work in an organisation's name, 50 years (then 70) from publication, long over. EU and Israel: life plus
  70 at most (see the makers below); the words and tune are traditional or anonymous.
- Makers and Mexico (life plus 100). The English preface (frame 21) names the compilers: "under the superintendence

  NDL authority 00724204 gives 1858-1943, inside Mexico's term. I have not confirmed the other five musicians'

  - Decision: both crops show only Sakura, the song this page sings, words, tune and its koto staff as the 1888 book

    protected in Mexico, the song itself would be too.

  - For the lead: the song file's "makers" is empty. If the geo rules treat the six 1888 music selectors as makers
    of Sakura's printing, that is a song-level question, not an image one.

- Item: "The music and musical instruments of Japan", creators "Piggott, Francis Taylor, Sir, 1852-1925" and
  "Southgate, T. L. (Thomas Lea), 1836-1917" (notes), London: B.T. Batsford, 1893. Copy: New York Public Library
  (IA "contributor": "New York Public Library", call number 1408877). Item https://archive.org/details/musicmusicalinst00pigg2
  (the story's [^piggott]); page https://archive.org/details/musicmusicalinst00pigg2/page/n150/mode/1up; file
  https://archive.org/download/musicmusicalinst00pigg2/page/n150.jpg (2117 x 3090), saved as raw/view-pigg-n150.jpg.
- Rights: IA metadata "possible-copyright-status": "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT", "copyright-evidence": "no visible notice of
  copyright; stated date is 1893." PD by age: published 1893; Piggott died 1925, Southgate 1917. US: PD (before
  1931). EU and Israel: life plus 70, ended by 1995. Mexico: life plus 100 from 1925 ended at the close of 2025, so PD
  from 1 January 2026. Faithful scan of a flat page adds no copyright.
- Who made the music on the page: Piggott writes that Japanese musicians transposed melodies for him; the tune is a
  koto beginners' piece with no named author. No one on the page died after 1925 as far as the book shows.
- Every printed word: "( 107 )"; "SAITA SAKURAI."; "In Hirajōshi (F♯ minor). [Written an octave higher than the
  Koto.]"; "In Kumoi (B minor). [Written to actual notes of the Koto.]"; "In Iwato (E minor). [Written an octave higher

- Crop: page margins trimmed (from y 230, x 110, 1900 x 2600); nothing printed is cut.
- Caption facts: story, "The older piece": "Saita Sakurai" as a beginners' koto piece (Piggott p. 67), and "Its tune,
  printed on the staff, opens like Sakura ... and then goes its own way" (p. 107).

## Looked at and not used

- Frame 22's Hime-matsu and the tuning chart, and frame 23's Hana-kurabe and Hotaru: cropped out (Mexico, above).
- Frame 6, the words in brush script with "旧 咲た桜" (formerly Saita sakura): the cursive could not be read
  character by character with certainty, and the Sakura words run across both pages with No. 1 and No. 3 beside them.
- Frame 20, the English title page: carries a library stamp and a revenue-type label with a small picture; not needed.
- The Met's koto photographs (Open Access, CC0: 500669 Yamada Koto, 500576 Koto): composite museum views or a tiny
  636 px file; and the one-photograph rule kept the cherry tree instead.
- Surimono and bird-and-flower prints (e.g. Met JP2319 Eisen, JP2005 Shunman): their poems are in cursive I cannot
  read in full, and cherry-blossom prints nearly always show people.
- Other Rijksmuseum cherry photographs: most show people (e.g. RP-F-2003-147-96, Arashiyama with strollers); the
  colour flower plates RP-F-2001-7-1557C-1 and 1575-3 are studio collotypes, less tied to the song than a tree.
- Uta no hon (1941): its words are the Ministry's, anonymous; not clearly out of Mexico's term (100 years from 1941
  would run to 2041); not used.
