## 4.jpg: Hymns Ancient and Modern, Revised Edition, Hymn 49, p. 35 (top half)

- Item: Internet Archive hymnsancientmode00monk, contributor "Harold B. Lee Library", sponsor "Brigham Young
  University", call_number "DEF5698", IA date "1874", IA publisher "New York : Pott, Young & Co." (the title page,
  read in the OCR raw/hamrev_djvu.txt, says "LONDON: PRINTED FOR THE PROPRIETORS BY WILLIAM CLOWES AND SONS,
  LIMITED"). This is the story's cited copy for the words we sing, [^hamrev].
  Page: https://archive.org/details/hymnsancientmode00monk/page/n50/mode/1up ; file .../page/n50.jpg (2623 x 4277),
  saved as raw/hamrev_n50.jpg; the page number "( 35 )" was checked by eye on the full page. Final is the top 1960
  pixels: the last line of Hymn 49's music and its five verses; Hymn 50 below is cropped away.
- No rights statement on the IA record, so PD by age:
  - The Revised Edition's preface is dated 21 January 1875 ([^ham1875]); this printing is undated (catalogued 1874,
    though it is a printing of the 1875 Revised Edition, under the imprint "William Clowes and Sons, Limited"); it is
    a nineteenth-century letterpress book by its type and imprint, in any case before 1931. The book's own note that hymns marked * are "copyright of the Compilers" is long expired.
  - Words: Neale (died 1866) as altered by the compilers, whose editor was H. W. Baker (died 1877); harmony and
    music editor W. H. Monk (died 1889). US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended at the end of
    1959 for the last named; anything treated as anonymous or the compilers' collective work ran 70 years from
    publication, long ended.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page (as above).
- What it shows: printed music, "A-men", the text "The Redeemer shall come to Zion", five verses with dynamics. A
  plain large initial "O", no figures, no logos, no watermark. Every word read: "Thine own from Satan's tyranny",
  "From depths of hell Thy people save"; "Israel" here is the hymn's own word for the waiting people. Nothing that
  demeans a people or faith.
- Caption facts: story, "What the words mean": "We sing the Revised Edition's words" ([^hamrev][^julian]); "How it
  is sung": the Revised Edition printed "mf at the start of each verse, p for 'That mourns in lonely exile here' and
  ff for every refrain" ([^hamrev]); "The Revised Edition, its preface dated 21 January 1875, made it No. 49"
  ([^ham1875][^julian][^hamrev]). "To be sung in Unison" is on p. 34 and is not in this crop, so the caption does not
  mention it.
