## 1.jpg and 3.jpg: Jubilee Songs (New York: Biglow & Main, 1872), cover and p. 6

- Item: Jubilee songs: as sung by the Jubilee singers of Fisk university (Nashville, Tenn.) under the auspices of the
  American Missionary Association. New York: Biglow & Main, 1872. Internet Archive identifier
  29208201.4853.emory.edu, contributor "Emory University, Manuscript, Archives and Rare Book Library", barcode
  010000882754, OCLC 29208201. This is the story's cited scan ([^seward1872]). Metadata saved as
  raw/jubilee1872_meta.json.
  - Cover: https://archive.org/download/29208201.4853.emory.edu/page/n1.jpg (image n1).
  - Page 6: https://archive.org/download/29208201.4853.emory.edu/page/n8.jpg (image n8).
- Rights statement, exact words in the IA record's description field: "The online edition of this book in the public
  domain, i.e., not protected by copyright, has been produced by the Emory University Digital Library Publications
  Program". The rights field is empty; no licence restriction stated.
- Public domain by age:
  - Published New York, 1872. Makers per the IA creator headings: "Seward, Theodore F. (Theodore Frelinghuysen),
    1835-1902" (music) and "White, George L. (George Leonard), 1838-1895" (words taken down). The songs themselves
    are traditional, makers unknown.
  - US: published before 1931, public domain.
  - EU and Israel (life plus 70): Seward's term ended at the end of 1972, White's at the end of 1965. Publisher's
    typography of 1872 is long out of any term.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 DSM Directive).
- What they show, read word by word:
  - Cover: type and a ruled border only; the words are the title, "as sung by the Jubilee Singers, of Fisk
    University, (Nashville, Tenn.) under the auspices of the American Missionary Association", "Price 25 cents" and
    the publisher's New York and Chicago addresses. A faint illegible pencil note at the top edge. No figures, no
    ornament with people, no watermark.
  - Page 6: running head "6 Jubilee Songs.", title "Swing Low, sweet Chariot." and the music with refrain and four
    verses in standard spelling ("Coming for to carry me home"). No dialect, no caricature, nothing else on the page.
    Passes the minstrel-stage rule.
- Caption facts: story paragraph "The Fisk singers" ([^seward1872], [^balladindex]): page 6, "the earliest known
  printing of the song with its music"; the words taken down by White, the music from the singers' own singing (Seward's
  preface); the lower staff silent under "Swing low, sweet chariot" and each verse's opening line, every part coming in
  on "Coming for to carry me home" (visible on the page itself).

## 2.jpg: Jubilee Singers, Fisk University, Nashville, Tenn. (carte de visite, 1870s)

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/item/2010647805/ (Wayback capture 2025-12-15,
  http://web.archive.org/web/20251215185604/https://www.loc.gov/item/2010647805/). LCCN 2010647805; call number
  "LOT 14022, no. 75 [P&P]"; reproduction number "LC-DIG-ppmsca-11008 (digital file from original item, front)";
  Gladstone Collection of African American Photographs (purchase from William A. Gladstone, 1995).
  File: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/ppmsca/11000/11008u.tif (front only).
- Record, exact words: "Photo shows a studio group portrait of the Fisk University Jubilee Singers, with (from l. to
  r.) Minnie Tate, Greene Evans, Isaac Dickerson, Jennie Jackson, Maggie Porter, Ella Sheppard, Thomas Rutling,
  Benjamin Holmes, and Eliza Walker." Names: "Black, James Wallace, 1825-1896, photographer"; "American Missionary
  Association, publisher". Created/published "[Nashville, Tenn.] : American Missionary Association, [between 1870
  and 1880]". Note: "Photographer attribution from similar photograph: LOT 14105, no. 8." Medium: "1 photographic
  print on carte de visite mount : albumen ; 6 x 10 cm."
- Rights, exact words: "Rights Advisory: No known restrictions on publication."
- Public domain by age as well: an 1870s photograph, published as a carte de visite by the AMA. US: published before
  1931. EU and Israel: the attributed photographer James Wallace Black died 1896, so life plus 70 ended in 1966; were
  it anonymous, 70 years from making ended by 1950.
- Spirituals rule: a dignified studio portrait of the Fisk singers in formal dress, made for the singers' own
  publisher; sitters photographed some 145 years ago, all long dead. Not a photograph of enslaved people.
  No caricature, no text on the front of the card. The final keeps the whole card front as scanned (the back, with
  Gladstone's ownership mark, LC-DIG-ppmsca-11009, is not used).
- Caption facts: the LoC record (names, date); story paragraph "Another origin story" ([^work1915], [^pdr]): Work names
  the baby as Ella Sheppard, "later one of the first Jubilee Singers".

## 4.jpg: Jubilee Hall, Fisk University (HABS photograph, 1970)

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/item/tn0017/ (Wayback capture 2023-04-06,
  http://web.archive.org/web/20230406151910/https://www.loc.gov/item/tn0017/). Title "Fisk University, Jubilee Hall,
  Seventeenth Avenue, North, Nashville, Davidson County, TN"; survey number HABS TN-19; call number "HABS
  TENN,19-NASH,7A-"; photo file 152882p,
  https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/tn/tn0000/tn0017/photos/152882pu.tif.
- Photo index (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/tn/tn0000/tn0017/data/tn0017cap.pdf, saved as
  raw/habs_tn0017cap.pdf), exact words: "Jack E. Boucher, Photographer August 1970"; "TN-19-1 South front." The
  file is TN-19-1 (the negative's own edge marking reads "HABS #TENN-19"). Commons holds the same file
  ("South front - Fisk University, Jubilee Hall ... HABS TENN,19-NASH,7A-1.tif", credit link
  https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/tn0017.photos.152882p), categorised "PD US NPS", author Jack Boucher, 1970.
- Rights, exact words: "No known restrictions on images made by the U.S. Government; images copied from other sources
  may be restricted." with https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/114_habs.html. This is an original HABS survey photograph
  made by the survey's photographer for the National Park Service, not a copy of an outside photograph: a work of the
  US government, 17 U.S.C. 105. A photo of a building, so the brief's 3D rule is met by the photographer's own
  (government) public-domain status.
- What it shows: the building, lawn, drive, trees, a street lamp, and a small light-coloured marker far right in the
  distance. No people, no signs legible, no trademark. The final crops away the film's black border and the
  "HABS #TENN-19" negative marking (crop 4500 x 3280 from x 200, y 220 of the master).
- Caption facts:
  - Tennessee Encyclopedia, "Jubilee Singers of Fisk University" (the story's [^tnenc]): "had raised nearly fifty
    thousand dollars for Fisk University's first permanent structure"; "The imposing Victorian Gothic six-story
    building, named in honor of the Jubilee Singers, was dedicated on January 1, 1876."
  - HABS record significance note: "the handsome building was completed in 1876 with part of the money made by the
    first group of Jubilee Singers."

## Considered and not used

- Hampton and Its Students (1874), p. 179 (story's [^hampton1874]): its texts are printed in dialect spellings
  ("Oh de good ole chariot swing so low"); rejected under the spirituals note.
- The Galaxy, October 1871 (Pollard's essay, story's [^galaxy]): a title-only mention in a magazine essay, not a
  fit image; its page was not used or checked for wording.
- Marsh, The Story of the Jubilee Singers (1880): its plates are engravings of people; not used.
- Spencer Academy: images would come from the Oklahoma Historical Society, which may not be linked.
- The 1909 Victor disc label: carries the Victor trademark; not used.
