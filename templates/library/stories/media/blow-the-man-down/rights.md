## 1.jpg: George's Dock, Liverpool, glass stereo slide, 1860 to 1890 (Rijksmuseum RP-F-F08488)

- Item: Rijksmuseum RP-F-F08488, persistent id https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200339619 ; object page
  https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/RP-F-F08488 . Found by the museum's search API
  (https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/search/collection?title=Liverpool&type=foto, saved as pages/rijks_s1.json).
- Records saved: Dublin Core pages/rijks/200339619.xml (https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/200339619?_profile=dc) and Linked
  Art pages/rijks/200339619_la.json.
- Record fields (exact): title "Gezicht op George's Dock in Liverpool"; date "1860 - 1890"; creator "anonymous"
  ("photographer: anonymous, Liverpool"); object types "stereoglasdia" / "stereoscopic photograph", "glas",
  "diapositief (techniek)"; size "height 85 mm x width 170 mm"; printed inscription "Les Docks et Liverpool
  (instantanée), Angleterre" and "number: '8382'" ("nummer, recto, afgedrukt"); rights
  "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/" (Public Domain Mark).
- Downloaded: https://iiif.micr.io/jZHaO/full/max/0/default.jpg (the record's image relation), 6558 x 3284, saved as
  raw/rijks_RP-F-F08488.jpg. The final is the left frame of the stereo pair only (crop 2515 x 2241 from x 765, y 574),
  leaving out the slide's mount, the handwritten "7" and the printed French title strip.
- Licence: PD-old (the museum marks it Public Domain Mark 1.0).
- Reasoning:
  - An anonymous photograph made between 1860 and 1890, sold as a numbered commercial stereo slide (No. 8382).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain; even if treated as unpublished, an anonymous work's term is 120 years
    from creation (1890 + 120 = 2010), long ended.
  - EU: an anonymous work is protected 70 years from lawful publication or creation, which ended by 1960 at the latest.
  - Israel: photographs taken before the 2007 law keep the old term (50 years from the negative); and anonymous works
    run 70 years from publication. Both long ended.
  - A glass slide is a flat photographic positive; the museum's digital image of it adds no new copyright.
- What it shows and every word in the crop: the shed's signs "GEORGE'S DOCK" and "TO THE GEORGES LANDING STAGE"; a
  small notice on the shed and faded painted signs on the warehouse walls are illegible even at full size (zoom checked,
  work/z1.jpg). Sailing ships' masts and rigging on the left; carts, horses and people on foot in the street. People
  appear only as photographed passers-by, no drawn figures, no trademark, no watermark.
- Caption claims: Liverpool as the Black Ball packets' port from New York: the story's [^amereras] and [^rpsl]
  ("regular sailings between New York and Liverpool"); the song set in Liverpool, blowing the man down: Whall p. 92 ("O
  blow the man down in Liverpool town!", "They gave me three months in Walton Jail For booting and kicking and blowing
  him down."). Date and object type: the museum record. The caption does not claim the Black Ball ships used George's
  Dock.

## 2.jpg: Whall, Ships, Sea Songs and Shanties, third edition (1913), p. 92

- Item: https://archive.org/details/shipsseasongssha00whal (scan image n111, printed page 92; the story's
  [^whallscan] cites "scan leaves 112 and 113", the same page counted from 1).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/shipsseasongssha00whal/page/n111.jpg (2444 x 3404), saved as
  raw/whall_n111.jpg. The final is the whole page.
- IA record (pages/whall_meta.json): title "Ships, sea songs and shanties"; creator "Whall, W. B", "Whall, R. H";
  publisher "Glasgow, J. Brown & son"; date 1913; contributor and sponsor "Smithsonian Libraries"; call number
  "39088006794259".
- Rights statement, Smithsonian Libraries and Archives item page
  https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/shipsseasongssha00whal (saved as pages/silib_whall.html), exact words:
  "CC0 - Creative Commons (CC0 1.0) This content is in the public domain (free of copyright restrictions). You can copy,
  modify, and distribute this work without contacting the Smithsonian." and "No Copyright - United States". (The page
  prints an em dash after "CC0"; written here with a hyphen.)
- Licence: CC0.
- Also public domain by age, for the EU and Israel:
  - Printed Glasgow 1913. The title page (pages/whall_djvu.txt) reads "Collected by W. B. WHALL, Master Mariner",
    "The Songs harmonised by R. H. WHALL, Mus.Bac., F.R.C.O.", "Illustrations by VERONICA WHALL".
  - Page 92 carries only W. B. Whall's headnote and the traditional tune and words: no harmony (R. H. Whall's part)
    and no drawing (Veronica Whall's part; her drawings were not considered, as her death year was not checked).
  - W. B. Whall had died by 1919 (the story's [^mence], the 1919 preface to Practical Seamanship). Life + 70 ended by
    the end of 1989 in the EU and Israel. US: published before 1931.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Every word on the page, read: the heading "Blow the Man Down."; the headnote "This comes from the old Atlantic
  sailing packet ships. "Blow" in those days was equivalent to "knock." The third mate in those ships was endearingly
  termed the third "blower and striker," the second mate being the "greaser.""; the music marks "Brisk.", "Solo.",
  "Chorus." and the sung words; the five verses as the story prints them (Paradise Street, the Black Baller, the
  "poor Dutchman", the "Flying Fish sailor", Walton Jail); "There was another set of words to this shanty, which went
  as follows:-"; the verses "Come all you young fellows that follow the sea, ..." and "T'was aboard a Black Baller I
  first served my time, ..."; page number "92".
  - Nothing demeans a people or faith. Two words for the checker to weigh: "greaser" is here sailors' slang for the
    second mate, not the ethnic slur it later became in America; and the "poor Dutchman" is the robbery's victim in the
    policeman's accusation, a verse the story already describes. Neither mocks anyone, so the page is used whole;
    cropping the headnote would lose the "blow" = "knock" line the story rests on.
- Caption claims: the version we sing, p. 92: the story ("These are the words in W. B. Whall's ... third edition
  (1913), pages 92 and 93"); "from the old Atlantic packet ships" and "blow" meant "knock": the headnote on this page.

## 3.jpg: Laura Alexandrine Smith, The Music of the Waters (1888), p. 18

- Item: https://archive.org/details/cu31924022400059 (scan image n53, printed page 18; found from page 5 at n40).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/cu31924022400059/page/n53.jpg (1531 x 2450), saved as raw/smith_n53.jpg. The
  final is the whole page.
- IA record (pages/smith_meta.json): title "The music of the waters. A collection of the sailors' chanties, or working
  songs of the sea, of all maritime nations. ..."; creator "Smith, Laura Alexandrine"; publisher "London, Kegan Paul,
  French & Co." (sic); date 1888; contributor "Cornell University Library"; call number "ML3780 .S65"; no rights field.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London 1888. Author Laura Alexandrine Smith: Wikipedia
    (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Alexandrine_Smith): "Laura Alexandrine Smith was born in Newcastle upon Tyne in
    1861." and "Laura Alexandrine Smith died on 7 June 1902 in Whitechapel, London." (citing the England and Wales
    birth index and the Government Probate Death Index). Even without the death date, someone born in 1861 and
    publishing in 1888 could not plausibly have lived past 1955.
  - The tune and words are traditional; Smith's notation and prose are hers.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 (1902 + 70) ended 31 December 1972. The UK typographical-arrangement right (25 years)
    ended in 1913.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Every word on the page, read: running head "18 MUSIC OF THE WATERS."; the paragraph "One of the best and jolliest
  quick-time songs, and certainly one of the most well-known, is "Blow the Man Down." It is very tuneful, and though,
  perhaps, the words are scarcely to be admired, still it is a genuine chanty, and has a verve and vigour about it that
  speak of its value as an incentive to the labour of hoisting the topsail-yards or any other hauling work:-"; the
  music marked "Solo." and "Chorus." with the words "I'm a true English sailor, Just come from Hong-Kong, Tibby,- Heigh,
  ho, blow the man down! My stay on the old English shore won't be long, Then give me some time to blow the man down."
  and "Then we'll blow the man up, and we'll blow the man down, Tibby! Heigh, ho, blow the man down! So we'll blow the
  man up, and we'll blow the man down! Then give me some time to blow the man down."; and "Solo.-As I was a-walking down
  Winchester Street- Heigh-ho, blow the man down; A pretty young girl I happened to meet, Oh, give me some time to blow
  the man down. Chorus.-So we'll blow the man up, and we'll blow the man down, Heigh-ho, blow the man down." Nothing
  demeans anyone; no figures or decoration.
- Caption claims: all on this page ("one of the most well-known", "hoisting the topsail-yards", "Just come from
  Hong-Kong"); the story's lead paragraph and [^smith] agree (1888, with its tune, one of the most well-known).

## Considered and rejected

- The Black Ball packet Montezuma (with the black ball on her fore-topsail), and the England and the Yorkshire, plates
  facing pp. 40, 44 and 48 of Arthur H. Clark, The Clipper Ship Era (1910), Cornell copy cu31924020891416, scan images
  n66, n72, n78. Rejected: halftones of paintings or lithographs whose artist and date the book does not give, so the
  EU and Israeli terms cannot be shown; tiny painted figures on deck; and the brief asks for photographs. The lead could
  revisit the Montezuma if the original lithograph's maker is found.
- Rijksmuseum RP-F-F01212-20-2, Waterloo grain warehouses in Liverpool (1878 to 1890, PDM): a faint "Frith's series"
  style stamp in a corner, and the dock is shown with barges, not deep-sea ships; the George's Dock slide says more.
- Other Rijksmuseum Liverpool items (adverts, the Exchange, Lime Street, hotels, an elephant carte-de-visite): off the
  story.
- No photograph of a New York Black Ball packet with a stated free licence was found; the ones in search results were
  stock-agency prints (Granger, Heritage Images) or Royal Museums Greenwich items with no free licence shown.
- Whall's ship drawings (by Veronica Whall, death year not checked): not considered.
