## 1.jpg: Allan Cunningham, The Songs of Scotland, vol. 3 (London, 1825), p. 256

- Item: "The songs of Scotland, ancient and modern; with an introduction and notes, historical and critical, and
  characters of the lyric poets. In four volumes", vol. 3 (London: Printed for John Taylor, 1825). Contributor
  University of California Libraries, call number SRLF_UCLA:LAGE-1057086. Item
  https://archive.org/details/songsofscotlanda03cunn ; file https://archive.org/download/songsofscotlanda03cunn/page/256.jpg
  (1680 x 2854), raw/cunn-256.jpg. This is the story's [^cunningham1825] copy and page.
- Rights: IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/songsofscotlanda03cunn/metadata) "possible-copyright-status:
  NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". PD by age: printed 1825; Allan Cunningham (1784 to 1842); the old verses said to be by Douglas
  of Fingland (about 1700); the note quotes C. K. Sharpe (1781 to 1851). US: published before 1931. EU, UK, Israel
  (life+70) and Mexico (life+100): all authors died by 1851. A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new
  copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive; UK: IPO 2015 guidance).
- Words on the page, all read: "256 SCOTTISH SONGS.", "ANNIE LAURIE.", verse 1 ("Maxwelltown banks are bonnie ...
  I'd lay down my head and die."), verse 2 ("She's backet like a peacock, She's breasted like a swan, She's jimp about
  the middle, Her waist you weel may span ... And she has a rolling eye"), and the note: "I found this song in the
  little 'Ballad Book,' collected and edited by a gentleman to whom Scottish literature is largely indebted, Charles
  Kirkpatrick Sharpe of Hoddam. It is accompanied by the following notice: 'Sir Robert Laurie, first Baronet of the
  Maxwelton family (created 27th March 1685), by his second wife, a daughter of Riddell of Minto, had three sons and"
  (continues on p. 257, not used). An admiring love song; nothing mocks anyone. No figures, stamps or watermarks.
- Caption facts: Cunningham reprinted it from Sharpe in 1825 (story "The old song", [^cunningham1825][^graham], and the
  page itself); the peacock verse she replaced and "a book of Allan Cunningham's" (story "Lady John Scott's song" and
  "What the words mean", [^warrender1911][^ross1910][^moffat]). The caption does not claim this is the copy she read.

## 2.jpg: G. F. Graham, The Songs of Scotland, vol. 3 (Edinburgh, 1848 to 1849), p. 25

- Item: "The songs of Scotland, adapted to their appropriate melodies" (Edinburgh: Wood and Co., IA date 1848), 3 v.
  in 1. Contributor University of Toronto, Faculty of Music Library, call number M1746 .G47 S6. Item
  https://archive.org/details/songsofscotlanda00grah ; file https://archive.org/download/songsofscotlanda00grah/page/n386.jpg
  (raw/graham-n386.jpg). Page 25 of vol. 3 (page 24, n385, carries the first half of the song headed "Arranged by
  T. M. Mudie"). This is the story's [^graham] copy; the story cites "pp. 24 to 25 ... and note".
- Rights: IA record has no rights or licence field (https://archive.org/metadata/songsofscotlanda00grah/metadata).
  PD by age: printed 1848 to 1849; George Farquhar Graham (1789 to 1867), editor and author of the note; Thomas
  Molleson Mudie (1809 to 1876), arranger; Lady John Scott (died 1900), words and air; the note quotes Robert Chambers
  (died 1871), Allan Cunningham (died 1842) and C. K. Sharpe (died 1851). US: published before 1931. EU, UK, Israel
  (life+70) and Mexico (life+100): every author dead by 1900. Faithful scan of a flat page, as above.
- Words on the page, all read: "ANNIE LAURIE. 25", the sung end of verse 1 ("bon-nie An-nie Lau-rie, I'd lay me down
  and dee."), "For first two verses.", "For last verse.", verses 2 and 3 ("Her brow is like the snaw-drift, Her neck is
  like the swan ... Like dew on the gowan lying ... And she's a' the world to me"), and the note: Chambers on the old
  verses "said to have been written about 150 years ago" by "Mr. Douglas of Fingland, upon Anne, one of the four
  daughters of Sir Robert Laurie"; "It is painful to record that ... he did not obtain the heroine for a wife: she was
  married to Mr. Ferguson of Craigdarroch"; the peacock stanza "evidently borrowed ... from a stanza, not quotable, in
  an old version of 'John Anderson, my Jo.'"; "The air of Annie Laurie is quite modern, having been composed by Lady

  stanza is not printed. No figures or ornaments beyond a wavy rule; no stamps.
- Caption facts: "quite modern" and "Lady J" "S" "t" with dashes (page; story "Printed without her name", [^graham]);
  late 1840s (story; IA 1848); arranged by T. M. Mudie (p. 24 heading, same song, same copy).

  The Craigdarroch Castle files are a different place (Victoria, Canada). No photo used.
