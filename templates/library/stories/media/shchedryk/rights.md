## 1.jpg: Kyiv's Merchants' Assembly, photochrom, about 1890 to 1900

- Item: "[Commercial Club, Kiev, Russia, (i.e., Ukraine)]", 1 photomechanical print: photochrom, color. Print no.
  "8967". Printed label in the lower margin: "8967. P. Z. Kiew. Le Club de Commerce." (left) and "8967. Kiev.
  Kupecheskoe sobranie" (right, in pre-1918 Russian spelling: "Кіевъ. Купеческое собраніе", Merchants' Assembly).
  Part of the lot "Views of architecture and other sites primarily in Poland, Russia, and the Ukraine", whose record
  names "Photoglob Co., publisher", "ca. 1890-ca. 1900".
- Holder: Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, Photochrom Print Collection. Call number LOT 13419,
  no. 029 [item]. Digital ID ppmsc 03813; reproduction number LC-DIG-ppmsc-03813; LCCN 2001697422.
  Record: https://www.loc.gov/item/2001697422/ (loc.gov answers 403 to plain requests; read through the Wayback
  Machine capture http://web.archive.org/web/20220706002536/https://www.loc.gov/item/2001697422/ ).
  Lot record: https://www.loc.gov/item/2001696357/ (Wayback capture 2025-12-07).
- Rights statement, exact words on the record: "Rights Advisory: No known restrictions on reproduction." and "The
  Library of Congress does not own rights to material in its collections."
- Files: LoC tile https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/ppmsc/03800/03813v.jpg (1024 x 760), saved as
  raw/loc-ppmsc-03813v.jpg, to confirm the image; the final is made from the LoC's own full-size file as hosted on
  Commons, "File:Commercial Club, Kiev, Russia, (i.e., Ukraine)-LCCN2001697422.jpg" (3429 x 2545; Commons credit
  "Library of Congress Catalog: http://lccn.loc.gov/2001697422", licence "Public domain"), saved as
  raw/commons-ppmsc-03813-2400.jpg. Same picture, same print number. The final is the whole print with its dark mount
  edge, resized.
- Public domain by age:
  - A photochrom published by Photoglob Co. (Zürich) between about 1890 and 1900; no photographer is named, so it is
    an anonymous work.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Switzerland: an anonymous work's term is 70 years from when it was lawfully made available; published by
    1900 at the latest, so it expired by 1971.
  - Israel: anonymous works 70 years from publication; photographs made under the old (1911) law, 50 years from the
    negative; both long expired.
  - A faithful reproduction of a flat print adds no new copyright (Bridgeman v. Corel in the US; Art. 14 of the 2019
    DSM Directive in the EU).
- What it shows: a square with a round fenced garden and fountain, a pavilion with a green roof, trams, telegraph
  poles, an ornate white arch at the right and, at the left, the large stone building with arched windows and a small
  dome. Small street figures (a few passers-by, two men walking at the lower right, a man bending over paving stones)
  are part of the photograph. **For the checker:** a photochrom is a colour print made photomechanically from a
  photographic negative, with colour applied by lithographic stones; the people are photographed, not drawn, but the
  colouring is hand-made. If the "no drawn or painted figures" rule is read to cover photochrom colouring, drop this
  image or crop to the building and the garden. No logos, no watermarks, no text beyond the place labels.
- Caption facts: story, "**The setting.**" section: "Koshyts conducted it ... at a Christmas concert in the hall of
  Kyiv's Merchants' Assembly, on 29 December 1916 by the old calendar [^ukrainer][^zn][^vn-bib]". The building at
  the left is identified by the print's own label ("Kupecheskoe sobranie"), with the LoC title "Commercial Club";
  the Commons description of the same LoC file reads "the Merchants Assembly building (now National Philharmonic
  Society of Ukraine concert hall)". The caption calls it the building at the left; it does not claim the concert
  date for the print.

## 3.jpg: Polishchuk's Zbirnychok (Kyiv, 1913), No. 23, p. 43

- Item: Zbirnychok naikrashchykh ukrainskykh pisen (Збірничок найкращих українських пісень), compiled by K. L.
  Polishchuk (Kyiv: Vyd. Y. T. Hubanova, 1913). No. 23, "Щедрик, щедрик щедривочка", p. 43: one line of music (two
  flats, 3/4, "Помалу", the four-note figure B-flat, A, B-flat, G in every bar), six numbered couplets, and at the foot
  "м. Краснопіль, Житомирського повіту (на Волині)".
- Holder of the copy: University of Toronto, Faculty of Music Library (IA "contributor"), call number M1764 .P65 Z3,
  OCLC 976984467, scanned by the Internet Archive in 2022. Item: https://archive.org/details/zbirnychoknakras00poli ;
  page: https://archive.org/details/zbirnychoknakras00poli/page/n154/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/zbirnychoknakras00poli/page/n154.jpg (2166 x 3480), saved as
  raw/ia-zbirnychok-1913-n154.jpg. The final is the whole page, resized.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - Published in Kyiv in 1913 (IA metadata "date": "1913", "publisher": "Kyïv Vyd. Y.T. Hubanova").
  - The song: anonymous folk words and tune.
  - The compiler: Klym (K. L.) Polishchuk, born 25 November 1891 in Krasnopil, Zhytomyr county, Volhynia; shot on 3
    November 1937 at Sandarmokh, Karelia. The Encyclopedia of Ukraine's entry says: "In 1913 he published a
    collection of Ukrainian songs." https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages%5CP%5CO%5CPolishchukKlym.htm . The
    song on this page is from his birthplace, Krasnopil. (Even if the compiler were someone else, the page's own
    content is anonymous folk material published in 1913.)
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70; Polishchuk died in 1937, so PD since 1 January 2008.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed page adds no new copyright.
- What it shows: page number, title, one line of music, six couplets, the place note, a rule. No figures, no
  ornament, no adverts. I read every word: the six couplets are the swallow's New Year wish (sheepfold, lambs,
  livestock, money or chaff, dark-browed wife). Nothing demeaning to anyone.
- Caption facts: story, "### The full words": "These are the words as a Kyiv songbook printed them in 1913, six
  numbered couplets from Krasnopil, in Volhynia ... [^zbirnychok]"; the source entry gives "No. 23 ... p. 43". "Four-note
  tune": the page's music line, and the story's opening paragraph ("a short choral setting built on its four-note tune").
