  The finals are the whole images, resized.
- Rights statement, exact words on the item page under "Rights & Access": "Although the Library is not aware of any
  U.S. copyright protection (see Title 17, U.S.C.) or any other restrictions in the Music for the Nation: American
  Sheet Music materials, there may be content protected by copyright or neighboring-rights laws of other nations."
  Credit line on the page: "Library of Congress, Music Division".
- Public domain by age, which also answers the "other nations" caveat:
  - Published in Boston in 1857 ("Entered according to act of Congress AD 1857 by O. Ditson & Co." on the cover).
  - James Lord Pierpont, words and music, died 1893 (story, [^historycom][^connectsav]). The cover's lettering and
    scrollwork are by an unnamed engraver: anonymous, 70 years from publication, ended 1927.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD. EU, UK and Israel: life plus 70 ended 1963.
  - Faithful scans of flat pages.
- What they show: the cover is type, ornamental scrolls and a small star ornament, with the handwritten deposit note
  "Deposited Sept. 16. 1857", a second handwritten line beginning "Recorded", and a large handwritten number. No figures,
  no pictures of people, no trademark beyond the publisher's imprint at the foot. Page 4 is music only: "CHORUS",
  four voice staves and piano, plate number 18200.
- Caption facts: story paragraph "**The song.**" ("Deposited Sept. 16. 1857", the dedication "to John P. Ordway,
  Esq.", Ordway ran Ordway Hall and led a minstrel company: [^ditson1857][^wittmann][^historycom][^bostonia]); the
  Did you know "The first edition's chorus is a little part-song: its four voice parts are printed on four staves of
  their own, above the piano" ([^ditson1857]); and "In 1857 the second 'Jingle bells' jumps higher than the first
  ... and the words are 'Oh! what joy it is to ride'" ([^ditson1857]). The chorus words are visible on the page.

## 3.jpg: Carmina Collegensia (Boston, 1876), music section p. 18, "Jingle, Bells"

- Item: IA metadata: title "Carmina collegensia : a complete collection of the songs of the American colleges ...";
  creator "Waite, Henry Randall, 1845-1909"; publisher "Boston : Oliver Ditson & Co."; date 1876; contributor
  "Scott - York University Libraries"; sponsor Ontario Council of University Libraries. Item
  https://archive.org/details/carminacollegens00wait ; page
  https://archive.org/details/carminacollegens00wait/page/n299/mode/1up (the book has three runs of page numbers;
  this is the music section's p. 18, found from the OCR "JINGLE, BELLS." and the item's page_numbers.json, leaf
  300); file https://archive.org/download/carminacollegens00wait/page/n299.jpg (2437 x 3651),
  raw/ia-carminacollegens00wait-p18-n299.jpg. The final is the whole page, resized.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so PD by age:
  - Published Boston 1876. US: before 1931, so PD.
  - Editor Henry Randall Waite died 1909 (IA's LC heading); Pierpont died 1893; the arranger of this setting is not
    named (anonymous: 70 years from publication, ended 1946). EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 1979.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page.
- What it shows: music and words only; no figures or marks.
- Caption facts: the page ("Chorus. *" and the footnote "* Accompanied by jingling glasses"); story: "The chorus sung
  today, with every 'Jingle bells' on one repeated note and 'Oh! what fun it is to ride', is in print by 1876, in a
  Boston book of college songs" and "both the 1876 college book and the 1909 Heart Songs mark the chorus
  'accompanied by jingling glasses'" ([^carmina1876]).

## Rejected and spare

- Heart Songs (1909), pp. 148-149 (University of Illinois copy, IA heartsongsdearto00chap, image n163): the page our
  arrangement reads. PD everywhere (Chapple died 1950, life plus 70 ended 2020), but its setting is nearly the same
  as the 1876 page, which makes the history point better. A good swap if the lead prefers the arrangement's own page.
- The story's Sources link [^ditson1857] to an Internet Archive copy
  (loc-american-sheet-mus-2023812429-the-one-horse-open-sleigh). That item is a re-upload by a private account
  ("Hessel Digital Library") with its own rights text, the same kind of mirror the licence checker replaced for
  Silent Night. I used the Library of Congress's own page and image server instead, and suggest the story's link
  change to https://www.loc.gov/item/2023812429/ .
- Objects: the Gemini 6 harmonica (National Air and Space Museum) is a branded Hohner instrument (story,
  [^nasm-story]), so a trademark would be front and centre; not pursued. The Smithsonian Open Access search did not
  return the bells from the same flight with a CC0 image (the demo API key then hit its rate limit). Smithsonian (NMAH) sleigh-bell records have
  CC0 metadata but no CC0 image. The Met's Brewster & Co. sleigh drawings are PD, but the closest in date (about
  1860) is "The 'Albert' sleigh", a two-seat design marked Montreal, not the one-horse open sleigh of the song.
- A photograph of Pierpont: none found with a clear free licence from a holder.
