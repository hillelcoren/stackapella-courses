# Haul on the Bowline: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. All four are public domain by age, three of them printed pages from the books and magazines the
story cites, and one a photograph printed in an 1899 book. Records saved beside this file in pages/: oaw_meta.json,
harpers_meta.json, sharp_meta.json and stev_meta.json (Internet Archive metadata), stev_djvu.txt (OCR of Stevenson's
book) and stev_pagenums.json. www.loc.gov, collection.sl.nsw.gov.au, collections.sea.museum, natlib.govt.nz and
collection.maritimemuseum.co.nz answered 403 or a bot check to plain requests from here, so no item is used whose rights
could only be read there.

## 1.jpg: "On Shanties", Once a Week, 1 August 1868, p. 93 (detail)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/019OnceAWeekJulyToDec1868 (scan image n115, printed page 93; running head
  "Aug. 1, 1868.] ONCE A WEEK. 93").
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/019OnceAWeekJulyToDec1868/page/n115.jpg (3858 x 5729).
- IA record: title "019 Once A Week July To Dec 1868", collections opensource, community; no creator, date, rights or
  licence field. The page carries a "Digitized by Google" mark at the foot, outside the crop: a Google Books scan
  uploaded by a member of the public, holding library not named.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London, 1 August 1868; the article "On Shanties" is unsigned (the magazine's editor was E. S. Dallas,
    1828-1879).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: an anonymous work's term is 70 years from publication (1868 + 70 = 1938). Anyone writing in 1868 of his own
    voyages died long before 1956 in any case.
  - Israel: life + 70; anonymous works 70 years from publication. The same result.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright.
- Crop and words read: the top of the left column only, from "We now come to the hauling shanties : first, there is
  the hand over hand song" to "Here the concluding word of each couplet, haul, gives the clue ; there are many of this
  sort," (a dash follows in print). It holds the "So handy, my girls, so handy" verse and the four "Haul the bowline" lines. Every word was read;
  nothing in it demeans anyone. Left out on purpose: the rest of the page, whose shanty verses include "Would kill a Turk,
  cheerily men" and "And for Christians, just think, cheerily men" (lower left and top right), the running head (it spans
  both columns), and the Google mark.

## 4.jpg: Stevenson, By Way of Cape Horn (1899), plate "Blow, my bully boys, blow"

- Item: https://archive.org/details/bywayofcapehornf01stev (scan image n122, a plate leaf facing printed page 104; the
  list of illustrations gives "\"Blow, my bully boys, blow\" ... 104"). The plate is printed sideways; it was turned
  upright with sips and cropped to the photograph and its printed caption.
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/bywayofcapehornf01stev/page/n122.jpg (1695 x 2905).
- IA record: title "By way of Cape Horn : four months in a Yankee clipper"; creator "Stevenson, Paul Eve, 1868-1910";
  publisher "Philadelphia : J.B. Lippincott"; date 1899; contributor and sponsor "The Library of Congress"; lccn
  "98001678"; possible-copyright-status "The Library of Congress is unaware of any copyright restrictions for this
  item."
- The title page reads "ILLUSTRATED FROM PHOTOGRAPHS TAKEN BY THE AUTHOR"; the verso "Copyright, 1898"; the preface is
  signed "P. E. S., New York, October 10, 1898".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - A photograph by Paul Eve Stevenson, published in Philadelphia in 1899. Stevenson died in 1910.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 (1910 + 70) ended 31 December 1980.
  - The halftone is a faithful reproduction of a flat printed plate; no new copyright.
  - The men in it were photographed about 1898 and are not living people; none is named.
- Content check: the only words in the image are the printed caption "\"Blow, my bully boys, blow\"", a line from a
  shanty. No drawing; the plate is a halftone of a photograph. The book's text elsewhere uses a racial slur (p. 100,
  about all-Black crews); it is not on the plate and is not quoted or linked from our caption.
- Caption sources: the book's preface and title page (the voyage was made for pleasure in a "Yankee clipper"); p. 104
  ("Some of the best ones for hauling are, \"Blow, my Bully Boys, Blow,\" \"A Long Time Ago,\" and \"A Poor Old
  Man\""). The caption does not say which rope the men are hauling, or on which voyage the photograph was taken: the
  book does not say, and Stevenson had made an earlier voyage to Calcutta.

## Rejected

- "The Sobraon photographed by F. C. Gould off Gravesend, ca. 1875" (State Library of New South Wales, MLMSS 7715/Box
  1X/Item 2; Flickr Commons, "No known copyright restrictions"): the ship lies at anchor with no sails set, so it shows
  nothing of the sails or the work; the print has a blue fleck; the photographer's death year could not be confirmed
  (the SLNSW catalogue answered 403). File in dropped/.
- "Barque MANUREWA (1884) under sail" (New Zealand Maritime Museum, 2012.0.11138 and others; Flickr Commons "No known
  copyright restrictions"): a fine photograph, but DigitalNZ gives creator "Unknown", usage "Unknown", date only
  "1930-1940: Production range for collection" (the ship had that name only from 1899 to 1922), and the museum's own
  record answered 403. Not clearly OK. File in dropped/.
- "Iron barque Clan Macleod, which was later named the James Craig" (State Library of Queensland, Flickr Commons, "You
  are free to use this image without permission"): creator unidentified and no date on the Flickr page (the catalogue
  is JavaScript-only); the ship is moored with sails hung out to dry, and the sails look retouched. Dropped.
- "Crew men pulling on a rope near a mast on board MAGDALENE VINNEN" (ANMM, Samuel J. Hood Studio, March 1933): the
  museum's own record answered 403 and no Flickr copy at full size was found; 1933 is also long after the shanty's
  working life. Not used.
- San Francisco Maritime NHP deck photographs by Wilhelm Hester (npgallery.nps.gov): "This image is for research and
  study purposes only". Rejected.
- Stevenson's other hauling plate, "Hauling taut the braces" (facing p. 387, scan n416): same rights as 4.jpg, but only
  three men and no sail; kept in dropped/ as a spare.
- Bullen's and Terry's pages: not needed (four images is the limit) and the earliest printing, the earliest tune and
  the page we sing from cover the story.
