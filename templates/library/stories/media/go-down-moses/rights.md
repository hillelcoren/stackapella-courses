# Go Down, Moses: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. www.loc.gov answered 403 (a bot check) to plain requests from here, so Library of Congress
records were read from lccn.loc.gov's MARCXML (https://lccn.loc.gov/<lccn>/marcxml), which did answer, and from the
Wikimedia Commons file pages that mirror the LoC scans. Saved copies of every page read are in pages/.

## 1.jpg: The Key to East Virginia (New York: W. Schaus, c1861), top part

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/item/99439180/ (catalogue: https://lccn.loc.gov/99439180; handle
  http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.gmd/g3880.cw0545000). Call number G3880 1861 .C6, LC Civil War Maps (2nd ed.) 545.
- LoC record (MARCXML, read 2026-11-13): 245 "The key to East Virginia showing the exact relative positions of Fortress
  Monroe, Rip Raps, Newport News, Sewalls [sic] Point, Norfolk, Gosport Navy Yard and expressing the soundings of every
  part of Hampton Roads & Elizabeth River / Compiled from government surveys & drawn by M. K. Couzens, Civ. Eng. Litho.
  Lang & Laing, N.Y."; 260 "New York, W. Schaus, c1861."; 852 "Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division".
  The record has no 540 (rights) field.
- On the sheet: a received stamp "Nov. 15, 1861" (upper right) and the line "Entered according to Act of Congress in the
  year 1861 by W. Schaus ... Southern District of New York" (bottom).
- Downloaded from Wikimedia Commons (LoC's own tile server gave 404 for this map from here):
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_key_to_East_Virginia_showing_the_exact_relative_positions_of_Fortress_Monroe,_Rip_Raps,_Newport_News,_Sewalls_(sic)_Point,_Norfolk,_Gosport_Navy_Yard_and_expressing_the_soundings_of_every_part_of_LOC_99439180.jpg
  (5974 x 7766). Commons file page: source "https://www.loc.gov/item/99439180/", permission "{{LOC-map|id=g3880.cw0545000}}",
  licence "{{PD-Art|PD-old-100-1923}}", author "Couzens, M. K.", date 1861.
- Cropped by us to the top part (title, Hampton, Fortress Monroe, Hampton Roads, Sewalls Point); no other change.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published New York, copyright 1861.
  - US: published before 1931 (and its 1861 copyright term long expired), so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70. M. K. Couzens's death year was not found, but a civil engineer drawing maps in 1861 was
    born before about 1843; to be in copyright now he would have had to die in 1956 or later, aged over 110. The
    lithographers and publisher are firms of the 1860s. Public domain.
  - A faithful scan of a flat printed map adds no new copyright.
- Words on the map checked: title, place names, the "Key to Fortress Monroe" list and the "Description of Fortress
  Monroe" (all military and descriptive; nothing demeaning). The cropped image shows only the title and the upper map.
- Caption source: NPS, "Building #1, Quarters No. 1" (https://www.nps.gov/places/building-1-quarters-no-1.htm, a story
  source, [^nps-q1]): "That night three enslaved men, known today as Frank Baker, Shepard Mallory, and James Townsend,
  bravely fled from their labors constructing gun positions for the Confederate forces at Sewell's Point across the
  waters of Hampton Roads and sought freedom at Fort Monroe." (Butler arrived 22 May 1861; the escape was the next
  night.)

## 2.jpg and 3.jpg: The Song of the "Contrabands" (New York: Horace Waters, 1861), cover and p. 2

- Item: https://filsonhistorical.omeka.net/items/show/6797 (the story's [^contrabands1861] source). Title "The song of
  the "contrabands;" "O let my people go," 1861"; Source "Sheet Music Collection, The Filson Historical Society,
  Louisville, Kentucky"; Creator "Enslaved people; Baker, Thomas"; Contributor "Lockwood, L. C."; Date 1861. Call
  number 781.62 B168s, written in pencil on p. 3.
- Downloaded: the item's first two image files (full size, 6635 x 8569 and 6624 x 8563) from the page's own file links
  (d1y502jg6fpugt.cloudfront.net/34455/archive/files/3b91b03ab94c0f293c50448405981e68.jpg and
  .../1da5c19eebb04760049722e72e812460.jpg).
- Rights field on the item page, exact words:
  "No Copyright - United States
  The organization that has made the Item available believes that the Item is in the Public Domain under the laws of
  the United States, but a determination was not made as to its copyright status under the copyright laws of other
  countries. The Item may not be in the Public Domain under the laws of other countries. Please refer to the
  organization that has made the Item available for more information.
  http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-US/1.0/
  Property rights in the collection belong to The Filson Historical Society. The Filson Historical Society can provide
  high-resolution scans of original source materials from its holdings for non-commercial and commercial use."
  (The last two sentences concern ownership of the physical collection and the Filson's paid scanning service; they
  claim no copyright and set no reuse condition. Checker: please confirm you read it the same way.)
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published New York 1861: the cover reads "Entered according to Act of Congress A.D. 1861 by [name hard to read]
    in the Clerk's Office of the District Court of the Southern District of New-York"; engraver's mark "Stackpole Sc."
  - The words and tune are traditional (no maker known). The arranger Thomas Baker and the engraver were working
    adults in 1861; neither can have died in 1956 or later. L. C. Lockwood, who supplied the song, was an ordained
    missionary in 1861, so the same holds for him (his death year was not checked).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain (the Filson says so too: NoC-US).
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 has run out for everyone involved; anonymous parts 70 years from 1861.
  - Faithful scans of flat pages add no new copyright.
- Words checked: cover (title, Lockwood credit, arranger, publisher, copyright line; decoration is scrolls and
  star-and-stripe shields, no figures) and p. 2 (title, the headnote "This Song, originated among the "Contrabands," and
  was first heard sung by them on their arrival at Fortress Monroe; and was introduced here by their Chaplain: Rev.
  L. C. Lockwood.", verse 1 "The Lord by Moses to Pharaoh said: "O let my people go! If not, I'll smite your
  first-born dead"). Standard English, no dialect, nothing demeaning. "Contrabands" is the period name the story itself
  explains.

## 4.jpg: Jubilee Songs (New York: Biglow & Main, 1872), p. 22

- Item: https://archive.org/details/29208201.4853.emory.edu (page image n24 = printed p. 22). Contributor and sponsor
  "Emory University, Manuscript, Archives and Rare Book Library".
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/29208201.4853.emory.edu/page/n24.jpg (2820 x 4349).
- Rights words in the IA record's description: "The online edition of this book in the public domain, i.e., not
  protected by copyright, has been produced by the Emory University Digital Library Publications Program".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning: published New York 1872. Theodore F. Seward (music) died 1902; George L. White (words) died 1895 (IA
  record: "Seward, Theodore F. (Theodore Frelinghuysen), 1835-1902", "White, George L. (George Leonard), 1838-1895").
  US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life + 70 ran out in 1972 at the latest. Faithful scan of a flat page.
- Words checked: p. 22 has the title, verse 1 and the refrain under the music ("Tell ole Pha-roh", the book's spelling,
  which the story keeps), and verses 2 to 4. Nothing in caricature dialect. Page 23 (verses 5 to 24, with "yer" and
  "de" in verses 22 and 23) is NOT used.

## Considered and not used

- Library of Congress, "Jubilee singers, Fisk University, Nashville, Tenn. / negative by Black" (cabinet card, [1872?],
  LCCN 2015650289, LC-DIG-ppmsca-39589). LoC record 540: "No known restrictions on publication."; 520 names Minnie Tate,
  Greene Evans, Isaac Dickerson, Jennie Jackson, Maggie Porter, Ella Sheppard, Thomas Rutling, Benjamin Holmes and Eliza
  Walker. Cleared and dignified, but left out because Steal Away, Swing Low and Were You There already show the same
  group in the LoC carte de visite (ppmsca-11008). The raw file is kept in raw/fisk-1872-commons.jpg if the lead wants it
  for the Fisk paragraph instead of 3.jpg.
- HABS photographs of Quarters No. 1, Fort Monroe (HABS VA,28-HAMP,2A-1 and -4): the building where Butler made his
  decision, but the LoC record names no photographer and dates the set only "Documentation compiled after 1933", and the
  HABS statement warns "images copied from other sources may be restricted". Not clearly OK, so dropped.
- E. P. Dutton's map "Fort Monroe and vicinity" (LoC 99439185): its inset "View of Fort Monroe" has small drawn figures.
- Engravings and lithographs of Fort Monroe from Harper's Weekly, Frank Leslie's and E. Sachse & Co.: drawn human
  figures; one Leslie's print has a demeaning title. Not used.
