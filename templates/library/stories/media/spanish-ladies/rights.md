## 1.jpg: Chappell, A Collection of National English Airs (1840), p. 98, No. 204 (detail)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/collectionofnati00crot (scan image n123, printed page 98).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/collectionofnati00crot/page/n123.jpg (3201 x 4252).
- IA record: title "A collection of national English airs : consisting of ancient song, ballad, & dance tunes ...";
  creators "Crotch, William, 1775-1847", "Macfarren, G. A. (George Alexander), 1813-1887", "Wade, J. Augustine (Joseph
  Augustine), 1796-1845", "Chappell, W. (William), 1809-1888"; publisher "London : Chappell"; date 1840; contributor
  "Music Library, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill"; call number "b7453895"; no rights or licence field.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London 1840. Every contributor died in the 1800s: Chappell 1888, Macfarren 1887 (the "M." over the
    harmony), Crotch 1847, Wade 1845. The tune and words are traditional.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: life + 70 ended by 1958 at the latest. Any UK typographical-arrangement right (25 years) ended long ago.
  - Israel: life + 70, the same.
  - The scan is a faithful photograph of a flat page and adds no new copyright.
- Crop and words read: the crop is No. 204 only. The page also prints No. 202, "Cook Lorrel" ("Cook Lorrel would needs
  have the Devil his Guest ... at the charge of a Sinner"), and No. 203, "Trip and go"; both are cut out, so nothing
  about devils or sinners shows. Words in the crop: "Slow.", "M.", "204.", "So Farewell to you ye fine Spanish Ladies
  So farewell to you ye Ladies of Spain, For we've receiv'd orders to sail for Old England And perhaps we may never
  come back here again.", and the plate number "5658." Nothing demeans anyone; no figures or decoration.
- Caption: No. 204, "Slow", minor key, raised seventh in Chappell's printings: the story ("The tune in print" and
  "Sharp and Captain Lewis" paragraphs, [^chappell1840], [^sharp]); the F sharps are visible in the key of two flats.

- Caption: No. 124, 1909, Captain Lewis of Minehead, the soprano sings Sharp's voice part note for note: the story

## 3.jpg: Van Keulen, sea chart of the third part of the Channel

- Holder: Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam, RP-P-1896-A-19368-3050. Persistent id https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200396063 ; item
  page https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/RP-P-1896-A-19368-3050 ; Dublin Core record
  https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/200396063?_profile=dc
- Record fields (exact): title "Paskaart van Het Kanaal tussen Engeland en Frankrijk, deel 3"; description "Paskaart
  van een gedeelte van Het Kanaal, de zeestraat tussen Groot-Brittannië en Frankrijk, met een kompasroos. Linksonder
  een cartouche met de titel, het adres van de uitgever en de schaalverdeling."; creator "anoniem"; date
  "1688 - 1803"; rights https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ (Public Domain Mark).
- Downloaded: https://iiif.micr.io/MRmkc/full/max/0/default.jpg (the record's image relation), 6345 x 5820, saved as
  raw/rijks-RP-P-1896-A-19368-3050.jpg. The final crops away the colour card, ruler and the pencilled museum number
  below the sheet, keeping the whole printed chart.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - The imprint on the sheet: "t Amsterdam By Ioannes van Keulen Aande Nieuwe brug inde Gekroonde Lootsman met
    Previlegie Opgestelt door Gerard van Keulen". Gerard van Keulen, the compiler, died 1726. The museum dates the
    print between 1688 and 1803; whoever engraved or printed it died long before 1956.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 ended long ago, and an anonymous work's 70 years from publication too.
  - The museum marks it with the Public Domain Mark. A faithful image of a flat print adds no new copyright.
- Looked at in full and zoomed (Scilly, Ushant, the Cornish coast): no figures, ships or decoration; the cartouche is
  a plain ruled box of text. Words read on the sheet: the title in Dutch, French and English ("the New Sea Map of the Third
  Partie of the Chanell", as engraved), the imprint above, the four scale bars, a magnetic variation note by the
  compass rose, "BRETAGNE", "CORNWAL", "DEVON SHIRE" and the coastal names and soundings. Nothing that demeans anyone.
- Caption: on the sheet, zoomed: "Isle Douessant" (Ushant) off Brittany, "Scilly of Sorlinges", "Dootmans hooft" (the
  Dodman) by "Gall Rock" and "Blak head", "Ramehead" by "Plymouth Sound". The story: Ushant and the Scilly isles mark
  the two sides of the Channel's mouth ([^pilot]); "The first land we made, it is called the Deadman, Next Ram Head"
  (Chappell's verse 3) and the Deadman is the Dodman ([^whall]).
