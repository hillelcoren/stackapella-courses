# Oh, How Lovely Is the Evening: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/. Finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata), scaled to 1600 px
on the long side. A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v.
Corel, in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive, and in Israel for lack of originality. Every image looked at
in full and every printed word on each crop read; none shows a drawn, painted or engraved human figure, a logo or a
watermark. Total 4 files, about 1.1 MB.

Makers check (Mexico, life plus 100): the song's makers are Schulz (died 1850) and the Educational Music Course editors
(Mason 1896, Veazie 1915, Gilchrist 1916, McLaughlin 1925, Dole 1935). The only non-makers whose work is on a crop are
Henry Liebhart (editor, 1882; died 1895) and Henry Francis Sefton (1869; died 21 May 1892, Canadian Encyclopedia and
Dictionary of Canadian Biography). Both are past life plus 100. Rejected for this rule: Hollis Dann's Fourth Year Music
(1916, "Lovely Evening"): Dann died 1939, so his book is protected in Mexico until 2039.

Reuse check: no media.json under stories/media/ cites Liebhart, Sefton, the Educational Music Course, Cooper Union or
any HAER Cooper Union photograph. (are-you-sleeping 3.jpg shows a 1916 Normal Instructor column that also prints "Oh,
How Lovely Is the Evening"; not reused.)

## 3.jpg: Cooper Union, the Great Hall, HAER photograph 10 (Jack E. Boucher, about 1971)

- Holder's record: https://www.loc.gov/item/ny0359/ (read through the Wayback snapshot
  http://web.archive.org/web/20250813212900/https://www.loc.gov/item/ny0359, since loc.gov answers 403 here; saved as
  raw/loc_item_ny0359.html). Title "Cooper Union for the Advancement of Science & Art, Third & Fourth Avenues, Astor
  Place, Seventh Street, New York County, NY"; "Boucher, Jack E, photographer"; Created/Published "Documentation
  compiled after 1968"; Medium "Photo(s): 32, Measured Drawing(s): 20, Data Page(s): 68"; call number "HAER
  NY,31-NEYO,81-"; Rights Advisory: "No known restrictions on images made by the U.S. Government; images copied from
  other sources may be restricted." (https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/114_habs.html).
- Photo item: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ny0359.photos.120049p/ = HAER NY,31-NEYO,81-10 (as the Commons
  mirror's file page records it: accession "HAER NY,31-NEYO,81-10", source that URL, file 120049pu.tif, permission
  {{PD-USGov-NPS}}). File: the LoC master
  https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ny/ny0300/ny0359/photos/120049pu.tif (5000 x 3575),
  saved as raw/haer_120049pu.tif; the final is made from it, not from Commons.
- Date and maker: the HAER data pages (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ny/ny0300/ny0359/data/ny0359data.pdf,
  raw/haer_data.pdf, "Project information", p. 67): "It was cosponsored during the summer of 1971 by the National Park
  Service (HAER) and the Cooper Union ... The formal photography was done by Jack E. Boucher." Hence "about 1971".
- Licence: PD-US-gov. An original survey photograph made for HAER, a National Park Service program, by its
  photographer (Boucher was the HABS/HAER staff photographer of the NPS); not a copy of an older outside photograph,
  so the "images copied from other sources" caveat does not apply.
- Which room: the survey has no photo caption pages. The data pages name one assembly hall in the building, the Great
  Hall in the basement ("Great Hall opened" 10 May 1859; "the main bulk of the Cooper Union building was supported on
  cast iron columns over the Great Hall"; an 1858 description: "It contains 2,500 iron chairs"). The photograph shows
  that room: a large basement hall of rows of seats among iron columns under arched vaults. The checker may confirm on
  the loc.gov photo page if it can be read.
- Crop: x 840 to 3750, y 195 to 3450 of the master. Cut away, for the no-figures rule: a large painting with many
  figures on the right-hand wall and a portrait in the far doorway at the back left; also a flag at the left, the black
  film border and the survey label "HAER NY-20". Checked the final in full: no people, no figures; the only words are
  two "EXIT" signs. One photograph on this page.
- Caption facts: Harper's Weekly, 5 November 1892, pp. 1074 to 1075 (raw/harpers_n18.jpg and the item's OCR text):
  "there were twenty-five hundred present"; "the meetings of the singing class at Cooper Union every Sunday afternoon
  at four o'clock"; "They sang till the vaulted arches rang"; then "he introduced a simple tune, with the words: [O],
  how lonely is the evening ..." and sang it as a round in three sections. NYT, 30 October 1892: "begun last Sunday at
  the Cooper Institute, when Frank Damrosch ... gave 2,500 persons their first lesson". Story: "On Sunday 23 October
  1892, at Cooper Union in New York ... 2,500 came".
