## 1.jpg: the Jubilee Singers, frontispiece photograph, 1876

- Item: J. B. T. Marsh, The story of the Jubilee Singers with their songs (London: Hodder and Stoughton; printed by
  the Gresham Press, 1876). Internet Archive storyofjubileesi00mars_3, contributor and sponsor Getty Research
  Institute, call number 9927194460001551, OCLC 1435505056 (IA metadata, https://archive.org/metadata/storyofjubileesi00mars_3).
  This is the copy our story cites as [^marsh1876] (the sixth edition).
- IA metadata description, exact words: "Frontispiece portrait of the Jubilee Singers is a mounted photograph".
- Page: https://archive.org/details/storyofjubileesi00mars_3/page/n7/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/storyofjubileesi00mars_3/page/n7.jpg (2378 x 3495, bound sideways), saved as
  raw/ia-marsh1876-n7.jpg. The final is rotated 90 degrees and cropped to the photograph plus the printed names.
- Printed under the photograph, every word: "Maggie Porter. E. W. Watkins. H. D. Alexander. F. J. Loudin. Thomas
  Rutling. / Jennie Jackson. Mabel Lewis. Ella Sheppard. Maggie Carnes. America W. Robinson." Nothing else is printed.
- Rights: the IA record states no licence, so this rests on PD by age:
  - A photograph published in London in 1876 (the book's date; the sitting was in or before 1876). No photographer
    or studio is named on the mount or in the record.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - UK and EU: a photograph of unknown authorship runs 70 years from being made available (ended by 1946). Even if
    a studio photographer were later identified, a professional at work in 1876 would have had to live past 1955 for
    the work to be in copyright; and in the UK, photographs taken before 1957 by a known author run life plus 70.
  - Israel: photographs made under the 1911 Act run 50 years from the negative; anonymous works 70 years from
    publication. Both long expired.
  - A faithful scan of a flat, public-domain print adds no new copyright (Bridgeman v. Corel in the US; Art. 14 of
    the 2019 DSM Directive in the EU).
- Spirituals rules: a dignified studio group portrait of the Fisk singers, named as the book names them, all adults
  in 1876 and so long dead (no one born by the 1850s is living). Not a photograph of enslaved people. No drawn
  figures; the table carries a small carved ornament only. No logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: our story, "The singers who carried it" and Sources [^marsh1875], [^marsh1876]; the 1876 sixth
  edition is the Getty copy cited there.

## 2.jpg: "Deep River", No. 77, p. 196 (1876)

- Same book and copy as 1.jpg. Page: https://archive.org/details/storyofjubileesi00mars_3/page/n209/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/storyofjubileesi00mars_3/page/n209.jpg (2231 x 3436), saved as
  raw/ia-marsh1876-n209.jpg. Page 197 (n210) also saved, not used.
- Crop: only the No. 77 part of p. 196 (heading, three staves, page number). The top two thirds of the page, No. 76
  "Lord, I wish I had a come", are cropped away so that no other song's words are shown.
- Every word on the crop: "No. 77. Deep River." / "Deep . . ri-ver, My home is o-ver Jor-dan," / "Deep . . . . ri -
  ver, Lord, I" / "want to cross o-ver in-to camp ground, Lord, I" / "196", plus the marks f and pp. Standard
  spelling throughout; nothing demeaning; no dialect.
- Rights: no licence stated by IA; PD by age:
  - Published London 1876 (Marsh's "Note" is dated 6 November 1875). The page is a traditional tune and words,
    set down by the singers' music transcribers; Theodore F. Seward, who wrote the "Preface to the Music", died in 1902.
    Marsh's own prose is not in the crop. A Smithsonian catalogue entry, seen only in a search result, gives Marsh
    as 1840 to 1887; this is not relied on.
  - US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70; anyone who wrote or set down this page in 1875 died long
    before 1956.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.
- Why not the 1875 Giessen scan our story cites as the "original": the holder's own rights note, on
  https://digisam.ub.uni-giessen.de/ubg-ihd-gb/content/titleinfo/5173800 (saved as pages/giessen-titleinfo-5173800.html),
  reads "Nutzungsrechte Eingeschränkt - Freier Zugriff - Nicht kommerziell" and links
  https://rightsstatements.org/vocab/InC/1.0/. That is a non-commercial restriction, so no Giessen image is used. The
  1876 sixth edition prints the song on the same pages (story, [^marsh1876]); the caption says 1876.

## 3.jpg: Burleigh, Deep River for high voice and piano (Ricordi, 1917), p. 3

- Item: "Deep river : old Negro melody", New York: G. Ricordi, 1917, "For high voice and piano", "At head of title:
  Respectfully dedicated to Miss Mary Jordan". Internet Archive deepriveroldnegr00newy, contributor Harold B. Lee
  Library (Brigham Young University), call number a3866408, collection sheetmusicbyu (IA metadata,
  https://archive.org/metadata/deepriveroldnegr00newy).
- Page: https://archive.org/details/deepriveroldnegr00newy/page/n2/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/deepriveroldnegr00newy/page/n2.jpg (3501 x 4628), saved as raw/ia-byu-dr1917-n2.jpg.
  The final is the whole page, resized.
- Every word on the page: "Respectfully dedicated to Miss Mary Jordan" / "3" / "Deep River" / "Old Negro melody" /
  "Arranged by H. T. BURLEIGH" / "Lento" / "Voice" / "Piano" / the sung words "Deep river, my home is over Jordan,
  Deep river, Lord, I want to cross over into camp-ground." / "116059-3" / "Copyright MCMXVII by G. Ricordi & Co.,
  Inc. All rights of execution, reproduction, translation and transcription are strictly reserved". "Negro" is the
  period's usual word, as in our story's own source titles; there are no dialect spellings and nothing that mocks.
  A faint show-through from the reverse is visible; it is not readable.
- Rejected pages of the same item: the front cover (n0) lists the whole Ricordi series, many titles in dialect
  spellings, so it fails the spirituals rule; the preface (n1, "H. T. B., New York, 1917") uses phrases such as
  "wholly untutored minds", so it is not shown either.
- Rights: no licence stated by IA; PD by age:
  - Published New York 1917, with the notice above; the Copyright Office registered Burleigh's Deep River for voice
    and piano on 3 June 1916 (story, [^cce1916]).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain (the 1917 notice and reservation have long expired).
  - EU and Israel: life plus 70. Burleigh died in 1949 (story, [^hymnarchive-burleigh], [^hymnary-burleigh]), so his
    arrangement is public domain from 1 January 2020. The words and tune are traditional. Ricordi's engraving is
    not a new work.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.

## 4.jpg: the river Jordan, about 1900 (Library of Congress, Matson Collection)

- Item: "River Jordan; Looking up the River Jordan", G. Eric and Edith Matson Photograph Collection, 1 negative
  (a stereo pair). Library of Congress catalogue record mpc2005003381; handle https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/matpc.11396 ;
  reproduction number LC-DIG-matpc-11396. Date 1900 as given in the record mirrored on Commons; the caption says
  "about 1900".
- loc.gov answered 403 to every plain request from here (curl and WebFetch), so the item record was read through its
  Wikimedia Commons mirror: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:River_Jordan;_Looking_up_the_River_Jordan_LOC_matpc.11396.jpg
  (source "Library of Congress", catalogue https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/matpc/item/mpc2005003381/PP ,
  original url https://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/matpc.11396 , permission field: "No known restrictions on publication.").
  A checker with a browser should open the loc.gov record once to confirm the date.
- File: the full-size scan the Library published, as mirrored on Commons:
  https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fd/River_Jordan%3B_Looking_up_the_River_Jordan_LOC_matpc.11396.jpg
  (5854 x 4347), saved as raw/commons-loc-matpc-11396.jpg. The Library's own 1024 px copy,
  https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/matpc/11300/11396v.jpg , was also fetched (raw/loc-matpc-11396v.jpg)
  and shows the same picture. The final is the left half of the stereo pair, cropped to drop a paper strip at the top,
  the negative's number and label at the bottom, and the centre divider.
- Rights statement, exact words, Library of Congress, "G. Eric and Edith Matson Photograph Collection: Rights and
  Restrictions Information", https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/258_mats.html (read through the Wayback Machine,
  http://web.archive.org/web/20240520011252/https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/258_mats.html , saved as
  pages/loc-258_mats-2024.html): "Publication and other forms of distribution: No known restrictions. The Kensington
  Episcopal Home conveyed the collection to the Library in 1978. In 2003, the Home dedicated the intellectual property
  and related rights to the collection to the American public". The lantern-slide book limit in the same statement
  does not apply: this is a negative, not a lantern slide. Credit line it asks for: "Library of Congress, Prints &
  Photographs Division, [reproduction number, e.g., LC-DIG-matpc-00718]".
- Also PD by age: a photograph of about 1900 by an unnamed photographer of the collection, published or not, is long
  out of term in the US, the EU and Israel. It is a landscape photographed by the original photographer, not a later
  photo of a 3D object, so no other photographer's licence is needed.
- What it shows: water, bushes and trees on the left bank, clay cliffs on the right. No people, no buildings, no
  marks, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: our story, "What the words mean" (the river is the Jordan, crossed by the Israelites into the
  promised land, [^kjv]).
