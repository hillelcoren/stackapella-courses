## 1.jpg: Shaker Meetinghouse, Canterbury, N.H., side elevation looking south, 1969

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/nh0070.photos.105374p/ (HABS NH-186). Call number HABS NH,7-CANT,10-2.
  Title as catalogued: "Historic American Buildings Survey [photographer's name], Photographer 1969 SIDE ELEVATION,
  LOOKING SOUTH - Shaker Meetinghouse, Shaker Village Road, Canterbury, Merrimack County, NH". The photographer's
  death year could not be found, so the name is left out of our records (living photographers are described, not
  named).
- Downloaded: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nh/nh0000/nh0070/photos/105374pu.tif
  (5000 x 3958). Cropped to remove the film border, the hand-written "HABS NH-186" and the film maker's edge print.
- Licence: PD-US-gov. The title carries no line crediting another source (unlike the "Gift of New York State
  Department of Education" photos rejected for I Will Bow). LoC HABS/HAER/HALS rights statement
  (https://guides.loc.gov/p-and-p-rights-and-restrictions/rights#114_habs.html): "most of the photographs and data
  pages in HABS/HAER/HALS were created for the U.S. Government and are considered to be in the public domain", with
  material from other sources "noted by the presence of a line crediting the original source". The HABS cover sheet
  (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/nh/nh0000/nh0070/data/nh0070data.pdf) reads "Historic
  American Buildings Survey, Office of Archeology and Historic Preservation, National Park Service, Department of the
  Interior". Commons mirror of the LoC record:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Historic_American_Buildings_Survey_Elmer_R._Pearson,_Photographer_1969_SIDE_ELEVATION,_LOOKING_SOUTH_-_Shaker_Meetinghouse,_Shaker_Village_Road,_Canterbury,_Merrimack_County,_NH_HABS_NH,7-CANT,10-2.tif
  (source https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/nh0070.photos.105374p; its tag is not evidence). The www.loc.gov item page
  answered 403 (bot check) from here: a checker with a browser should confirm its Rights Advisory line, "No known
  restrictions on images made by the U.S. Government; images copied from other sources may be restricted."
- No people in the picture (the upper right window, zoomed, shows a torn blind, not a figure); no trademark after
  the crop.
- Caption facts: NPS, Canterbury Shaker Village (https://www.nps.gov/places/canterbury-shaker-village.htm):
  "Canterbury's formal call-to-order came in the summer of 1792 with the construction of the Meeting House." Holy
  Ground = Canterbury: our story, from Andrews (1940) and De Vinne (2003); "signed 'Holy Ground'": Hazzard p. 70 as
  the story reads it. The caption says only that the name is signed under the song, not that the song came from
  Canterbury (Andrews gives Enfield).

## 2.jpg: Millennial Praises (Hancock, 1813), p. 199, Hymn XXVI "The true Believer's Treasure"

- Item: https://archive.org/details/millennialpraise00well (Princeton Theological Seminary Library, call number SCB;
  record: "Millennial praises : containing a collection of gospel hymns, in four parts; adapted to the day of Christ's
  second appearing", "Hancock, [Mass.] : Printed by Josiah Tallcott, Junior", 1813, creator "Wells, Seth Youngs,
  1767-1847", "Compiled by Seth Youngs Wells").
- Page: https://archive.org/details/millennialpraise00well/page/n212 (printed p. 199), downloaded as
  https://archive.org/download/millennialpraise00well/page/n212_w1600.jpg. Cropped to the lower part: the heading
  "HYMN XXVI.", the title, verse 1 and the first four lines of verse 2.
- Cropped out on purpose: the top of the page ends Hymn XXV, whose fifth verse has "From the beast and bloody whore",
  apocalyptic language aimed at another church. Not shown.
- Every word in the crop read: "Upon a sure foundation / My faith is firmly plac'd, / By time nor desolation, / It
  cannot be eras'd. / Let famous marble pillars / All moulder into dust, / The true believer's treasure / Is safe from
  moth and rust: / 2 With faith more firm and stable / Than a stupendous mount, / No human skill is able / Its value to
  recount:". Nothing demeaning.
- Licence: PD-old. Printed 1813. Compiler Seth Youngs Wells died 1847 (record). Hymns unsigned. Public domain in the
  US (published before 1931), the EU and Israel (life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for anonymous works) and
  Mexico (life plus 100: Wells died 1847, and a writer of 1813 would have had to live 112 more years to matter). A scan
  of a flat printed page adds no new rights. The Internet Archive record states no restriction.
- Caption facts: the page itself ("safe from moth and rust"); the story: the song's stored-up treasure "echoes
  Jesus's saying about laying up treasures in heaven", Matthew 6:20 "where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt".

## Held: Peter Foster, "Diagram of the south part of Shaker Village, Canterbury, NH" (1849)

- Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division, G3744.S5 1849 .F6 TIL, https://www.loc.gov/item/00552211/,
  image service g3744s.ct000129 (downloaded to raw/loc-g3744s-ct000129.jpg). Pen and ink and watercolour; no human
  figures (checked zoomed); every word is a building name in the key, the title, the artist's apology note, "Road",
  "Granite Walk", "Aqueduct", "Peter Foster Artist 1849". Ideal for this song (Holy Ground, 1849, the Meeting House
  is No. 1).
- Not used because Foster's death year could not be found (LoC exhibit page, LoC name authorities, NPS, searches).
  It is surely public domain in the US (unpublished, made over 120 years ago) and under life plus 70, but Mexico's
  life plus 100 needs him dead before 1926. Usable once a source gives his death year.

## 2.jpg: the Harvard Shaker Church covenant, Article VII, "Dedication and Release" (1831; printed 1873)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/shakerscovenantn00harv (Boston Public Library, call number 7548.83; record:
  title "The Shakers' covenant, (never before published,) with a brief outline of Shaker history", creator
  "Harvard (Mass.). Church of the United Society. Shaker", associated name "Grosvenor, Roxalana L", publisher
  "Boston, W.C. Allan, Printer", date 1873, "ii, 12 pages"; no rights field).
- Page: https://archive.org/details/shakerscovenantn00harv/page/n17 (printed p. 8), downloaded as
  https://archive.org/download/shakerscovenantn00harv/page/n17.jpg (3098 x 4173), kept as
  raw/ia-shakerscovenantn00harv-n17.jpg. Cropped from "ARTICLE VII." to the foot of the text; the end of Article VI
  above it is left out (it is plain covenant text too). Made with sips through a BMP round trip; 1600 x 1432,
  585,235 bytes.
- Title page: "Entered According to Act of Congress April 16, 1873, by Roxalana L. Grosvenor,
  in the office of the Librarian of Congress at Washington. Boston: W.C. Allan, Printer, 50 Bromfield Street, 1873."
  (The front matter's advertisements are on other leaves and are not used.)
- Every word in the crop read: "ARTICLE VII. Dedication and Release. Section 1. Dedication and consecration of
  persons, property and service. According to the faith of the Gospel which we have received and agreeable to the
  uniform practice of the Church of Christ from its first establishment in this Society, we covenant and agree to
  dedicate, devote, consecrate and give up, and by this covenant we do solemnly and conscientiously dedicate, devote,
  consecrate and give up ourselves and services together with all our temporal interest to the service of God and
  the support and benefit of the Church of this community, and to such other pious and charitable purpose as the
  Gospel may require, to be under the care and direction of such elders, deacons, and trustees as are or may be
  appointed and established in the Church by the authority aforesaid. Section 2. Dedication and release of private
  claim. Whereas, in pursuance of the requirement of the Gospel, ... we have freely and voluntarily sacrificed all
  self-interest, and consecrated and devoted our persons, services and property, as aforesaid, to the pious and
  benevolent purposes of the Gospel; Therefore, we do hereby ... release and quit claim to the deacons, or acting
  trustees of the Church ... all our private personal right, title, interest, claim and demand ... [and] will never
  hereafter ... make nor require any account of any interest, property, labor or service ... nor bring any charge of
  debt or damage ... on account of any property or service given, rendered, devoted or consecrated to the aforesaid
  sacred and charitable purposes. In confirmation of all the aforesaid statements, covenants, promises, and articles
  of agreement, we have hereunto subscribed our names and affixed our seals, commencing on this sixteenth day of
  November, in the year of our Lord one thousand eight hundred and thirty-one." (The full section 2 is on the crop;
  the dots here only shorten this note.) Nothing crude or demeaning; no figures, ornaments or trademarks.
- Licence: PD-old.
  - Published 1873 in the US: public domain there (published before 1931; the 1873 registration has long expired).
  - The words shown are the covenant of the Church of the United Society at Harvard, Mass., a corporate, unsigned
    text dated 16 November 1831 (the preamble on p. 1 names "the Church of the United Society, in the town of
    Harvard"). Its makers were adult Shakers in 1831 and all died long before 1926.
  - The book's editor, Roxalana L. Grosvenor, was born in 1813: Library of Congress name authority n94093499,
    https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n94093499.html ("Grosvenor, Roxalana L., 1813-"; "(Sister Roxalana L.
    Grosvenor; b. 1813; Shaker; Harvard, Mass. ...)"). No death year is given, but a woman born in 1813 died before
    1926 unless she lived past 112. In any case the crop shows only the 1831 covenant, not her own writing.
  - EU and Israel (life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for anonymous works): public domain. Mexico (life plus
    100): public domain, as above.
  - A scan of a flat printed page adds no new rights. The Internet Archive record states no restriction.
- Caption facts, all from the page itself: Article VII's words; "commencing on this sixteenth day of November ...
  1831"; the preamble's "Church of the United Society, in the town of Harvard"; printed 1873 (title page). The tie to
  the song is the story's own sentence: "They held their property in common: members of the senior order, the Church
  Family, gave theirs to the community when they signed its covenant" (sourced there to Andrews and De Vinne). The
  caption says "Church members", as the covenant does, not "Church Family".
- Note for the checker: Grosvenor writes in the introduction as a former sister ("as when I was owned by them as a
  'sister in the Gospel'") and questions how future leaders might use the covenant's powers. The introduction is
  not shown and the caption does not describe her or the book's purpose.
