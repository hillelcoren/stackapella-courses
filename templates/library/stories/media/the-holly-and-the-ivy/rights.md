- Holder: Wellcome Collection, work https://wellcomecollection.org/works/xbjj5xhu (other ids b12973312, b28775557;
  call number EPB/B/49106, one per volume). The page says "Public Domain Mark" and "You can use this work for any

  The Wellcome catalogue API gives the digitized item (https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/presentation/v2/b28775557)

- Internet Archive record https://archive.org/details/b28775557_0018 (contributor "Wellcome Library"), rights field:
  "This work is available under the Creative Commons, Public Domain Mark" (links
  http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/).
- Leaf: the text page "[1267] HEDERA Helix. Common Ivy." is leaf n179 (found in the djvu XML); the plate is the next
  leaf, n180: https://archive.org/details/b28775557_0018/page/n180/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/b28775557_0018/page/n180.jpg , saved as raw/englishbotany-v18-t1267-n180.jpg.
- Plate imprint, read on the scan: "Feby. 1. 1804. Published by Jas. Sowerby, London." Plate number 1267 top right.
- PD by age: published 1804; Sowerby died 1822, Smith 1828. US: before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1899
  at the latest.
- What it shows: the plant only (stem, leaves, flower head, berries, outline leaves, small flower and berry details),
  the plate number and the imprint. No figures, words beyond the imprint, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: "Hedera" is ivy; the plate shows flowers and berries; the carol names the ivy in its first line (story,
  "The ivy is named only here"). The text page says ivy is "the latest of all our flowering plants, blossoming in
  October and November, and ripening its berries in the spring" (not used in the caption; available if wanted).

## 2.jpg: Market House and Town Hall, Campden (photograph printed 1901)

- Item: "Transactions of the Bristol and Gloucestershire Archaeological Society", vol. 24, Bristol: The Society, 1901.
  Contributor Allen County Public Library Genealogy Center (sponsor Internet Archive); call number 31833006686171. The
  IA record has no rights or copyright-status field.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/transactionsofbr24bris . Page 49 ("CAMPDEN. 49", the report of the Society's
  meeting at Chipping Campden, August 1901) is leaf n76: https://archive.org/details/transactionsofbr24bris/page/n76/mode/1up ;
  file https://archive.org/download/transactionsofbr24bris/page/n76.jpg , saved as raw/bgas24-1901-n76.jpg (crop as
  raw/bgas24-1901-n76-crop.jpg). The volume's List of Illustrations has "Chipping Campden ... Market House and Town
  Hall . 49".
- The same plate is on Wikimedia Commons from the Internet Archive Book Images Flickr stream, "No known copyright
  restrictions" (File:Transactions of the Bristol and Gloucestershire Archaeological Society (1901) (14760617906).jpg);
  I used the holder's own scan, not Commons.
- Photographer: not named. The volume credits other plates in print ("H. Ormerod, Photo.", "F. F. Tuckett, Photo.",
  "From a photo by M. Lavington"), but the Campden plates on pp. 49 to 54 carry no credit line, and the text (OCR in
  raw/text/bgas24.txt) names none for them.
- PD reasoning: a photograph published 1901 with no author named. US: published before 1931. EU: for an anonymous
  work, 70 years from lawful publication (Term Directive art. 1(3)), ended 1971; UK the same. Israel: the Copyright
  Act 2007 gives anonymous works 70 years from publication, ended 1971. Even if a photographer were later identified,
  he would have had to live past 1955 for any term to remain, for a photo taken by 1901.
- This is a photograph of a building, so it rests on the photo's own public-domain status, as above.
- What it shows: the Market House (open arcade), a clock-turret building behind, a house with a shop front ("STATIONER"
  sign, illegible at our size) on the right, and posters on one pier (one reads "ASCOT"). No legible advertising or
  trademark front and centre; no person clearly visible (at most a speck by the town hall steps). Printed caption "MARKET HOUSE AND TOWN HALL, CAMPDEN."
  Every word on the rest of p. 49 (cropped out) is the meeting report about the church and the town; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the place is Chipping Campden (story: "On 13 January 1909, at Chipping Campden, a Cotswold town, he
  noted songs from Mrs Mary Anne Clayton" [^fees1988][^glostrad-clayton][^sutcliffe-clayton]). The caption says only
  that she sang there, in the town, not at this building. The page itself says the town owes "its picturesque Market
  House, built in 1627" to Baptist Hicks (p. 50), not used in the caption.

- Record: https://archive.org/details/englishfolkcarol00shar . Page 17 is leaf n34 (as in the story's [^sharp1911],
  "images n34 to n35"): https://archive.org/details/englishfolkcarol00shar/page/n34/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/englishfolkcarol00shar/page/n34.jpg , saved as raw/sharp1911-n34.jpg. Also saved for the

  Chipping Campden." at the foot of p. 63, the "obviously incorrect" sentence at the top of p. 64).
- PD by age: published 1911; Sharp, who wrote the accompaniment and notes, died 1924. US: before 1931. EU and Israel:
  life plus 70 ended in 1994. The tune and words are traditional.
- What it shows: type and music only: "VII. THE HOLLY AND THE IVY.", "Andante moderato", "Play 6 times.", verse 1 and
  the start of the refrain, page number 17. No figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the story says Sharp printed it "as No. VII of the book, on pages 17 and 18, for voice and piano, in
  G and 3/4, marked Andante moderato", and "Our arrangement reads the tune and verses 1 to 3 ... from Sharp's No. VII"

## 4.jpg: English Botany, vol. 7 (London, 1798), plate 496, Ilex Aquifolium, Common Holly

- Same work and holder as 1.jpg: Wellcome Collection https://wellcomecollection.org/works/xbjj5xhu, "Public Domain
  Mark", "You can use this work for any purpose without restriction under copyright law."; IA record
  https://archive.org/details/b28775557_0007, rights "This work is available under the Creative Commons, Public Domain
  Mark".
- Vol. VII title page (leaf n8, saved as raw/englishbotany-v7-title-n8.jpg): "London: printed by J. Davis, and sold by
  the Proprietor J. Sowerby ... MDCCXCVIII" (1798). The plate imprint reads "... Published by Js. Sowerby London" with
  the date too faint to read with certainty, so the date given is the volume's, 1798. Smith's preface is dated
  "Norwich, Nov. 1, 1797".
- Leaves: text page "[496] ILEX Aquifolium. Common Holly." is n265 (raw/englishbotany-v7-t496text-n265.jpg); the
  plate is n266: https://archive.org/details/b28775557_0007/page/n266/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/b28775557_0007/page/n266.jpg , saved as raw/englishbotany-v7-t496-n266.jpg.
- PD by age: published 1798; Sowerby died 1822, Smith 1828. US: before 1931. EU and Israel: ended 1899 at the latest.
- What it shows: a holly sprig in flower, a single flower, a cut berry and seeds, the plate number 496 and the imprint.
  No figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts, from the text page (n265; OCR in raw/text/eb7.txt): "In woods, hedges and thickets not uncommon,
  flowering in May, the berries ripening in autumn" and "The branches of this tree laden with berries, and mixed with
  misseltoe and the spindle-tree, are used in many parts of England to ornament churches and houses at Christmas, and
  hence the holly is in Norfolk vulgarly called Christmas." The page prints the long s ("ufed", "houfes",
  "Chriftmas"); the caption quotes it in modern letters. The story (Verse 2) says holly has "small, dull white flowers in
  May and June" [^trees][^pfaf].
