# Bully in the Alley: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. All four are public domain by age: three printed pages from books the story cites, and one
photograph (the only photograph in the set, with no people in it). Records saved beside this file in pages/:
sharp_meta.json and hotten_meta.json (Internet Archive metadata), hotten_djvu.txt (OCR of Hotten), rijks_record.json and
rijks_parent.json (the Rijksmuseum's linked-data records for the page and the book).

## 3.jpg: Hotten, The Slang Dictionary (1874), p. 89 (detail)

- Item: https://archive.org/details/cu31924086360488 (scan image n106, printed page 89; running head "THE SLANG
  DICTIONARY. 89").
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/cu31924086360488/page/n106.jpg (1396 x 2002).
- IA record: title "The slang dictionary: etymological, historical, and anecdotal"; creator "Hotten, John Camden,
  1832-1873"; publisher "London, Chatto and Windus"; date 1874; contributor "Cornell University Library"; call_number
  "PE3721 .H7 1874"; collections cornell, americana; no rights field.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning:
  - Published London 1874; author John Camden Hotten, died 1873 (the 1874 edition appeared after his death; any reviser
    adult in 1874 would have had to live past 1956, past 100, to keep it in copyright in the EU).
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70 (1873 + 70) ended 31 December 1943.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Crop and words read: three lines only: "Bob, a shilling. Formerly BOBSTICK, which may have been the original.
  BOB-A-NOB, a shilling a-head." and "Bob, "s'help me BOB," a street oath, equivalent to "so help me God."". A trace of
  the line above and the tops of the line below show as unreadable specks at the edges.
- Left out on purpose: the rest of the "Bob" entry, which goes on "...all equally ridiculous. Ignorant people have a
  singular habit of saying "so help my," ..." and, on p. 90, "This shows how little they think of the meanings of the
  phrases most in use among them" (it sneers at street speakers); and p. 90 entirely, whose entries "Bog-Oranges" and
  "Bog-Trotter" mock the Irish. The rest of p. 89 (Blue Moon to Boat) was read; nothing in the crop demeans anyone.

## Considered and not used

- Sharp's note on p. 71 (scan n90): "No. 35. BULLY IN THE ALLEY. Sung by Mr. John Short, at Watchet. I have no
  variants of this nor do I know of any printed version of it." Clean (the whole page was read: notes 32 to 38 and a
  footnote on Ranzo/Rantzau); a good substitute for 3.jpg if the strip is too thin for the layout. Not made.
- The left-hand Watchet oval on the same Rijksmuseum page (harbour with pier and rails): small dark specks on the
  beach that might be figures; not used.
- Modern Watchet photographs on Commons: Geograph files are CC BY-SA (not allowed); Adrian Pingstone's 2004 marina
  photo is public domain but modern and not one of the allowed licence values; "Watchet.jpg" is CC BY-SA 2.5.
- The 1833 "Long Time Ago" sheet music and anything from the minstrel stage: never.
- The 1897 Darkness and Daylight "Shinbone Alley" plate: a slum-tour book; not used.
