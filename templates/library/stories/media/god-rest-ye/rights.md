# God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-12. raw/ was empty at the start (no earlier downloads to reuse). Originals in raw/; finals made with
sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata), resized to 1600 px on the long side, nothing cropped.

All three are Internet Archive scans of flat printed pages with no licence field in the record, so each rests on public
domain by age, with the reasoning below. A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright: in the US under
Bridgeman v. Corel, and in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive and for lack of originality; Israel likewise
requires originality.

## 1.jpg: Sandys, Christmas Carols, Ancient and Modern (London, 1833), p. 102

- Item: "Christmas carols, ancient and modern; including the most popular in the west of England, and the airs to which
  they are sung", London, R. Beckley, 1833. Creator "Sandys, William, 1792-1874". Contributor University of California
  Libraries (sponsor MSN); call number nrlf_ucb:GLAD-17147400. IA field "possible-copyright-status": NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT.
  The record's description is "Photocopy": UC's copy is a photocopy of the 1833 book, which is what was scanned.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsa00sandrich (metadata
  https://archive.org/metadata/christmascarolsa00sandrich). Page 102 is leaf 250 in the item's page_numbers.json:
  https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n249/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n249.jpg , saved as raw/sandys1833-p102-n249.jpg.
- PD by age: published 1833; Sandys died 1874. US: before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1944.
- What it shows: type only, page 102: the end of the previous carol, then "GOD REST YOU MERRY, GENTLEMEN." and two
  verses. No figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the page itself (comma after "merry"; "For Jesus Christ our Saviour Was born upon this day"; "Jury").
  The story: "His book of 1833 ... printed the carol in its second part, 'carols still used in the West of England'"
  and "Sandys put it after 'merry'" [^sandys1833]; this is the item and pages it cites (pp. 102 to 104).

## 2.jpg: Chappell, Popular Music of the Olden Time, vol. 2, p. 753

- Item: "Popular music of the olden time", vol. 2, London: Cramer, Beale & Chappell, IA date [1859?]. Creators
  "Chappell, W. (William), 1809-1888" and "Macfarren, G. A. (George Alexander), Sir, 1813-1887". Contributor Robarts
  - University of Toronto (sponsor MSN); call number ACM-1703. IA field "possible-copyright-status": NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/popularmusicofol02chapuoft . Page 753 is leaf 377 in page_numbers.json:
  https://archive.org/details/popularmusicofol02chapuoft/page/n376/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/popularmusicofol02chapuoft/page/n376.jpg , saved as raw/chappell-v2-p753-n376.jpg.
- PD by age: published in parts 1855 to 1859. Chappell died 1888, Macfarren (harmonies) 1887, and Edward F. Rimbault,
  who noted the tune down, 1876 (Wikidata Q2565796, 1816-1876). US: before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in
  1958 at the latest.
- What it shows: page 753, "TRADITIONAL TUNES OF UNCERTAIN DATE.", text, then the tune with piano, "Moderate time".
  Type and music only; no figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts, all on the page: "The carol is sometimes sung in a major key, and sometimes in a minor" and "Having
  printed Hone's version, and one in a major key, in National English Airs, I now give a third copy, noted down by Dr.
  Rimbault." The tune printed is the minor tune of our arrangement (same opening and refrain as Stainer's No. 1). The
  story quotes the same sentence [^chappell].
- **Different copy from the story's link.** The story's [^chappell] links the National Library of Scotland copy,
  https://archive.org/details/popularmusicov2185559chap . That copy is NOT usable: its record says "National Library of
  Scotland holds full rights in this digital resource and agrees to license the resource under the Creative Commons
  License: Attribution-Noncommercial-Share Alike 2.5 UK: Scotland" (licenseurl
  http://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/2.5/scotland/). I downloaded nothing from it. The Toronto copy is the
  same work and edition, with no such claim. The lead may want to point [^chappell] at the Toronto copy too.

## 3.jpg: Bramley and Stainer, Christmas Carols New and Old (London, 1870s printing), No. 1, p. 2

- Item: "Christmas carols, new and old : [1st series]", London: Novello, Ewer, IA date [187?]; "44 p. ; 19 cm"; "22
  carols, with music". Creators "Stainer, John, Sir, 1840-1901" and "Bramley, Henry Ramsden, 1833-1916". Contributor
  University of California Libraries (sponsor MSN); call number srlf_ucla:LAGE-3239423. IA field
  "possible-copyright-status": NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsn00staiiala . The item has no page numbers; I identified leaf n7 as
  printed page "(2)" by its footer (n8 is "(3)", the other six verses). Page:
  https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsn00staiiala/page/n7/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/christmascarolsn00staiiala/page/n7.jpg (1712 x 2844), saved as raw/bramley-p2-n7.jpg.
- PD by age: first series published November 1867 (story); this printing 1870s. Stainer died 1901, Bramley 1916. US:
  before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1986.
- What it shows: "1." and "God rest you, merry Gentlemen." with four-part music and words; type and music only. This
  undated printing has no pictures on this page (the illustrated edition of 1871 is a different book). No figures,
  logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the story: "God rest you merry opens the book as No. 1, in four parts in E minor" and "Our arrangement
  reads Stainer's four parts for No. 1 in a printing of the 1870s, in E minor as printed" [^bramley]; the first
  series came out in November 1867 [^mt1867][^hcc-bs]. This is the copy the story cites.

## Rejected

- Three New Christmas Carols, Bow Church Yard, [1760?] (IA bim_eighteenth-century_three-new-christmas-caro_1760, a
  microfilm scan): the sheet is framed by six woodcuts of saints (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Peter, Paul), drawn human
  figures. A crop of the text alone was possible, but the microfilm's holder and terms are not stated on the IA record
  (contributor "Internet Archive", source "microfilm"), so it is not clearly OK. Dropped.
- Wellcome Collection's records for the 1760, 1770 and 1780 "Three new Christmas carols" sheets (works dqze32dq,
  p3d2sh5a, v9ub4yp8) are online only through a licensed database (ECCO), with no free image of the holder's own.
- Four Choice Carols for Christmas Holidays (British Library, Roxburghe 3.452, EBBA 31130): EBBA's facsimile carries no
  free licence that I saw, and the sheet has woodcuts. Linked as "original" only, never hosted.
- Hone, Ancient Mysteries Described (1823), pp. 97 to 98, the carol list: PD and usable (IA ancientmysteries00honeuoft,
  NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT), but a list of first lines adds little beside 1.jpg. Not used.
- Chappell, National Library of Scotland copy: CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 Scotland (see 2.jpg).
