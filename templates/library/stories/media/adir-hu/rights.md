## Divine names (batch 8 rule): what each crop keeps and leaves out

Every printed word in each final was read, the Hebrew in zoomed strips at full scan size.

- **1.jpg keeps** (Prague 1526, image n80, top of the page): "חסל סדור פסח / כהלכתו ככל / משפטו וחקתו כאשר זכינו /
  לסדר אותו כן נזכה לעשותו / זך שוכן מעונה קומם קהל / מי מנה קרב נהל נטעי כנה / פדוים לציון ברנה" (Chasal siddur
  Pesach), then the opening of Adir Hu, its first letter in a woodcut block: "[א]דיר הוא יבנה ביתו / בקרוב במהרה
  בימינו". No divine name. **Left out:** the next line, "בקרוב׃ אל בנה אל בנה בנה ביתו בקרוב", where אל (El) is
  printed twice in large type as an aleph-lamed ligature (work/pr80-refrain.jpg shows it); and the three lines of
  epithets below it (בחור, גדול, דגול ... עזוז; no name, but under the El line). The crop ends at y 2165 of the scan,
  above the ligature's top at about y 2175; the bottom strip was checked at full size (work/c1-bottom.jpg).
- **2.jpg keeps** (Prague 1526, image n81, middle of the page): the Judeo-German Baugesang in Hebrew letters, ten lines:
  "אלמעכטיגר גוט בויא דיין טעמפל שירי אלזו שיר אין אונזרן ט[אגן] / טאגן שירי יא שירי", then seven rows of three epithets (two more end line 2)
  each followed by "גוט" (as far as the worn type reads: ברמהערציגר גוט, גרעכטר גוט, דעמטיגר גוט, הובער גוט, ווירדגר
  גוט, ... רייכר גוט, שטרקר גוט), then "אונ׳ בימבט מער אונ׳ בויא דיין טעמפל שירי אלזו שיר אין אונזרן / טאגן שירי יא שירי". In German:
  "Allmächtiger Gott, bau dein Tempel schiere, also schier in unsern Tagen schiere, ja schiere"; Barmherziger Gott,
  Gerechter Gott and so on. **No Hebrew divine name.** Two look-alikes, for the checker: "שירי" is German "schiere"
  (shin, yod, resh, yod), not שדי; "אלזו" is German "also", not El. **Gott (גוט) is shown**, 24 times: it is
  German, not one of the Hebrew names the rule lists, and the story itself prints "Allmächtiger Gott, nun bau dein'
  Tempel schiere" in both English and Spanish. If the lead counts Gott too, drop 2.jpg and 3.jpg. **Left out:** the
  top two lines in large type, "פודה צדיק קדוש רחום / שדי תקיף", where שַׁדַּי (Shaddai) is spelled in full; the boxed
  poem below the German (its columns end "רה"); and the colophon, which has the abbreviation "יי" and the preposition
  "אל" ("והבאתי אתכם אל הארץ").
- **3.jpg keeps** (JE p. 187, example A, first line only): "A", "ADDIR HU", "Ad - dir hu yib - neh be - to be - ka -
  rob, bim - he - rah be -", and in italics "Allmächtiger Gott, nun bau dein' Tem - pel bal - de, Ach bal - de! In".
  No Hebrew divine name (Gott as above). **Left out:** the second line, "ya - me - nu be - ka - rob, El..... be - neh",
  with El in transliteration; the third line; examples B (with "Shad - dai Hu" and "El, be-neh") and C (with "El, be -
  neh"); and the article prose.
- **4.jpg keeps** (Union Haggadah 1923, p. 81, top two systems): "Addir Hu", "CHORUS. Maestoso", "Traditional", "mf",
  "1. Addir hu, addir hu, yivneh ve-so b'- ko-rov,", "2. Bohur hu, godol hu, yohid hu, (Refrain)", "3. Tzaddik hu,
  kodosh hu, rahum hu, (Refrain)", "bim-he-roh, bim-he-roh b'- yo-me-nu b'- ko-rov,". No divine name. **Left out:**
  the third system, "el b'- ne, el b'- ne, b'ne ves-cho b'- ko-rov.", with El in transliteration twice (the crop ends
  at y 2245, above its accents at about y 2284), and the page number 81.

## 1.jpg and 2.jpg: Prague Haggadah, 1526

- Item: "Haggadah from Prague, from the year 1526", Internet Archive PragueHaggadah1526,
  https://archive.org/details/PragueHaggadah1526
  - Metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/PragueHaggadah1526): creator "Gershom Katz"; date "1526"; description: "An
    early printed Haggadah in the Ashkenazi tradition from Prague in 1526. This scan is a copy of the page images
    appearing at the National Library of Israel." The link is NLI Rosetta dps_pid=IE34061667.
  - Licence on the item page: "Public Domain Mark 1.0" (licenseurl http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/),
    set by the uploader (the Open Siddur Project's account), not by NLI. As for the 1590 Prague Haggadah on Echad Mi
    Yodea, the case rests on age.
  - The story's [^prague] cites these very images (n80 and n81) and reads the colophon on n81 as Tevet 5287, the
    winter of 1526 to 1527.
- Files: https://archive.org/download/PragueHaggadah1526/page/n80.jpg (2324 x 3597, raw/ia-prague1526-n80.jpg),
  cropped 1870 x 2015 at x 330, y 150; https://archive.org/download/PragueHaggadah1526/page/n81.jpg (2470 x 3579,
  raw/ia-prague1526-n81.jpg), cropped 1640 x 740 at x 360, y 680.
- Public domain by age: printed in Prague in 1526/27 by the brothers Gershom and Gronem ben Solomon ha-Kohen (named
  in the colophon on n81: "גרשם בן שלמה הכהן ... אחיו גרונם"), who died in the 16th century; the Hebrew hymn and the
  Judeo-German version are anonymous and older still. No term anywhere can still run: PD in the US (published before
  1931), the EU and Israel (life plus 70, or 70 years from publication for anonymous works). The scan is a faithful
  photograph of a flat page and adds no copyright: US (Bridgeman v. Corel), EU (DSM Directive 2019/790 art. 14),
  Israel (no originality in a faithful copy).
- Figures: none. 1.jpg's woodcut initial was checked enlarged (work/init-big.jpg): acanthus leaves, scrolls and
  perhaps a small bird at the top left; no human figure or face. 2.jpg is type only. Nothing demeaning on either: the
  Hebrew is the seder's closing poem and the hymn; the German is the hymn's words of praise and its plea.
- Caption facts: story, "A Judeo-German version" paragraph: "There it stands below the Hebrew hymn, in Hebrew letters,
  with its words of praise also in the order of the Hebrew alphabet: Barmherziger (merciful), Gerechter (just) and so
  on." The page shows the Hebrew hymn's last epithets ending at the top of n81 and the German below. For 1.jpg, the
  story's FAQ: "after the meal, the Hallel psalms and the words that close the formal service"; the page shows
  "חסל סדור פסח" directly before Adir Hu.

## 4.jpg: Union Haggadah, revised edition, 1923, p. 81

- Item: "The Union Haggadah :home service for the Passover /", Internet Archive unionhaggadahhom00unse,
  https://archive.org/details/unionhaggadahhom00unse (the same book as Echad Mi Yodea 3.jpg).
  - Metadata (re-read today): date "c1923"; publisher "[s.l.] : The Conference"; contributor "University of Florida,
    George A. Smathers Libraries"; "possible-copyright-status": "Copyright status reviewed by UF staff - Out of
    copyright".
- File: https://archive.org/download/unionhaggadahhom00unse/page/n100.jpg (2392 x 3696, raw/ia-union1923-p81.jpg),
  cropped 1790 x 1945 at x 250, y 300.
- Public domain:
  - US: published in the US in 1923 with notice; US copyright in all works published in 1923 ended on 31 December 2018.
  - Who made the setting: the page says only "Traditional". The book's history (pp. 157 to 158) says the 1908 edition
    "contained the familiar Passover music, as edited by the Society of American Cantors"; the 1923 additions it lists
    by name (Lucas, Gutheim and Brandt, Singer, Davis, Cohen, "a variation of the 'Addir Hu' melody for Psalm CXVIII:
    1-4", Halevy, Naumbourg, Kaiser) do not include this page. So it is a collective edit with no named person.
  - EU and Israel: anonymous or collective, 70 years from publication: from 1908 it ended in 1978, from 1923 in 1993.
  - Residual risk, for the checker: if a named cantor were shown to have harmonised this page, life plus 70 would apply
    to him. None is named in the book or the story's sources.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page: no new copyright.
- Caption facts: story, "How it is sung": "Our arrangement is read from page 81 of the Union Haggadah, revised edition
  ... There it is headed 'Chorus', marked Maestoso and 'Traditional', in C major and 4/4, for voice and piano."
- No figure, no ornament, nothing demeaning.

## Rejected or not found

  (repository.library.brown.edu, bdr:759951) answers 403 and the item API asks for a login, so no rights statement or
  image could be read; Brown's "Use" page says the John Hay Library "does not hold copyright" and cannot grant
  permission. Not used. The Braginsky copy (337) is a private collection. The Internet Archive's
  LibraVeritatisEtDePaschateTractatus is the 1698 reprint, which the story does not cite. The JE transcription (3.jpg)
  stands in for it.
- Prague 1526 n80 bottom (the El refrain and epithets) and n81 top (Shaddai), the n81 colophon: see the crop notes above.
- JE examples B and C: El and Shaddai in transliteration in every line after the first.
- No photograph: none needed; no place or object photograph with a clear free licence was looked for beyond this.

## Gott, for the lead

and the story prints "Allmächtiger Gott" itself (en and es). If Gott is to be treated as a name, drop both and the
set is 1.jpg and 4.jpg (still two images).
