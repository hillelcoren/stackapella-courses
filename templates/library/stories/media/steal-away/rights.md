# Steal Away: licence evidence for each image

Gathered 2026-11-13. www.loc.gov item pages answer 403 (a bot check) to plain requests from here, so the Library of
Congress rights words below were read from each item's own catalogue record, served by the Library at
lccn.loc.gov/<LCCN>/marcxml (field 540). The image files came straight from the Library (tile.loc.gov master TIFFs).
A checker with a browser should open the two loc.gov item pages and confirm the "Rights Advisory" line.

The Oklahoma Historical Society is not used or linked (coordinator's ruling).

## 1.jpg: Jubilee Singers, Fisk University, Nashville, Tenn. (carte de visite, 1870s)

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/item/2010647805/ (record read at https://lccn.loc.gov/2010647805/marcxml).
- Call number: LOT 14022, no. 75. Digital ID: LC-DIG-ppmsca-11008 (front), ppmsca-11009 (back).
- Downloaded: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/ppmsca/11000/11008u.tif (5126 x 3044).
- Record: "Black, James Wallace, 1825-1896, photographer." "[Nashville, Tenn.] : American Missionary Association,
  [between 1870 and 1880]". "1 photographic print on carte de visite mount : albumen ; 6 x 10 cm."
  "Photographer attribution from similar photograph: LOT 14105, no. 8."
  "Photo shows a studio group portrait of the Fisk University Jubilee Singers, with (from l. to r.) Minnie Tate, Greene
  Evans, Isaac Dickerson, Jennie Jackson, Maggie Porter, Ella Sheppard, Thomas Rutling, Benjamin Holmes, and Eliza
  Walker. (Source: similar photograph: LOT 14105, no. 8)". Purchased from William A. Gladstone, 1995.
- Rights statement (field 540): "No known restrictions on publication."
- Licence: LoC-no-known-restrictions.
- PD reasoning as a backstop: made 1870 to 1880; photographer James Wallace Black died 1896.
  - US: made and issued (a commercial carte de visite) before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70, 1896 + 70 = 1966; public domain.
- Spirituals note: a dignified studio portrait of the singers, students in the 1870s and so born around 1850 at the
  latest; all died long ago. Not a photograph of enslaved people (it is a concert troupe's studio card). Nothing is printed on the front of the
  mount. Names given exactly as the Library's record gives them.

## 2.jpg: Addison Avenue looking north at Fisk University, showing Jubilee Hall (about 1899)

- Item: https://www.loc.gov/item/90712951/ (record read at https://lccn.loc.gov/90712951/marcxml); handle
  http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.pnp/cph.3c00551.
- Call number: LOT 11299 (African American Photographs Assembled for 1900 Paris Exposition). Reproduction number:
  LC-USZ62-100551.
- Downloaded: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/cph/3c00000/3c00000/3c00500/3c00551u.tif (4096 x 3270),
  a scan of the copy negative; cropped to the photograph (the copy negative's black frame and the handwritten
  "100551" were cut away). Nothing else changed.
- Record title: "[Addison Avenue looking north at Fisk University, Nashville, Tennessee, showing Jubilee Hall, Fisk
  memorial chapel, and the Daniel Hand Model School]", "[1899?]", "1 photographic print."
- Rights statement (field 540): "No known restrictions on publication."
- Licence: LoC-no-known-restrictions. A photograph of buildings, so it needs the photographer's own free status: the
  Library states no known restrictions, and by age:
  - US: photographer not named; made about 1899 and shown publicly at the Paris Exposition of 1900. Published before
    1931, so public domain; even if treated as unpublished, an anonymous work made 1899 is public domain (120 years
    from creation).
  - EU: anonymous work, 70 years from being made available (1900 + 70 = 1970); public domain.
  - Israel: same (70 years for anonymous works); public domain.
- Wikimedia Commons carries the same file as "Public domain", citing the Library (not relied on; file taken from the
  Library).
- Caption source: Fisk University, "History" (https://www.fisk.edu/about/history/, a source of the story, [^fisk]):
  heading "1876 Jubilee Hall is Built"; "The Fisk Jubilee Singers® ... raised funds that preserved their University and
  permitted construction of Jubilee Hall". Which building is Jubilee Hall: the Library's title names it, and the large
  brick building with a tower at the end of the street matches the HABS survey photographs of Jubilee Hall
  (HABS TENN,19-NASH,7A).

## 3.jpg: Jubilee Songs (1872), page 28, "Steal Away."

- Item: https://archive.org/details/29208201.4853.emory.edu (page image n30, printed page 28). The story's
  earliestPrinting and "original" link name this exact page.
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/29208201.4853.emory.edu/page/n30.jpg (2820 x 4349, bitonal scan).
- Holder: Emory University, Manuscript, Archives and Rare Book Library, barcode 010000882754, OCLC 29208201.
- Rights statement (Internet Archive record, "description"): "The online edition of this book in the public domain,
  i.e., not protected by copyright, has been produced by the Emory University Digital Library Publications Program".
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning: published New York, Biglow & Main, 1872 (record "date": "1872"). Creators per the record: Theodore F.
  Seward, 1835-1902; George L. White, 1838-1895; American Missionary Association; Fisk University.
  - US: published 1872, before 1931; public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70; the last named creator died 1902 (1902 + 70 = 1972); public domain.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright.
- Words on the page read in full: title, the refrain and four verses, "CHO.--Steal away, &c." Only "haint"/"hain't",
  an old spoken form the story itself quotes; no caricature dialect, no other headings, no adverts, no figures.

## 4.jpg: Pike, The Singing Campaign for Ten Thousand Pounds (1875), page 33

- Item: https://archive.org/details/singingca00pike (page image n56, printed page 33). Story source [^pike1875] cites
  "the London report of the concert, pp. 33 to 34".
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/singingca00pike/page/n56.jpg (2100 x 3504).
- Holder: Princeton Theological Seminary Library (call number in IA record "SCB"; bookplate "From the library of Rev.
  Louis FitzGerald Benson, D. D., bequeathed by him to the Library of Princeton Theological Seminary"). IA record
  carries no licence restriction.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning: title page "New York: American Missionary Association ... 1875", "Revised edition"; "Entered, according
  to Act of Congress, in the year 1875, by The American Missionary Association". Author Gustavus D. Pike, born 1831,
  died 29 January 1885 in Hartford, Connecticut (The American Missionary, vol. 39 no. 3, March 1885, "Death of Rev.
  G. D. Pike, D.D."; Project Gutenberg ebook 30459).
  - US: published 1875; public domain.
  - EU and Israel: life + 70, 1885 + 70 = 1955; the quoted London report is anonymous, 1873; public domain.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright.
- Words on the page read in full: the day of the concert, the guests (the Duke and Duchess of Argyll, Lady Edith
  Ashley and others), Lord Shaftesbury's remarks, and the start of the music paper's report ("They arranged themselves
  in front of the platform in a phalanx three deep ... Their first song was, 'Steal away to Jesus.' It was sung
  slowly; the first chords came floating on our senses like gentle fairy music ..."). Nothing demeaning; no figures.
  The caption's date and place come from the invitation card printed on p. 31 ("At Willis's Rooms, On Tuesday Next,
  the 6th of May", dated "April 30th, 1873").

## Looked at and not used

- New York Public Library item 510d47da-7338-a3d9-e040-e00a18064a99 (a Fisk / Jubilee Hall image): the site answered
  with a bot wall, so its rights could not be read. Dropped.
- The National Archives (UK), COPY 1/25/182 photograph of the singers (1874), which the story cites: not pursued,
  since its reuse terms were not checked and the Library of Congress portrait (1.jpg) is clearly free.
- Pike 1875, p. 31, the printed invitation card to the Willis's Rooms concert: plain type, fine, but page 33 names
  Steal Away and carries the story's own quote, so it was chosen instead.
- Spencer Academy (Choctaw Nation), where the Willis tradition places Wallace and Minerva: not searched further, as the
  Oklahoma Historical Society may not be used or linked.
- Book frontispieces of the singers were not looked at: drawn or engraved figures are never used.
