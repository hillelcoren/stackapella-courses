## 2.jpg: Piae Cantiones (Greifswald, 1582), Runeberg page "94", Tempus adest floridum begins

- Copy: National Library of Finland. Scanned by Helsinki University Library (now the National Library), 2006, for its
  Doria "Helmi" collection, item fem19990004 (old page http://s1.doria.fi/helmi/bk/rv/fem19990004/ , read in the
  Wayback Machine, capture 2025-10-07, saved as pages/doria-helmi-index-wayback.html; its footer reads "Helsingin
  yliopiston kirjasto 2006"). The item's current Doria and Finna records sit behind a Cloudflare check (403), so I
  could not read the holder's own rights field.
- Host: Project Runeberg, https://runeberg.org/piaecant/ (saved as pages/runeberg-index.html): "It was digitized in
  Finland. The scanned images were copied in December 2014 from Doria, a web site of the National library of Finland.
  The pagination used here is the same that Doria uses." Page https://runeberg.org/piaecant/0099.html (saved as
  pages/runeberg-0099.html); file https://runeberg.org/img/piaecant/0099.1.jpg (1734 x 1408, the opening of pages 93
  and 94), saved as raw/runeberg-0099.1.jpg.
- Host's terms: Runeberg's "What is Copyright?" page, https://runeberg.org/admin/copyright.html (saved as
  pages/runeberg-copyright.html): "Project Runeberg publishes electronic editions of Nordic literature. In most cases,
  this means literature by Nordic authors who died more than 70 years ago." and "When one of Project Runeberg's
  editions is covered by copyright, a text of warning appears at the top of each page together with the copyright
  symbol, the letter C in a ring." No such warning appears on the index, 0099 or 0100 (checked: no "copyright", ©
  or "upphovsrätt" in any of the three pages).
- Second sign: Wikimedia Commons hosts a small combined copy of the same two Doria pages,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tempus_adest_floridum.jpg (360 x 680), tagged PD-Art, "Digitalized by
  Finnish National Library". Not used (too small); evidence only.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning: printed in Greifswald in 1582; everyone who made it died before 1700. PD in the US, the EU and Israel by
  age. The scan is a faithful reproduction of a flat page (see top).
- One point for the checker: Finland's Copyright Act gives ordinary photographs a separate 50-year right (s. 49a);
  the DSM Directive's Art. 14 removes protection for reproductions of public-domain visual art, and a printed page may
  or may not count as that. The host presents it as free, the brief allows it if the host's terms allow reuse, and the
  scan is 2006; I flag it, I do not settle it.
- Crop: the right-hand page only (sips crop 872 x 1408 from x 862), so the left page (93) is left out. Page 93
  ends a song about Finland's conversion whose lines speak of driving out the "Daemonum" cult; I kept it off our page.
- What it shows, every word read: the end of No. LXXIII "In vernali tempore" (its tune and verses: spring, birds,
  sun, "O quam mira gloria, quantus decor Dei"), the handwritten number "LXXIV", a decorated initial T, and the first
  staff of Tempus adest floridum with "Tempus adest floridum, surgunt" and "Vernales in omnibus, imi-", catchword
  "namq;", signature "N 3". The initial T is foliage; I looked at it at full size (work/initial.png) and saw no human
  figure, but it is only 170 px wide in the scan, so the checker should look too. If ruled out, use alternates/p95.jpg
  (see below). No logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the story's "The tune." paragraph ([^pc1582]): Tempus adest floridum is the last song, "under the
  heading 'De tempore vernali cantiones'", in "the National Library of Finland's copy"; "Neale ignored the spring
  words and set his Bohemian legend to the tune" ([^hymnarchive][^obc]).

## 3.jpg: The Condensed Vocal Parts (London, 1854), Index and Notice

- Holder: Harvard University, digitized by Google; HathiTrust item hvd.hxcs75, record 011630024

- Record fields (exact): "titles": "The condensed vocal parts to the carols for Christmas-tide"; MARC 260 "London,
  1854."; 300 "pp. (2), 96."; OCLC 77762978; item "orig": "Harvard University", "htid": "hvd.hxcs75", "rightsCode":
  "pd", "usRightsString": "Full view"; MARC 974 "s google", "r pd", "t non-US bib date1 < 1901".
- File: the same scan as Wikimedia Commons, "File:The Condensed Vocal Parts to the Carols for Christmas-tide.pdf"
  (source given there: https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/011630024; author Neale and Helmore; date 1854; licence
  {{PD-old-70-1923}}; record saved as pages/commons-cvp.json), downloaded from
  https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/The_Condensed_Vocal_Parts_to_the_Carols_for_Christmas-tide.pdf
  and saved as raw/cvp1854.pdf (113 PDF pages). This is the scan the story cites ([^cvp1854], PDF pp. 9, 10 and 43 to
  46). PDF p. 10 rendered at 6x to raw/cvp-pdf10.png.
- Licence: PD-old.
- Reasoning: published London 1854 (title page, PDF p. 9: "London: Printed by J. Alfred Novello ... 1854."). Neale
  died 1866 (story [^julian][^hymnarchive]), Helmore on 6 July 1890 (Wikidata Q246073, saved as
  pages/wikidata-Q246073.json). US: before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1960.
- Watermark: the "Digitized by Google" mark at the foot is cropped out (rows 250 to 2450 of 2823 kept). No figures,
  ornaments or logos remain.
- What it shows, every word read: the Index of twelve carols, I to XII ("Here is joy for every age" ... "Toll! toll!
  because there ends"), and the Notice: "We have put forth a fuller account of these Carols in the harmonised Folio
  Edition. It is here sufficient to observe: that the original words and music range from the end of the twelfth to
  the beginning of the fifteenth century: that we give the music without alteration: and that the words are free
  imitations, for the most part, of these and other ancient Christmas Carols. T. H. J. M. N. SS. Simon and Jude,
  1853." Nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the story's "The carol." paragraph ([^cvp1854]): "A small edition of the vocal parts followed, dated
  1854 on its title page, with a notice by both men, dated on the feast of Saints Simon and Jude, 1853, saying they
  had already 'put forth' the harmonised folio edition." The caption says no more, and does not repeat the Notice's own
  claim about the centuries.

## 4.jpg: The Condensed Vocal Parts (London, 1854), No. XI, "Good King Wenceslas", p. 37

- Same item, record, file and licence as 3.jpg. PDF p. 43 rendered at 6x to raw/cvp-pdf43.png.
- Crop: rows 380 to 2650 of 2828, cutting off the "Digitized by Google" mark under "(37)". No figures, ornaments or
  logos remain.
- What it shows, every word read: "XI." "Good King Wenceslas." and verse 1, "Good King Wenceslas look'd out, On the
  Feast of Stephen; When the snow lay round about, Deep, and crisp, and even: Brightly shone the moon that night,
  Though the frost was cruel, When a poor man came in sight, Gath'ring winter fuel.", "(37)". Four systems of two
  staves, three sharps.
- Caption facts: the story's "How it is sung" ([^cvp1854][^bramley]): "Our arrangement reads the tune and verses 1, 4
  and 5 from No. XI of The Condensed Vocal Parts to the Carols for Christmas-tide (London, 1854), in A as printed
  there"; "The full words": "No. XI, pages 37 to 40".
