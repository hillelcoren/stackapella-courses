- Page: IA leaf n199, headed "Trinity Sunday." and "Hymn 135."; file
  https://archive.org/download/hymnsancientmode00unse/page/n199.jpg (2382 x 3287), saved as raw/ham1861_n199.jpg.
  Whole page, resized.
- Public domain by age: published London 1861. Tune and harmony by John Bacchus Dykes (died 22 January 1876, per
  the story [^dnb-dykes][^fowler]); words by Heber (died 1826); musical editor Monk (died 1889). US: published
  before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 for Monk, the last to die, ended at the end of 1959. Faithful scan of a
  flat page, as above.
- What it shows: the music in three systems (four sharps), "A-men.", the Revelation 4:8 line, Heber's four verses
  ending "Amen.", and a past owner's pencil tick "V" and a mark at top left. No figures, ornaments with figures,
  logos or watermarks. Every word read: the hymn and the Bible verse; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the story: "In the book of 1861, Heber's hymn is No. 135, under Trinity Sunday, with Dykes's tune
  and his own harmony" [^ham1861][^hymnary-nicaea]; NICAEA was "written for the first edition of Hymns Ancient and
  Modern in 1861"; and "Hymns Ancient and Modern printed Revelation 4:8 above the hymn in 1861" [^ham1861]. The
  page does not name the tune (tunes were unnamed in 1861), hence "now called NICAEA".
