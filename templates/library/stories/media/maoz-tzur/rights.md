  https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/912984 (saved as raw/met-912984.json):
  "isPublicDomain": true, "primaryImage": "https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/es/original/DP-42314-003.jpg".
- Downloaded file: that primaryImage, 4000 x 3958, saved as raw/met-912984-DP-42314-003.jpg.
- Licence: CC0. The Met's API documentation, https://metmuseum.github.io/ : the API "gives access to all of The Met's
  Open Access data and to corresponding high resolution images (JPEG format) that are in the public domain"; under
  "Creative Commons Zero (CC0)": "To the extent possible under law, The Metropolitan Museum of Art has waived all
  copyright"; the datasets are "for unrestricted commercial and noncommercial use"; "isPublicDomain ... When "true"

  unrestricted use under Creative Commons Zero (CC0)". The photograph DP-42314-003 is the Met's own (DP = Met

- The object itself: made 1788, maker died 1808: public domain everywhere by age (and silverwork of 1788 predates any
  copyright law that could still apply).
- What it shows: the lamp in three-quarter view on a grey ground; embossed scrolls, flowers and leaves; eight oil
  spouts; one cup on an arm. A tiny maker's mark is stamped on the backplate (a hallmark, not a trademark). No people,

- Caption facts: lamp facts from the Met record; "in Ashkenazi homes it is sung once the Hanukkah lights are kindled"

## 2.jpg: A. Z. Idelsohn, Sefer ha-Shirim (5672 = 1911 to 1912), No. 83, p. 77

- Item: Sefer ha-shirim : kovets shirim ivrim le-gane yeladim, le-vate-sefer amamiyim ve-tikhonim, by A. Z. Idelsohn
  (IA "creator": "Idelsohn, A. Z. 1882-1938."), publisher "Berlin : Hevrat ha-Ezrah li-Yehude Germaniyah" (the
  Hilfsverein der deutschen Juden), IA "date_created": 1912; OCLC 18648555. "Unaccompanied melodies for 1-4 voices.
  Music printed right to left." Copy: George Washington University Libraries, "Presented by the Dr. Ari Kiev family
  to the I. Edward Kiev Judaica Collection"; scan sponsor George Washington University Libraries.
- Item page: https://archive.org/details/32882013391126-seferhashirimko ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/32882013391126-seferhashirimko/page/n88/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/32882013391126-seferhashirimko/page/n88.jpg (1542 x 2435), saved as
  raw/id1912-n88.jpg. Metadata saved as raw/id1912-meta.json. The IA record carries no rights statement of its own.
- Public domain by age:
  - Published Berlin 1911 to 1912 (5672). Idelsohn, the compiler and arranger, died 1938. The tune is traditional and
    the words are a 13th-century poem.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU (Germany, country of publication): life plus 70 from 1938 ended at the close of 2008.
  - Israel: life plus 70 ended at the close of 2008 too.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright (EU DSM Directive art. 14; Bridgeman v. Corel in the US).
- What it shows: page 77, "83" and the title Maoz Tzur, the tempo words "halicha bechazaka" at the right and
  "neima mekubala" at the left, four systems of music with stanzas I and II under the notes, stanzas III to V below.
  No pictures, no ornaments, no stamps.
- Words read: only the hymn's own five stanzas, as the story translates them (stanza 4 tells the Purim story;
  stanza 6, the "Chasof zeroa" plea, is not printed). Nothing else is on the page.

## 3.jpg: Union Hymnal (1897), No. 107, "Rock of Ages (Chanuccah)", p. 117

- Item: Union Hymnal, "Text edited by the Central Conference of American Rabbis. Music selected and arranged by the
  Society of American Cantors" (title page). IA: "year": 1897, "possible-copyright-status": "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT",
  "copyright-region": "US", "Book digitized by Google from the library of Harvard University"; the scan's stamp reads
  Andover-Harvard Theological Library, Cambridge, Mass. OCLC 21513346.
- Item page: https://archive.org/details/unionhymnal00rabbgoog ; page:
  https://archive.org/details/unionhymnal00rabbgoog/page/n121/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/unionhymnal00rabbgoog/page/n121.jpg (2940 x 4196), saved as
  raw/uh1897-n121-p117.jpg. Pages 116 (n120, No. 106 "The Sun Goes Down") and 118 (n122, stanzas 2 and 3 of No. 107
  and No. 108 "The Mighty Deeds") also saved, for checking only.
- Google's front-matter page asks users to "Make non-commercial use of the files". That is a request attached to
  Google's PDF, not a licence on the public-domain page; earlier sets used Google scans of PD pages with the Google
  mark cropped (../IMAGE-CHECK-2.md). This leaf carries no Google mark and no library stamp, so nothing is cropped.
- Public domain by age:
  - Published in the United States in 1897, so public domain in the US.
  - The page names no author. The English words are credited elsewhere (1932 edition, Hymnary.org) to Marcus Jastrow
    (died 1903) and Gustav Gottheil (died 1903), after a German version by Leopold Stein (died 1882); the tune is
    traditional (the book's index: "Rock of Ages, let our song / Traditional"); the harmony is the collective work
    of the Society of American Cantors, published anonymously 128 years ago. EU and Israel: life plus 70 for the
    named authors ended by 1973; an anonymous work's 70 years from publication ended in 1967.
  - A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new copyright.
- What it shows: No. 107 complete with its music and verse 1. No pictures, no ornaments. Words read: "Rock of Ages,
  let our song / Praise Thy saving power; / Thou amidst the raging foes, / Wast our shelt'ring tower. / Furious they
  assailed us, / But Thine arm availed us, / And Thy word / Broke their sword / When our own strength failed us."
  Nothing demeaning. (Verses 2 and 3, on p. 118 and not shown, were read too: "Kindling new the holy lamps ...",
  "Children of the Martyr-race ... All men free, Tyrants disappearing.")
- Citation note for the lead: the story's [^uh1897] says "No. 107, 'Rock of Ages', pp. 116 to 117". In this scan
  No. 107 is on printed pages 117 (music and verse 1) and 118 (verses 2 and 3); page 116 is No. 106. The book's own
  index of first lines gives "116", which is probably where that came from.
  The book's index calls the tune "Traditional"; the story ([^uh1897][^je]) is the source for its being the Maoz
  Tzur tune.

## Rejected

- Isaac Nathan's Hebrew Melodies (London, 1815), the tune's earliest known printing: its words are Byron's "On
  Jordan's Banks", whose second line speaks of "the False One's votaries" praying on Zion's hill, a slur on another
  faith that sits under the notes. Not used.
- The Met's other Hanukkah lamps (889186, 928696, 192064, 751313): fine on rights, but 912984 is German, as the
  hymn is, and plain of any figure. Italian lamps often carry a figure of Judith; none was used.
- National Library of Israel manuscripts: not needed once the two printed pages the story cites were found.
