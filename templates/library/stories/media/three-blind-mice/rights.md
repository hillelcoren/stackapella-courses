# Three Blind Mice: rights evidence

All four images are faithful scans of flat printed pages; a faithful photograph of a flat public-domain work adds no
new copyright. Checked 2026-11-13. Every printed word on each crop was read (alts quote them in full).

## 1.jpg: Deuteromelia (London, 1609), title page
- Item: https://archive.org/details/bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_deuteromelia-or-the-sec_ravenscroft-thomas_1609/page/n0
- IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/bim_early-english-books-1475-1640_deuteromelia-or-the-sec_ravenscroft-thomas_1609):
  creator "Ravenscroft, Thomas."; date 1609; publisher "England : Printed [by T. Snodham] for Thomas Adams, dwelling in
  Paules Church-yard at the signe of the white Lion"; source "IA40312817-27, microfilm"; no rights or licenseurl field.
- Makers and death years: compiler Thomas Ravenscroft (preface signed only "T. R."), died about 1630 to 1635, by some
  guesses 1640 (Grove 1900; LC name authority n82020265, "1592?-1635?"); printer Thomas Snodham died 1624; bookseller
  Thomas Adams died 1620. The words and tunes are anonymous.
- PD: published 1609. US: before 1931. EU and Israel (life+70) and Mexico (life+100): every maker died before 1926.
- Content: title, Latin motto ("Qui canere potest canat"; "Vt Mel Os, sic Cor melos afficit, & reficit"), imprint.
  Nothing demeaning. The border is printers' flower ornament (interlaced leaves), checked at full size: no faces or
  figures. Cropped out: a later handwritten "Ravenscroft, Thomas" and "33" above the border, and a film frame mark.

## 2.jpg: Deuteromelia (London, 1609), p. 12, No. 13, second and third staves

## 3.jpg: Halliwell, The Nursery Rhymes of England (London, 1842), p. 43, No. LXX

  MDCCCXLII."
- IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/in.ernet.dli.2015.89222): title "Early English Poetry Ballads And Popular

  or licenseurl field.
- Makers and death years: editor James Orchard Halliwell (Halliwell-Phillipps) died 1889; printer T. Richards, at work
  in 1842 and so dead long before 1926; the rhyme is traditional.
- PD: published 1842. US: before 1931. EU, Israel, Mexico: all makers died before 1926.
- Content: three lines of the rhyme only (LXIX, LXXI and LXXII cropped out). The cut tails are words, not a picture.

## 4.jpg: Home Songs (Boston: Oliver Ditson Company, 1906), p. 95
- Item: https://archive.org/details/homesongscollect00unse_0/page/n100
- IA metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/homesongscollect00unse_0): date 1906; publisher "Boston : O. Ditson Co.";
  contributor and sponsor University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; call number 784.3 H752; notes "No copyright page
  found."; no rights or licenseurl field.
- Makers: none named for the round (words and tune traditional; no editor or arranger in the book). The page is the
  song's own source (song file credits: tune "traditional, as printed in Home Songs (Oliver Ditson, 1906)"), so it
  rides with the song page. Oliver Ditson died 1888. Even counted from publication, 1906 + 100 ended in 2006.
- PD: published 1906. US: before 1931. EU, Israel, Mexico: anonymous, published 120 years ago.
- Content: title, "(Round in Four Parts)", entry numbers 1 to 4, the 1906 words. Cropped out: "There's Music in the
  Air" above it (George F. Root, died 1895).
