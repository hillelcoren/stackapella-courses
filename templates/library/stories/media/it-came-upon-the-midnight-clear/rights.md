All four are faithful scans of flat printed pages. A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright

- Item: Christian Register 1849-12-29, vol. 28, issue 52. Internet Archive

  source "IA1519118-07, microfilm", ISSN 0503-2644, publisher field "Unitarian Universalist Association" (the later

  ; file .../page/n1.jpg (7624 x 9768, greyscale), saved as raw/ia-register1849-n1.jpg. This is the page our story
  cites as [^register1849] and as its "original".
- Crop: one column section only (x 4030, y 1060, 935 x 2560): the editor's note and the poem, ending at the rule
  under "E. H. S.". The rest of the page (an article on the Sabbath and the Jewish church, crime, the resurrection,
  slavery in Kentucky, and others) is cropped away.
- Every word on the crop: "If there is one grand strain that might be supposed to have floated down through the ages,
  from the celestial anthem sung over Bethlehem, we are almost ready to believe it was caught, interpreted and
  reported, in that glorious hymn printed in all our hymn-books, (any hymn-book would be fatally defective without
  it) beginning, "Calm on the listening ear of night." Hardly any circumstance has befallen us in the few months of
  our editorial duty, more agreeable, or for which we offer more eager thanks, than the reception of the following
  verses from the same writer." Then "CHRISTMAS CAROLS." and the five stanzas exactly as our story gives them, signed
  "E. H. S.". Nothing demeaning to anyone. Show-through from the reverse is faint and unreadable.
- PD reasoning: printed 1849. Poem by Edmund H. Sears, died 1876. The editor's note is unsigned; the story names the
  editor as John H. Morison; anyone writing as an editor in 1849 would have had to live past 1955 for it to be in
  copyright. US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 (or 70 years from publication if anonymous),
  long expired.
- Caption facts: story, "The words, 1849" and the paragraph beginning "The Register printed it" ([^register1849],
  [^canterbury]); earliest known printing: story frontmatter earliestPrinting.
