# Las Mañanitas: licence evidence

Sourced 2026-11-13. Every image opened and looked at in full. No drawn, painted or engraved human figure, and no
photograph, appears in any of the four. Every printed word left in each crop was read: the traditional song, García
Cubas's prose about the ride and Friedenthal's German prose and paraphrase; nothing demeans any people or faith.

Song file makers (templates/library/las-mananitas.json): Albert Friedenthal, edition, died 1921. Words and tune
traditional Mexican. The country of the song is Mexico, so every page was also checked for text by a non-maker still
protected under Mexico's life plus 100: none is left in any crop (see each entry).

- A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Article 14 of

## Rejected

- El laúd hispano-americano cover (n4): its design is "copyrighted March 1921 by C. Santos", whose dates are not
  found, so it cannot be cleared under Mexico's life plus 100. Dropped.
- García Cubas p. 374 (n375): the same tune again, under an engraving of Chapultepec castle; one page of the book is
  enough, and the engraving was not needed.
- The 1911 Vanegas Arroyo broadside at the Amon Carter Museum (1978.161, "Public domain" on its page): a type-metal
  engraving by José Guadalupe Posada, subject tags "musicians", "singing"; it carries engraved human figures. Not
  downloaded.
- Photograph of the old Basilica of Guadalupe, Abel Briquet, about 1885 to 1889 (SMU DeGolyer Library Ag1985.0372,
  https://digitalcollections.smu.edu/digital/collection/mex/id/3640): people stand in the foreground, and a crop
  above them still shows statues in niches and carved relief figures on the facade. Dropped.
- Library of Congress views titled "Guadaloupe" (LCCN 2009632144, W. H. Jackson; LCCN 2014688713, Bain): they appear
  to show Guadalupe, Zacatecas, not the basilica in Mexico City; place unclear, so dropped.
