- What it shows: verses 7 and 8 and the italic sign-off "Berryhead, September, 1847." Type only; no figures.
  Every word read; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the story's "Myth and record" paragraph: the Remains prints a letter from Berry Head dated
  25 August 1847, whose next line, published from the manuscript in The Times in 1947, sends "a few lines ... as my
  latest effusion", and "The lines were the hymn" ([^remains1850][^hymnarchive]); the sign-off is on the page.
- For the lead: the page prints "Berryhead" as one word, in italics. The story (EN and ES, "His family told the rest"
  paragraph) quotes the book as signing it "Berry Head, September, 1847". If that quote is meant to be the Remains'
  own words, it should read "Berryhead, September, 1847"; Julian may spell it as two words, so check which source the
  quote follows.

## Rejected

- Photographs of All Saints' church, Brixham (Lyte's church) on Wikimedia Commons: all but two are CC BY-SA 2.0
  (Geograph) or CC BY-SA 4.0, not allowed. The two CC0 files (File:Brixham (2022-04-18) 74.jpg and 79.jpg, own work,
  {{self|cc-zero}}) were dropped: 79 is only a garden gate; 74 shows the church tower down a street of cottages, and
  that tower was added between 1900 and 1906 (the listing text, as quoted by a third-party listings site, says the
  church was "largely rebuilt between 1884 and 1906"; Historic England's own page, list entry 1293023, answered 403),
  so it is not a building Lyte knew and the caption could not be both true and useful.
- Berry Head House (Lyte's home, now the Berry Head Hotel): the only Commons photograph is CC BY-SA 3.0. Rejected.
- Library of Congress photochroms of Brixham harbour, about 1890 to 1900 (LCCN 2002696425, 2002696427, 2002696428,
  via Commons, PD): every view has people in it (fishermen, boys, figures on the quay) and 2002696428 shows a
  statue on the quay. Rejected under the no-people rule.
- Portraits of Lyte or Monk: none used (engravings are drawn figures; no photograph wanted on this page).
