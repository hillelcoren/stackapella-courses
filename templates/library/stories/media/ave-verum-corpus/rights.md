## 1.jpg: Mozart's autograph score, leaf 1r (Vienna, ÖNB, Mus.Hs.18975/3)

- Item: "Ave Verum Corpus. Motette: [Für 4 Singstimmen, 2 Violinen, Viola, Baß und Orgel. KV 618]", Partitur,
  Autograph, Baden, dated 17.06.1791, brown ink, 23.5 x 32.6 cm. Catalogue: https://data.onb.ac.at/rec/AC14017296
  (Primo record ONB_alma21299433610003338, saved as pages/onb-pnx.json). Its "Volldigitalisat" link is
  https://data.onb.ac.at/rep/1321BF0C, which opens https://onb.digital/result/1321BF0C and the viewer
  https://viewer.onb.ac.at/1321BF0C.
- File: IIIF manifest https://api.onb.ac.at/iiif/presentation/v3/manifest/1321BF0C (pages/onb-manifest.json),
  canvas 12, image https://api.onb.ac.at/iiif/image/v3/1321BF0C/uk4nGb4kQnQgKXxd/full/max/0/default.jpg
  (3940 x 3012), saved as raw/onb-1321BF0C-c12.jpg. The manifest's requiredStatement: "Attribution: Austrian
  National Library"; provider "Department of Music". The set's 20 images also show the leather case and the other
  autographs kept with it; canvas 12 is the Ave verum's first page.
- What is on the page (read at full size): Mozart's heading "di Wolfgango Amadeo Mozart mp / Baaden. li 17 di
  giunnio 1791", the title "Ave verum Corpus", "Adagio", stave labels 2 Violini, Viola, Canto, Alto, Tenore, Basso,
  Organo e Basso, "Sotto voce" / "S:V:" at each voice, the words "Ave ave verum corpus natum de Maria virgine vere
  passum immolatum in", and at the foot "Ex rebus Joseph Schellhammer". A pencilled folio number "2" at top right.
  No figures, no decoration, no logos, nothing demeaning.
- Rights statement, the library's terms of use, https://www.onb.ac.at/nutzung (saved as pages/onb-nutzung.html):
  "Die Österreichische Nationalbibliothek hält auf ihren Websites Inhalte bzw. Digitalisate von ihren Beständen
  online abrufbar. An diesen Inhalten macht die Österreichische Nationalbibliothek kein eigenes urheberrechtliches
  Verwertungsrecht geltend. Sie erklärt sich insofern auch mit einer Nachnutzung dieser Inhalte in der abrufbaren
  Webauflösung ausdrücklich einverstanden." (It claims no copyright of its own in these contents and expressly
  agrees to their reuse at the resolution offered.) The only exception it names is scans made by Google, which are
  non-commercial for a time. This is a manuscript in the music department, not a Google book scan: IMSLP lists the
  same autograph as "PDF scanned by A-Wn" (the library's own siglum), file #293690. The library asks for a credit
  with title, date and the portal, "[Portal]/Österreichische Nationalbibliothek": our credit line gives it.
- Public domain by age:
  - Written by Mozart at Baden in June 1791; Mozart died 5 December 1791.
  - US: created and unpublished before 1931 by an author dead more than 70 years, so PD.
  - EU (Austria) and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1861.
  - A faithful photograph of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright (Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive,
    Austrian UrhG § 68a; Bridgeman v. Corel in the US).
  - Later hands on the page: "Ex rebus Joseph Schellhammer" (an owner's note, 19th century, not a work) and a pencil
    folio number. Nothing of later authorship.
- Caption facts: story, "The manuscript." ([^autograph], [^koechel1862], [^nma]): the heading as Köchel transcribed
  it, the title "Ave verum Corpus", "sotto voce" at each voice's entry, Schellhammer's name at the foot of the first
  page.
- Flag for the lead: the ÖNB record says "Partitur, Autograph, 3 Bl."; the story says two leaves, four written pages
  (from Köchel, the OMI brochure and the NMA). Not a caption claim, but worth knowing.

## 2.jpg and 3.jpg: André's first edition, about 1808 (Munich, BSB, 2 Mus.pr. 209)

- Item: "AVE VERUM CORPUS von W. A. Mozart. Komponirt in Baaden den 18.ten Junius 1791", Offenbach a. M., bei Joh.
  André, [1808], 7 S., "Preis 30 Xr.", No. 2478. Viewer: https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00107581 ;
  record: https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb00107581 ; OPAC
  https://opacplus.bsb-muenchen.de/title/BV007846426 . This is the edition the story cites as [^andre1808].
- Files: IIIF https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00107581_00003/full/full/0/default.jpg (title
  page, 4098 x 2929, raw/bsb00107581_00003.jpg) and .../bsb00107581_00005/full/full/0/default.jpg (p. 3, 4086 x
  2930, raw/bsb00107581_00005.jpg).
- Rights statement: the IIIF manifest https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/presentation/v2/bsb00107581/manifest
  (pages/bsb00107581-manifest.json) has "license": "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/"; the record
  page (pages/bsb00107581-details.html) has "license": "https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/" with the
  label "Public Domain Mark 1.0 Universal" (acronym "PDM"). Attribution: "München, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek".
- Flag for the checker: Europeana's older copies of this record (ids /1488/item_LWFRIZLDTJ535VEZZK6IXNF23W6YHLFY and
  /368/item_VQ3VK5CLNSSN6DFJUOIWJRV3IVOO7CH3) still show CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The holder's own current record and
  manifest say PDM, so I follow the holder. Even without the mark, the print is PD by age (below) and a faithful
  scan of it carries no new right under Art. 14 DSM (German UrhG § 68).
- Public domain by age:
  - Printed in Offenbach about 1808 (undated; BSB and Sotheby's give 1808). Mozart died 1791. The publisher Johann
    Anton André died 1842. The engraver is unnamed. The notice on p. 2 is unsigned publisher's text.
  - US: published before 1931, so PD.
  - EU (Germany) and Israel: life plus 70 for every named person expired by 1913; an anonymous 1808 publication ran
    out 70 years after publication.
- What is on the pages (read at full size): 2.jpg, the title page, flourished lettering only (title, "von W. A.
  Mozart", "Komponirt in Baaden den 18ten Junius 1791", "No 2478", "(Partitur und Klavierauszug)", "Preis 30 Xr.",
  "Offenbach a/M, bei Joh. André"); no figures, no ornament beyond pen flourishes. 3.jpg, p. 3, the score's first
  page: staves Violini, Viola, Canto, Alto, Tenore, Basso, Organo e Basso ("tasto solo"), Cembalo, the words "Ave ave
  verum corpus natum", plate number "2478" at the foot, and the library's old stamp "BIBLIOTHECA REGIA MONACENSIS"
  (the holder's own mark; no trademark). Nothing demeaning on either page. The unused notice page (image 4) says the
  Ave may be used at the consecration in the Mass and is one of Mozart's last works.
- Caption facts: story, "Into print." and "Did you know?" ([^andre1808], [^sothebys]): earliest known printing,
  André, Offenbach, undated, placed in 1808, plate 2478, title "Komponirt in Baaden den 18ten Junius 1791" following
  the catalogue's date; old soprano, alto and tenor clefs and the keyboard part labelled "Cembalo". "How it is sung"
  ([^nma]): the strings begin alone.

## Rejected or not used

- Mozart's own thematic catalogue, as printed by André in 1805 (the story's [^cat1805]): the BSB copy
  (bsb10528153, 4 Mus.th. 1860) is marked "https://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-NC/1.0/" in its manifest (a
  Google partnership scan, non-commercial only). The Internet Archive copy (10528153bsb) is a re-upload of the same
  scan with no licence of its own. Dropped.
- Mozart's handwritten catalogue (British Library, Zweig MS 63): the British Library's digitised-manuscripts viewer
  still only redirects to a general page; no item page or rights statement could be read. Commons' "Verzeichnüss
  aller meiner Werke.jpg" shows only the cover and gives its source as just "https://dme.mozarteum.at/". Not used.
- Commons "Ave verum corpus.png" (the autograph): sourced from an IMSLP PDF rather than the holder; replaced by the
  holder's own scan.
- Baden's parish church of St Stephen: every photograph found on Commons is CC BY-SA 3.0 or 4.0. The one CC0 file,
  "Biserica Sf. Ștefan din Baden (Pfarrkirche).jpg", is a scan of a printed reproduction of an old drawing (credited
  to the Rollett Museum Baden) with small drawn human figures, uploaded as "own work": unclear source and drawn
  people. Also, none of the story's sources ties Stoll or the motet to that church (Jahn and the Mozarteum letter
  notes say only "schoolteacher and choirmaster in Baden"), so a church caption could not be sourced. No place image.
- No portraits of Mozart (all painted or engraved).
