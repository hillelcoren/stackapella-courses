# Frère Jacques (Are You Sleeping?): image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; item metadata in notes/. Finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips
metadata); 1.jpg and 2.jpg scaled to 1600 px on the long side, 3.jpg and 4.jpg kept at their native crop size (not
upscaled). A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v. Corel, in
the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive, and in Israel for lack of originality. Every image looked at in full;
none shows a drawn, painted or engraved human figure, a logo or a watermark.

## 1.jpg: La Clé du Caveau (Paris, 1811), p. 309, airs Nos. 724 to 727

- Item: "La clé du Caveau, à l'usage de tous les chansonniers français, des amateurs, auteurs, acteurs du vaudeville
  & de tous les amis de la chanson", Paris, Capelle et Renard [the title page reads "Renand"], de l'Impr. de Richomme,
  1811. Creator "Capelle, Pierre Adolphe, 1772-1851"; "C***". Contributor "Music - University of Toronto"; sponsor
  University of Ottawa; call number AHJ-2346; OCLC 1047476949. No rights or licence field. notes/cle-meta.json.
- Record https://archive.org/details/laclducaveau00cape ; leaf n320 = printed p. 309 (also "39" at the foot, the music
  section's own numbering). File https://archive.org/download/laclducaveau00cape/page/n320.jpg -> raw/cle-n320.jpg.
  The story's own citation: "air No. 726, p. 309 (image n320)".
- PD by age: printed 1811; Capelle died 1851. US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 1921.
- Content: notes only, no words at all; the four numbers 724 to 727, page numbers 309 and 39. Not cropped.
- Caption facts: the story: "Number 726, on page 309, is our tune: one line of notes in G, marked 2 ... with no words";
  "The table of tunes names it 'Air des Matines du frère Jacques, ou frère Jacques, dormez-vous?'" [^cle1811]. I read
  the table myself: raw/cle-n448.jpg (p. 57), last line "des Matines du frère Jacques, ou frère Jacques, dormez-vous?
  .... 726".

## 2.jpg: Vieilles chansons pour les petits enfants (Paris, 1883), p. 18, cropped

- Item: "Vieilles chansons pour les petits enfants", [Paris]: E. Plon, Nourrit et Cie, date 1883. Creators "Widor,
  Charles-Marie, 1844-1937, arr." and "Boutet de Monvel, Louis Maurice, 1850-1913, illustrator". Contributor McGill
  University Library (Rare Books; "Children's Collection - By Consultation M1993 W53 V5 1900z"); OCLC 217264948. No
  rights or licence field. notes/widor-mcgill-meta.json. Note: McGill's call number ends "1900z", so this copy may be a
  later printing of the 1883 book; the Met's copy (mma_vieilles_chansons_pour_les_petits_enfants_346549) is dated 1883.
  The pages are the ones the story cites (McGill copy, images n21 and n22).
- Record https://archive.org/details/McGillLibrary-rbsc_vieilles-chansons-petits-enfants_M1993W53V51900z-25297 ; leaf
  n21 = p. 18. File .../page/n21.jpg -> raw/widor-n21.jpg (3505 x 3084, the open book on a dark cushion).
- PD by age: published 1883 (or about 1900 for this copy); Widor died 1937, Boutet de Monvel 1913. US: published
  before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 2007 (Widor) and 1983 (Boutet de Monvel).
- Crop, for the no-figures rule: the page frames the music with Boutet de Monvel's drawing of friars hauling on bell
  ropes (two kneeling friars at the sides, a standing friar from behind, and the robes and feet of three more under the
  music panel). Cropped to x 1122 to 2681, y 499 to 2334 of the scan: only the music panel and the strip of three bells
  in their wooden frame above it. Checked the cropped file: no part of any figure (no hands, robes, heads or feet); the
  heading "FRÈRE JACQUES." lies outside the panel and is cut too. Page 19 (raw/widor-n22.jpg) has a friar at each side
  of the panel and could only be cropped the same way, so it is not used.
- Every word read: "Frère Jacques, Frère Jacques, dormez-vous? Dormez-vous? Sonnez les matines, Sonnez les ma-" and
  "PIANO." Nothing else.
- Caption facts: the story: "The earliest page with the French words and the tune together that we have read is in
  Vieilles chansons ... 1883 with piano parts by Charles-Marie Widor"; "a canon for three voices in G, each coming in
  two bars after the last" [^widor1883]. Visible on the crop: voice 2 enters at bar 3, voice 3 at bar 5.

## 3.jpg: Normal Instructor and Primary Plans, February 1916, p. 22, middle column cropped

- Item: "Normal Instructor and Primary Plans 1916-02: Vol 25 Iss 4", date 1916-02, publisher field "Scholastic Inc."
  (the later owner of the title), collections pub_instructor / sim_microfilm / periodicals; digitized from microfilm.
  No rights or licence field. notes/sim_instructor_1916-02_25_4-meta.json.
- Record https://archive.org/details/sim_instructor_1916-02_25_4 ; leaf n23 = printed p. 22 (header "22 ... NORMAL
  INSTRUCTOR AND PRIMARY PLANS ... February 1916"). File .../page/n23.jpg -> raw/normal1916-n23.jpg. The story cites
  "pp. 21 to 22 (images n22 and n23)".
- PD by age: published in the US, February 1916 (before 1931). The article's author Peter W. Dykema died 1951, so
  life plus 70 ended 2021 in the EU and Israel; the round texts in the crop are anonymous and traditional ("Early to
  Bed" is the Franklin proverb). PD everywhere we need.
- Crop: x 1528 to 2653, y 456 to 1466 of the scan: the four song texts at the head of the middle column only. Every
  word read: "Oh, How Lovely Is the Evening" (bells ringing), "Are You Sleeping" (as in the alt), "Early to Bed and
  Early to Rise", "Three Blind Mice" (the farmer's wife and the carving knife). Nothing demeaning. The rest of the page
  (the song list, which names minstrel-era songs, and essays on America and The Star Spangled Banner) is cut away.
- Caption facts: the story: "The earliest printing of the English words we have found is in a teachers' magazine,
  Normal Instructor and Primary Plans, in February 1916. Peter W. Dykema ... listed 'Are You Sleeping' among five
  rounds for a National Week of Song ... and printed its words" [^normal1916].

## 4.jpg: Rodeheaver's Sociability Songs (Chicago, 1928), p. 121, cropped

- Item: "Rodeheaver's Sociability Songs", creator Homer A. Rodeheaver, date 1928 ("Dated 1928 from copyright date"),
  collection opensource (community upload). licenseurl https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ (the
  uploader's Public Domain Mark; our case rests on age, below). notes/rodeheavers-sociability-songs-meta.json.
- Record https://archive.org/details/rodeheavers-sociability-songs ; leaf n122 = p. 121. File .../page/n122.jpg ->
  raw/rodeheaver-n122.jpg. The story cites "p. 121 ... (image n122)" as the page our tune is read from.
- Rights checked, as the coordinator asked (Rodeheaver died 1955):
  - US: published 1928 with notice; every US work published before 1931 is public domain (1928 works since 1 January
    2024).
  - EU: life plus 70 from Rodeheaver's death in 1955 ran to 31 December 2025, so public domain since 1 January 2026;
    the EU's rule of the shorter term (US term already over) gives the same answer. No arranger or other author is
    named on the page; the rounds are traditional.
  - Israel: life plus 70, also over at the end of 2025.
  - Caveat for the lead: countries with longer terms (Mexico life plus 100, Colombia life plus 80, for example) may
    still protect the book's compilation or engraving. The brief's test is the US, the EU and Israel, which pass; if
    the site withholds by country, this image could follow the same rule as the song page (the song itself is read
    from this page).
- Crop: x 106 to 1368, y 832 to 1683: "Three Blind Mice" with its note "(The two rounds below may be sung together)"
  and "Are You Sleeping?" only. Cut away, for the no-mocking rule: the four alternative texts at the foot, one of which
  ("Perfect Posture") ends "Hide that hump! Hide that hump!"; also "Lovely Evening" and "Good Night" above. Faint
  show-through of the reverse side is the scan's. Every word in the crop read; nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: the story: "By 1928 it was in Homer Rodeheaver's Sociability Songs, the book our tune is read from";
  "the 1928 page numbers four entries"; "Our 1928 page prints Three Blind Mice and Are You Sleeping one above the
  other, both in E-flat, with a note that the two rounds may be sung together" [^rodeheaver1928].
