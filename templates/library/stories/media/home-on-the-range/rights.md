## 1.jpg: Alexander Gardner, "On the Great Plains, in Kansas, near Monument", about 1867

- Holder's own record: Boston Public Library via Digital Commonwealth,
  https://ark.digitalcommonwealth.org/ark:/50959/70796d97t (read as the record's JSON from
  https://www.digitalcommonwealth.org/search.json?q=%22near+Monument%22+Gardner and the IIIF manifest
  https://ark.digitalcommonwealth.org/ark:/50959/70796d97t/manifest).
- Title: "On the Great Plains, in Kansas, near Monument, 385 miles west of Missouri River." Series: "Across the continent
  on the Kansas Pacific Railroad: route of the 35th parallel", plate No. 41. Local accession 10_08_000041. Albumen print,
  image 14.9 x 20.3 cm. Dates: created [ca. 1867], issued [ca. 1869]. Photographer: Gardner, Alexander, 1821-1882.
- Rights words in the record: rights "No known copyright restrictions."; licence "No known restrictions on use.";
  rights statement "No Copyright - United States" (http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-US/1.0/). The IIIF manifest's
  "Terms of Use" repeats "No known copyright restrictions." and "No known restrictions on use."
- Record note: some plates attributed to Gardner "may have been taken by one of the other photographers Gardner
  supervised on the expedition". Either way the photograph is from 1867.
- Downloaded: https://iiif.digitalcommonwealth.org/iiif/2/commonwealth:70796d983/1430,990,2340,1730/full/0/default.jpg
  (the photograph itself, cut from the 5251 x 3484 scan of the mounted print), then trimmed to the print's edge (no mount
  text and no library stamp in the final file).
- PD reasoning:
  - US: published about 1869, before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel (life + 70): Gardner died 1882, so public domain since 1953. If a supervised assistant took it, anyone
    photographing in 1867 died long before 1955; as an anonymous work, 70 years from publication (about 1869) ran out
    about 1939.
  - A faithful scan of a flat print adds no new copyright.
- Content check: open grassland and sky; a few distant animals and one grazing animal at right. No people, no text.
- Place: the plate puts Monument 385 miles west of the Missouri River, in western Kansas, far from Smith County; the caption says
  only "the open Kansas plains near Monument" and does not claim it shows Higley's country.

## 3.jpg: The Kirwin Chief, 26 February 1876, front page, first column

- Seen in: Kirke Mechem, "Home on the Range", The Kansas Historical Quarterly 17, no. 4 (November 1949), plate between
  pp. 328 and 329, whose contents list reads "reproduction of an 1876 printing of the poem in the Kirwin Chief, between
  pp. 328, 329". Internet Archive item sim_kansas-historical-quarterly_1949-11_17_4 (microfilm scan, publisher Kansas
  State Historical Society), leaf https://archive.org/download/sim_kansas-historical-quarterly_1949-11_17_4/page/n21.jpg
  (2092 x 3380).
- The final file is a crop of only the newspaper's first column (from the "PLAGIARISM." heading to the end of the first
  verse and chorus of "Western Home."), 520 x 1040, not enlarged. It holds none of the 1949 journal's own text (its
  caption, "First page of the Kirwin Chief of February 26, 1876 ... the earliest publication yet found", is left out).
- PD reasoning:
  - The newspaper page was printed 26 February 1876 (masthead date "FEBRUARY 26, 1876", "VOL. 3.", as
    shown on the facing half of the same plate, leaf n22). Anything published in the US before 1931 is public domain. The poem's author,
    Higley, died 1911. The unsigned editorial note is an anonymous 1876 work (70 years from publication ran out in 1946).
    So: public domain in the US, the EU and Israel.
  - The 1949 reproduction is a photograph of a flat public-domain page and adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel;
    EU: Article 14 of Directive 2019/790; the same reasoning used for page scans throughout these sets). The journal's
    own 1949 copyright, whatever its status, covers its own text, which the crop leaves out.
- Words checked (every word in the crop): the heading "PLAGIARISM.", a paragraph saying the editor of the Stockton News
  had printed the poem on 3 February 1876 under the name of a Rooks County woman, that it was "written by Dr. B.
  Higley, of Beaver creek, Smith county, Kansas, and first published in the Kirwin Chief, March 21st, 1874", and that
  "Bro. Newell must look to his laurels"; then "Western Home." "BY DR. HIGLEY." and the first verse and chorus. Nothing
  demeaning. The 1876 woman is named in the print (as in Mechem); she died long ago and the caption does not name her.
- Rest of the same page, deliberately cut out: the right-hand columns carry a printed poem with the slur "squaw"
  ("Behind the scared squaw's birch canoe") and a Sumner speech calling Kansas "open only to the savage", plus a drawn
  "Homestead scene" engraving. None of it is in the crop.

## 4.jpg: Lomax, Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads (1910), "Home on the Range", music, first page

- Item: https://archive.org/details/cowboysongsother00lomauoft (University of Toronto, Robarts Library, call number
  AET-4603; "Digitized by the Internet Archive in 2007 with funding from Microsoft Corporation"). Leaf n72, an unnumbered
  music plate after p. 40 (the words are on pp. 39 to 40, leaves n70 and n71).
- Downloaded: https://archive.org/download/cowboysongsother00lomauoft/page/n72.jpg (2014 x 3320 class page image).
- Title page: "COWBOY SONGS AND OTHER FRONTIER BALLADS / COLLECTED BY JOHN A. LOMAX ... STURGIS & WALTON COMPANY 1910";
  verso: "Copyright 1910 By STURGIS & WALTON COMPANY / Set up and electrotyped. Published November, 1910".
- IA record: possible-copyright-status "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"; copyright-evidence "visible notice of copyright; stated date
  is 1910".
- Who made the music: Lomax, quoted by Mechem 1949 pp. 315 to 316: "Henry Leberman, a blind teacher of music at the State
  School for the Blind in Austin, a few weeks afterwards set down the music ... This music, printed in the 1910 edition of
  Cowboy Songs, makes up the core of the tune that has become popular." Corcoran (michaelcorcoran.net, "Saving Home on
  the Range") gives Lebermann's death "at age 68 in 1941" and quotes his widow that he "arranged the proper music,
  harmonizing chords and runs".
- PD reasoning:
  - US: published November 1910, before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU and Israel (life + 70): Lomax died 1948 (PD since 2019), Lebermann 1941 (since 2012), Higley 1911, Kelley 1905. No
    other contributor is credited for the music.
  - Faithful scan of a flat page; no new copyright.
- Words on the page: the title and the first verse only ("Oh, give me a home where the buf-fa-lo roam ... Where sel-dom
  is heard a dis-cour-ag-ing word"). The book's p. 39 (leaf n70) carries the "red man" verse; that page is not used.

## Rejected

- Lomax 1910 p. 39 (words page): carries the "red man" verse; the music page was used instead.
- Full Kirwin Chief front page (1876): slur "squaw" in a printed poem, "open only to the savage" in a printed speech,
  and a drawn engraving; only the first column is used.
- Portraits of Higley and Kelley in the 1949 Kansas Historical Quarterly (facing pp. 328 and 329): photographs lent by
  family, date and photographer unknown, first publication and renewal status not established. Not clearly OK, dropped.
- Kansas Memory (Kansas Historical Society) Higley photograph, item 207843: the item page returned an empty 202 to a
  plain request, so no rights statement could be read. Dropped, per the brief.
- University of Kansas real photo postcard of the cabin (ku-kscoll:1804, RH PH P2317): undated, photographer named, rights
  "Copyright Not Evaluated" (rightsstatements.org CNE). Dropped.
- Gardner plates on Commons from BPL's Flickr (CC BY 2.0 tags): not an accepted licence; the BPL's own record (used for
  1.jpg) states no known restrictions instead.
- Other Gardner Kansas plates (Missouri Historical Society, NYPL stereo near Fort Hays): show groups of men and
  railcars; the empty plain fits the song better.
- The 1925 San Antonio sheet music: Mechem names Oscar J. Fox of San Antonio as its arranger. Fox's death year was not
  checked, and a 1925 arrangement is not clearly public domain under life + 70 in the EU and Israel; no scan was sought.
  Not used.
- The Kirwin Chief on Chronicling America / loc.gov: loc.gov answered 403 (bot check) to plain requests, so it could not
  be searched from here.
