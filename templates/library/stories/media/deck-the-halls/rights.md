  [^dwb-parry]). "Earliest known printing under that name" is used because the story also reports one researcher's
  untitled earlier copies (1761, about 1752) ([^handyfinch1]).

- Item: IA metadata: title "Pennsylvania School Journal 1877-12: Vol 26 Iss 6"; publisher "Pennsylvania State
  Education Association"; date 1877-12; collections pub_pennsylvania-school-journal, sim_microfilm (the Internet
  Archive's Serials in Microfilm). Item https://archive.org/details/sim_pennsylvania-school-journal_1877-12_26_6 ;
  page https://archive.org/details/sim_pennsylvania-school-journal_1877-12_26_6/page/n39/mode/1up (printed p. 226,
  leaf 39); file https://archive.org/download/sim_pennsylvania-school-journal_1877-12_26_6/page/n39.jpg
  (2760 x 4072), raw/ia-psj1877-12-p226-n39.jpg. The final is cropped (sips) to the page head, the two text columns
  above the carol, and the carol; the text below is cut.
- Rights: no licence on the IA record, so PD by age:
  - Published December 1877. US: before 1931, so PD.
  - The carol's reviser and arranger are not named: anonymous, protected in the EU and Israel 70 years from
    publication, which ended 1947. If the reviser was the journal's long-time editor J. P. McCaskey, he died 1935
    (Wikipedia, citing his obituary), so life plus 70 ended 2005. Oliphant (died 1873) and the traditional tune are
    long PD.
  - The holder of the filmed copy is not named; for a 1877 printed page whose every author is long dead, and a
    faithful copy of a flat page, that does not affect its status.
- What it shows: type and music. No figures, no marks.
- Caption facts: the page ("Welsh Air", four parts on two staves, "Don we now our gay apparel"); story: "In December
  1877 the Pennsylvania School Journal printed the carol in four parts as a 'Welsh Air', with Oliphant's drinking
  lines gone" and "The line is an American replacement, in print by 1877, for Oliphant's 'Fill the mead-cup, drain
  the barrel'" ([^psj1877]).

## Rejected

- Edward Jones, Musical and Poetical Relicks (1784), pp. 29-30: the only scan the story cites is an Internet Archive
  microfilm copy (bim_eighteenth-century_...) with no holder named and a hard black-and-white film image. Parry 1781
  makes the same point better, from a named holder with a PDM statement.
- Jones 1794 (Oxford copy, Google scan): fine by rights (IA "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"), but a third tune printing was more
  than the page needs.
- John Thomas, Welsh Melodies (1862), No. 15: the story's facsimile is a PDF hosted by Handy Finch, not a holder; the
  IMSLP page is vol. 1 only. Not used.
- The Golden Book of Favorite Songs (Chicago, 1923), the page our arrangement reads: PD in the US, but its six
  editors (John W. Beattie and others) were working music educators in 1923 and I did not establish their death
  years, so life plus 70 is not shown to have ended in the EU and Israel. Dropped.
- Met Welsh triple harp 15.117 ("18th century (?)", no maker named, also CC0): not viewed; 89.4.1087 was preferred
  because it is dated and attributed.
