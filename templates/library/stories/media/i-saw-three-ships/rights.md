# I Saw Three Ships: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata), long side 1600 px.
Records and working files in notes/. A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new copyright: in the US
under Bridgeman v. Corel, in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive, and in Israel for lack of originality.

## 1.jpg: Sandys, Christmas Carols, Ancient and Modern (London, 1833), p. 112

- Item: "Christmas carols, ancient and modern; including the most popular in the west of England, and the airs to which
  they are sung. Also specimens of French provincial carols...", London, R. Beckley, 1833. Creator "Sandys, William,
  1792-1874". Contributor University of California Libraries (sponsor MSN); call number nrlf_ucb:GLAD-17147400. IA
  field "possible-copyright-status": NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT; no licence or rights field. Description "Photocopy" (UC's copy is
  a photocopy of the 1833 book). Metadata saved as notes/sandys-meta.json.
- Record: https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsa00sandrich . Page 112 is leaf 260 in page_numbers.json
  (notes/sandys-pages.json), i.e. https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n259/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n259.jpg , saved as raw/sandys-n259.jpg. The same page
  the story's `original:` link points at (page/112).
- PD by age: published 1833; Sandys died 1874. US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1944.
- Every word read: the end of the previous carol ("Then up to Heaven I did ascend, ... May come unto the general dance.
  Sing, oh! &c."), then "I SAW THREE SHIPS." and verses 1 to 4 ("Our Saviour Christ and his lady" in verse 3). Nothing
  demeaning. Type only; no figures, logos or watermarks. Not cropped.
- Caption facts: the page itself, and the story: "William Sandys printed nine verses in London in 1833" and "Sandys has
  'Our Saviour Christ and his lady' instead" [^sandys1833].

## 2.jpg: Sandys, the same book, air No. 9, "I Saw Three Ships"

- Same item and evidence as 1.jpg. Leaf n348, https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n348/mode/1up ;
  file https://archive.org/download/christmascarolsa00sandrich/page/n348.jpg , saved as raw/sandys-n348.jpg. The story
  cites this image ("the air No. 9 ... image n348").
- PD by age as 1.jpg (published 1833, Sandys d. 1874).
- Cropped to the top 1040 px from y 140 (of 3554): the heading and the two systems of No. 9 only. The rest of the page
  (No. 10 "Joseph was an old man", No. 11 "When Jesus Christ was twelve") is cut away; their words, read in full, were

- Caption facts: the page (No. 9, 3/4, the words); the story: "At the back of the book is the air, tune No. 9, 'I Saw
  Three Ships', in G major and 3/4 time, with dotted rhythms over a bass line" and "an earlier form of its tune"
  [^sandys1833].

## 3.jpg: Bramley and Stainer, Christmas Carols New and Old, complete edition, No. 64, p. 164

- Item: https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsn00lond (metadata notes/ccno-meta.json). Contributor Harold B. Lee
  Library, sponsor Brigham Young University, call number 4260580, OCLC 1569820353, scanned 2024. No licence, rights or
  copyright-status field. The IA record is mis-titled "Christmas carols new and old. Second series", date 1880, but the
  scan is the complete edition: 198 images, No. 64 on p. 164, and its title page (OCR in notes/ccno-djvu.txt) reads
  "Christmas Carols New and Old ... The music edited by Sir John Stainer, M.A., Mus. Doc. ... London: Novello and
  Company, Limited and Novello, Ewer and Co., New York". This is the copy the story cites as [^ccno-complete].
- Page: https://archive.org/details/christmascarolsn00lond/page/n169/mode/1up ; file
  https://archive.org/download/christmascarolsn00lond/page/n169.jpg , saved as raw/ccno-n169.jpg. Not cropped.
- PD by age: Stainer's setting was first published in London in October 1878 (Musical Times, 1 October 1878,
  [^mt1878oct]); this printing is undated but after 1888 (Stainer knighted 1888; DNB 1912, [^dnb-stainer]). John
  Stainer died 1901, Henry Ramsden Bramley 1916. US: first published 1878, before 1931 (a later reprint adds nothing).
  EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended in 1986 at the latest. Tune and words traditional.
- Every word read: "64 I saw three Ships.", "Briskly", "mf", "1. I saw three ships come sail-ing in, On Christ-mas day,
  on Christ-mas day; I saw three ships come sail-ing in, On Christ-mas day in the morn-ing,", "Or this (in 3 parts).",
  the same words again, "( 164 )". Nothing demeaning; no figures, logos or watermarks.
- Caption facts: the story ("In the later complete edition, No. 64 fills pages 164 and 165, marked 'Briskly'"; "its
  title page names 'Sir John Stainer', so it was printed after he was knighted"; "On 1 October 1878 Novello advertised
  that series as ready on 15 October"; "the alto, tenor and bass are adapted from Stainer's harmony").

## Looked at and not used

- Cantus, Songs and Fancies (Aberdeen, 1666), the ship verse: the IA copy is an Early English Books 1641-1700 microfilm
  (bim_ collection), a commercial microfilm; not used without a clear free statement. Two Sandys images already show
  the source our story leans on.
- Husk, Songs of the Nativity, p. 190 (the 6/8 tune): would make a fifth image; four is the limit.
- Matson matpc.06760 (Bethlehem view with seated people in the middle ground) and matpc.04038, 12198 (dated 1934 to
  1939 and 1898 to 1946): 06762 is cleaner and has the narrower early date.
- Only one photograph is used (4.jpg), as the extra rule asks.

## Checker's changes (IMAGE-CHECK-8)

- 3.jpg caption (en, es) rewritten to say only what the printed page shows, as the lead asked: "No. 64, "I saw three
  Ships", on page 164 of an undated printing of Bramley and Stainer's Christmas Carols New and Old: John Stainer's
  four-part setting, marked "Briskly", first published in October 1878, from which our arrangement is adapted." The
  words "complete edition printed after Stainer was knighted in 1888" are gone from the caption. The date is now
  "undated (setting first published 1878)", and the source line notes that the IA record is catalogued as "Second
  series", 1880, pp. 46-91, while this scan runs to No. 64 on p. 164. The old media.json is in dropped/.
- 4.jpg correction to the note above: the photograph does show people, very small. At full size (7204 px) there is
  a group of about eight figures standing on a terrace in front of the large building at the left, a single walker
  on a path below them, and perhaps one or two more specks on the right-hand terraces. In the 1600 px final each is
  a few pixels tall and none is recognisable. They were photographed between 1898 and 1914, so none is a living
  person, and no rule bars incidental people in a real photograph (the bans are on drawn figures and living people).
  The image is kept. The LoC MARC record was re-read through SRU on 2026-11-13: 540 "No known restrictions on
  publication.", 110 "American Colony (Jerusalem). Photo Department, photographer.", 260 "[between 1898 and 1914]".

- Lead, 2026-11-13: 4.jpg (Bethlehem, LoC matpc-06762) dropped: at full size it shows a group of people that cannot be cropped out; place photos carry no people.
