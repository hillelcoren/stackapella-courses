# The First Noel: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; finals made with sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata), at most 1600 px on
the long side. Record pages and metadata saved in pages/. Tools I wrote (a PDF page renderer and text dump) are in
_tools/, not in raw/.

Three images are scans of flat printed pages, public domain by age. A faithful scan of a flat page adds no new
copyright: in the US under Bridgeman v. Corel, and in the EU under Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive and for lack of
originality; Israel likewise requires originality. The fourth is a Library of Congress print with a "No known
restrictions" advisory, and public domain by age as well.

## 1.jpg: Gilbert, Some Ancient Christmas Carols, second edition (London, 1823), Carol IX, p. 28

- File: the PDF the story cites, https://hymnologyarchive.com/s/Gilbert-SomeAncientChristmasCarols-1823-2ndEd.pdf ,
  saved as raw/gilbert1823.pdf (143 PDF pages). PDF p. 1 is Google Books' cover leaf ("This is a reproduction of a
  library book that was digitized by Google ..."); PDF p. 3 reads "THE PENNSYLVANIA STATE UNIVERSITY LIBRARIES" (the
  holding copy). PDF p. 69 is printed p. 28, rendered at 4x to raw/gilbert1823-p28-pdf69.png.
- I could not find the Google Books ID: the Books API refused (daily quota) and HathiTrust's catalogue returns 403 to
  plain fetches. The PDF is the scan the story cites [^gilbert1823]; a checker with a browser may add the Google or
  HathiTrust id.
- Title page (PDF text): "SOME ANCIENT CHRISTMAS CAROLS ... TWO ANCIENT BALLADS, A DIALOGUE, &c. COLLECTED BY DAVIES
  GILBERT, F. R. S. F. A. S. &c. THE SECOND EDITION. LONDON: PRINTED BY JOHN NICHOLS AND SON, 25, PARLIAMENT STREET.
  1823."
- PD by age: printed 1823. The words are traditional (no known author); the collector Davies Gilbert died 1839
  (DNB, cited in the story). US: published before 1931. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 1909; an anonymous
  traditional text has no term left either.
- Google's "Digitized by Google" mark at the foot and the black edge at the right are cropped off; the Google cover
  leaf is not used. Precedent: IMAGE-CHECK-2 accepted Google scans with the Google mark cropped off. Google's usage
  request on its cover leaf is a request, not a licence term on a public-domain work.
- What it shows: type only: "28 A COLLECTION OF", "CAROL IX.", verses I to IV with "CHORUS." No figures, no logos.
  I read every word: the four verses (shepherds, star, wise men, Bethlehem), nothing demeaning. "Bethlehem Jury" is
  Bethlehem in Jewry (Judea), as the story explains.
- Caption facts: page 28, Carol IX, words only with no music, "certain poor Shepherds" in verse I: all on the page
  and in the story [^gilbert1823]; "earliest known printing of the words": story lead and Timeline [^gilbert1823]
  [^cam-blog][^summerly].

- Caption facts: 3/8, two sharps (D major), bass staff below: on the page; "earliest known printing of the tune",

- Also downloaded and not used: raw/sandys1833-p74-n221.jpg, the words on p. 74 ("Was to three poor Shepherds"); fine

## 4.jpg: Newlyn, general view, Cornwall (photochrom, between about 1890 and 1900)

- Holder's record: https://www.loc.gov/item/2002696597/ . loc.gov answers plain fetches with a Cloudflare challenge,
  so I read it in the Wayback Machine:
  http://web.archive.org/web/20250217072002/https://www.loc.gov/item/2002696597/ , saved as
  pages/loc-2002696597-wayback-20250217.html. Fields: Title "[Newlyn, general view, Cornwall, England]"; Created /
  Published "[between ca. 1890 and ca. 1900]."; Medium "1 photomechanical print : photochrom, color."; Call
  Number/Physical Location "LOT 13415, no. 249 [item] [P&P]"; Source Collection "Views of the British Isles"; Notes
  "Title from the Detroit Publishing Co., Catalogue J foreign section, Detroit, Mich. : Detroit Publishing Company,
  1905." and "Print no. \"10748\"."; Digital Id "ppmsc 08227"; Reproduction Number "LC-DIG-ppmsc-08227 (digital file
  from original)"; **Rights Advisory "No known restrictions on reproduction."**
- File: the Library's own 1024 px copy, https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/pnp/ppmsc/08200/08227v.jpg ,
  saved as raw/loc-ppmsc-08227v-newlyn.jpg (1024 x 761); the master TIFF on tile.loc.gov 404s. For more pixels I used
  Wikimedia Commons' rendering of LoC's master TIFF, File:Newlyn,_general_view,_Cornwall,_England-LCCN2002696597.tif
  (3668 x 2727; Commons licence "Public domain", credit "Library of Congress", catalogue http://lccn.loc.gov/2002696597),
  1920 px rendering saved as raw/commons-loc-ppmsc-08227-newlyn-2400.jpg. Same image as the LoC copy (checked side by
  side: same frame, same "10748. NEWLYN, GENERAL VIEW." caption).
- PD by age as well: a photochrom made from a photograph, published by about 1900 (in Detroit Publishing's 1905
  catalogue), no photographer named (the photochroms were made by Photoglob of Zurich and Detroit Publishing). US:
  published before 1931. EU and Israel: an anonymous work, 70 years from publication ended by 1975. A photochrom is a
  photomechanical colour print from a photographic negative, so this is a photograph of a place, not a drawing.
- What it shows: stone and timber cottages with slate roofs in the foreground, the harbour with fishing boats and a
  long pier, the bay and a town in the distance. No people visible at full size, no drawn figures, no logos or
  trademarks. Printed text: "10748. NEWLYN, GENERAL VIEW." only.
- Caption facts: "Newlyn ... in the far west of Cornwall" (Newlyn is in Penwith, beside Penzance; the record says
  "Cornwall"); "a fishing town": the harbour of fishing boats in the picture. The quotation is the story's, How it is
  sung: "especially in the western parts of Cornwall", singers still went "from house to house" [^sandys1833].
