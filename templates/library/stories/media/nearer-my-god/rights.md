# Nearer, My God, to Thee: image rights evidence

Checked 2026-11-13. Originals in raw/; IA metadata and the Conway text and scandata saved in pages/. Finals made with
sips only (BMP round trip strips metadata; resized to 1600 px tall at formatOptions 82). 1.jpg is cropped with
sips -c 2910 1904 --cropOffset 150 0 to drop the top margin and a black ink blot and scan smudge below the hymn's
second verse (no words lost; the page ends there). Total 1,116,026 bytes (1.jpg 261,692; 2.jpg 181,626; 3.jpg 422,127;
4.jpg 250,581). No photograph in this set, so no photo is shared with another song page; no figures of any kind.

Makers check (song file templates/library/nearer-my-god.json): Sarah Flower Adams (words, died 1848) and Lowell
Mason (music and arrangement, died 1872). Every person whose text, music or editing is on a page used here died
before 1926 (latest: Edwards A. Park, 1900; Moncure D. Conway, 1907), so each page is out of copyright under life
plus 100 (Mexico) as well as in the US (published before 1931), the EU, UK and Israel (life plus 70). A faithful scan
of a flat printed page adds no new copyright (US: Bridgeman v. Corel; EU: Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive).

  date 1841, contributor "New York Public Library", sponsor Google, "possible-copyright-status: NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT".
  A Google scan: Google's usage note asks for non-commercial use, a request on the file, not a licence on a
  public-domain page (as accepted for Maoz Tzur and Come, Ye Thankful People). No Google watermark on this page.
- PD by age: printed 1841; Adams (1805 to 1848); editor W. J. Fox (1786 to 1864). The LXXXIV text above is Psalm 55:6

- Words on the page, all read: "LXXXIV." "O that I had wings like a dove! Then would I flee away, and be at rest As
  for me, I will call upon God, And he will save me. Evening, and morning, and at noon, Will I pray and cry aloud; And
  he will hear my voice." "LXXXV." verse 1 ("... Still all my song would be ...") and verse 2 ("Though like the
  wanderer, The sun gone down, Darkness be over me, My rest a stone; Yet in my dreams I'd be ..."). Nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: No. 85 in Fox's Hymns and Anthems 1841, earliest known printing, words only, "would be" in verse 1
  (story intro, "The words", "Versions and other words"; [^fox1841][^julian]).

## 2.jpg: Conway, Centenary History of the South Place Society (1894), plate of the draft

- Item https://archive.org/details/cu31924029481342 , file
  https://archive.org/download/cu31924029481342/page/n77.jpg (1316 x 2079), raw/conway-n77.jpg; second page n78

- Rights: the scan's own front leaf (pages/conway.txt): "The original of this book is in the Cornell University
  Library. There are no known copyright restrictions in the United States on the use of the text." Call number
  BX9934 .C76 (same leaf). IA metadata: creator "Conway, Moncure Daniel, 1832-1907", date 1894.
- The book's List of Illustrations (pages/conway.txt): "MANUSCRIPT OF 'NEARER, MY GOD, TO THEE' From the Original
  Draft of the Hymn sent by the Author to Mr. Fox, and now in the possession of Mrs. Bridell Fox."
- PD by age: handwriting of Adams (died 1848), written 1840, first published in this facsimile 1894; Conway died 1907.
  Not an unpublished manuscript (published 1894), so no publication-right question.
- The plate shows only handwriting: verses 1 to 3, each with "Response. Nearer my God to thee, Nearer to thee",
  corrections above the line. Nothing demeaning. The book's drawings of Adams (n70, looked at) and of Eliza
  Flower (named in its List of Illustrations) are NOT used: drawn figures.
- Caption facts: sent to Fox, reproduced by Conway (List of Illustrations; story "The words", [^conway]); November
  1840 (n78 of the plate); choir and congregation answering each other (story "The tune", [^conway][^dnb-eliza]).
