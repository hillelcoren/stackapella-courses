  - Brahms died 1897: no personality-right issue; nobody living is shown.
- What is in the picture: Brahms alone, standing, studio curtain; printed mount. No other people, no logos beyond
  the studio's name, nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: date, place and photographer from the record; "traces this print back to Brahms himself" is the
  record's provenance, "Vorbesitzer*in: Brahms, Johannes". The story dates the lullaby to July 1868 ("The lullaby.").
- Reuse: no other song page is by Brahms; this photograph is used on no other song page.

## 2.jpg and 3.jpg: the first edition, Simrock, Berlin, 1868 (Lübeck, Brahms-Institut, Inv. 1999.11.7e)

  Datensatz: Brahms-Institut an der Musikhochschule Lübeck, CC0 1.0". The LIDO gives all 16 images

  1808; the publisher's firm is not an author. EU and Israel: life plus 70 ended 1967. US: published 1868, public
  domain. A faithful scan of a flat public-domain page adds no new right (UrhG § 68, DSM Directive Art. 14), and the
  holder waives any in any case (CC0).
- What is on the pages (read at full size):
  - Title page: green border of rococo scrolls, roses, leaves and vines; no figures, faces, animals or emblems. Text:
    the title, the song lists of Op. 46, 47, 48 and 49 (titles, poets and first lines; "Böhmisch", "Magyarisch",

    Berlin, Jägerstrasse 18". A later blue crayon underline under "OP. 49". Nothing demeaning.
  - p. 11: "Wiegenlied. (An B. F. in Wien.)", "Zart bewegt", "Joh. Brahms, Op. 49. No 4", Singstimme and Pianoforte,
    three flats, 3/4, the one German verse, closing repeat sign, plate number 323 at the foot. Show-through of the
    facing page is faint. Nothing demeaning.
- Caption facts: story, "Into print." ("An B. F. in Wien"; "aus Simrock's Kinderbuch"; No. 4 of Op. 49), "The words."
  (one verse), "How it is sung" (Zart bewegt, E-flat major, 3/4; "the first edition has one verse, with a repeat
  sign to sing it again"); all visible on the pages.

## Considered and dropped

- Simrock's German and English issue, about 1874 (BSB 4 Mus.pr. 2012.658, bsb11179423), the first "Lullaby and good
  night": the IIIF manifest's licence is https://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-NC/1.0/ (non-commercial). Dropped.
- Des Knaben Wunderhorn vol. 3, 1808 (BSB bsb11259171, the story's [^wunderhorn] copy): also NoC-NC. Other copies
  (Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin Yd 4416-3 R; DDB records) could not be read (JavaScript or bot checks); not used.
- The IMSLP / Internet Archive mirror of the first edition (imslp-lieder-op49-brahms-johannes): no licence on the
  item; replaced by the holder's CC0 images above.
- Brahms's autographs of the Wiegenlied: Moscow (Glinka Museum), Hamburg (SUB, Brahms-Archiv), a Swiss private
  collection: no free digitisation with a stated licence found.
- The Brahms-Portal's work record for the Wiegenlied (dc00007214): its media are "CC BY-SA 4.0". Not used.
- Other 1867 to 1869 photographs (Brahms with Joachim, 1867; with Reinthaler, Bremen 1868; with Allgeyer and Levi,
  about 1869): they show other people; the solo 1868 portrait is the better fit and the page's one photo.
