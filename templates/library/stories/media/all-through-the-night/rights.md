# All Through the Night: rights evidence for each image

Checked 2026-11-13. Every image was opened and looked at: no drawn, painted or engraved human figures, no living people, no logos. Every word on the two printed pages was read: love-song and lullaby words only, nothing that demeans anyone. One photograph in the set (3.jpg, an object, no people).

## 1.jpg: Edward Jones, Musical and Poetical Relicks of the Welsh Bards (London, 1784), p. 56, "Ar hŷd y nos"

- Item: https://archive.org/details/bib_fict_4103341 (The Johns Hopkins University Sheridan Libraries, Bibliotheca Fictiva, bib_num 4103341; JHU catalogue record https://catalyst.library.jhu.edu/permalink/01JHU_INST/1lu78g9/alma991039124119707861). Page 56 is image n73: https://archive.org/details/bib_fict_4103341/page/n73/mode/1up
- Downloaded from: https://archive.org/download/bib_fict_4103341/page/n73.jpg (3716 x 5148). Cropped to the page (the camera's dark surround removed), then resized.
- Item record (archive.org metadata): date "1784"; publisher "London : Printed for the Author"; creator "Jones, Edward ['Bardd y Brenin'] ; de Loutherbourg, Philip J. (Philip James) (ill.) ; Hall, John (engr.) ; Middiman, Samuel (engr.) ; Thornthwaite (engr.)". The record has no rights field.
- The named illustrators and engravers made the frontispiece and the instrument plate, which we do NOT use (the frontispiece has figures). Page 56 is engraved music and words only.
- Public domain by age: printed 1784. Edward Jones died 1824 (Dictionary of Welsh Biography, cited by the story); the music engraver is not named.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: life plus 70; Jones's term ended 1894; an unnamed engraver's work counts as anonymous, 70 years from publication, ended 1854.
  - Israel: life plus 70, the same.
- A photograph of a flat public-domain page: no new copyright in the photograph.
- Words on the page: one Welsh verse (beginning "Er bod rhai yn taeru'n galed") and the English "Fain would some with vows persuade me, That my faithful swain has fled me; But my beating heart will falter, Ere it thinks his heart can alter", plus "Chorus / Ar hŷd y nos", "Maestoso", "Variation 1st", "Var: 2d", "Harmonic", "Var: 3d". Nothing demeaning.

## 2.jpg: Songs of the Four Nations (London: J. B. Cramer & Co., 1892), No. 26, "All Through the Night", p. 134

- Item: https://archive.org/details/songsoffournatio00boul (Boston Public Library, call number 39999057094276). Page 134 is image n157: https://archive.org/details/songsoffournatio00boul/page/n157/mode/1up
- Downloaded from: https://archive.org/download/songsoffournatio00boul/page/n157.jpg (2650 x 3360).
- Item record (archive.org metadata): date "1892"; publisher "London : Cramer & Co."; creator "Boulton, Harold Edwin, ed" and "Somervell, Arthur, Sir, 1863-1937"; "Pl. no.: J.B.C. & Co. 10,527". No rights field.
- Public domain by age: published 1892.
  - Harold Boulton, words, died 1935 (LC Name Authority n85013614, "Boulton, Harold, 1859-1935"). Arthur Somervell, arrangement, died 1937 (the item record).
  - G. M. Probert, whose Welsh version is printed on p. 138 and whose death year we do not know, is only NAMED on p. 134 ("Welsh simile by G. M. Probert"); none of his words appear on this page. That is why p. 138 (Boulton's three verses with Probert's Welsh below) was not used.
  - US: published before 1931, so public domain.
  - EU: life plus 70; Somervell's term, the later one, ended 2007.
  - Israel: life plus 70, ended 2007.
- A scan of a flat public-domain page: no new copyright in the scan.
- Words on the page: the headings, credits, "Andante", "Voice", "Piano", and "Sleep my love and peace attend thee, All through the night; Guardian angels God will lend thee, All through the night." Nothing demeaning. No figures or decoration beyond a small star ornament.

## 3.jpg: Welsh Triple Harp, John Richards, Llanrwst, about 1750, The Met 89.4.1087

- Object page: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/501665 (the site itself refused a plain fetch; the record below is from the Met's own API).
- Met Collection API record https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/501665 (saved as raw/met501665.json): "isPublicDomain": true; title "Welsh Triple Harp"; culture "British (Welsh)"; artistDisplayName "John Richards"; artistRole "Maker"; artistDisplayBio "British (born Wales), Llanrwst 1711–1789"; objectDate "ca. 1750"; city "Llanrwst"; medium "Wood, various materials."; creditLine "The Crosby Brown Collection of Musical Instruments, 1889"; primaryImage https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/mi/original/DT267636.jpg (1153 x 1781, downloaded).
- The Met's Open Access terms, https://github.com/metmuseum/openaccess (README, read 2026-11-13): "Companion artworks listed in the dataset covered by the policy are identified in the Collection section of the Museum's website with the Creative Commons Zero (CC0) icon." The API's isPublicDomain flag is how the Met marks those objects.
- This is a 3D object. The photograph is the Met's own and is released under its Open Access (CC0) policy, which covers the photo itself. The maker died 1789.
- Looked at: the harp alone on a plain background; painted gold flecks and stripes on the body and pillar, no figures, no people, no text.
- Caption sources: maker, place and date from the Met record; "Edward Jones, himself a harper, set Ar hŷd y nos for voice and harp, with five harp variations" from the story ([^dnb-jones], [^jones1784], [^hymnarchive]) and the page in 1.jpg. The caption does not claim Jones played this harp or this kind of harp.
